\section{A quadratic Levi with a complete cubic-packet consumer}
\label{sec:quadratic-eisenstein-consumer}

This is a further source-dependent comparison, not a new analytic
estimate or a newly formalized automorphic theorem. It uses
\cite[Propositions 5.9--5.10 and Theorem 5.11]{GaoInverseEis} for
the double Kazhdan--Patterson cover of \(\mathrm{GL}_3\), with
\(p=0,q=1\). The inducing theta representation is on its
\(\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathrm{GL}_1\) Levi. It is not the
nongeneric theta representation on \(\mathrm{GL}_3\) itself.

\paragraph{The exceptional datum and character image.}
Take \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\), so the source hypothesis \(\mu_4\subset K\)
holds. For the upper-triangular bisector with diagonal zero and
off-diagonal parameter one, the central torus has coordinates
\((ta^2,tb^2,t)\). Its cocycle is \((t,u)_2\).
Use the normalized quadratic Weil cochain \(\gamma_\psi\), with
\[
 \gamma_\psi(t)\gamma_\psi(u)
      =(t,u)_2\gamma_\psi(tu),\qquad
 \gamma_\psi(a^2)=1.
\]
Its adelic rational product formula is the usual global Weil-index
input; the local convention is as in \cite[Section 2.1]{PrasannaWeil}.
This input is not formalized here. Define
\[
 \chi_0(\zeta,t_1,t_2,t_3)
    =\epsilon(\zeta)\gamma_\psi(t_3)
      |t_1/t_2|_{\mathbb A}^{1/4}.
\]
The cochain identity proves multiplicativity on the covered centre,
and the product formulas prove rational descent. Its root values are
\[
 (\chi_0)_{\alpha_1}=|\cdot|_{\mathbb A},\qquad
 (\chi_0)_{\alpha_2}=|\cdot|_{\mathbb A}^{-1/2}.
\]
For example the root lifts at \(a^2\) have coordinates
\((a^2,a^{-2},1)\) and \((1,a^2,a^{-2})\); their Weil factors
are \(1\). Both Levi central norm exponents vanish.
Twisting by \(\tau\circ\det_{\mathrm{GL}_2}\) preserves
exceptionality and multiplies the second root character by \(\tau^2\).
Consequently every finite odd-order Hecke character \(\eta\)
is realized on this fixed quadratic field, by choosing \(\tau^2=\eta\).
The source factorization becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}
 E_{\eta,\psi}(s)=B_\eta(s)/L_K^S(2s+3/2,\eta).
\end{equation}
The earlier finite-point window argument applies with its same
explicit analytic hypotheses and no moving norm assertion.

\paragraph{What cancels in the entire unramified integral.}
The integration unipotent is abelian of dimension two. Gao's exact
shell argument leaves the integral region and the joint shell
\((v(x_1),v(x_2))=(-1,-1)\); mixed negative supports vanish.
Write \(Q\) for the residue cardinality,
\(a_v=\chi(h_{\alpha_2}(\varpi^2))\), and \(g\ne0\) for the
normalized nonprincipal quadratic Gauss integral with the source
additive convention. The joint shell, including its Levi value, is
\[
 Q^{-2s}\left(Q^{-1}a_vg^{-1}\right)
                 \left(-Q^{-1}\right)g
       =-a_vQ^{-2s-2}.
\]
The integral region contributes \(1\). Since
\(a_v=Q^{1/2}\eta(v)\), the full result is
\(1-\eta(v)Q^{-2s-3/2}\), as required by
\eqref{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}. No Gauss factor has been bounded
in this calculation: its inverse cancels it with its phase intact.
The principal Ramanujan factor \(-Q^{-1}\) survives.
Thus improving the isolated nonprincipal Gauss norm is not an
independent estimate of this global inverse observable.

\paragraph{A restricted cubic packet with its principal row retained.}
Let \(\chi\) be primitive cubic of prime conductor \(q\), and put
\(\eta_j=\chi^j\circ N_{K/\mathbb Q}\), \(j=0,1,2\).
For \(u=2s+3/2\), define
\[
 F_j(u)=L_{\mathbb Q}^S(u,\chi^j),\quad
 M_j(u)=L_{\mathbb Q}^S(u,\chi^j\chi_{-4}),\quad
 L_j(u)=F_j(u)M_j(u)=L_K^S(u,\eta_j).
\]
Then \(E_j(s)=B_j(s)/L_j(u)\). Put
\(B_* = B_0B_1B_2\) and
\(B_{\ne j}=\prod_{k\ne j}B_k\). Product differentiation gives
the complete signed operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-full-cubic-packet}
 \sum_{j=0}^2 B_{\ne j}(s)L_j(u)E_j'(s)-B_*'(s)
 +2B_*(s)\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{M_j'(u)}{M_j(u)}
 =-2B_*(s)\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{F_j'(u)}{F_j(u)}.
\end{equation}
Indeed each differentiated native identity contributes
\(B_j'-2B_jL_j'/L_j\); the \(B_j'\) terms sum to \(B_*'\),
and \(L_j'/L_j=F_j'/F_j+M_j'/M_j\). This is a meromorphic
identity. It retains the reciprocal \(M_j\), every section derivative,
the row \(j=0\), and all common-window convolutions.
It uses a sum of logarithmic readouts, not a product of three
inverse detectors with all six \(L\)-factor lengths multiplied.
Each row still has its actual two-factor plain companion, and
moments of different rows retain their cross terms.

At a zeta zero the residue on the right, in coordinate \(u\), is
\(-2B_*(s_\rho)(m_\zeta+m_\chi+m_{\chi^2})\).
It is nonzero if the chosen window multiplier is nonzero.
The two nonprincipal characters need not share that zero.
This is a zero-free-packet implication including zeta; a bound for
the whole packet is not asserted equivalent to RH for zeta alone.

Its physical prime-power expression, up to the displayed window
multiplier and finite \(S\)-corrections, is the centred sum
\[
 3\sum_{\substack{p^k\le X,\ p\notin S\\\chi(p)^k=1}}
           \log p-X.
\]
All \(k\ge1\) occur. In particular, when \(3\mid k\), every
\(p\notin S\) occurs, even if \(\chi(p)\ne1\).
The main term is \(X\), not \(3X\), because just the principal
Dirichlet row has a pole at \(u=1\).

\paragraph{An alternative consumer that keeps all six factors.}
For detecting a zeta zero it is unnecessary to subtract the quadratic
companions. Keeping them as positive rows avoids that subtraction.
With a common analytic window \(R\), put \(Z_j=RE_j\). The native
factorization gives the complete alternative operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-positive-six-packet}
 \sum_{j=0}^2 B_{\ne j}L_j Z_j'
       -3R'B_*-RB_*'
   =-2RB_*\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{L_j'}{L_j}.
\end{equation}
All quantities on the left are evaluated at \(s\), and the
logarithmic derivatives on the right at \(u=2s+3/2\).
This is a direct product-rule consequence of
\eqref{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}: each row contributes
\(R'B_*+RB_{\ne j}B_j'-2RB_*L_j'/L_j\).
It retains every section and window derivative. There is no
companion-logarithmic-derivative subtraction in this consumer.

The six rational factors \(F_j,M_j\) all have weight one.
At a zeta zero their orders add, so the native-coordinate residue
is \(-R(s_\rho)B_*(s_\rho)\sum_j(m_{F_j}+m_{M_j})\).
Thus it is nonzero under the explicit window condition
\(R(s_\rho)B_*(s_\rho)\ne0\). This condition and the global native
identity must still be discharged for any analytic application;
the present algebra does not supply them. The unwindowed arithmetic
readout, including every prime power and the principal centering, is
\[
 6\!\!\sum_{\substack{p^k\le X,\ p\notin S\\
             \chi(p)^k=1,\ \chi_{-4}(p)^k=1}}\log p-X.
\]
For the cubic character modulo seven these are exactly the powers
\(p^k\equiv1,13\pmod{28}\), away from the excluded primes.
This changes the observable and its arithmetic estimate, rather than
merely inserting another local coefficient into the old cubic proof.
The three native rows still carry their full degree-two lengths and
all mixed terms. No estimate of this restricted prime-power sum is
deduced from positivity of the zero multiplicities.

\paragraph{The full cost and rate comparison.}
The three native denominators have total degree six and conductor
product \(2^6q^4\): the principal one has conductor \(4\),
and each other one has conductor \(4q^2\). A single nonprincipal
row has degree two, conductor \(4q^2\), and one quadratic-twisted
rational companion isolates its chosen \(F_j\). The logarithmic
packet \eqref{eq:quadratic-full-cubic-packet} uses its actual row
lengths, rather than the length of the inverse-product detector.
This reduces representation and conductor costs compared with the
Sp4 cubic packet, but gives no saving on its own.

The affine window map is now \(Y=X^2\) with amplitude \(2X^{3/2}\),
so a complete spectral rate \(r\) would give boundary \(3/2+2r\).
The corresponding rates for \(7/8,3/4,1/2\) are respectively
\(-5/16,-3/8,-1/2\). Every ramified \(q\)-window, archimedean
norm, reciprocal companion, height and truncation loss must enter
that rate. No cubic \(3/16\) or prefix-sieve bound transfers to it
by identity alone. The independent global estimate remains open;
the certified boundary is \(7/8\). The point of this comparison is
the complete alternative consumer and the exact surviving signed
term, not a claim that a local calculation has improved the boundary.

\subsection{Reference-independent nonreal power compensation}
\label{sec:nonreal-power-compensation}

The power probe admits a stronger signal theorem than the previously used
reference-strip argument. Let \(L_i(s)=L(s,\chi_i)\) be a finite collection
of Dirichlet \(L\)-functions of a common positive modulus, let
\(w_i\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), and let \(G\) be analytic on
\(\Re s>0\). Put
\[
 R(s)=-\sum_i w_i\frac{L_i'(s)}{L_i(s)}+G(s),\qquad
 q_d(s)=\frac{s+d-1}{d},\qquad
 P_dR(s)=R(s)-\frac{s}{s+d-1}R(q_d(s)).
\]
Here \(d>1\) is any fixed real number. If
\(\Re\rho>0\), \(\Im\rho\ne0\), and \(L_{i_0}(\rho)=0\)
for an index of positive weight, then \(P_dR\) is not analytic at
\(\rho\). No zero-free hypothesis at \(q_d(\rho)\), no RH
hypothesis, and no simplicity or disjointness assumption on the zeros
is needed.

\emph{Proof.}
Neither \(\rho\) nor \(q_d(\rho)\) equals \(1\), and both have positive
real part. Local analytic factorization gives finite nonnegative
orders \(n_i\) at \(\rho\) and \(m_i\) at \(q_d(\rho)\).
Set
\[
 A=\sum_iw_i n_i>0,\qquad B=\sum_iw_i m_i\in\mathbb R.
\]
The analytic correction has zero scaled residue. Keeping the reference
zeros, the affine derivative \(q_d'=1/d\), and the complete scalar
factor gives
\[
 \lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}(s-\rho)P_dR(s)
   =r=-A+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}B.
\]
The denominator is nonzero since its real part is positive. Direct
multiplication, without estimating the reference term separately, yields
\[
 \Im\bigl(r(\rho+d-1)\overline{\rho}\bigr)
       =A(d-1)\Im\rho.
\]
The right side is nonzero. Thus \(r\ne0\); analyticity would instead
force the displayed limit to be zero. Moreover the same identity gives
the quantitative certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-residue-height-cost}
 |r|\ \ge\
 \frac{A(d-1)|\Im\rho|}{|\rho+d-1|\,|\rho|}.
\end{equation}
This proves the assertion with every shared-zero multiplicity retained.
\(\square\)

The maintained Lean declarations are
\texttt{CarmichaelWeightedSignal.real\_packet\_scaled\_residue},
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_scaled\_limit},
\texttt{real\_packet\_power\_nonreal\_zero\_not\_analytic}, and
\texttt{powerProbe\_nonreal\_zero\_not\_analytic}, all under
\texttt{PrimeFactorOscillations}. Their owner is
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean}.

Real weights are essential to this proof: the reference residue \(B\)
is real, so its contribution to the displayed imaginary part vanishes.
Nonnegative real parts of arbitrary complex weights do not supply that
argument. The assertion also excludes real target zeros. It is a
one-way signal-preservation theorem, not a pointwise equivalence between
all poles of the compensated probe and target zeros: reference zeros
can introduce additional poles.

\paragraph{Its place in the complete exponent argument.}
The old theorem \texttt{powerProbe\_order\_iff\_zero} uses
\(\Re\rho>1-d/8\) to place the reference in the installed
\(7/8\) zero-free region. The new nonreal theorem removes that premise;
powers \(d=1+\varepsilon\), with fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), are therefore
admissible for signal preservation. It does not remove the physical
reference scale \(x^d\), the height and \(d-1\) losses in
\eqref{eq:power-residue-height-cost}, or any mixed compensation term.
The rational main-pole remainder remains
\(-\kappa(d-1)/(s+d-1)\).

This is not a new ability of the overall detector library to see the
critical strip. The existing three-scale Mellin wavelet already has
multiplier
\[
 m(s)=\frac{(1-2^{-s})(1-2^{1-s})}{6},
\]
which is nonzero for \(0<\Re s<1\). Its constant dilations preserve
critical-strip zeros and cancel the origin and radial principal
Poisson mode. That alternative must be compared with power compensation
using its own arithmetic consumer, rather than the cubic moment
consumer by default.

In particular, for the previously proved fixed smooth compact
character-portfolio estimate, the complete difference between the
power-compensated and original signed quadratic packets is bounded by
\[
 \frac73 K^2\left(
  2D^{(15-d)/8+(d+1)u}+D^{(8-d)/4+2du}\right).
\]
Here \(d\) is a natural number, \(u>0\), \(7/8+u\le2\);
\(K\) depends on the fixed window, character portfolio and depletion
mask, but not on \(D>0\) or the divisor cutoff. This is
\texttt{CarmichaelPowerCompensation.portfolio\_power\_compensation\_bound}.
It retains the mixed terms in the difference and leaves the original
signed packet unestimated. It is not uniform in a growing conductor
or an arbitrary noncompact carrier. At the \(D^{3/2}\) target scale its
first exponent requires \(d\ge3\) as \(u\downarrow0\), with slack
needed at \(d=3\); at the \(D\) scale it requires \(d\ge7\).
Consequently making \(d\) approach one in the new signal theorem cannot
by itself improve this arithmetic consumer. There is also no transfer
here from the natural-power estimate to nonintegral \(d\).

A new global construction may replace both this packet estimate and
the old cubic moment stage. It must supply its own exact transformed
observable, signal normalization, full signed arithmetic bound, and
conductor, height, window and truncation costs. The quadratic
\(\mathrm{GL}_3\) consumer above needs \(r\le-3/8\) to reach \(3/4\);
the unchanged cubic consumer needs a full low error at most
\(X^{1/16+o(1)}\), together with control of every other error.
The theorem proved here supplies signal preservation only. No
independent arithmetic saving or improved QRH boundary is asserted;
the certified boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{Two-point multiplicity recovery and stability}
\label{sec:power-multiplicity-recovery}

The complex residue above contains more information than nonvanishing.
Write \(\gamma=\Im\rho\ne0\), \(a=d-1>0\), and define, for \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 {\cal A}(z)=
 \frac{\Im\!\left(z(\rho+a)\overline\rho\right)}{a\gamma},
 \qquad
 {\cal B}(z)=
 \frac{\Im\!\left(z(\rho+a)\right)/\gamma+{\cal A}(z)}{d}.
\]
For the full signed residue \(r=-A+d\rho B/(\rho+a)\), one has
\[
 {\cal A}(r)=A,\qquad {\cal B}(r)=B.
\]
Indeed, multiplication gives
\[
 r(\rho+a)=\rho(dB-A)-aA,
\]
so its imaginary part is \(\gamma(dB-A)\), while multiplying once
more by \(\overline\rho\) gives imaginary part \(aA\gamma\).
These two equations prove the assertion. They retain the entire
reference contribution, including shared zeros and repeated zeros.
Only reality of \(A,B\) is needed for the inversion. For a single
\(L\)-function they are its two local integer orders; for a weighted
packet they are the two weighted totals, not the individual orders
of its constituent rows.

Both readout maps are continuous real linear functions of \(z\).
Consequently, applying them to
\((s-\rho)P_dR(s)\) recovers \(A\) and \(B\) in the limit.
This is formalized by
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_multiplicity\_recovery}, using the same
local scaled-limit hypotheses as the preceding residue theorem.

The recovery also has an explicit stability cost. For arbitrary
\(z,z'\in\mathbb C\), the exact difference identity implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-multiplicity-stability}
 |a\gamma|\,|{\cal A}(z)-{\cal A}(z')|
 \le |z-z'|\,|\rho+a|\,|\rho|.
\end{equation}
Here the norm estimates only the separate observation error;
no signed arithmetic main term or compensation term is discarded.
Taking \(z=r+e\), \(z'=r\), and \(|e|\le\varepsilon\), gives
\[
 |{\cal A}(r+e)-A|
 \le \varepsilon\,\frac{|\rho+a|\,|\rho|}{a|\gamma|}.
\]
For a single function, a right side below \(1/2\) uniquely
determines the integer \(A\) by rounding. This rounding statement is
an elementary consequence of the displayed proved error bound.
The height cost is explicit and is not uniform as \(\gamma\to0\);
at real targets the two real equations lose rank.

The additional maintained declarations in
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean} are
\texttt{power\_residue\_target\_multiplicity},
\texttt{power\_residue\_reference\_multiplicity}, and
\texttt{powerTargetMultiplicity\_error\_bound}, together with the
limit theorem just named. These are multiplicity readouts, not an
independent upper estimate for their values or a proof of simplicity.

\paragraph{Relation to the thin probability detector.}
The existing \texttt{Helpers/ProbabilityZeroRecovery.lean} uses two
adjacent Bernoulli masses at one moving rank, with known centering
and normalization, at quartic cutoffs \(N=n^4\).
For a qualified quadratic prime law, its eventual comparison is
equivalent to RH. Under RH its harmonic coefficient at frequency
\(\gamma\) is exactly
\[
 \frac{m(1/2+i\gamma)}{1/4+\gamma^2}.
\]
The RH assumption on this reconstruction must be distinguished
from the unconditional logical equivalence.
The single eventual comparison retains less information than the
values of the normalized observable. The generalized compensated
residue retains multiplicity information, but does not by itself
provide the probability-law adapter and harmonic limit for every
new family.

There is also an existing multiplicity energy theorem in
\texttt{Helpers/XiMultiplicityBudget.lean}. Under RH, its excess
energy can be written as
\[
 {\cal D}=
 \sum_{\substack{\rho=1/2+i\gamma\\\text{distinct zeros}}}
 \frac{m(\rho)(m(\rho)-1)}{(1/4+\gamma^2)^2}\ge0,
\]
and \({\cal D}=0\) if and only if every zero is simple.
Thus an independent amplitude or excess-energy bound can yield
multiplicity results without a better QRH exponent. The identities
and readout stability alone do not supply that bound.

\subsection{Integer powers constrain weak zero responses}
\label{sec:integer-power-multiplicity}

Fix the same underlying logarithmic readout \(R\) as in
Section~\ref{sec:nonreal-power-compensation}, and let \(\rho\) be a nonreal
target zero. For an integer \(d\ge2\), write \(m\in\mathbb N\) for its
target order and \(n_d\in\mathbb N\) for the order at \(q_d(\rho)\).
Put \(\gamma=\Im\rho\ne0\), \(C_d=\rho+d-1\), and
\[
 r_d=\lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}(s-\rho)P_dR(s)
       =-m+\frac{d\rho}{C_d}n_d.
\]
For a single \(L\)-function the coefficient of its logarithmic derivative
is normalized to one. The argument also applies to integer-weighted
packets with integral aggregate orders. It does not assume integrality
for general real-weighted aggregates.

There is the following exact alternative:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-residue-alternative}
 m=d n_d
 \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
 |\gamma|\le |r_d|\,|C_d|.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-residue-multiplicity}
 |r_d|\,|C_d|<|\gamma|
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m=d n_d.
\end{equation}
Thus a weak response forces divisibility of the target multiplicity
and identifies the reference order exactly.

\emph{Proof.}
Keep the full reference term and multiply before estimating:
\[
 r_d C_d=\rho(d n_d-m)-(d-1)m,\qquad
 \Im(r_d C_d)=(d n_d-m)\gamma.
\]
If the integer \(d n_d-m\) is nonzero, its absolute value is at least
one. Therefore
\[
 |\gamma|\le |d n_d-m|\,|\gamma|
 =|\Im(r_d C_d)|\le |r_d C_d|=|r_d|\,|C_d|,
\]
which proves both assertions. The norm is applied to the full
compensated residue after its signed reference contribution has been
retained. No separate arithmetic main-term estimate is used.
\(\square\)

Two powers give a joint consequence. Let \(d,e\ge2\) be coprime
integers, apply both compensations to the same \(R\) at the same \(\rho\),
and suppose \(0<m<de\). Then at least one of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-power-response}
 |\gamma|\le |r_d|\,|\rho+d-1|,
 \qquad
 |\gamma|\le |r_e|\,|\rho+e-1|
\end{equation}
holds, irrespective of the two reference orders.
Indeed, if both strict reverse inequalities held,
\eqref{eq:small-residue-multiplicity} would give \(d\mid m\) and
\(e\mid m\). Coprimality would give \(de\mid m\), contrary to
\(0<m<de\). This is a multiplicity restriction from joint weak responses;
it supplies no new upper estimate for those responses.

The maintained declarations are
\texttt{power\_residue\_integer\_separation},
\texttt{power\_residue\_small\_forces\_multiplicity\_relation}, and
\texttt{coprime\_powers\_force\_large\_residue}, under
\texttt{PrimeFactorOscillations.CarmichaelWeightedSignal}.
The companion theorem
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_integer\_residue\_dichotomy}
takes the two actual scaled-limit premises and produces the compensated
limit with \eqref{eq:integer-residue-alternative}. Their owner is
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean}.
No RH, simplicity, or zero-free reference premise is used.
The separate arithmetic upper estimate remains open and the certified
QRH boundary is \(7/8\). No novelty priority is asserted here.

\subsection{Exact Mellin action on general windows}
\label{sec:general-window-mellin}

Let
\[
 \mathcal M W(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}W(x)\,dx,\qquad
 \widetilde W(x)=\frac{W(x)-3W(2x)+2W(4x)}6.
\]
For every \(s\in\mathbb C\) where the first integral is absolutely
convergent, the filtered integral is also absolutely convergent and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-window-mellin}
 \mathcal M\widetilde W(s)
 =\frac{(1-2^{-s})(1-2^{1-s})}{6}\,\mathcal M W(s).
\end{equation}
This is an integral identity for a general window, not only a calculation
on the formal power response \(D^s\).

\emph{Proof.}
For any positive real \(a\), the substitution \(u=ax\) gives
\[
 \mathcal M[W(a\,\cdot)](s)=a^{-s}\mathcal M W(s)
\]
and preserves Mellin convergence. Linearity applied to the three
convergent integrals gives the coefficient
\((1-3\cdot2^{-s}+2\cdot4^{-s})/6\), which factors as in
\eqref{eq:general-window-mellin}. \(\square\)

If \(0<\beta=\Re s<1\), the existing multiplier estimate now applies
directly to the actual integral:
\[
 \frac{(1-2^{-\beta})(2^{1-\beta}-1)}6\,|\mathcal M W(s)|
 \le |\mathcal M\widetilde W(s)|.
\]
Its coefficient is positive. Thus a nonzero base Mellin value survives
the filter. The base value itself is still present in this bound;
no uniform lower bound for it in height is asserted.
The maintained proofs are
\texttt{GaussianMellinWavelet.window\_hasMellin},
\texttt{window\_mellin\_ne\_zero}, and
\texttt{window\_mellin\_norm\_lower}, in
\texttt{Assembly/DetectorPackets/GaussianMellinWavelet.lean}.
The construction of a responsive radial bandlimited base window
remains a separate obligation.

\subsection{An exact band-limited Type I estimate}
\label{sec:bandlimited-type-i}

A different window class removes the principal Type I block exactly.
Here \emph{Gaussian} refers to the lattice \(\mathbb Z[i]\).
An exponential Gaussian test function is not band-limited.

Let \(g\) be a complex Schwartz function on \(\mathbb C\), with
\(\widehat g(0)=0\) and \(\widehat g(\xi)=0\) for \(|\xi|\ge1\).
For every real \(q>0\), \(X>0\), \(q^2\le X\), and \(r\in\mathbb C\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandlimited-coset-zero}
 \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^2}
   g\!\left(\frac{qn+r}{\sqrt X}\right)=0.
\end{equation}
The sum is absolutely convergent. Identifying \(n=n_1+in_2\),
affine Poisson summation gives
\[
 \frac{X}{q^2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^2}
   e^{2\pi i\, k\cdot r/q}\,
   \widehat g\!\left(\frac{\sqrt X}{q}k\right).
\]
The zero frequency vanishes by hypothesis. Every other frequency has
norm at least \(\sqrt X/q\ge1\), and vanishes by support.
Every shift phase remains in this calculation.
For an integer \(q\ge1\), the complete combination of all \(q^2\)
cosets with weights \(\chi(r_1^2+r_2^2)\) therefore also vanishes,
for any Dirichlet character modulo \(q\). The theorem includes
\(q=1,2\) and composite moduli.

Suppose \(g(z)=V(|z|^2)\) and \(V(0)=0\). For nonzero ideals and
a nonzero ideal \(H\), write
\[
 S_H(V;X)=\sum_{\substack{C\ne0\\(C,H)=1}}V(N(C)/X).
\]
The four-unit reindexing and complete divisor mask give
\[
 S_H(V;X)=\sum_{E\mid H}\mu(E)
          \sum_{C\ne0}V\bigl(N(C)/(X/N(E))\bigr).
\]
Hence \(S_H(V;X)=0\) whenever \(N(H)\le X\). For any finite sets
\(A,B\) of nonzero ideals, the entire signed block satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandlimited-type-i-zero}
 \sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}\mu(a)\mu(b)
       S_H\!\left(V;\frac{D}{N(a)N(b)}\right)=0
\end{equation}
if \(D>0\) and \(N(H)N(a)N(b)\le D\) for every retained pair.
All divisor terms, including ramified and repeated factors, remain.
No norm estimate is applied to this main block.
