\section{A quadratic Levi with a complete cubic-packet consumer}
\label{sec:quadratic-eisenstein-consumer}

This is a further source-dependent comparison, not a new analytic
estimate or a newly formalized automorphic theorem. It uses
\cite[Propositions 5.9--5.10 and Theorem 5.11]{GaoInverseEis} for
the double Kazhdan--Patterson cover of \(\mathrm{GL}_3\), with
\(p=0,q=1\). The inducing theta representation is on its
\(\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathrm{GL}_1\) Levi. It is not the
nongeneric theta representation on \(\mathrm{GL}_3\) itself.

\paragraph{The exceptional datum and character image.}
Take \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\), so the source hypothesis \(\mu_4\subset K\)
holds. For the upper-triangular bisector with diagonal zero and
off-diagonal parameter one, the central torus has coordinates
\((ta^2,tb^2,t)\). Its cocycle is \((t,u)_2\).
Use the normalized quadratic Weil cochain \(\gamma_\psi\), with
\[
 \gamma_\psi(t)\gamma_\psi(u)
      =(t,u)_2\gamma_\psi(tu),\qquad
 \gamma_\psi(a^2)=1.
\]
Its adelic rational product formula is the usual global Weil-index
input; the local convention is as in \cite[Section 2.1]{PrasannaWeil}.
This input is not formalized here. Define
\[
 \chi_0(\zeta,t_1,t_2,t_3)
    =\epsilon(\zeta)\gamma_\psi(t_3)
      |t_1/t_2|_{\mathbb A}^{1/4}.
\]
The cochain identity proves multiplicativity on the covered centre,
and the product formulas prove rational descent. Its root values are
\[
 (\chi_0)_{\alpha_1}=|\cdot|_{\mathbb A},\qquad
 (\chi_0)_{\alpha_2}=|\cdot|_{\mathbb A}^{-1/2}.
\]
For example the root lifts at \(a^2\) have coordinates
\((a^2,a^{-2},1)\) and \((1,a^2,a^{-2})\); their Weil factors
are \(1\). Both Levi central norm exponents vanish.
Twisting by \(\tau\circ\det_{\mathrm{GL}_2}\) preserves
exceptionality and multiplies the second root character by \(\tau^2\).
Consequently every finite odd-order Hecke character \(\eta\)
is realized on this fixed quadratic field, by choosing \(\tau^2=\eta\).
The source factorization becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}
 E_{\eta,\psi}(s)=B_\eta(s)/L_K^S(2s+3/2,\eta).
\end{equation}
The earlier finite-point window argument applies with its same
explicit analytic hypotheses and no moving norm assertion.

\paragraph{What cancels in the entire unramified integral.}
The integration unipotent is abelian of dimension two. Gao's exact
shell argument leaves the integral region and the joint shell
\((v(x_1),v(x_2))=(-1,-1)\); mixed negative supports vanish.
Write \(Q\) for the residue cardinality,
\(a_v=\chi(h_{\alpha_2}(\varpi^2))\), and \(g\ne0\) for the
normalized nonprincipal quadratic Gauss integral with the source
additive convention. The joint shell, including its Levi value, is
\[
 Q^{-2s}\left(Q^{-1}a_vg^{-1}\right)
                 \left(-Q^{-1}\right)g
       =-a_vQ^{-2s-2}.
\]
The integral region contributes \(1\). Since
\(a_v=Q^{1/2}\eta(v)\), the full result is
\(1-\eta(v)Q^{-2s-3/2}\), as required by
\eqref{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}. No Gauss factor has been bounded
in this calculation: its inverse cancels it with its phase intact.
The principal Ramanujan factor \(-Q^{-1}\) survives.
Thus improving the isolated nonprincipal Gauss norm is not an
independent estimate of this global inverse observable.

\paragraph{A restricted cubic packet with its principal row retained.}
Let \(\chi\) be primitive cubic of prime conductor \(q\), and put
\(\eta_j=\chi^j\circ N_{K/\mathbb Q}\), \(j=0,1,2\).
For \(u=2s+3/2\), define
\[
 F_j(u)=L_{\mathbb Q}^S(u,\chi^j),\quad
 M_j(u)=L_{\mathbb Q}^S(u,\chi^j\chi_{-4}),\quad
 L_j(u)=F_j(u)M_j(u)=L_K^S(u,\eta_j).
\]
Then \(E_j(s)=B_j(s)/L_j(u)\). Put
\(B_* = B_0B_1B_2\) and
\(B_{\ne j}=\prod_{k\ne j}B_k\). Product differentiation gives
the complete signed operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-full-cubic-packet}
 \sum_{j=0}^2 B_{\ne j}(s)L_j(u)E_j'(s)-B_*'(s)
 +2B_*(s)\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{M_j'(u)}{M_j(u)}
 =-2B_*(s)\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{F_j'(u)}{F_j(u)}.
\end{equation}
Indeed each differentiated native identity contributes
\(B_j'-2B_jL_j'/L_j\); the \(B_j'\) terms sum to \(B_*'\),
and \(L_j'/L_j=F_j'/F_j+M_j'/M_j\). This is a meromorphic
identity. It retains the reciprocal \(M_j\), every section derivative,
the row \(j=0\), and all common-window convolutions.
It uses a sum of logarithmic readouts, not a product of three
inverse detectors with all six \(L\)-factor lengths multiplied.
Each row still has its actual two-factor plain companion, and
moments of different rows retain their cross terms.

At a zeta zero the residue on the right, in coordinate \(u\), is
\(-2B_*(s_\rho)(m_\zeta+m_\chi+m_{\chi^2})\).
It is nonzero if the chosen window multiplier is nonzero.
The two nonprincipal characters need not share that zero.
This is a zero-free-packet implication including zeta; a bound for
the whole packet is not asserted equivalent to RH for zeta alone.

Its physical prime-power expression, up to the displayed window
multiplier and finite \(S\)-corrections, is the centred sum
\[
 3\sum_{\substack{p^k\le X,\ p\notin S\\\chi(p)^k=1}}
           \log p-X.
\]
All \(k\ge1\) occur. In particular, when \(3\mid k\), every
\(p\notin S\) occurs, even if \(\chi(p)\ne1\).
The main term is \(X\), not \(3X\), because just the principal
Dirichlet row has a pole at \(u=1\).

\paragraph{An alternative consumer that keeps all six factors.}
For detecting a zeta zero it is unnecessary to subtract the quadratic
companions. Keeping them as positive rows avoids that subtraction.
With a common analytic window \(R\), put \(Z_j=RE_j\). The native
factorization gives the complete alternative operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-positive-six-packet}
 \sum_{j=0}^2 B_{\ne j}L_j Z_j'
       -3R'B_*-RB_*'
   =-2RB_*\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{L_j'}{L_j}.
\end{equation}
All quantities on the left are evaluated at \(s\), and the
logarithmic derivatives on the right at \(u=2s+3/2\).
This is a direct product-rule consequence of
\eqref{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}: each row contributes
\(R'B_*+RB_{\ne j}B_j'-2RB_*L_j'/L_j\).
It retains every section and window derivative. There is no
companion-logarithmic-derivative subtraction in this consumer.

The six rational factors \(F_j,M_j\) all have weight one.
At a zeta zero their orders add, so the native-coordinate residue
is \(-R(s_\rho)B_*(s_\rho)\sum_j(m_{F_j}+m_{M_j})\).
Thus it is nonzero under the explicit window condition
\(R(s_\rho)B_*(s_\rho)\ne0\). This condition and the global native
identity must still be discharged for any analytic application;
the present algebra does not supply them. The unwindowed arithmetic
readout, including every prime power and the principal centering, is
\[
 6\!\!\sum_{\substack{p^k\le X,\ p\notin S\\
             \chi(p)^k=1,\ \chi_{-4}(p)^k=1}}\log p-X.
\]
For the cubic character modulo seven these are exactly the powers
\(p^k\equiv1,13\pmod{28}\), away from the excluded primes.
This changes the observable and its arithmetic estimate, rather than
merely inserting another local coefficient into the old cubic proof.
The three native rows still carry their full degree-two lengths and
all mixed terms. No estimate of this restricted prime-power sum is
deduced from positivity of the zero multiplicities.

\paragraph{The full cost and rate comparison.}
The three native denominators have total degree six and conductor
product \(2^6q^4\): the principal one has conductor \(4\),
and each other one has conductor \(4q^2\). A single nonprincipal
row has degree two, conductor \(4q^2\), and one quadratic-twisted
rational companion isolates its chosen \(F_j\). The logarithmic
packet \eqref{eq:quadratic-full-cubic-packet} uses its actual row
lengths, rather than the length of the inverse-product detector.
This reduces representation and conductor costs compared with the
Sp4 cubic packet, but gives no saving on its own.

The affine window map is now \(Y=X^2\) with amplitude \(2X^{3/2}\),
so a complete spectral rate \(r\) would give boundary \(3/2+2r\).
The corresponding rates for \(7/8,3/4,1/2\) are respectively
\(-5/16,-3/8,-1/2\). Every ramified \(q\)-window, archimedean
norm, reciprocal companion, height and truncation loss must enter
that rate. No cubic \(3/16\) or prefix-sieve bound transfers to it
by identity alone. The independent global estimate remains open;
the certified boundary is \(7/8\). The point of this comparison is
the complete alternative consumer and the exact surviving signed
term, not a claim that a local calculation has improved the boundary.

\subsection{Reference-independent nonreal power compensation}
\label{sec:nonreal-power-compensation}

The power probe admits a stronger signal theorem than the previously used
reference-strip argument. Let \(L_i(s)=L(s,\chi_i)\) be a finite collection
of Dirichlet \(L\)-functions of a common positive modulus, let
\(w_i\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), and let \(G\) be analytic on
\(\Re s>0\). Put
\[
 R(s)=-\sum_i w_i\frac{L_i'(s)}{L_i(s)}+G(s),\qquad
 q_d(s)=\frac{s+d-1}{d},\qquad
 P_dR(s)=R(s)-\frac{s}{s+d-1}R(q_d(s)).
\]
Here \(d>1\) is any fixed real number. If
\(\Re\rho>0\), \(\Im\rho\ne0\), and \(L_{i_0}(\rho)=0\)
for an index of positive weight, then \(P_dR\) is not analytic at
\(\rho\). No zero-free hypothesis at \(q_d(\rho)\), no RH
hypothesis, and no simplicity or disjointness assumption on the zeros
is needed.

\emph{Proof.}
Neither \(\rho\) nor \(q_d(\rho)\) equals \(1\), and both have positive
real part. Local analytic factorization gives finite nonnegative
orders \(n_i\) at \(\rho\) and \(m_i\) at \(q_d(\rho)\).
Set
\[
 A=\sum_iw_i n_i>0,\qquad B=\sum_iw_i m_i\in\mathbb R.
\]
The analytic correction has zero scaled residue. Keeping the reference
zeros, the affine derivative \(q_d'=1/d\), and the complete scalar
factor gives
\[
 \lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}(s-\rho)P_dR(s)
   =r=-A+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}B.
\]
The denominator is nonzero since its real part is positive. Direct
multiplication, without estimating the reference term separately, yields
\[
 \Im\bigl(r(\rho+d-1)\overline{\rho}\bigr)
       =A(d-1)\Im\rho.
\]
The right side is nonzero. Thus \(r\ne0\); analyticity would instead
force the displayed limit to be zero. Moreover the same identity gives
the quantitative certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-residue-height-cost}
 |r|\ \ge\
 \frac{A(d-1)|\Im\rho|}{|\rho+d-1|\,|\rho|}.
\end{equation}
This proves the assertion with every shared-zero multiplicity retained.
\(\square\)

The maintained Lean declarations are
\texttt{CarmichaelWeightedSignal.real\_packet\_scaled\_residue},
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_scaled\_limit},
\texttt{real\_packet\_power\_nonreal\_zero\_not\_analytic}, and
\texttt{powerProbe\_nonreal\_zero\_not\_analytic}, all under
\texttt{PrimeFactorOscillations}. Their owner is
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean}.

Real weights are essential to this proof: the reference residue \(B\)
is real, so its contribution to the displayed imaginary part vanishes.
Nonnegative real parts of arbitrary complex weights do not supply that
argument. The assertion also excludes real target zeros. It is a
one-way signal-preservation theorem, not a pointwise equivalence between
all poles of the compensated probe and target zeros: reference zeros
can introduce additional poles.

\paragraph{Its place in the complete exponent argument.}
The old theorem \texttt{powerProbe\_order\_iff\_zero} uses
\(\Re\rho>1-d/8\) to place the reference in the installed
\(7/8\) zero-free region. The new nonreal theorem removes that premise;
powers \(d=1+\varepsilon\), with fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), are therefore
admissible for signal preservation. It does not remove the physical
reference scale \(x^d\), the height and \(d-1\) losses in
\eqref{eq:power-residue-height-cost}, or any mixed compensation term.
The rational main-pole remainder remains
\(-\kappa(d-1)/(s+d-1)\).

This is not a new ability of the overall detector library to see the
critical strip. The existing three-scale Mellin wavelet already has
multiplier
\[
 m(s)=\frac{(1-2^{-s})(1-2^{1-s})}{6},
\]
which is nonzero for \(0<\Re s<1\). Its constant dilations preserve
critical-strip zeros and cancel the origin and radial principal
Poisson mode. That alternative must be compared with power compensation
using its own arithmetic consumer, rather than the cubic moment
consumer by default.

In particular, for the previously proved fixed smooth compact
character-portfolio estimate, the complete difference between the
power-compensated and original signed quadratic packets is bounded by
\[
 \frac73 K^2\left(
  2D^{(15-d)/8+(d+1)u}+D^{(8-d)/4+2du}\right).
\]
Here \(d\) is a natural number, \(u>0\), \(7/8+u\le2\);
\(K\) depends on the fixed window, character portfolio and depletion
mask, but not on \(D>0\) or the divisor cutoff. This is
\texttt{CarmichaelPowerCompensation.portfolio\_power\_compensation\_bound}.
It retains the mixed terms in the difference and leaves the original
signed packet unestimated. It is not uniform in a growing conductor
or an arbitrary noncompact carrier. At the \(D^{3/2}\) target scale its
first exponent requires \(d\ge3\) as \(u\downarrow0\), with slack
needed at \(d=3\); at the \(D\) scale it requires \(d\ge7\).
Consequently making \(d\) approach one in the new signal theorem cannot
by itself improve this arithmetic consumer. There is also no transfer
here from the natural-power estimate to nonintegral \(d\).

A new global construction may replace both this packet estimate and
the old cubic moment stage. It must supply its own exact transformed
observable, signal normalization, full signed arithmetic bound, and
conductor, height, window and truncation costs. The quadratic
\(\mathrm{GL}_3\) consumer above needs \(r\le-3/8\) to reach \(3/4\);
the unchanged cubic consumer needs a full low error at most
\(X^{1/16+o(1)}\), together with control of every other error.
The theorem proved here supplies signal preservation only. No
independent arithmetic saving or improved QRH boundary is asserted;
the certified boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{Two-point multiplicity recovery and stability}
\label{sec:power-multiplicity-recovery}

The complex residue above contains more information than nonvanishing.
Write \(\gamma=\Im\rho\ne0\), \(a=d-1>0\), and define, for \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 {\cal A}(z)=
 \frac{\Im\!\left(z(\rho+a)\overline\rho\right)}{a\gamma},
 \qquad
 {\cal B}(z)=
 \frac{\Im\!\left(z(\rho+a)\right)/\gamma+{\cal A}(z)}{d}.
\]
For the full signed residue \(r=-A+d\rho B/(\rho+a)\), one has
\[
 {\cal A}(r)=A,\qquad {\cal B}(r)=B.
\]
Indeed, multiplication gives
\[
 r(\rho+a)=\rho(dB-A)-aA,
\]
so its imaginary part is \(\gamma(dB-A)\), while multiplying once
more by \(\overline\rho\) gives imaginary part \(aA\gamma\).
These two equations prove the assertion. They retain the entire
reference contribution, including shared zeros and repeated zeros.
Only reality of \(A,B\) is needed for the inversion. For a single
\(L\)-function they are its two local integer orders; for a weighted
packet they are the two weighted totals, not the individual orders
of its constituent rows.

Both readout maps are continuous real linear functions of \(z\).
Consequently, applying them to
\((s-\rho)P_dR(s)\) recovers \(A\) and \(B\) in the limit.
This is formalized by
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_multiplicity\_recovery}, using the same
local scaled-limit hypotheses as the preceding residue theorem.

The recovery also has an explicit stability cost. For arbitrary
\(z,z'\in\mathbb C\), the exact difference identity implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-multiplicity-stability}
 |a\gamma|\,|{\cal A}(z)-{\cal A}(z')|
 \le |z-z'|\,|\rho+a|\,|\rho|.
\end{equation}
Here the norm estimates only the separate observation error;
no signed arithmetic main term or compensation term is discarded.
Taking \(z=r+e\), \(z'=r\), and \(|e|\le\varepsilon\), gives
\[
 |{\cal A}(r+e)-A|
 \le \varepsilon\,\frac{|\rho+a|\,|\rho|}{a|\gamma|}.
\]
For a single function, a right side below \(1/2\) uniquely
determines the integer \(A\) by rounding. This rounding statement is
an elementary consequence of the displayed proved error bound.
The height cost is explicit and is not uniform as \(\gamma\to0\);
at real targets the two real equations lose rank.

The additional maintained declarations in
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean} are
\texttt{power\_residue\_target\_multiplicity},
\texttt{power\_residue\_reference\_multiplicity}, and
\texttt{powerTargetMultiplicity\_error\_bound}, together with the
limit theorem just named. These are multiplicity readouts, not an
independent upper estimate for their values or a proof of simplicity.

\paragraph{Relation to the thin probability detector.}
The existing \texttt{Helpers/ProbabilityZeroRecovery.lean} uses two
adjacent Bernoulli masses at one moving rank, with known centering
and normalization, at quartic cutoffs \(N=n^4\).
For a qualified quadratic prime law, its eventual comparison is
equivalent to RH. Under RH its harmonic coefficient at frequency
\(\gamma\) is exactly
\[
 \frac{m(1/2+i\gamma)}{1/4+\gamma^2}.
\]
The RH assumption on this reconstruction must be distinguished
from the unconditional logical equivalence.
The single eventual comparison retains less information than the
values of the normalized observable. The generalized compensated
residue retains multiplicity information, but does not by itself
provide the probability-law adapter and harmonic limit for every
new family.

There is also an existing multiplicity energy theorem in
\texttt{Helpers/XiMultiplicityBudget.lean}. Under RH, its excess
energy can be written as
\[
 {\cal D}=
 \sum_{\substack{\rho=1/2+i\gamma\\\text{distinct zeros}}}
 \frac{m(\rho)(m(\rho)-1)}{(1/4+\gamma^2)^2}\ge0,
\]
and \({\cal D}=0\) if and only if every zero is simple.
Thus an independent amplitude or excess-energy bound can yield
multiplicity results without a better QRH exponent. The identities
and readout stability alone do not supply that bound.

\subsection{Integer powers constrain weak zero responses}
\label{sec:integer-power-multiplicity}

Fix the same underlying logarithmic readout \(R\) as in
Section~\ref{sec:nonreal-power-compensation}, and let \(\rho\) be a nonreal
target zero. For an integer \(d\ge2\), write \(m\in\mathbb N\) for its
target order and \(n_d\in\mathbb N\) for the order at \(q_d(\rho)\).
Put \(\gamma=\Im\rho\ne0\), \(C_d=\rho+d-1\), and
\[
 r_d=\lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}(s-\rho)P_dR(s)
       =-m+\frac{d\rho}{C_d}n_d.
\]
For a single \(L\)-function the coefficient of its logarithmic derivative
is normalized to one. The argument also applies to integer-weighted
packets with integral aggregate orders. It does not assume integrality
for general real-weighted aggregates.

There is the following exact alternative:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-residue-alternative}
 m=d n_d
 \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
 |\gamma|\le |r_d|\,|C_d|.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-residue-multiplicity}
 |r_d|\,|C_d|<|\gamma|
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m=d n_d.
\end{equation}
Thus a weak response forces divisibility of the target multiplicity
and identifies the reference order exactly.

\emph{Proof.}
Keep the full reference term and multiply before estimating:
\[
 r_d C_d=\rho(d n_d-m)-(d-1)m,\qquad
 \Im(r_d C_d)=(d n_d-m)\gamma.
\]
If the integer \(d n_d-m\) is nonzero, its absolute value is at least
one. Therefore
\[
 |\gamma|\le |d n_d-m|\,|\gamma|
 =|\Im(r_d C_d)|\le |r_d C_d|=|r_d|\,|C_d|,
\]
which proves both assertions. The norm is applied to the full
compensated residue after its signed reference contribution has been
retained. No separate arithmetic main-term estimate is used.
\(\square\)

Two powers give a joint consequence. Let \(d,e\ge2\) be coprime
integers, apply both compensations to the same \(R\) at the same \(\rho\),
and suppose \(0<m<de\). Then at least one of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-power-response}
 |\gamma|\le |r_d|\,|\rho+d-1|,
 \qquad
 |\gamma|\le |r_e|\,|\rho+e-1|
\end{equation}
holds, irrespective of the two reference orders.
Indeed, if both strict reverse inequalities held,
\eqref{eq:small-residue-multiplicity} would give \(d\mid m\) and
\(e\mid m\). Coprimality would give \(de\mid m\), contrary to
\(0<m<de\). This is a multiplicity restriction from joint weak responses;
it supplies no new upper estimate for those responses.

The maintained declarations are
\texttt{power\_residue\_integer\_separation},
\texttt{power\_residue\_small\_forces\_multiplicity\_relation}, and
\texttt{coprime\_powers\_force\_large\_residue}, under
\texttt{PrimeFactorOscillations.CarmichaelWeightedSignal}.
The companion theorem
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_integer\_residue\_dichotomy}
takes the two actual scaled-limit premises and produces the compensated
limit with \eqref{eq:integer-residue-alternative}. Their owner is
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean}.
No RH, simplicity, or zero-free reference premise is used.
The separate arithmetic upper estimate remains open and the certified
QRH boundary is \(7/8\). No novelty priority is asserted here.

\subsection{Unconditional multiplicity energy survives compensation}
\label{sec:power-multiplicity-energy}

Here the symmetry of the actual zeta zero set gives a global estimate,
rather than only a pointwise residue identity. For \(T\in\mathbb R\), let
\[
 S_T=\{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\quad 0<\Re\rho<1,\quad
                         0<\Im\rho\le T\},
 \qquad m(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta.
\]
The elements of \(S_T\) are distinct zeros; multiplicity is carried by
\(m\), not by repetition in the set. This set is finite unconditionally.
If \(T\le0\), it is empty. Write \(\gamma_\rho=\Im\rho\).

\emph{Theorem (multiplicity-energy coercivity).}
For every real \(d\ge2\), put
\[
 q_d(\rho)=\frac{\rho+d-1}{d},\qquad
 a_\rho=\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho},\qquad
 b_\rho=\frac{d\,m(q_d(\rho))}{\gamma_\rho},
 \qquad E_T=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}a_\rho^2.
\]
At a nonzero value of zeta the analytic order is zero. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-multiplicity-energy}
 \frac{E_T}{12}\le
 H_{d,T}:=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}(a_\rho-b_\rho)^2 .
\end{equation}
There is no RH, zero-free-strip, simplicity, or zero-free-reference
hypothesis.

\emph{Proof.}
Conjugation of zeta and its functional equation preserve analytic
multiplicities and give
\[
 m(1-\overline\rho)=m(\rho),\qquad
 \Im(1-\overline\rho)=\gamma_\rho.
\]
Consider the finite support
\(P=\{\rho\in S_T:m(q_d(\rho))\ne0\}\), and its image
\(Q=q_d(P)\). The map is injective. Every \(\tau=q_d(\rho)\in Q\)
is a zeta zero, with
\[
 \tfrac12<\Re\tau<1,\qquad
 0<\Im\tau=\gamma_\rho/d\le T/d\le T.
\]
Thus \(Q\subseteq S_T\), and its reflection
\(Q^*=\{1-\overline\tau:\tau\in Q\}\) is a disjoint subset of
\(S_T\) with exactly the same multiplicity-height energy. Keeping the
complete reference support, including all reference zeros, gives
\[
 B_{d,T}:=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}b_\rho^2
 =\sum_{\tau\in Q}\frac{m(\tau)^2}{(\Im\tau)^2}
 \le\frac12\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)^2}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 =\frac{E_T}{2}.
\]
The cross term is not estimated separately. Direct expansion gives
the exact certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-energy-certificate}
 12H_{d,T}
 =E_T+2\sum_{\rho\in S_T}(2a_\rho-3b_\rho)^2
       +3(E_T-2B_{d,T}).
\end{equation}
Both retained corrections are nonnegative, proving
\eqref{eq:power-multiplicity-energy}. \(\square\)

This estimate applies to the actual logarithmic readout. Let
\(G:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) be analytic at every point of
\(0<\Re s<1\), and define
\[
 R_G(s)=-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}+G(s),\qquad
 P_dR_G(s)=R_G(s)-\frac{s}{s+d-1}R_G(q_d(s)).
\]
For \(d>1\) and a nonreal \(\rho\) in that strip, local analytic
factorization at both \(\rho\) and \(q_d(\rho)\) gives the punctured limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-power-multiplicity-residue}
 r_d(\rho):=\lim_{\substack{s\to\rho\\s\ne\rho}}
                 (s-\rho)P_dR_G(s)
 =-m(\rho)+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}m(q_d(\rho)).
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(\zeta(s)=(s-\rho)^m u(s)\), with \(u\) analytic and
nonzero near \(\rho\), gives residue \(-m\) for \(R_G\).
The affine substitution contributes the factor \(d\) at the reference.
Both points remain inside the strip; no analyticity at the pole \(1\)
is required of \(G\). The rational denominator is nonzero because
\(\rho\) is nonreal.

Keeping the full residue and taking one real projection yields
\[
 \frac{\Im(r_d(\rho)(\rho+d-1))}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 =\frac{d\,m(q_d(\rho))-m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho}
 =b_\rho-a_\rho.
\]
Consequently, for every real \(d\ge2\) and \(T\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-power-readout-energy}
 \frac1{12}\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)^2}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \le
 \sum_{\rho\in S_T}
 \left(\frac{\Im(r_d(\rho)(\rho+d-1))}{\gamma_\rho^2}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
The result also holds for any function of residues specified by the
actual punctured limits, by uniqueness of those limits.

Thus a single real projection retains a fixed proportion of the
multiplicity energy, even when target and reference zeros collide.
This is an unconditional lower bound on the detector response, or
equivalently an inverse estimate for multiplicity energy from that
response. An independent upper bound for the right-hand side would
bound the left-hand side. No such new arithmetic upper bound, numerical
multiplicity bound, simplicity theorem, or zero-free improvement is
claimed here. The cutoff \(T\) is a zero-height cutoff, not a sampling
or divisor cutoff. The sums include original zeta zeros only, and do
not assert an identity for every additional reference-only detector
pole. General complex-character portfolios require their own
conjugation and reflection adapters.

\subsection{Signed height weights and surviving multiplicity carriers}
\label{sec:power-weighted-trace}

The squared-energy estimate has a signed linear companion, uniform over
an entire class of height weights. Keep the finite set \(S_T\), the map
\(q_d\), and the actual residues \(r_d\) from
Section~\ref{sec:power-multiplicity-energy}. Let \(d\ge2\), \(T\in\mathbb R\),
\(c\ge0\), and \(w:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-trace-weight}
 w(t)\ge0,\qquad d\,w(dt)\le c\,w(t)\qquad(t>0).
\end{equation}
No continuity or differentiability of \(w\) is required. Define
\[
 A_w=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}m(\rho)w(\gamma_\rho),\quad
 B_w=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}d\,m(q_d(\rho))w(\gamma_\rho),\quad
 H_w=A_w-B_w.
\]
These sums use ordinary multiplicity, rather than its square.

\emph{Theorem (weighted signed trace and carrier).}
Under \eqref{eq:power-trace-weight},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-weighted-trace}
 (2-c)A_w\le2H_w
 =-2\sum_{\rho\in S_T}
       \frac{w(\gamma_\rho)}{\gamma_\rho}
       \Im\bigl((\rho+d-1)r_d(\rho)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Moreover, let
\[
 C_{d,T}=\{\rho\in S_T:
              \Im((\rho+d-1)r_d(\rho))<0\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-weighted-carrier}
 \sum_{\rho\in C_{d,T}}m(\rho)w(\gamma_\rho)
 \ge (1-c/2)A_w.
\end{equation}
The lower proportions are positive when \(c<2\) and \(A_w>0\).
Both assertions are unconditional for the normalized zeta readout
with any correction analytic throughout the open critical strip.

\emph{Proof.}
Retain the complete nonzero reference support
\(P=\{\rho\in S_T:m(q_d(\rho))>0\}\) and \(Q=q_d(P)\).
The previously proved geometry gives an injection onto \(Q\), with
\(Q\) lying in the right half of \(S_T\). Changing variables in the
finite sum gives the exact transport identity
\[
 B_w=d\sum_{\tau\in Q}m(\tau)w(d\gamma_\tau)
       \le c\sum_{\tau\in Q}m(\tau)w(\gamma_\tau).
\]
Reflection supplies a disjoint left copy of the complete right-half
zero set, preserving both multiplicity and height. Nonnegativity
therefore gives
\[
 2\sum_{\tau\in Q}m(\tau)w(\gamma_\tau)\le A_w,\qquad
 2B_w\le cA_w.
\]
No separate norm of the signed trace is introduced: subtract exactly,
\[
 2H_w-(2-c)A_w=cA_w-2B_w\ge0.
\]
The full canonical residue
\eqref{eq:canonical-power-multiplicity-residue} satisfies
\[
 \Im((\rho+d-1)r_d(\rho))
   =\gamma_\rho\bigl(d\,m(q_d(\rho))-m(\rho)\bigr),
\]
which proves the equality in \eqref{eq:power-weighted-trace}.
The actual punctured limits equal these canonical residues by the
proved scaled-limit theorem and uniqueness of limits.

For the carrier assertion, put
\(U=\{\rho\in S_T:m(\rho)\le d\,m(q_d(\rho))\}\).
On this precise support the nonnegative weights give
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in U}m(\rho)w(\gamma_\rho)
 \le\sum_{\rho\in U}d\,m(q_d(\rho))w(\gamma_\rho)
 \le B_w\le(c/2)A_w.
\]
The displayed imaginary-part identity and \(\gamma_\rho>0\) identify
\(C_{d,T}=S_T\setminus U\). Subtracting its complement mass from
\(A_w\) proves \eqref{eq:power-weighted-carrier}. \(\square\)

Two explicit weight choices are checked in Lean. For \(w(t)=1/t\),
the scaling constant is \(c=1\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-height-signed-trace}
 \sum_{\rho\in S_T}
       \frac{m(\rho)-d\,m(q_d(\rho))}{\gamma_\rho}
 \ge\frac12\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho}.
\end{equation}
For \(w(t)=1/t^2\), the scaling constant is \(c=1/d\), giving
\[
 H_{1/t^2}\ge(1-1/(2d))A_{1/t^2},\qquad
 \sum_{\rho\in C_{d,T}}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \ge(1-1/(2d))\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}.
\]
In particular the last all-\(d\) theorem implies, at \(d=2\), that at
least three quarters of the inverse-square-height multiplicity mass
has the strict desired residue sign. This is a weighted mass fraction,
not an unweighted density of zeros and not a zero-free exponent.

Thus the generalized detector retains a quantitatively large carrier
for one signed real projection, even with reference-zero collisions.
It remains necessary to prove an independent upper estimate for that
response to obtain a numerical multiplicity upper bound or a simplicity
result. No QRH exponent improvement follows from these lower estimates
alone; the installed boundary remains \(7/8\). The finite support remains
the original zero set \(S_T\), with \(T\) a zero-height cutoff.

\subsection{Sharp joint carriers for coprime affine powers}
\label{sec:coprime-power-carrier-sharpness}

For the actual zeta readout, the preceding theorem immediately gives
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in C_{d,T}\cup C_{e,T}}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \ge \left(1-\frac1{2e}\right)
       \sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \qquad(2\le d<e).
\]
The union counts each zero once, and both residues come from the same
readout. The following construction shows how closely this coefficient
can be approached by finite symmetric spectra, even while every full
complex zero signal survives.

\emph{Theorem (coprime-power spectral sharpness).}
Let \(d,e\) be coprime integers with \(2\le d<e\).
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a finite positive-height spectrum
in the open critical strip, invariant under
\(\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho\), with positive natural multiplicities
preserved by reflection, for which the joint strict-sign carrier of the
\(d\)- and \(e\)-power compensations has inverse-square-height
multiplicity fraction less than
\[
 1-\frac1{2e}+\varepsilon.
\]
The readout is an actual finite logarithmic-derivative sum.
At every spectral point, its full compensated residue is nonzero for
every real power \(c>1\), including when the affine reference is another
spectral point. More precisely, the explicit models below satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-carrier-gap}
 0\le \frac{U_n}{A_n}-\left(1-\frac1{2e}\right)
 \le \frac1e\left(\frac de\right)^n
 \qquad(n\ge0).
\end{equation}

\emph{Proof.}
For \(0\le i,j\le n\), put
\[
 \gamma_{ij}=(e/d)^i e^j,\qquad
 \mu_{ij}=e^{i+j}d^{n-i},\qquad
 \delta_n=\frac1{4\gamma_{nn}},
\]
and take both points
\[
 \rho^R_{ij}=1-\delta_n\gamma_{ij}+\mathrm i\gamma_{ij},
 \qquad
 \rho^L_{ij}=\delta_n\gamma_{ij}+\mathrm i\gamma_{ij},
\]
each with multiplicity \(\mu_{ij}\).
The bases \(e/d\) and \(e\) exceed one, so
\(0<\delta_n\gamma_{ij}\le1/4\). The two rails lie on opposite
sides of the critical line and are reflected copies with equal weights.
Their heights are all distinct: an equality of two heights gives
\[
 e^{i+j}d^k=e^{k+\ell}d^i.
\]
Coprimality first forces \(i=k\); since \(e>1\), it then forces
\(j=\ell\). Thus the displayed atoms have exactly the stated
multiplicities, without hidden collisions. Crucially,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-grid-linear-weight}
 \mu_{ij}=d^n\gamma_{ij}.
\end{equation}

Let \(\mathcal S_n\) be this finite set, extend its multiplicity by zero
outside \(\mathcal S_n\), and define, away from its poles,
\[
 R_n(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal S_n}
                  \frac{\mu(\rho)}{s-\rho},
 \qquad
 R_{n,c}(s)=R_n(s)-\frac{s}{s+c-1}R_n(q_c(s)).
\]
Equivalently, \(R_n\) is the finite weighted sum of negative logarithmic
derivatives of \(s-\rho\). Local factorization, followed by the proved
affine scaled-limit transfer, gives the complete residue
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-model-residue}
 r_{n,c}(\rho)=
 -\mu(\rho)+\frac{c\rho}{\rho+c-1}\mu(q_c(\rho)).
\end{equation}
This formula includes every target and reference atom.

All reference supports can be determined exactly. For \(c\ge2\), the
reference of a left-rail atom is absent. A right-rail reference cannot
hit the left rail. Indeed, using its imaginary part to fix the target
height and then comparing real parts would force, respectively,
\(\delta_n\gamma_{ij}=1/2\) or
\(\delta_n\gamma_{ij}=c/2\), contrary to the quarter bound
(the left-to-left case would require \(c=1\)).
On the right rail,
\[
 q_e(\rho^R_{i,j+1})=\rho^R_{ij},\qquad
 q_d(\rho^R_{i,j+1})=\rho^R_{i+1,j}.
\]
Height injectivity proves that these are the only possible matches.
Consequently an \(e\)-reference is present exactly when \(j>0\),
and a \(d\)-reference exactly when \(i<n\) and \(j>0\).

Whenever a reference is present, its height is \(\gamma_\rho/c\);
\eqref{eq:coprime-grid-linear-weight} therefore gives
\(c\mu(q_c(\rho))=\mu(\rho)\). Hence the exact selected projection is
\[
 Y_c(\rho):=\Im\bigl((\rho+c-1)r_{n,c}(\rho)\bigr)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 0,&q_c(\rho)\in\mathcal S_n,\\
 -\mu(\rho)\gamma_\rho,&q_c(\rho)\notin\mathcal S_n.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the two specified powers the common zero-projection set is exactly
\(\{\rho^R_{ij}:i<n,\ 1\le j\le n\}\).
All other atoms belong to the joint strict-negative carrier.
The full residue has not vanished on the common set: when a reference
is present, \eqref{eq:coprime-model-residue} becomes
\[
 r_{n,c}(\rho)=-\frac{(c-1)\mu(\rho)}{\rho+c-1}\ne0.
\]
When it is absent, the residue is \(-\mu(\rho)\ne0\).
This nonvanishing argument works for every \(c>1\).

Set \(a=d/e\), \(b=1/e\), and
\(G_k(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}x^r\).
Each atom has mass
\[
 \frac{\mu_{ij}}{\gamma_{ij}^2}=d^n a^i b^j.
\]
Keeping both rails and every boundary row, the total mass and the mass
missing from the joint carrier are therefore
\[
 A_n=2d^nG_{n+1}(a)G_{n+1}(b)>0,\qquad
 W_n=d^nG_n(a)\bigl(G_{n+1}(b)-1\bigr).
\]
Thus \(U_n=A_n-W_n\) and
\[
 \frac{U_n}{A_n}
 =1-\frac{G_n(a)\bigl(G_{n+1}(b)-1\bigr)}
            {2G_{n+1}(a)G_{n+1}(b)}.
\]
For completeness, put
\(x=a^n/G_{n+1}(a)\) and
\(y=b^{n+1}/G_{n+1}(b)\).
The finite geometric identities give the exact gap
\[
 \frac{U_n}{A_n}-\left(1-\frac b2\right)
 =\frac{b x+(1-x)y}{2}.
\]
Since \(0<b\le a<1\), both geometric blocks are at least one,
\(0\le x\le1\), and \(0\le y\le b a^n\).
It follows that the gap lies between zero and \(b a^n\).
This proves \eqref{eq:coprime-carrier-gap}; the geometric decay supplies
the required \(n\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\). \(\square\)

The construction shows that adding a second coprime power cannot force
a uniformly larger joint sign-carrier fraction from reflection and
natural multiplicities alone in this finite spectral class.
It also separates loss of one chosen real projection from loss of the
full multiplicity signal. Additional arithmetic information can exclude these spectra. In particular,
the rightmost rail tends to \(\Re\rho=1\), so the established \(7/8\)
strip excludes long grids as zeta models. No Euler-product realization
is claimed. The result identifies the scope of the reflection-only
carrier argument; it supplies no new numerical upper bound on zero
multiplicity and leaves the certified QRH boundary at \(7/8\).

\subsection{A zero strip strengthens joint multiplicity carriers}
\label{sec:strip-joint-carriers}

The reflection-only sharpness theorem permits zeros arbitrarily close to
the edge of the critical strip. A known smaller zero strip supplies an
additional constraint. Two reference maps couple that constraint to
multiplicity mass, including when the powers are not coprime.

Fix \(T>0\), and let
\[
 S_T=\{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ 0<\Re\rho<1,\ 0<\Im\rho\le T\},
 \qquad m(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta,\qquad
 w(\rho)=\frac{m(\rho)}{(\Im\rho)^2}.
\]
All sums below run over explicitly specified finite sets. Write
\(A_T=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}w(\rho)\), and, for real \(d,e\ge2\), set
\[
 \begin{split}
 U_{d,e}(T)&=\{\rho\in S_T:
 m(\rho)\le d\,m(q_d(\rho)),\
 m(\rho)\le e\,m(q_e(\rho))\},\\
 \mathcal C_{d,e}(T)&=S_T\setminus U_{d,e}(T),\qquad
 q_a(s)=\frac{s+a-1}{a}.
 \end{split}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C_{d,e}(T)\) is a union, and an overlap is counted once.

\paragraph{Theorem (Strip-dependent joint carrier mass).}
\label{thm:strip-joint-carrier}
Let \(2\le d<e\), \(1/2\le\sigma<1\), and suppose every
\(\rho\in S_T\) with \(\Re\rho>1/2\) satisfies \(\Re\rho\le\sigma\).
Put \(r=e/d\). If \(L\ge1\) is an integer such that
\[
 r^L(1-\sigma)\ge\frac12,\qquad H_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^j,
\]
then
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal C_{d,e}(T)}w(\rho)
 \ \ge\
 \left(1-\frac{1-H_L^{-1}}{2e}\right)A_T.
\]
No RH or simplicity assumption is made. In particular, coprimality of
\(d,e\) is not required.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Let \(R_T=\{\tau\in S_T:\Re\tau>1/2\}\), and define functions on
the entire complex plane by zero extension:
\[
 b(\tau)=
 \begin{cases}w(\tau),&\tau\in R_T,\\0,&\tau\notin R_T,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 c(\tau)=
 \begin{cases}
 e\,w(s_e(\tau)),&s_e(\tau)\in U_{d,e}(T),\\
 0,&s_e(\tau)\notin U_{d,e}(T),
 \end{cases}
 \quad s_e(\tau)=e\tau-(e-1).
\]
If \(\rho\in U_{d,e}(T)\), positivity of \(m(\rho)\) forces both
reference multiplicities to be positive. For \(a=d,e\), the reference
\(q_a(\rho)\) lies in \(R_T\): its real part is greater than \(1/2\)
and less than \(1\), and its height is \(\Im\rho/a\le T\).
The corresponding weak inequality gives the exact weighted comparison
\[
 a\,w(\rho)\le w(q_a(\rho)).
\]
For \(a=e\) this proves \(0\le c\le b\), including outside \(R_T\).

The other reference map must be retained at the same source. Its
transport on the \(e\)-reference coordinate is
\[
 M(\tau)=q_d(s_e(\tau))=1-r(1-\tau).
\]
The \(d\)-comparison gives
\[
 c(\tau)\le r\,b(M(\tau))
\]
on the whole complex plane. Set \(D=b-c\). Both \(c\) and \(D\) are
nonnegative and supported on \(R_T\). In particular, the complete signed
recurrence is
\[
 b(\tau)\le D(\tau)+r\,b(M(\tau)).
\]
Iteration, with every term retained, yields
\[
 b(\tau)\le
 \sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^jD(M^j(\tau))
 +r^Lb(M^L(\tau)).
\]
For \(\tau\in R_T\),
\[
 \Re M^L(\tau)=1-r^L(1-\Re\tau)
 \le1-r^L(1-\sigma)\le\frac12,
\]
so the terminal term is zero. Each \(M^j\) is injective, and \(D\)
vanishes outside the finite set \(R_T\). Consequently
\[
 \sum_{\tau\in R_T}D(M^j(\tau))
 \le\sum_{\tau\in R_T}D(\tau).
\]
Writing \(B=\sum_{\tau\in R_T}b(\tau)\) and
\(C=\sum_{\tau\in R_T}c(\tau)\), summation gives
\[
 B\le H_L(B-C),\qquad C\le(1-H_L^{-1})B.
\]

Reflection \(\rho\mapsto1-\overline{\rho}\) preserves height and
multiplicity, pairs the right and left half-planes, and leaves
nonnegative mass on the critical line. Thus \(2B\le A_T\).
The map \(q_e\) is injective, is inverse to \(s_e\), and sends
\(U_{d,e}(T)\) into \(R_T\). The definition of \(c\) therefore gives
\[
 C=e\sum_{\rho\in U_{d,e}(T)}w(\rho).
\]
Since \(H_L\ge1\), these inequalities imply
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in U_{d,e}(T)}w(\rho)
 \le\frac{1-H_L^{-1}}{2e}A_T.
\]
Subtract this finite mass from \(A_T\).
\hfill\textit{QED.}

The statement applies to the actual compensated residues. For a
correction analytic throughout the critical strip, the previously proved
local identity gives
\[
 \Im\!\bigl(r_a(\rho)(\rho+a-1)\bigr)
   =\bigl(a\,m(q_a(\rho))-m(\rho)\bigr)\Im\rho.
\]
Thus membership in \(\mathcal C_{d,e}(T)\) is exactly strict negativity
of at least one of these two projected residues. Both reference
multiplicities and every common-reference overlap remain in the proof.

\paragraph{Theorem (Unconditional fixed-power consequences).}
\label{thm:strip-carrier-seven-eighths}
For every \(T>0\), actual zeta compensated readouts satisfy
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal C_{2,3}(T)}w(\rho)\ge\frac{111}{130}A_T,
 \qquad
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal C_{2,4}(T)}w(\rho)\ge\frac{11}{12}A_T.
\]
\paragraph{Proof.}
Use the installed unconditional nonvanishing theorem for
\(\Re s>7/8\), so \(\sigma=7/8\) is admissible.
For \((d,e)=(2,3)\), take \(L=4\). Then
\[
 (3/2)^4(1/8)=81/128\ge1/2,\qquad H_4=65/8,
\]
and the carrier coefficient is \(111/130\).
For \((d,e)=(2,4)\), take \(L=2\). Then
\(2^2(1/8)=1/2\), \(H_2=3\), and the coefficient is \(11/12\).
\hfill\textit{QED.}

The reflection-only guarantees for these fixed pairs are respectively
\(5/6\) and \(7/8\). The new coefficients are larger by \(4/195\)
and \(1/24\). There is no conflict with the earlier finite-model
sharpness theorem: its long models approach \(\Re\rho=1\), whereas
the present theorem uses a fixed upper strip. This is a quantitative
improvement in multiplicity mass retained by specified observables.
It proves neither a smaller zero-free boundary nor an upper bound on
individual multiplicities. The geometric transport summation is
elementary; publication priority for this particular coupled
compensated-residue application has not been established.

\subsection{Optimal fixed-strip quadratic and quartic carriers}
\label{sec:dyadic-fixed-strip-sharpness}

The coefficient \(11/12\) has an exact finite-model interpretation.
It is optimal using only a fixed zero strip, reflection, positive natural
multiplicities and the full quadratic and quartic compensation references.
The models below are finite rational logarithmic readouts; they are not
asserted to be zero sets of zeta or of an Euler product.

Let \(S\) be a finite set of distinct points \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\), with
\(\gamma>0\), \(1/8\le\beta\le7/8\), and positive natural multiplicities
\(m(\rho)\). Suppose \(1-\overline\rho\in S\) with the same multiplicity.
Extend \(m\) by zero outside \(S\), and put
\[
 A=\sum_{\rho\in S}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma^2},\qquad
 U=\{\rho\in S:m(\rho)\le2m(q_2(\rho)),\
                       m(\rho)\le4m(q_4(\rho))\},
 \qquad C=\sum_{\rho\in S\setminus U}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma^2}.
\]
Every summation here has the displayed finite support. For the actual
rational readout \(R(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in S}m(\rho)/(s-\rho)\), write
\[
 P_a(s)=R(s)-\frac{s}{s+a-1}R(q_a(s)),\qquad
 r_a(\rho)=\lim_{s\to\rho}(s-\rho)P_a(s).
\]
Its complete residue is
\[
 r_a(\rho)=-m(\rho)+\frac{a\rho}{\rho+a-1}m(q_a(\rho)).
\]
Consequently \(S\setminus U\) is exactly the union of the strict-negative
projected residue carriers for \(a=2,4\).

\paragraph{Theorem (Optimal fixed-strip carrier coefficient).}
\label{thm:dyadic-fixed-strip-optimality}
Every such spectrum satisfies \(C\ge(11/12)A\).
Conversely, for every \(\epsilon>0\) there is a finite spectrum already
inside \(1/7\le\Re\rho\le6/7\), with \(A>0\), such that
\[
 \frac CA<\frac{11}{12}+\epsilon.
\]
The witnesses have positive natural multiplicities and actual atomic
readouts. Every one of their full complex residues is nonzero for every
real compensation power \(a>1\). Thus a real coefficient \(c\) satisfies
\(C\ge cA\) for all spectra in the stated class if and only if
\(c\le11/12\).

\paragraph{Proof of the universal bound.}
Write \(W=\sum_{\rho\in U}m(\rho)/\gamma^2\), so \(A=W+C\).
For \(\rho\in U\), the positive multiplicity forces both reference
points to belong to \(S\). The upper bound on \(q_4(\rho)\) gives
\(\beta\le1/2\). Hence
\[
 9/16\le\Re q_2(\rho)\le3/4,\qquad
 25/32\le\Re q_4(\rho)\le7/8.
\]
Both reference maps are injective and their images of \(U\) are disjoint.
For \(a=2,4\), their actual masses satisfy
\[
 \frac{m(q_a(\rho))}{(\gamma/a)^2}
 \ge a\,\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma^2}.
\]
Thus the union of these images has mass at least \(6W\).
It lies strictly to the right of \(1/2\), so its reflected image is
disjoint, has the same mass and also belongs to \(S\).
Therefore \(A\ge12W\). Substituting \(A=W+C\) proves \(C\ge11A/12\).
No reference image or reflection overlap has been counted twice.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

\paragraph{Construction and complete reference classification.}
Use words in the two letters \(2,4\). The root and child coordinates are
\[
 x_{\varnothing}=1/4,\qquad
 x_{2w}=(1-x_w)/2,\qquad x_{4w}=(1-x_w)/4.
\]
All \(x_w\) lie in \([1/7,3/7]\); the two child images lie respectively
in \([2/7,3/7]\) and \([1/7,3/14]\). They are disjoint, and the root
lies strictly in the intervening gap. Induction on words therefore
proves injectivity across all finite lengths, including the empty word.

Fix a depth \(N\ge1\) and any \(H>0\). Let \(s(w)\) count a letter \(2\)
with weight one and a letter \(4\) with weight two. For every word of
length at most \(N\), include the two points
\[
 L_w=x_w+iH2^{-s(w)},\qquad
 R_w=1-x_w+iH2^{-s(w)},\qquad
 m(L_w)=m(R_w)=2^{\,2N-s(w)}.
\]
Since \(s(w)\le2|w|\le2N\), every multiplicity is a positive natural
number. The two rails are disjoint, all points are distinct, and their
real parts lie in \([1/7,6/7]\).

The full reference table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 \text{source}&q_2\text{ reference}&q_4\text{ reference}\\ \hline
 L_w,\ |w|<N&R_{2w}&R_{4w}\\
 L_w,\ |w|=N&\text{absent}&\text{absent}\\
 R_{2w}&R_{4w}&\text{absent}\\
 R_{\varnothing},\ R_{4w}&\text{absent}&\text{absent}.
\end{array}
\]
The child identities follow directly from the coordinates and heights.
Injectivity excludes any replacement reference when a child lies beyond
the depth cutoff. No reference hits the left rail. For the other absent
cases, \(q_2(R_{\varnothing})\) has real part \(7/8>6/7\);
\(q_2(R_{4w})\) and every \(q_4(R_w)\) have real part at least
\(25/28>6/7\). The right-child quadratic collision is present:
\(q_2(R_{2w})=R_{4w}\), with
\(m(R_{2w})=2m(R_{4w})\). Its quadratic projection vanishes, but its
quartic reference is absent. The internal left multiplicities satisfy
\(m(L_w)=2m(R_{2w})=4m(R_{4w})\).
It follows that \(U\) is exactly the nonterminal left nodes.

\paragraph{Exact masses and sharpness.}
Put \(b_N=2^{2N}/H^2>0\). Each point at word \(w\) has mass
\(b_N2^{s(w)}\). The sum of the word weights at level \(k\) is \(6^k\).
Writing \(T_N=\sum_{k=0}^N6^k=(6^{N+1}-1)/5\), we obtain
\[
 A_N=2b_NT_N,\qquad W_N=b_NT_{N-1},\qquad C_N=A_N-W_N,
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-fixed-strip-ratio}
 \frac{C_N}{A_N}
 =\frac{11}{12}+\frac{5}{12(6^{N+1}-1)}.
\end{equation}
The final term tends to zero, uniformly in every \(H>0\).
The formal epsilon witness can alternatively use the elementary bound
\(T_N\ge N+1\); no infinite spectrum or interchange of limits is required.

Finally, the actual atomic readout has the displayed local scaled limits.
For every \(a>1\) and every point \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) of the model,
\[
 \Im\!\left(r_a(\rho)(\rho+a-1)\overline\rho\right)
   =m(\rho)(a-1)\gamma>0.
\]
Thus its full residue cannot vanish, including at every cancelled
projected row in the table. This proves the asserted realization and
completes the sharpness proof.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

\texttt{CarmichaelDyadicSharpness.universal\_carrier\_coefficient\_iff},
\texttt{exists\_depth\_carrier\_ratio\_lt}, and
\texttt{treeSpectrum\_joint\_readout\_carrier}.
The exact formula is
\texttt{treeSpectrum\_carrier\_ratio\_closed}; its Lean depth is \(N+1\)
for a natural parameter \(N\), so its exponent is \(N+2\).
The finite lower-bound owner is

This settles optimality within the stated finite-spectrum class.
Additional arithmetic information about actual zeta zeros may still

remains \(7/8\). Publication priority for this particular finite

\subsection{Multiplicity-sensitive carriers and actual zeta certificates}
\label{sec:multiplicity-sensitive-carriers}

The complete complex readout recovers multiplicity even when its affine
reference is another zero. The projected carrier asks a different question:
how much weighted zero mass has a negative signed projection in at least one
of the quadratic and quartic rows? The following estimate quantifies how
excess multiplicity can reduce that projected mass.

Let \(S=\{\rho_i:i\in I\}\) be a finite injectively indexed spectrum with
positive natural multiplicities \(m_i\), positive heights
\(\gamma_i=\Im\rho_i\), and real parts in \([1/8,7/8]\).
Assume reflection \(\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho\) preserves the spectrum
and multiplicities. Extend the atomic order \(m(z)\) by zero off \(S\).
With \(q_d(\rho)=(\rho+d-1)/d\), define the finite sets and masses
\[
 U=\{i\in I:m_i\le2m(q_2(\rho_i)),\
                   m_i\le4m(q_4(\rho_i))\},\qquad V=I\setminus U,
\]
\[
 A=\sum_{i\in I}\frac{m_i}{\gamma_i^2},\quad
 C=\sum_{i\in V}\frac{m_i}{\gamma_i^2},\quad
 P_U=\sum_{i\in U}\frac1{\gamma_i^2},\quad
 E_U=\sum_{i\in U}\frac{m_i-1}{\gamma_i^2}.
\]
Every displayed sum is over its indicated finite support.

\paragraph{Theorem (multiplicity-sensitive carrier bound).}
For every such spectrum,
\[
 A\ge40P_U,\qquad
 C\ge\max\left\{\frac{11}{12}A,\frac{39}{40}A-E_U\right\}.
\]
In particular, if all multiplicities are one then \(C\ge39A/40\).
If \(C<39A/40\), some \(i\in U\) has \(m_i\ge2\).
No simplicity or RH hypothesis is used in the general estimate.
The simple-spectrum coefficient is optimal in this finite model class;
Section~\ref{sec:simple-carrier-sharpness} proves sharpness.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For \(i\in U\), positivity of \(m_i\) forces both actual reference
orders to be positive. Hence the reference points belong to \(S\),
and their natural multiplicities are at least one. The established
weak-row geometry places every source in \(\Re\rho_i\le1/2\).
The quadratic reference image lies in \(1/2<\Re z\le3/4\);
the quartic reference image lies in \(\Re z\ge25/32\).
Both maps are injective and these images are disjoint.
Since \(\Im q_d(\rho_i)=\gamma_i/d\), their combined mass is at least
\[
 \sum_{i\in U}\left(\frac4{\gamma_i^2}
                         +\frac{16}{\gamma_i^2}\right)=20P_U.
\]
Their reflected copies have equal mass and lie strictly to the left
of \(1/2\), so are disjoint from both right-hand images. Thus
\(A\ge40P_U\). The exact finite partition gives
\(A=C+P_U+E_U\), whence \(C\ge39A/40-E_U\).
Combine this with the preceding independently proved \(11A/12\) bound.
All excess summands are nonnegative; if every weak atom were simple
then \(E_U=0\), contradicting a strict deficit.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

\paragraph{Theorem (unconditional actual-zeta specialization).}
For every real \(T\), take the actual finite set
\[
 S_T=\{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ 0<\Re\rho<1,\ 0<\Im\rho\le T\}
\]
with \(m(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta\).
The same max bound and repeated-weak-zero certificate hold, with both
reference orders evaluated in the actual zeta function.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The established unconditional seven-eighths theorem and reflection put
\(S_T\) in \([1/8,7/8]\). Reflection preserves both heights and analytic
multiplicities, which are positive natural numbers at these zeros.
For \(d\ge2\), if the actual order at \(q_d(\rho)\) is positive,
the proved power-map window theorem places that reference inside \(S_T\).
Injectivity of the indexing then makes its atomic order equal to its
actual zeta order. If that order is zero, no indexed zero can equal the
reference, and its atomic order is also zero.
Thus the complete reference orders agree in every case.
Transporting the finite sums through the subtype indexing proves the claim.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

To state the observable version, let \(G\) be analytic on
\(0<\Re s<1\), and let \(r_2(\rho),r_4(\rho)\) be the local scaled limits
of the corresponding affine compensated readouts of
\(\operatorname{zetaMultiplicityReadout}(G)\).
The local-limit hypotheses are retained explicitly in the Lean statement.
The exact residue identity gives
\[
 \Im\bigl(r_d(\rho)(\rho+d-1)\bigr)
   =\bigl(d\,m(q_d(\rho))-m(\rho)\bigr)\Im\rho.
\]
Consequently \(C\) is precisely the mass on the union of the two
negative-projection supports, and the same max bound holds for that
observable carrier. Theorem
\texttt{zeta\_readout\_carrier\_max\_bound} formalizes this transfer.

The certificate is one-sided: it provides a lower bound for weak excess
when the carrier has a deficit, not an upper bound for multiplicities.
Under RH the affine references of nontrivial zeros are not zeros, so
\(U\) is empty and \(C=A\), regardless of whether a critical-line zero
is multiple. Thus this carrier test alone does not settle simplicity.
It complements the full complex residue recovery and the existing
RH-dependent second-moment multiplicity budget.

The maintained owners are \texttt{CarmichaelMultiplicityCarrier},
\texttt{CarmichaelZetaFiniteSpectrum}, and
\texttt{CarmichaelZetaMultiplicityCarrier}. Their hypotheses and proofs
retain every reference order, finite-window endpoint and compensation term.

\subsection{Sharp simple-spectrum carriers}
\label{sec:simple-carrier-sharpness}

The preceding \(39/40\) coefficient is optimal over the stated finite
simple-spectrum class. This is a statement about complete atomic readouts,
not a claim that zeta zeros realize the examples.

\paragraph{Theorem (exact universal simple coefficient).}
For a real number \(c\), the following are equivalent:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{every finite reflected spectrum in }[1/8,7/8]
 \text{ with }m_i=1\text{ satisfies }cA\le C;\\
 c\le39/40.
 \end{gathered}
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), a finite depth can be chosen independently
of every positive root height \(H\), giving a complete simple atomic
readout with \(A>0\) and \(C/A<39/40+\varepsilon\).
All its points already lie in \([1/7,6/7]\), and its full complex
compensated residues are nonzero for every real \(d>1\).

\paragraph{Construction and exact finite support.}
For a finite word \(w\) in \(0,1\), let
\[
 x_{\varnothing}=\frac14,\quad
 x_{0w}=\frac{1-x_w}{2},\quad
 x_{1w}=\frac{1-x_w}{4},\qquad
 s(\varnothing)=0,\quad s(0w)=s(w)+1,\quad s(1w)=s(w)+2.
\]
At depth \(N+1\), retain both points
\[
 L_w=x_w+iH2^{-s(w)},\qquad
 R_w=1-x_w+iH2^{-s(w)}
       \quad (|w|\le N+1),
\]
each with multiplicity exactly one. The established word geometry makes
this entire finite indexing injective, including different word lengths;
it gives \(1/7\le x_w\le3/7\) and reflection interchanging the two rails.
Thus every point belongs to \([1/7,6/7]\) and has positive height.

For a nonterminal left point the actual references are
\[
 q_2(L_w)=R_{0w},\qquad q_4(L_w)=R_{1w}.
\]
Both reference orders are one. For a terminal left point the child words
are outside the finite support and both reference orders are zero.
For every right point,
\(\Re q_4(R_w)=1-x_w/4\ge25/28>6/7\), so its quartic reference order
is zero. Hence the common weak set is exactly
\[
 U=\{L_w:|w|\le N\}.
\]
The right quadratic row, including its possible reference collisions,
remains in the actual atomic readout. Its value cannot remove a right
point from the union carrier because that point's quartic signed
projection is already negative.

\paragraph{Exact mass and sharpness proof.}
Put
\[
 T_n=\sum_{|w|\le n}4^{s(w)}.
\]
The root and the two disjoint child supports give
\[
 T_0=1,\qquad T_{n+1}=1+(4+16)T_n=1+20T_n,\qquad
 19T_n+1=20^{n+1}.
\]
All sums here are over the indicated finite word support.
With every multiplicity one, \(E_U=0\), and the complete two-rail and
common-weak masses are
\[
 A=\frac{2T_{N+1}}{H^2}>0,\qquad W=\frac{T_N}{H^2},\qquad C=A-W.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac CA
 =1-\frac{T_N}{2T_{N+1}}
 =\frac{39}{40}+\frac1{40T_{N+1}}
 =\frac{39}{40}
       +\frac{19}{40(20^{N+2}-1)}.
\]
No term from the finite carrier or compensation has been omitted.
The denominator is positive. Also \(T_n\ge n+1\); choosing
\(N>1/(40\varepsilon)\) makes the correction smaller than
\(\varepsilon\), uniformly in \(H>0\).
Thus every coefficient strictly larger than \(39/40\) fails on some
finite simple spectrum. The converse is the preceding simple-spectrum
lower bound, together with nonnegativity of \(A\).

Finally use the complete atomic readout
\[
 R(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in S}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
Its scaled local limits give the actual target and reference orders.
The proved nonreal residue identity preserves a nonzero full complex
residue for every \(d>1\). Its quadratic-quartic negative-projection
union is exactly the carrier used above, so the examples are readout
realizations of the sharp constant.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

The unrestricted positive-natural-multiplicity class has sharp
coefficient \(11/12\); the all-simple subclass has sharp coefficient
\(39/40\). The excess-multiplicity certificate quantifies the difference.
These finite-model optimality statements provide no new upper bound on
actual zeta multiplicities and do not change the established \(7/8\)
zero-free boundary.

The five maintained owners are \texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeWeights},
\texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeSpectrum}, \texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeRows},
\texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeMass}, and \texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeSharpness}.
The exact universal statement is
\texttt{universal\_simple\_carrier\_coefficient\_iff}.

\subsection{Sharp multiplicity-support region and triple-zero certificates}
\label{sec:multiplicity-support-region}

Keep the finite reflected spectra in the closed strip
\([1/8,7/8]\), with positive heights and positive natural multiplicities.
Reference orders are the actual atomic orders, including zero for an absent
reference. Let \(U\) be the common-weak set for the complete powers \(2,4\);
thus \(m_\rho\le2m_{q_2(\rho)}\) and
\(m_\rho\le4m_{q_4(\rho)}\). All sums below are over the indicated finite
support. Define
\[
 P=\sum_{\rho\in U}\frac1{\gamma_\rho^2},\qquad
 Q=\sum_{\substack{\rho\in U\\m_\rho\ge2}}\frac1{\gamma_\rho^2},\qquad
 T_3=\sum_{\rho\in U}\frac{(m_\rho-2)_+}{\gamma_\rho^2},
 \qquad (t)_+=\max(t,0).
\]
Write \(A=\sum_{\rho\in S}m_\rho/\gamma_\rho^2\) and
\(C=\sum_{\rho\in S\setminus U}m_\rho/\gamma_\rho^2\).
Then \(0\le Q\le P\), \(E_U=Q+T_3\), and
\[
 A=C+P+Q+T_3.
\]

\paragraph{Theorem (complete affine support region).}
For real \(a,b\), the inequality \(aP+bQ\le C\) holds for every such
finite reflected spectrum if and only if
\[
 \boxed{\quad a\le39,\qquad a+b\le40.\quad}
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-multiplicity-support}
 \boxed{\quad C\ge39P+Q.\quad}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triple-excess-carrier}
 \boxed{\quad C+\frac{20}{21}T_3\ge\frac{20}{21}A.\quad}
\end{equation}
The coefficient \(20/21\) is optimal both in
\(c(A-T_3)\le C\) on the full class and in \(cA\le C\) when every
common-weak multiplicity is at most two. Multiplicities on the carrier
itself need not be bounded.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Index the atoms by \(i\), write \(i^*\) for reflection, and set
\[
 u_i=\gamma_i^{-2},\quad w_i=m_i u_i,\quad e_i=(m_i-1)u_i,\quad
 D_i=e_i+\mathbf1_{\{m_i\ge2\}}u_i.
\]
For \(m_i=1\), \(D_i=0\); for \(m_i\ge2\), \(D_i=w_i\).
Hence
\[
 0\le D_i\le w_i,\qquad D_i\le2e_i,\qquad D_{i^*}=D_i.
\]
Retain both compensation charges by defining the real weight
\[
 F_i=w_i-\frac12\mathbf1_U(i)D_i
          -\frac12\mathbf1_U(i^*)D_i.
\]
Both deductions are at most \(D_i/2\), so \(F_i\ge0\).
Reflection interchanges the two deductions, so \(F_{i^*}=F_i\), and
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}F_i=A-E_U-Q.
\]
This remains valid at a reflection-fixed atom: the two half charges add
to the full charge. The weight may vanish and is not asserted to define
a positive-natural-multiplicity spectrum.

Every common-weak atom lies at real part at most \(1/2\), by the existing
complete-reference argument. Therefore, at a point with real part \(>1/2\),
the first charge is absent, and
\[
 F_i\ge w_i-\frac12D_i\ge w_i-e_i=u_i.
\]
Let \(B=q_2(U)\cup q_4(U)\), interpreted through the actual reference
indices. Both maps are injective on \(U\); their images lie respectively
in \([9/16,3/4]\) and \([25/32,7/8]\), so they are disjoint and strictly
right of \(1/2\). Their heights are \(\gamma/2,\gamma/4\). Thus
\[
 \sum_{i\in B}F_i\ge\sum_{i\in B}u_i=(4+16)P=20P.
\]
The sets \(B,B^*\) are disjoint. Nonnegativity and reflection symmetry
give
\[
 A-E_U-Q=\sum_{i\in S}F_i
 \ge2\sum_{i\in B}F_i\ge40P.
\]
Substitute \(A=C+P+E_U\) to obtain
\eqref{eq:joint-multiplicity-support}. No compensation cross charge or
reference order has been discarded. If \(a\le39\) and \(a+b\le40\), then
\[
 aP+bQ=a(P-Q)+(a+b)Q
 \le39(P-Q)+40Q=39P+Q\le C.
\]

For necessity of \(a\le39\), restrict to the simple spectra of
Section~\ref{sec:simple-carrier-sharpness}. There \(Q=0\) and \(A=C+P\).
If \(a>39\), then \(a+1>0\), and \(aP\le C\) would imply
\[
 C\ge\frac{a}{a+1}A,\qquad \frac{a}{a+1}>\frac{39}{40},
\]
contradicting the proved simple-spectrum optimality.

The second face has an exact finite witness. For every \(H>0\), take
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 \rho&\frac12+iH&
       \frac14+iH/2&\frac34+iH/2&
       \frac18+iH/4&\frac78+iH/4\\ \hline
 m_\rho&2&1&1&1&1 .
 \end{array}
\]
Reflection fixes the center and exchanges each displayed pair.
The center has quadratic and quartic reference orders one. The right
point \(3/4+iH/2\) has quadratic reference \(7/8+iH/4\), of order one.
All other quadratic references and all other quartic references are
absent, as their real coordinates show. Thus the common-weak set is
exactly the center, and
\[
 P=Q=E_U=\frac1{H^2},\qquad T_3=0,\qquad
 A=\frac{42}{H^2},\qquad C=\frac{40}{H^2}.
\]
The inequality \(aP+bQ\le C\) therefore forces \(a+b\le40\).

Finally, \(Q\le P\) gives
\(39P+Q\ge20(P+Q)\). Using \(A=C+P+Q+T_3\) yields
\(21C+20T_3\ge20A\), proving \eqref{eq:triple-excess-carrier}.
The five-point model has \(C/A=20/21\), \(T_3=0\), and weak multiplicity
two, so neither universal coefficient can be enlarged.
For smaller real coefficients, \(A-T_3=C+P+Q\ge0\) justifies the
monotone coefficient comparison, including negative coefficients.

These are full readout examples. The complete atomic readout is
\(R(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in S}m_\rho/(s-\rho)\). Its actual scaled limits
retain every reference multiplicity, and its full complex residue is
nonzero at every atom for every real \(d>1\).
At the center the quadratic scalar projection vanishes, but the full
quadratic residue is \(-2/(\rho+1)\ne0\); the two facts are compatible.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

For each actual zeta window \(V_T\) used above, define \(P,Q,T_3\) by
the same finite sums with actual analytic orders. The existing
unconditional \(7/8\) strip and reflection theorem supply precisely
the finite-spectrum hypotheses, and complete transformed orders agree
with the finite model because the transformed height decreases.
Consequently \eqref{eq:joint-multiplicity-support} and
\eqref{eq:triple-excess-carrier} hold for actual zeta zeros without RH.
In particular,
\[
 C<\frac{20}{21}A
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{some common-weak zeta zero in }V_T\text{ has multiplicity at least }3.
\]
The complete compensated local-limit identities identify the same
carrier with the union of the two strict negative residue projections.
The formal readout theorem retains the analytic regular part and both
local-limit hypotheses.

The exact classification is
\texttt{universal\_affine\_multiplicity\_support\_iff};
the two optimal scalar coefficients are
\texttt{universal\_triple\_compensated\_coefficient\_iff} and
\texttt{universal\_double\_weak\_carrier\_coefficient\_iff}.
The actual-zeta certificate is
\texttt{zeta\_triple\_weak\_zero\_of\_deficit}.
Finite-model sharpness does not assert realization of the five points
as actual zeta zeros, prove any multiple zeta zero, or supply an upper
multiplicity bound. The certified zero-free boundary remains \(7/8\).
The limited primary-literature comparison does not establish publication
priority for this coefficient-region theorem.

\subsection{Sharp fifth-multiplicity stability}
\label{sec:fifth-multiplicity-stability}

For the same finite reflected spectra and common-weak set \(U\), define
\[
 R_5=\sum_{\substack{\rho\in U\\m_\rho\ge5}}\frac1{\gamma_\rho^2},
 \qquad \Delta=C-39P-Q.
\]
Every sum is over the displayed finite support. The quantity \(R_5\)
counts each zero of order at least five once, with inverse-square-height
weight.

\paragraph{Theorem (optimal fifth-tail coefficient).}
For real \(c\), the inequality
\[
 39P+Q+cR_5\le C
\]
holds for every finite reflected positive-natural-multiplicity spectrum
in \([1/8,7/8]\) if and only if \(c\le4\). In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fifth-tail-stability}
 \boxed{\qquad \Delta\ge4R_5.\qquad}
\end{equation}
Equality in the preceding joint support bound, \(\Delta=0\), forces
every common-weak multiplicity to be at most four.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Use the exact charged weight \(F_i\) of
Section~\ref{sec:multiplicity-support-region}, retaining both half
charges. At a right-hand point the atom itself is not common-weak, and
\(D_i\le w_i\); hence, in addition to \(F_i\ge u_i\), we have
\[
 F_i\ge w_i-\frac12D_i\ge\frac12w_i.
\]
If a common-weak source \(i\) has \(m_i\ge5\), its quadratic reference
has integer multiplicity \(m_j\) satisfying \(5\le m_i\le2m_j\).
Thus \(m_j\ge3\), and its height is \(\gamma_i/2\), giving
\[
 F_j\ge\frac32\,\gamma_j^{-2}=6u_i.
\]
For every other common-weak source the existing bound \(F_j\ge4u_i\)
still applies. Consequently the complete quadratic image satisfies
\[
 \sum_{j\in q_2(U)}F_j\ge4P+2R_5.
\]
The complete quartic image retains its previous bound \(16P\).
The two images are disjoint and strictly right of \(1/2\), and their
reflected copies are disjoint from them. Nonnegativity and symmetry
therefore give
\[
 A-E_U-Q=\sum_{i\in S}F_i
 \ge2(4P+2R_5+16P)=40P+4R_5.
\]
Substitution of \(A=C+P+E_U\) proves
\eqref{eq:fifth-tail-stability}. This is a joint signed budget:
the quadratic gain is inserted before the complete reflection sum,
with both compensation charges present.

Every unit weight is strictly positive. Therefore \(R_5=0\) if and
only if every atom in \(U\) has multiplicity at most four.
Together with \(\Delta\ge4R_5\) and \(R_5\ge0\), this proves the
equality-rigidity assertion.

To prove optimality, fix any \(H>0\) and take the following seventeen
points. Each pair in a table row has the indicated common height and
multiplicity.
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{real parts}&\text{height}&\text{multiplicity}\\ \hline
 1/2&H&5\\
 1/4,\;3/4&H/2&3\\
 1/8,\;7/8&H/4&2\\
 3/8,\;5/8&H/4&2\\
 3/16,\;13/16&H/8&1\\
 7/16,\;9/16&H/8&1\\
 7/32,\;25/32&H/16&1\\
 5/16,\;11/16&H/8&1\\
 5/32,\;27/32&H/16&1
 \end{array}
\]
Reflection fixes the center and exchanges the eight pairs. All points
are distinct and belong to the required closed strip. To check every
reference, let \(a_i=64\Re\rho_i\) and
\(b_i=H/\Im\rho_i\). The complete quadratic real-coordinate ownership
condition is
\[
 2a_j=a_i+64,
\]
and the quartic condition is \(4a_j=a_i+192\).
Every matching pair also satisfies \(b_j=2b_i\), respectively
\(b_j=4b_i\); thus these are exact point identities, not merely
real-coordinate matches. All other references are absent.

The quadratic references of the four common-weak points are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (1/2,H)&\longmapsto(3/4,H/2),&
 (1/4,H/2)&\longmapsto(5/8,H/4),\\
 (1/8,H/4)&\longmapsto(9/16,H/8),&
 (3/8,H/4)&\longmapsto(11/16,H/8).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here a displayed pair records real part and height.
The four additional right-hand quadratic references are

 \begin{aligned}
 (3/4,H/2)&\longmapsto(7/8,H/4),&
 (5/8,H/4)&\longmapsto(13/16,H/8),\\
 (9/16,H/8)&\longmapsto(25/32,H/16),&
 (11/16,H/8)&\longmapsto(27/32,H/16).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The only quartic references are those of the four common-weak points,
with targets

 (7/8,H/4),\quad (13/16,H/8),\quad

\subsection{The complete two-tail coefficient region}
\label{sec:two-tail-region}

The fifth tail has a spatial refinement. Keep the finite support \(S\),
the common-weak set \(U\), the unit weights \(u_i=\gamma_i^{-2}\), and
\(\Delta=C-39P-Q\) from the preceding section, and put
\[
 V_3=\sum_{\substack{i\in U\\ \Re\rho_i<1/2\\m_i\ge3}}u_i.
\]
The strict left-half condition is part of the definition: central
multiple zeros contribute to \(R_5\), but never to \(V_3\).

\paragraph{Theorem (complete coefficient region).}
For every real pair \(a,b\), the inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-tail-region}
 39P+Q+aR_5+bV_3\le C
\end{equation}
holds for every finite reflected positive-natural-multiplicity spectrum
in \([1/8,7/8]\) if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-tail-facets}
 \boxed{\quad a\le16,\qquad b\le1,\qquad a+4b\le4.\quad}
\end{equation}
In particular the two coupled inequalities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-tail-budgets}
 \Delta\ge V_3,\qquad \Delta+3V_3\ge16R_5.
\end{equation}
If \(V_3=0\), the fifth-tail bound improves to \(\Delta\ge16R_5\).
If \(\Delta=4R_5\), then necessarily \(V_3=4R_5\).

\paragraph{Proof of the coupled budgets.}
Write \(i^*\) for reflection. Retain the exact charged weight
\(F_i=w_i-D_i1_U(i)/2-D_i1_U(i^*)/2\) of
Section~\ref{sec:multiplicity-support-region}, where
\(w_i=m_i u_i\) and \(D_i=(m_i-1+1_{m_i\ge2})u_i\).
Define the one-sided marker and two symmetric weights by
\[
 t_i=1_U(i)1_{\Re\rho_i<1/2}1_{m_i\ge3}u_i,\qquad
 K_i=F_i-\frac{t_i+t_{i^*}}2,\qquad
 L_i=F_i+\frac32(t_i+t_{i^*}).
\]
Every charge, reference order and mirror remains present. The exact sums are
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}K_i=A-E_U-Q-V_3,\qquad
 \sum_{i\in S}L_i=A-E_U-Q+3V_3.
\]
Both weights are symmetric.

At a point strictly right of \(1/2\), \(t_i=0\).
If \(m_i\ge3\), then
\(F_i\ge w_i/2\ge3u_i/2\), while \(t_{i^*}\le u_i\).
Hence \(K_i\ge u_i\). If \(m_i<3\), reflection preserves multiplicity,
so \(t_{i^*}=0\), and the same conclusion follows from \(F_i\ge u_i\).
Reflection proves nonnegativity of \(K\) also on the strict left half;
on the critical line both markers vanish and \(K=F\ge0\).

Consequently the complete quadratic and quartic reference images carry,
respectively, at least \(4P\) and \(16P\) in the \(K\) weight.
The images are disjoint and strictly right of \(1/2\); applying the
symmetric nonnegative reflection budget gives
\[
 A-E_U-Q-V_3=\sum_{i\in S}K_i\ge2(4P+16P)=40P.
\]
Together with \(A=C+P+E_U\), this is \(\Delta\ge V_3\).

For \(L\), nonnegativity and the right unit floor follow from those of
\(F\). If a right-hand point has \(m_i\ge3\) and its mirror belongs to
\(U\), then \(t_{i^*}=u_i\), giving \(L_i\ge3u_i\).
If its mirror is not in \(U\), both original charges vanish at that
pair, so \(F_i=w_i\ge3u_i\), and again \(L_i\ge3u_i\).
A common-weak source of order at least five has quadratic reference
order at least three and reference unit weight \(4u_i\).
That reference therefore supplies at least \(12u_i\), rather than
the baseline \(4u_i\). Summing injectively over the complete quadratic
image yields \(4P+8R_5\). The complete quartic image still supplies
\(16P\). The same disjoint reflection budget now gives
\[
 A-E_U-Q+3V_3=\sum_{i\in S}L_i
 \ge2(4P+8R_5+16P)=40P+16R_5.
\]
This proves the second inequality in~\eqref{eq:two-tail-budgets}.
In particular, the earlier coefficient four is obtained by eliminating
the spatial tail, not by dropping it.

\paragraph{Sufficiency of all three facets.}
Both tails are nonnegative. If \(b\le-3\), then \(a\le16\) gives
\(aR_5+bV_3\le16R_5-3V_3\le\Delta\).
For \(-3\le b\le1\), multiply the first inequality in
\eqref{eq:two-tail-budgets} by \(b+3\) and the second by \(1-b\).
Adding and dividing by four gives
\[
 \Delta\ge4(1-b)R_5+bV_3.
\]
The condition \(a+4b\le4\) completes
\eqref{eq:two-tail-region}. This includes all negative coefficients.
If \(\Delta=4R_5\), the two budgets give
\(V_3\le4R_5\) and \(V_3\ge4R_5\), proving the stated rigidity.

\paragraph{Necessity and full-readout extremizers.}

\subsection{A sharp global offcritical multiplicity budget}
\label{sec:global-offcritical-excess}

The weak tails can be coupled to the multiplicities of every offcritical
point. Define
\[
 R_9=\sum_{\substack{i\in U\\m_i\ge9}}u_i,\qquad
 J_3=\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\\Re\rho_i<1/2}}(m_i-3)_+u_i,
 \qquad \Delta=C-39P-Q.
\]
Here \((m-3)_+=\max(m-3,0)\). The sum defining \(J_3\) has no weak-set
restriction: it counts one representative of each reflected offcritical
pair, including pairs outside \(U\).

\paragraph{Theorem (global multiplicity profile and sharp coefficients).}
For every finite reflected positive-natural-multiplicity spectrum in
\([1/8,7/8]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-offcritical-excess}
 \boxed{\Delta\ge4R_5+16R_9+J_3.}
\end{equation}
The coefficient vector \((4,16,1)\) is maximal under coordinatewise
enlargement: if \(a\ge4\), \(b\ge16\), \(c\ge1\), and
\[
 \Delta\ge aR_5+bR_9+cJ_3
\]
holds for every finite spectrum, then \(a=4\), \(b=16\), \(c=1\).
This is a sharpness statement for the displayed vector, not a
classification of all other admissible triples.

\paragraph{Proof of the signed inequality.}
Retain the complete charged weight \(F\) and all its original
compensation terms. Put
\[
 e_i=1_{\Re\rho_i<1/2}(m_i-3)_+u_i,\qquad
 K_i=F_i-\frac{e_i+e_{i^*}}2.
\]
The weight \(K\) is symmetric. At a point strictly right of the
critical line, \(e_i=0\) and
\(e_{i^*}=(m_i-3)_+u_i\). If \(m_i\le2\), the excess vanishes and
\(K_i=F_i\ge u_i\). If \(m_i\ge3\), the already proved half-mass
floor gives
\[
 K_i\ge\frac{m_i u_i}{2}-\frac{(m_i-3)u_i}{2}
       =\frac32u_i.
\]
Thus \(K_i\ge u_i\) at every right point, with the stronger
three-half floor whenever its order is at least three. Reflection
proves nonnegativity on the left. On the critical line both new
charges vanish, so \(K=F\ge0\). The exact total is
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}K_i=A-E_U-Q-J_3.
\]

For a common-weak source of order at least five, its quadratic
reference has order at least three and unit weight \(4u_i\).
Its contribution is at least \(6u_i\). Every other quadratic
reference still contributes at least \(4u_i\). Therefore
\[
 \sum_{q_2(U)}K_i\ge4P+2R_5.

For a common-weak source of order at least nine, its quartic
reference has order at least three and unit weight \(16u_i\).
It contributes at least \(24u_i\), while every other quartic
reference contributes at least \(16u_i\). Consequently
\[
 \sum_{q_4(U)}K_i\ge16P+8R_9.
\]
Both maps are injective on \(U\), and the two images are disjoint
and strictly right of \(1/2\). The complete reflection budget gives
\[
 A-E_U-Q-J_3=\sum_S K
 \ge2(4P+2R_5+16P+8R_9)
 =40P+4R_5+16R_9.
\]
Substituting \(A=C+P+E_U\) proves
\eqref{eq:global-offcritical-excess}. The excess charge has been
subtracted before applying the budget; it is not an unsigned
estimate of a remainder.

\subsection{Sharp recovery from the integer residue lattice}
\label{sec:integer-residue-decoding}

Exact multiplicity recovery has an arithmetic refinement: the two local
orders are integers. This gives a sharp noise threshold even when the
reference point is itself a zero. For \(d\in\mathbb N\), \(d\ge2\), write
\[
 c_d(\rho;a,b)=-a+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}b,\qquad
 D_d=\rho+d-1,\qquad \gamma=\Im\rho.
\]
Here \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\); actual orders are nonnegative, but differences of
two order pairs range over all integers.

\paragraph{Theorem (exact high-height separation).}
\label{thm:integer-residue-separation}
Suppose \(|\gamma|\ge d(d-1)\). For every two distinct integer pairs,
\[
 |c_d(\rho;a,b)-c_d(\rho;a',b')|\,|D_d|\ge d(d-1).
\]
The constant is attained by the pairs \((d,1)\) and \((0,0)\).
Thus the minimum distance of the full residue lattice is exactly
\(d(d-1)/|D_d|\), with no upper bound on either integer coordinate.
\paragraph{Proof.}
Linearity reduces the assertion to a nonzero pair \((a,b)\). Keep the
complete complex numerator:
\[
 D_dc_d(\rho;a,b)=\rho(db-a)-(d-1)a.
\]
Put \(k=db-a\in\mathbb Z\). If \(k\ne0\), its imaginary coordinate has
absolute value \(|k\gamma|\ge|\gamma|\ge d(d-1)\). If \(k=0\), then
\(a=db\) and \(b\ne0\); the complete numerator is the nonzero real number
\(-d(d-1)b\), whose absolute value is at least \(d(d-1)\).
For \((a,b)=(d,1)\) it is exactly \(-d(d-1)\). Since \(\gamma\ne0\),
\(D_d\ne0\), and division gives the stated distance.

\paragraph{Corollary (optimal worst-case noise tolerance).}
\label{cor:integer-residue-noise}
Under the same height condition, a complex observation \(v\) is compatible
with at most one integer pair satisfying
\[
 |c_d(\rho;a,b)-v|<\frac{d(d-1)}{2|D_d|}.
\]
The inequality must be strict. The midpoint
\(v=c_d(\rho;d,1)/2\) has exactly this distance from both
\(c_d(\rho;d,1)\) and \(c_d(\rho;0,0)\), so every larger radius also
admits ambiguity.
\paragraph{Proof.}
Two compatible pairs would have distance strictly smaller than the
minimum in the separation theorem. The displayed
midpoint and the attaining pair prove sharpness. The triangle inequality
here controls measurement errors; the signed main residue was kept
exact throughout the separation proof.

There is a second, larger accuracy scale. At every nonreal \(\rho\), if
\[
 |c_d(\rho;a,b)-c_d(\rho;a',b')|\,|D_d|<|\gamma|,
\]
the integer imaginary coordinate forces
\[
 a-a'=d(b-b'),\qquad a\equiv a'\pmod d.
\]
Consequently two candidate residues each within
\(|\gamma|/(2|D_d|)\) of one observation must have the same target
multiplicity modulo \(d\). This statement does not require the
high-height condition. In particular, for fixed real part and increasing
height, the modular accuracy threshold tends to \(1/2\), whereas the
unrestricted two-order threshold is of order \(1/|\gamma|\).
An independently established target-order range \(0\le a<d\) turns this
modular information into its exact order; no such range is assumed here.

For the actual zeta readout, let
\[
 R_G(s)=-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)+G(s),\qquad
 P_{d,G}(s)=R_G(s)-\frac{s}{s+d-1}R_G(q_d(s)).
\]
If \(G\) is analytic throughout \(0<\Re s<1\), the previously proved
full local limit at every nonreal point \(\rho\) in that strip is
\[
 \lim_{s\to\rho}(s-\rho)P_{d,G}(s)
 =c_d\bigl(\rho;m(\rho),m(q_d(\rho))\bigr).
\]
The separation and modular conclusions therefore apply to the actual
analytic orders, including a zero at the reference point. No RH,
reference nonvanishing, simple-zero hypothesis or multiplicity cap is
used. Sharpness refers to the abstract residue model; no realization
of its extremizing order pairs by zeta zeros is asserted. The accuracy
bounds on an observed residue remain explicit hypotheses, rather than
new estimates for those observations.

These are integer-amplitude statements at a known spectral point.
They differ from continuous-parameter local stability in confluent
Prony reconstruction, such as
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.1137}{Batenkov--Yomdin,
On the accuracy of solving confluent Prony systems}.
Novelty priority is unestablished.
The maintained owners are \texttt{IntegerResidueLattice},
\texttt{IntegerResidueDecoding} and
\texttt{CarmichaelMultiplicityDecoding}; all local multiplicities and
compensation terms occur in their compiled statements.
The certified zero-free boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{Adaptive powers attain the optimal absolute tolerance}
\label{sec:adaptive-multiplicity-decoding}

A larger compensation power gives a different regime from the preceding
spacing theorem, with an exact, height-independent optimal tolerance. Keep
\[
 c_d(\rho;a,b)=-a+v_db,\qquad
 v_d=\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1},\qquad
 \beta=\Re\rho,\quad H=|\Im\rho|.
\]

\paragraph{Theorem (optimal adaptive multiplicity decoding).}
Suppose \(0\le\beta\le1\), \(H\ge2\), and \(d\in\mathbb N\) satisfies
\(d\ge2H\). The full integer residue lattice has minimum distance exactly
one:
\[
 |c_d(\rho;a,b)-c_d(\rho;a',b')|\ge1
 \quad\text{whenever }(a,b)\ne(a',b').
\]
It follows that an observation \(w\) is compatible with at most one
integer pair satisfying
\[
 |c_d(\rho;a,b)-w|<\frac12.
\]
The explicit choice
\[
 d_*(\rho)=\max\{4,\lceil2|\Im\rho|\rceil\}
\]
always satisfies the degree condition. Neither integer coordinate has an
upper bound. The absolute tolerance \(1/2\) is globally optimal over all
compensation powers, including powers outside the preceding small-power
regime.

\paragraph{Proof of the degree estimate.}
Retain the entire complex reference coefficient. Its exact imaginary part is
\[
 \Im v_d=\frac{d(d-1)\Im\rho}
                 {(\beta+d-1)^2+(\Im\rho)^2}.
\]
The hypotheses imply \(d\ge4\), \(0\le\beta+d-1\le d\), and \(H\le d/2\).
Therefore
\[
 |\rho+d-1|^2
   =(\beta+d-1)^2+H^2
   \le d^2+H^2
   \le\frac54d^2,
 \qquad
 d(d-1)H\ge2d(d-1)\ge\frac32d^2.
\]
The denominator is positive, so \(|\Im v_d|\ge1\).

\paragraph{Proof of exact spacing and recovery.}
Linearity reduces a difference of two residues to
\(c_d(\rho;a,b)\) at a nonzero integer pair. If \(b\ne0\), then
\[
 |\Im c_d(\rho;a,b)|=|\Im v_d|\,|b|\ge1.
\]
If \(b=0\), then \(a\ne0\) and
\(|c_d(\rho;a,0)|=|a|\ge1\). The pair \((1,0)\) attains one exactly.
Two residues within strict distance \(1/2\) of one observation would
have distance less than one, which is impossible. The degree choice
\(d_*\) satisfies \(d_*\ge4\) and \(d_*\ge2H\) by the defining ceiling
inequality.

\paragraph{Proof of global optimality, including positive orders.}
For every power \(d\) and every point \(\rho\), the strictly positive
integer pairs \((1,1)\) and \((2,1)\) have residues differing by exactly
one. Their midpoint
\[
 w=c_d(\rho;1,1)-\frac12
\]
has distance exactly \(1/2\) from both. Consequently closed error balls
of radius \(1/2\), and open balls of every larger radius, admit
ambiguity. The adaptive construction attains the largest possible
worst-case strict radius. This sharpness statement concerns the integer
residue model; it does not assert that zeta realizes those two order
pairs at one spectral point.

\paragraph{Actual zeta certificate.}
Let \(G\) be analytic throughout the open critical strip, and use the full
readout \(P_{d,G}\) from the preceding section. For every point \(\rho\)
with \(0<\Re\rho<1\), \(H\ge2\), and \(d\ge2H\), let
\[
 r=\lim_{s\to\rho}(s-\rho)P_{d,G}(s)
   =c_d\bigl(\rho;m(\rho),m(q_d(\rho))\bigr).
\]
If an observation \(w\) and candidate natural orders \(A,B\) satisfy
\[
 |r-w|<\frac12,\qquad |c_d(\rho;A,B)-w|<\frac12,
\]
then \(A=m(\rho)\) and \(B=m(q_d(\rho))\).
Both reference and target orders remain in this statement. It assumes
neither RH nor a multiplicity cap nor reference nonvanishing.
The power \(d_*(\rho)\) is fixed for each spectral point before taking
its local limit; no simultaneous sampling or height limit is used.

The new result concerns the exact conditioning threshold of the
compensated observable. A uniform estimate guaranteeing the required
measurement error for increasing degrees remains an analytic input.
The maintained owners are \texttt{IntegerResidueUnitLattice},
\texttt{IntegerResidueAdaptive} and
\texttt{CarmichaelAdaptiveMultiplicity}. Their compiled statements
include the explicit degree, both local orders, the strict error
threshold and the positive-pair ambiguity example.
Priority is unestablished, and the certified zero-free boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{Exact spacing at every compensation degree}
\label{sec:exact-strip-residue-spacing}

Write
\[
 c_d(\rho;a,b)=-a+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}b,\quad
 H=|\Im\rho|,\quad D=|\rho+d-1|,\quad p=d(d-1).
\]
The reference coordinate is retained throughout.

\paragraph{Theorem (full strip spacing).}
For every integer \(d\ge2\), every \(0\le\Re\rho\le1\) with
\(H\ge2\), and every nonzero \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\),
\[
 |c_d(\rho;a,b)|\ge \lambda_d(\rho),\qquad
 \lambda_d(\rho)=\min\{1,p/D\}.
\]
This is the exact minimum: \((1,0)\) attains \(1\), while
\((d,1)\) attains \(p/D\). In particular, unit spacing holds
if and only if \(D\le p\). Both integer coordinates are unrestricted.

\paragraph{Proof.}
If \(H\ge p\), the earlier high-height lattice theorem gives
\(p\le D|c_d|\), and \(D\ge H\ge p\).
Suppose instead \(2\le H<p\); then \(d\ge3\).
Put \(\beta=\Re\rho\) and \(v=d\rho/(\rho+d-1)\).
The exact imaginary coordinate is
\[
 |\Im v|=\frac{pH}{D^2},\qquad
 D^2=(\beta+d-1)^2+H^2\le d^2+H^2\le2pH.
\]
Here \(d^2\le2p\le pH\) and \(H^2\le pH\).
Consequently every row \(|b|\ge2\) has \(|c_d|\ge1\).
The row \(b=0\) follows from integer separation, and simultaneous
negation reduces \(b=-1\) to \(b=1\).

In that remaining row keep the full numerator
\[
 N=c_d(\rho;a,1)(\rho+d-1)
   =(d-a)\rho-(d-1)a.
\]
For \(a\ge d\), its real part is at most \(-p\), so
\(|N|\ge p\). For \(a\le0\),
\[
 |\Im N|\ge dH\ge d+H\ge D.
\]
If \(2\le a\le d-1\), the strip gives
\(\Re N\le-d(a-1)\le-d\), whereas
\(|\Im N|\ge H\). Hence \(|N|^2\ge d^2+H^2\ge D^2\).
Finally, for \(a=1\),
\[
 |N|^2\ge(d-1)^2H^2\ge d^2+H^2\ge D^2,
\]
since \(d(d-2)H^2\ge4d(d-2)\ge d^2\) for \(d\ge3\).
These cases establish \(D|c_d|\ge\min\{D,p\}\).
The two explicit attaining pairs prove equality of the minimum.
Applying the bound to integer differences proves the pair separation.

\paragraph{Joint multiplicity certification.}
If two candidate residues have total distance from the same observation
strictly below \(\lambda_d(\rho)\), they have the same two integer
coordinates, by the triangle inequality and pair separation.
The actual-zeta consumer identifies those coordinates with
\[
 m(\rho),\qquad m((\rho+d-1)/d),
\]
using the full compensated local residue. It assumes an analytic
correction on the open strip and the stated achieved observation error;
it assumes neither RH, reference nonvanishing nor a multiplicity cap.
Thus the previous sufficient restriction \(d\ge2H\) is replaced by
an exact disk criterion for unit spacing, and every smaller degree
has its exact spacing available as well.

The maintained owners are \texttt{IntegerResidueStrip} and
\texttt{CarmichaelStripMultiplicity}. The seven lattice statements
and the actual-zeta consumer are kernel checked. This is a conditioning
and multiplicity theorem; no uniform observation-error estimate or new
zero-free exponent is asserted. Priority is unestablished, and the
certified boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{The exact minimum compensation degree}
\label{sec:optimal-residue-degree}

The full spacing formula solves the degree-selection problem completely.
Let \(H=|\Im\rho|\ge2\), \(0\le\Re\rho\le1\), and set
\[
 n=\lceil\sqrt H\rceil,\qquad
 D_d=|\rho+d-1|,\qquad p_d=d(d-1).
\]
Define the explicit integer
\[
 d_*(\rho)=
 \begin{cases}
 n,&D_n\le p_n,\\
 n+1,&D_n>p_n.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus only one exact disk test is needed.

\paragraph{Theorem (complete degree classification).}
For every integer \(d\ge2\),
\[
 \bigl[\ |c_d(\rho;a,b)|\ge1\text{ for every nonzero }(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\ \bigr]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\ge d_*(\rho).
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \sqrt H<d_*(\rho)<\sqrt H+2.
\]
In particular \(d_*\) is the exact minimum degree attaining unit spacing
in the unrestricted integer-code model, including disk-boundary equality.

\paragraph{Proof.}
We have \(n\ge2\) and \(H\le n^2\). The degree \(n+1\) always passes:
\[
 D_{n+1}^2\le(n+1)^2+H^2
 \le(n+1)^2+n^4\le n^2(n+1)^2=p_{n+1}^2.
\]
The final polynomial margin is \(2n^3-2n-1>0\) for \(n\ge2\).
Every feasible degree satisfies
\[
 H\le D_d\le d(d-1)<d^2,
\]
so \(\sqrt H<d\) and hence \(n\le d\).
If degree \(n\) passes, it is therefore minimal; if it fails,
\(n+1\) is minimal. This also proves that \(d_*\) itself passes.

To classify every larger degree, retain the complete denominator.
For integers \(e\ge d\ge2\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 D_e\le D_d+(e-d),\qquad
 p_e-p_d-(e-d)=(e-d)(e+d-2)\ge0.
\]
Thus disk feasibility is upward closed. Combine this with the proved
unit-spacing equivalence \(D_d\le p_d\) to obtain the asserted
classification. Applying \(\sqrt H<d\) to \(d_*\) proves the lower
bound. For the upper bound use
\(d_*\le n+1<\sqrt H+2\).

\paragraph{Actual-zeta consumer and quantitative scope.}
At this exact minimum degree the full local residue remains
\[
 r=-m(\rho)+\frac{d_*\rho}{\rho+d_*-1}\,
            m((\rho+d_*-1)/d_*).
\]
The compiled actual-zeta consumer certifies both orders if the actual
and candidate residues have total distance below \(1\) from the same
observation. The analytic correction is required on the open strip;
RH, reference nonvanishing and upper multiplicity assumptions are absent.

The previous explicit sufficient choice was
\(\max\{4,\lceil2H\rceil\}\), of order \(H\).
The new choice is exact and of order \(\sqrt H\), while retaining
unit spacing. An independent estimate achieving the observation error
as the degree varies remains necessary for numerical or arithmetic use.
No new zero-free exponent is asserted.

The maintained owners are \texttt{IntegerResidueOptimalDegree} and
\texttt{CarmichaelOptimalMultiplicity}; their fifteen degree statements
and actual-zeta consumer are kernel checked. Priority is unestablished.

\subsection{Exact spacing of a finite compensation panel}
\label{sec:residue-panel-spacing}

Several powers can be measured together without discarding any reference
multiplicity. Let \(D\subset\{2,3,\ldots\}\) be finite and nonempty, and put
\[
 K=\max D,\qquad L=\operatorname{lcm}\{d:d\in D\},\qquad
 c_d(z;a,b_d)=-a+\frac{dz}{z+d-1}b_d.
\]
The integer \(a\) is shared by every row, whereas each \(b_d\) is retained
independently. For \(0\le\Re z\le1\), \(H=|\Im z|\ge2\), define
\[
 \|c\|_D=\max_{d\in D}|c_d(z;a,b_d)|,\qquad
 \lambda_D(z)=\min\left(1,\frac{L(K-1)}{|z+K-1|}\right).
\]
Here a tuple is nonzero if \(a\ne0\), or \(b_d\ne0\) for some \(d\in D\).
No assertion concerns coordinates outside this finite support.

\paragraph{Theorem (exact panel spacing).}
The minimum of \(\|c\|_D\) over all nonzero supported integer tuples is
exactly \(\lambda_D(z)>0\). Both competing values are attained: the tuple
\(a=1,\ b_d=0\) has norm \(1\), and
\(a=L,\ b_d=L/d\) has norm \(L(K-1)/|z+K-1|\).
Consequently all nonzero tuples have norm at least \(1\) if and only if
\[
 |z+K-1|\le L(K-1).
\]
There is no bound on the integer target or on any reference coordinate.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Write \(z=\beta+i\gamma\), \(t_d=\beta+d-1\), and retain the full numerator
\[
 N_d=c_d(z;a,b_d)(z+d-1)
     =k_d(z+d-1)-d(d-1)b_d,\qquad k_d=db_d-a\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus \(\Re N_d=k_dt_d-d(d-1)b_d\) and
\(\Im N_d=k_d\gamma\), including the compensation term.
First suppose \(d\ge3\) and \(k_d\ne0\). Simultaneous negation lets us assume
\(k_d\ge1\). If \(k_d\ge d-1\), then
\[
 |N_d|^2\ge(d-1)^2H^2\ge d^2+H^2\ge|z+d-1|^2;
\]
the middle inequality follows from \(H\ge2\) and \(d\ge3\).
If \(1\le k_d\le d-2\), then \(b_d=0\) gives \(|c_d|=k_d\ge1\).
For \(b_d\ge1\), use \(t_d\le d\) to obtain \(\Re N_d\le-d\);
for \(b_d\le-1\), use \(t_d\ge d-1\) to obtain \(\Re N_d\ge d\).
In both remaining cases \(|N_d|^2\ge d^2+H^2\), proving \(|c_d|\ge1\).

For \(d=2\), the same conclusion holds when \(k_d\ne0\) and
\(a\notin\{-1,1\}\). Indeed \(k_d\ge2\) is controlled by the imaginary
part. When \(k_d=1\), the excluded targets remove \(b_d=0,1\);
all remaining integers have \(|\Re N_d|\ge2\). The exception is real:
\(d=2,z=1+2i,a=b_d=1\) has \(|c_d|^2=1/2\).
If \(K\ge3\) and this exceptional target occurs in a selected degree-two
row, the row of degree \(K\) is necessarily nonresonant, since
\(K b_K=\pm1\) is impossible. It therefore supplies unit norm itself.
If \(K=2\), the entire panel is \(D=\{2\}\), and the already proved
single-degree strip formula gives the stated radius, including its exception.
Thus whenever any row is nonresonant and \(K\ge3\), the panel norm is at least
\(1\), hence at least \(\lambda_D(z)\).

If every row is resonant, then \(db_d=a\) for all \(d\in D\), so \(L\mid a\).
A nonzero supported tuple has \(a\ne0\); otherwise every \(b_d\) would vanish.
It follows that \(|a|\ge L\), and the exact norm of each row is
\[
 |c_d|=\frac{|a|(d-1)}{|z+d-1|}.
\]
The reference fraction increases with \(d\). For completeness, with
\(t=d-1\), \(u=e-1\), \(d\le e\), the cross-multiplied square difference is
\[
 u^2\bigl((\beta+t)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)
 -t^2\bigl((\beta+u)^2+\gamma^2\bigr)
 =(u-t)\bigl(2tu\beta+(\beta^2+\gamma^2)(u+t)\bigr)\ge0.
\]
All denominators are positive because \(H\ge2\).
Hence the maximum resonant norm occurs at \(K\), proving its lower bound
\(L(K-1)/|z+K-1|\). Taking \(a=L,b_d=L/d\) attains that value, while
\(a=1,b_d=0\) attains \(1\). Taking the better of these tuples completes
the exact minimum and the unit-spacing classification.

\paragraph{Robust simultaneous decoding and the actual zeta readout.}
For two integer tuples \((a,b_d)\), \((a',b'_d)\) and observations \(w_d\),
if, for every \(d\in D\),
\[
 |c_d(z;a,b_d)-w_d|+|c_d(z;a',b'_d)-w_d|<\lambda_D(z),
\]
then \(a=a'\) and \(b_d=b'_d\) for every \(d\in D\).
Indeed subtraction preserves the same shared target difference in every row;
the triangle inequality would give a nonzero difference tuple norm strictly
below its proved minimum. The strict inequality is essential at a spacing tie.

This transfers to the full compensated zeta readout with analytic correction
\(G\) on \(0<\Re s<1\). Fix \(D\), and assume the actual local residue limits
\[
 r_d=\lim_{s\to\rho}(s-\rho)
   \mathcal R_d[\mathrm{zetaMultiplicityReadout}(G)](s),\qquad d\in D,
\]
where \(\mathcal R_d\) denotes the existing affine power readout and the
limit is punctured. The proved transfer identifies
\[
 r_d=c_d\bigl(\rho;m_\zeta(\rho),m_\zeta(q_d(\rho))\bigr),\qquad
 q_d(s)=\frac{s+d-1}{d}.
\]
If nonnegative candidate orders \(A,B_d\) and observations \(w_d\) satisfy
\[
 |r_d-w_d|+|c_d(\rho;A,B_d)-w_d|<\lambda_D(\rho)
 \quad(d\in D),
\]
then \(m_\zeta(\rho)=A\) and \(m_\zeta(q_d(\rho))=B_d\) for every selected row.
This requires neither RH, reference nonvanishing, nor a multiplicity cap.
The local-limit and error hypotheses remain explicit: no uniform achieved
observation-error estimate or growing-panel sampling limit is supplied here.

\paragraph{Cubic and quartic measurements.}
For \(D=\{3,4\}\), we have \(K=4\), \(L=12\), and exactly
\[
 \lambda_{\{3,4\}}(z)=\min\left(1,\frac{36}{|z+3|}\right),\qquad
 \|c\|_{\{3,4\}}=\max(|c_3|,|c_4|).
\]
The quartic row alone has spacing \(\min(1,12/|z+3|)\).
The joint panel therefore gives a factor of three when \(|z+3|\ge36\),
a factor \(|z+3|/12\) when \(12<|z+3|<36\), and equality when
\(|z+3|\le12\). This charges two measurements and certifies both reference
orders. It is an exact robustness gain, not a new arithmetic error estimate
or an improvement of the certified QRH boundary \(7/8\).

The reusable geometry, full panel theorem, and actual-zeta consumer are
kernel checked in \texttt{IntegerResiduePanelGeometry},
\texttt{IntegerResiduePanel}, and \texttt{CarmichaelPanelMultiplicity}.
The closest robustness comparison is CRT decoding with bounded uncertain
remainders, for example Guo--Xia, \emph{Exact Dynamic Range--Robustness Tradeoff
for Chinese Remainder Theorem under Non-Uniformly Bounded Remainder Errors}
(arXiv:2609.22615). Here the exact norm minimum concerns complex compensated
quotient coordinates and unrestricted integers. This distinction is proved;
priority for the resulting spacing theorem is not established by that comparison.

\subsection{Optimal two-power design and the cube-root degree law}
\label{sec:two-power-degree-budget}

The panel spacing theorem also solves a design problem: with at most two
power measurements, how small can the maximum power degree be while retaining
unit separation for every integer multiplicity tuple? This is an optimization
over the unrestricted code model, with the same target coordinate in both rows.
It does not impose additional zero-free information on the admissible tuples.

For \(2\le d\le e\), let
\(\Delta_{d,e}(z)=\lambda_{\{d,e\}}(z)\), allowing \(d=e\) to represent a
single measurement. Fix an integer cap \(K\ge3\) and put
\[
 \Lambda_K(z)=\min\left(1,
    \frac{K(K-1)^2}{|z+K-1|}\right).
\]
Assume throughout \(0\le\Re z\le1\) and \(H=|\Im z|\ge2\).

\paragraph{Theorem (complete two-power optimum).}
For every \(2\le d\le e\le K\),
\[
 \Delta_{d,e}(z)\le\Lambda_K(z),\qquad
 \Delta_{K-1,K}(z)=\Lambda_K(z).
\]
In particular some one- or two-power panel capped at \(K\) has unit spacing
for all nonzero supported integer tuples if and only if
\[
 |z+K-1|\le K(K-1)^2.
\]
The optimal value includes attainment of the inner integer-code minimum,
by the full panel theorem. No claim of a unique optimal pair is needed:
the truncation at \(1\) permits ties.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The pair formula gives
\[
 \Delta_{d,e}(z)=\min\left(1,
   \operatorname{lcm}(d,e)\frac{e-1}{|z+e-1|}\right).
\]
If \(d<e\le K\), then \(d\le K-1\) and
\(\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)\le de\le K(K-1)\).
If \(d=e\), the least common multiple is \(d\le K\le K(K-1)\).
The previously proved monotonicity of
\((e-1)/|z+e-1|\), with positive denominator and nonnegative numerator,
therefore proves
\[
 \operatorname{lcm}(d,e)\frac{e-1}{|z+e-1|}
 \le K(K-1)\frac{K-1}{|z+K-1|}.
\]
Taking the minimum with \(1\) preserves this inequality.
Consecutive integers are coprime, so
\(\operatorname{lcm}(K-1,K)=K(K-1)\), proving equality for that pair.
Since the denominator is positive, \(\Lambda_K\ge1\) is exactly the displayed
disk condition. This proves both directions of the existential unit criterion.
The lower cap \(K=2\) is outside this theorem and reduces to the earlier
single-degree strip formula; no degree-one row is introduced.

\paragraph{Theorem (sharp degree exponent).}
Unit spacing under cap \(K\) requires \(H<K^3\), while
\(H\le(K-1)^3\) guarantees it. More explicitly, for any \(R\ge0\) with
\(R^3=H\), every sufficient cap satisfies \(R<K\), and
\[
 K=\lceil R\rceil+1\ge3
\]
is sufficient and lies strictly below \(R+2\).
Thus the best possible maximum-degree exponent with two measurements is
\(1/3\) in this unrestricted integer-code model. The corresponding
single-power exponent established above is \(1/2\).

\paragraph{Proof.}
The exact unit criterion gives
\[
 H\le|z+K-1|\le K(K-1)^2<K^3,
\]
where the final strict inequality uses \(K\ge3\).
Conversely, writing \(z=\beta+i\gamma\), the closed-strip hypotheses give
\[
 |z+K-1|^2=(\beta+K-1)^2+H^2\le K^2+H^2,
 \qquad |z+K-1|\le K+H.
\]
For \(K\ge3\), we also have \(K\le(K-1)^2\). Hence
\[
 H\le(K-1)^3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |z+K-1|\le H+K\le(K-1)^3+(K-1)^2=K(K-1)^2.
\]
For the explicit choice, let \(n=\lceil R\rceil\).
Since \(R^3=H\ge2\), we have \(n\ge2\), and monotonicity of the cube on
nonnegative reals gives \(H\le n^3\). The sufficient bound applies to
\(K=n+1\). The ceiling inequality \(n<R+1\) gives \(K<R+2\).
Finally \(H<K^3\), together with \(R^3=H\) and nonnegativity, yields \(R<K\).
All these root and ceiling conclusions, as well as the cubic thresholds,
are formalized; they are not numerical growth heuristics.

\paragraph{Actual zeta multiplicity certification.}
Let \(\rho\) lie in the open strip at height \(H\ge2\), take
\(R\ge0\) with \(R^3=H\), and measure the powers
\[
 D=\{\lceil R\rceil,\lceil R\rceil+1\}.
\]
For analytic correction \(G\), assume the two actual punctured residue limits
\(r_d\) from the preceding section, and suppose observations \(w_d\) and
nonnegative candidate orders \(A,B_d\) obey
\[
 |r_d-w_d|+|c_d(\rho;A,B_d)-w_d|<1\qquad(d\in D).
\]
Then \(A=m_\zeta(\rho)\) and
\(B_d=m_\zeta(q_d(\rho))\) for both selected degrees.
Indeed the consecutive pair has spacing at least \(1\) by the formal
cube-root construction, and the actual panel readout theorem applies with
all reference orders retained. The strict total-error condition is an explicit hypothesis of this
certification statement; a uniform achieved observation
error is not proved here. RH and a multiplicity cap are not hypotheses.
The degree optimality concerns unrestricted integer codes, rather than the
smaller set of tuples that additional zero-free input could permit.
The certified QRH boundary remains \(7/8\).

The exact optimum and cube-root construction are proved in
\texttt{IntegerResidueTwoPower}; the actual transfer is
\texttt{zeta\_two\_power\_cube\_root\_certifies\_orders} in
\texttt{CarmichaelTwoPowerMultiplicity}.
For context, Yan--Gan--Liu--Hu, \emph{Moduli Selection in Robust Chinese
Remainder Theorem: Closed-Form Solutions and Layered Design}
(arXiv:2511.22757), optimize robust CRT moduli under dynamic-range and modulus
constraints. Our objective is instead the exact minimum complex norm for
unrestricted compensated quotient codes. This supplies a precise distinction;
it does not establish a literature priority claim.

\subsection{Uniform scalar recovery on a cubic orbit}
\label{sec:scalar-cubic-orbit}

The previous optimum treats different powers at one fixed point and allows
independent reference orders. A different observation pattern exploits the
coherence of the references themselves. Two real measurements of the same
cubic compensated readout, taken at successive power-map points, suffice for
uniform multiplicity recovery. The installed unconditional \(7/8\) theorem
supplies the terminal nonvanishing input.

Write
\[
 q(s)=\frac{s+2}{3},\qquad q(q(s))=\frac{s+8}{9},\qquad
 t(\rho)=\Re\frac{3\rho}{\rho+2}.
\]
Here cubic means the parameter \(d=3\) in the affine power compensation.
Fix a nonreal \(\rho\) with \(0<\Re\rho<1\), and an analytic correction
\(G\) on that strip. With the full readout \(\mathcal R_3\) used above, put
\[
 r_0=\lim_{s\to\rho}\Re\bigl((s-\rho)
     \mathcal R_3[\mathrm{zetaMultiplicityReadout}(G)](s)\bigr),
\]
\[
 r_1=\lim_{s\to q(\rho)}\Re\bigl((s-q(\rho))
     \mathcal R_3[\mathrm{zetaMultiplicityReadout}(G)](s)\bigr).
\]
Both are punctured local limits. Their existence is proved, rather than
assumed. Set \(A=m_\zeta(\rho)\), \(B=m_\zeta(q(\rho))\).

\paragraph{Theorem (two real channels, uniform half-unit recovery).}
The exact scalar values are
\[
 (r_0,r_1)=(-A+t(\rho)B,-B).
\]
For real observations \(y_0,y_1\) satisfying
\(|r_0-y_0|<1/2\) and \(|r_1-y_1|<1/2\), define
\[
 \widehat B=\left\lfloor-y_1+\frac12\right\rfloor,\qquad
 \widehat A=\left\lfloor t(\rho)\widehat B-y_0+\frac12\right\rfloor.
\]
Then \(\widehat A=A\) and \(\widehat B=B\).
There is no height lower bound, multiplicity cap, or RH hypothesis.
The point \(\rho\), and hence the coefficient \(t(\rho)\), is fixed exactly;
the noise hypotheses concern the two scalar observations.

\paragraph{Proof of the endpoint and limits.}
Both \(q(\rho)\) and \(q(q(\rho))\) remain in the open strip, and are nonreal.
Moreover
\[
 \Re q(q(\rho))=\frac{\Re\rho+8}{9}>\frac89>\frac78.
\]
The already proved unconditional nonvanishing theorem therefore gives
\(m_\zeta(q(q(\rho)))=0\). This input is compatible with a zero at
\(\rho\) or \(q(\rho)\); neither is excluded in the argument.
The existing unconditional local-limit theorem identifies the full complex
residues as
\[
 -A+\frac{3\rho}{\rho+2}B,
 \qquad
 -B+\frac{3q(\rho)}{q(\rho)+2}\,m_\zeta(q(q(\rho)))=-B.
\]
Taking real parts is continuous and proves the two asserted real limits.
Every target and reference term has been retained until the terminal
reference vanishes by the proved arithmetic input. The analytic correction
is covered by the local-limit theorem. No RH-dependent Fourier limit is used.

\paragraph{Proof of recovery and sharpness.}
For any real \(t\) and integers \(a,b\), consider the scalar code
\[
 T_t(a,b)=(-a+tb,-b).
\]
If \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), its maximum coordinate magnitude is at least \(1\):
when \(b\ne0\), the second coordinate supplies that bound; when \(b=0\),
the first is \(-a\). Equality is attained at \((a,b)=(1,0)\).
Thus the exact code spacing is \(1\), independently of \(t\).

For the explicit inverse, the elementary floor identity
\[
 |x-a|<\frac12,\quad a\in\mathbb Z
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left\lfloor x+\frac12\right\rfloor=a
\]
first gives \(\widehat B=B\). With that integer recovered exactly,
\(t\widehat B-y_0-A=r_0-y_0\), so the same identity gives \(\widehat A=A\).
There is no coefficient-times-noise error propagated from the second row.
In the unrestricted integer-code model, the half-unit threshold is sharp:
the observation \((-1/2,0)\) is at maximum-coordinate distance exactly
\(1/2\) from both \(T_t(0,0)\) and \(T_t(1,0)\).
This sharpness is a code-model statement, not a claim that every integer
order pair is realized by the fixed zeta function.

The formula holds for all real coefficients. If the coefficient itself is
perturbed, its error is multiplied by the recovered integer \(B\); the stated
uniform noise theorem does not include such a perturbation. It also does not
supply a uniform finite-sampling error estimate for the residue observations.

\paragraph{A signed quantized signal.}
For nonnegative integers \(A,B\) with \(A+B\ge1\),
\[
 \min(r_0,r_1)\le-1.
\]
Indeed \(B\ge1\) implies \(r_1=-B\le-1\); otherwise \(B=0\), so
\(A\ge1\) and \(r_0=-A\le-1\). This is a one-sided joint statement:
the first row may contain the full compensating reference contribution,
while the second row determines that reference exactly.
It is a nonzero-signal theorem, not an independent inequality ruling out
such a signal. The certified QRH boundary remains \(7/8\).

\paragraph{Why the second scalar channel matters.}
Even bounded nonzero integer orders can become invisible to a single
unrotated scalar measurement. In the algebraic code model take
\(a=3,b=1\), \(z=\beta+i\gamma\), \(0\le\beta\le1\), \(H=|\gamma|\ge2\).
The complete cubic code is
\[
 c_3(z;3,1)=-3+\frac{3z}{z+2}=-\frac6{z+2},\qquad
 \Re c_3(z;3,1)=-\frac{6(\beta+2)}{(\beta+2)^2+H^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
 |\Re c_3(z;3,1)|\le\frac{18}{H^2}.
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), choosing
\(z=1/4+i(2+20/\varepsilon)\) makes this magnitude less than
\(\varepsilon\). At that fixed real part the first reference has real part
\(3/4\), while the terminal reference has real part \(11/12\), so the
endpoint nonvanishing input alone does not remove the intermediate integer.
The second scalar channel remains \(-1\) and prevents the loss.
This is a uniform code-model obstruction; it asserts no arithmetic example
with zeta orders \((3,1)\).

The generic scalar spacing, explicit floor inverse, sharpness and one-channel
obstruction are kernel checked in \texttt{AnchoredScalarMultiplicity}.
The endpoint, unconditional real limits, actual inverse and signed signal
are proved in \texttt{CarmichaelScalarCubicOrbit}.
Triangular integer rounding is classical, related to Babai's nearest-plane
method (\emph{Combinatorica} 6 (1986), DOI:10.1007/BF02579403) and subsequent
successive-interference-cancellation decoding, for example Liu--Ling--Stehle,
\emph{Decoding by Sampling} (arXiv:1003.0064). The proposed new application is
the scalar-only cubic-orbit theorem for the full compensated zeta readout;
priority for that application remains unestablished. No transfer of a global
metaplectic or character estimate is asserted here.

\subsection{All-character scalar recovery, including real strip points}
\label{sec:dirichlet-scalar-orbit}

The scalar cubic-orbit construction extends to every Dirichlet character,
including principal characters and modulus~\(1\). More significantly, the
local residue calculation can be established on the real axis, where the
nonreal residue wedge used earlier gives no information.
Write \(L_\chi(s)=L(s,\chi)\), and for \(\rho\ne1\) put
\[
 m_\chi(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_\rho L_\chi\in\mathbb N_0,
 \qquad R_{\chi,G}(s)=-\frac{L_\chi'(s)}{L_\chi(s)}+G(s),
 \qquad q_d(s)=\frac{s+d-1}{d}.
\]
The order here is finite, including at a nonzero point where it is zero.
The modulus is any positive integer; no primitivity, nonprincipality or
nonreal-point hypothesis is imposed.

\paragraph{Theorem (exact all-character affine residues).}
For every \(\rho\ne1\) and \(G\) analytic at \(\rho\),
\[
 \lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}(s-\rho)R_{\chi,G}(s)=-m_\chi(\rho).
\]
If \(d>1\), \(0<\Re\rho<1\), and \(G\) is analytic throughout that open
strip, define
\[
 P_{\chi,d,G}(s)=R_{\chi,G}(s)
       -\frac{s}{s+d-1}R_{\chi,G}(q_d(s)).
\]
Then the actual punctured-neighborhood limit exists and equals
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{\chi,d}(\rho)
 :=\lim_{s\to\rho}(s-\rho)P_{\chi,d,G}(s)
 =-m_\chi(\rho)
   +\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}m_\chi(q_d(\rho)).
 \label{eq:dirichlet-power-multiplicity}
\end{equation}
In particular, the conclusion does not assume the existence of a residue
limit, does not discard the reference multiplicity, and is independent of
\(G\).

\paragraph{Proof.}
The maintained finite-factorization theorem gives
\(L_\chi(s)=(s-\rho)^n g(s)\) near \(\rho\ne1\), with \(g\) analytic
and \(g(\rho)\ne0\). Finiteness follows from analyticity off~\(1\) and
nonvanishing at~\(2\), so the identically zero case is excluded. Uniqueness
of analytic order identifies \(n=m_\chi(\rho)\); it is positive precisely
when \(L_\chi(\rho)=0\). The logarithmic derivative of the factorization
has residue \(n\), while \((s-\rho)G(s)\to0\). This proves the first
limit. For the affine reference, \(q_d(s)-q_d(\rho)=(s-\rho)/d\).
Consequently its scaled residue is \(-d\,m_\chi(q_d(\rho))\), and the
subtraction in \(P_{\chi,d,G}\) supplies the plus sign and the coefficient
\(d\rho/(\rho+d-1)\) in~\eqref{eq:dirichlet-power-multiplicity}.
Both points stay inside the open strip, and \(\Re(\rho+d-1)>0\), so neither
the pole at~\(1\) nor a zero denominator is encountered. This proof applies
to real \(\rho\) without change.

\paragraph{Theorem (uniform scalar cubic-orbit inverse and zero test).}
For every character \(\chi\) and every \(0<\Re\rho<1\), set
\[
 A=m_\chi(\rho),\quad B=m_\chi(q_3(\rho)),\quad
 t=\Re\frac{3\rho}{\rho+2},\qquad
 r_0=\Re\mathcal R_{\chi,3}(\rho),\quad
 r_1=\Re\mathcal R_{\chi,3}(q_3(\rho)).
\]
The two limits exist and satisfy
\begin{equation}
 (r_0,r_1)=(-A+tB,-B).
 \label{eq:dirichlet-scalar-orbit}
\end{equation}
For observations \(y_0,y_1\) with \(|r_j-y_j|<1/2\) for \(j=0,1\),
\[
 \widehat B=\left\lfloor-y_1+\frac12\right\rfloor=B,
 \qquad
 \widehat A=\left\lfloor t\widehat B-y_0+\frac12\right\rfloor=A.
\]
There are also the exact equivalences
\begin{align*}
 L_\chi(\rho)=0\ \text{or}\ L_\chi(q_3(\rho))=0
 &\iff \min(r_0,r_1)\le-1,\\
 &\iff \min(y_0,y_1)<-\frac12
       \quad\text{under the stated observation-error hypotheses}.
\end{align*}
The Boolean test needs no evaluation of \(t\); the multiplicity inverse
uses its exact value. The recovered pair distinguishes a zero at the target
from one only at the first reference.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The twice-iterated point is \(q_3(q_3(\rho))=q_9(\rho)\), with
\[
 \Re q_9(\rho)=\frac{\Re\rho+8}{9}>\frac89>\frac78,
 \qquad \Re q_9(\rho)<1.
\]
The installed unconditional all-character QRH theorem therefore gives
\(L_\chi(q_9(\rho))\ne0\). Its principal-character exception is avoided
because this point is not~\(1\). The terminal order is zero. Applying
\eqref{eq:dirichlet-power-multiplicity} at the first two orbit points and
taking real parts gives~\eqref{eq:dirichlet-scalar-orbit}.
The second observation recovers the integer \(B\) first. Substituting that
exact integer into the first rounding step avoids amplifying its observation
error by \(t\), and recovers \(A\) under the same half-unit tolerance.
If \(B\ge1\), then \(r_1\le-1\); if \(B=0\) and \(A\ge1\), then
\(r_0=-A\le-1\). If both orders vanish, both residues are zero. This proves
the first equivalence. Strict half-unit error moves a coordinate at most
\(-1\) below \(-1/2\), while both zero coordinates remain above \(-1/2\),
which proves the noisy equivalence with every compensation term retained.

\paragraph{Why the real-axis extension matters.}
At \(\rho=1/2\) the cubic coefficient is exactly \(3/5\). Thus the
nonzero integer pair \((A,B)=(3,5)\) has
\[
 -3+\frac{3(1/2)}{1/2+2}\,5=0,
 \qquad (r_0,r_1)=(0,-5).
\]
Even a complex observation of the first residue alone loses this pair,
whereas the second scalar coordinate restores it. The reference points are
\(q_3(1/2)=5/6\) and \(q_9(1/2)=17/18\); the QRH endpoint argument removes
the latter order, not the former. This is an exact integer-code example,
not a claim that an actual character has zero orders \((3,5)\).

The local limits and order criterion are kernel checked in
\texttt{CarmichaelDirichletResidues}; all-character endpoint nonvanishing,
the two real limits, the decoder and both zero tests are checked in
\texttt{CarmichaelDirichletScalarOrbit}. The central collision is checked in
\texttt{AnchoredScalarMultiplicity}. The tolerance is independent of
conductor, height and multiplicity. Convergence is asserted pointwise:
no common finite observation radius or arithmetic sampling-error rate is
proved. Standard analytic continuation and the principal-character pole
are classical background (DLMF, Section~25.15); the proposed application is
the coupled all-character scalar recovery, and its literature priority
remains unestablished. The QRH boundary stays \(7/8\); none of these recovery
theorems excludes a zero or supplies the missing arithmetic cancellation.

\subsection{Three quadratic scalar channels and a two-channel obstruction}
\label{sec:quadratic-scalar-chain}

Cubic rows are not required for scalar multiplicity recovery. Three quadratic
rows suffice uniformly, and an exact two-row collision explains the additional
channel in the order-only model.

\paragraph{Theorem (finite anchored scalar chains).}
Let \(N\ge0\), \(m_0,\ldots,m_N\in\mathbb Z\), \(m_N=0\), and let
\(t_0,\ldots,t_{N-1}\in\mathbb R\) be arbitrary. Put
\[
 r_j=-m_j+t_jm_{j+1}\quad(0\le j<N).
\]
For observations with \(|r_j-y_j|<1/2\), the backward recursion
\[
 \widehat m_N=0,\qquad
 \widehat m_j=\left\lfloor t_j\widehat m_{j+1}-y_j+\frac12\right\rfloor
 \quad(j=N-1,\ldots,0)
\]
recovers every \(m_j\) exactly. The tolerance is independent of the chain
length and the sizes or signs of the coefficients. If the orders are
nonnegative, there are also the equivalences
\begin{equation}
 \exists j<N:\ m_j>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists j<N:\ r_j\le-1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists j<N:\ y_j<-\frac12,
 \label{eq:anchored-chain-sign}
\end{equation}
where the last equivalence uses the observation-error hypotheses. The empty
chain \(N=0\) is included, with all three existential statements false.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Start from the known terminal integer. If the successor is already recovered,
then \(t_j\widehat m_{j+1}-y_j=m_j+(r_j-y_j)\); its distance from \(m_j\)
is strictly below \(1/2\), so the displayed floor is \(m_j\). Backward
induction proves the inverse. In particular, an uncertainty in an observation
is never substituted as a successor order, so there is no error amplification
by \(t_j\) or by the length of the chain.
For nonnegative orders, select the largest index with positive order. Its
successor order is zero, including when the successor is the terminal point,
and its entire signed row is \(-m_j\le-1\). If there is no positive order,
every supported row is zero. This proves the first equivalence. Strict
half-unit errors then give the second: a row at most \(-1\) stays below
\(-1/2\), while every zero row stays above \(-1/2\). Every reference term
is retained until its vanishing follows from the selected index.

\paragraph{Theorem (three quadratic-only Dirichlet observations).}
For every Dirichlet character of positive modulus, let
\[
 z_0=\rho,\qquad z_{j+1}=q_2(z_j)=\frac{z_j+1}{2},\qquad
 m_j=m_\chi(z_j),\qquad
 t_j=\Re\frac{2z_j}{z_j+1}.
\]
Suppose \(0<\Re\rho<1\) and \(G\) is analytic on that open strip. The
actual quadratic real residue limits at every \(z_j\) exist and equal
\[
 r_j=\Re\mathcal R_{\chi,2}(z_j)=-m_j+t_jm_{j+1}.
\]
The first three observations \(j=0,1,2\), each with error below \(1/2\),
therefore recover the three orders \(m_0,m_1,m_2\) by the preceding recursion.
Furthermore,
\begin{align*}
 \exists j\in\{0,1,2\}: L_\chi(z_j)=0
 &\iff \exists j\in\{0,1,2\}: r_j\le-1,\\
 &\iff \exists j\in\{0,1,2\}: y_j<-\frac12
       \quad\text{under the stated errors}.
\end{align*}
This includes principal characters, modulus~\(1\), and real strip points.
It needs no multiplicity cap. The inverse uses the exact coefficients
\(t_j\); the Boolean signed test does not need their numerical values.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Induction gives \(z_j=1+(\rho-1)/2^j\), and all these points remain in the
open strip. In particular,
\[
 z_3=\frac{\rho+7}{8},\qquad \frac78<\Re z_3<1.
\]
The installed unconditional all-character QRH theorem gives
\(L_\chi(z_3)\ne0\), hence \(m_3=0\); its principal-character exception
is avoided because \(z_3\ne1\). The exact affine-residue theorem applies
at every \(z_j\), including real points, with \(d=2\). Thus its target
and reference orders give precisely the rows above. Apply the finite-chain
theorem with \(N=3\). Finally, finite analytic order is positive exactly
at a zero, giving both zero tests. All three observations use the same
quadratic compensated readout at different strip points; no unproved
probability-family or growing-cutoff transfer is implicit.

\paragraph{Exact two-row blindness in the order model.}
Take \(\rho=1/2\). The consecutive points are
\[
 (z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3)=\left(\frac12,\frac34,\frac78,\frac{15}{16}\right).
\]
The positive integer orders \((m_0,m_1,m_2)=(4,6,7)\) give
\[
 -4+\frac{2(1/2)}{1/2+1}\,6=0,\qquad
 -6+\frac{2(3/4)}{3/4+1}\,7=0.
\]
Both complete complex residues, and therefore both scalar observations,
are exactly zero. The strict zero-free input \(\Re s>7/8\) permits this
order model at its three support points: its last point is exactly \(7/8\).
It does not provide a terminal anchor after only two quadratic steps.
After the third step the terminal order is zero and the third row is
\(-7\), exposing the pattern. Consequently three channels are sufficient
for the actual all-character theorem, while two cannot uniformly distinguish
all order codes allowed by nonnegative integrality and that strict
zero-free input. No claim is made that an actual \(L\)-function realizes
\((4,6,7)\), or that further arithmetic information could not rule it out.

The generic inverse and signed tests, together with the exact collision,
are kernel checked in \texttt{AnchoredScalarChain}. The actual endpoint,
row limits, inverse and zero tests are checked in
\texttt{CarmichaelQuadraticScalarChain}. Back substitution is classical
nearest-plane or successive-interference-cancellation decoding; the proposed
application is the quadratic-only all-character residue construction and
its exact order-model obstruction. Literature priority for that application
remains unestablished. Convergence is pointwise; no uniform finite observation
radius or arithmetic error rate has been proved. The global QRH boundary
remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{A vanished real channel forces a high-order reference zero}
\label{sec:scalar-erasure-cost}

The scalar-chain inverse prevents loss by adding observations. A different
question is what an actual loss in a single real channel would cost. Here
that cost can be bounded sharply in terms of zero multiplicity, without
assuming RH or any multiplicity upper bound.

Fix an integer \(d\ge2\), a character \(\chi\) of positive modulus, and
\(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(0<\beta<1\). Write
\[
 A=m_\chi(\rho),\qquad B=m_\chi(q_d(\rho)),\qquad
 r=\Re\mathcal R_{\chi,d}(\rho),\qquad
 x=\beta+d-1,\quad k=d(d-1).
\]
The actual local limit defining \(r\) exists by the all-character residue
theorem for every analytic reconstruction error \(G\) on the open strip.
An erasure means \(r=0\) while at least one of the target or reference
points is a zero. Thus the nontriviality condition is \(A+B>0\); the
ordinary zero value at a pair of nonzeros is excluded.

\paragraph{Theorem (unconditional erasure constraints).}
Every such erasure satisfies
\begin{equation}
 d\le7,\qquad B\ge1,\qquad \beta\le1-\frac d8.
 \label{eq:scalar-erasure-domain}
\end{equation}
If \(d\ge3\), or if \(B\ge2\), then
\begin{equation}
 64\gamma^2+49d^2\le56d^2(d-1)B.
 \label{eq:scalar-erasure-height}
\end{equation}
The remaining case is exactly \(d=2,B=1\); in that case
\begin{equation}
 A=1,\qquad \beta^2+\gamma^2=1.
 \label{eq:scalar-erasure-unit}
\end{equation}
In particular its height is strictly below~\(1\). All characters are
allowed, including principal characters and modulus~\(1\); real points
are included. The statements are necessary constraints on an erasure,
not assertions that any such zero pattern is realized.

\paragraph{Proof: the integer cost and the complete denominator.}
The real coefficient has the exact defect
\begin{equation}
 \delta_d(\rho):=d-\Re\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}
   =\frac{kx}{x^2+\gamma^2},\qquad 0<\delta_d(\rho)<d.
 \label{eq:scalar-erasure-defect}
\end{equation}
The upper inequality uses \(\beta>0\); the lower one already holds for
\(\beta\ge0\). Retaining the entire reference contribution gives
\[
 r=dB-A-\delta_d(\rho)B.
\]
If \(B=0\), erasure gives \(A=0\), contrary to nontriviality. Therefore
\(B\ge1\), and erasure makes
\[
 I:=dB-A=\delta_d(\rho)B>0.
\]
The left side is an integer, so \(I\ge1\). Multiplying by the positive
exact denominator yields
\begin{equation}
 I(x^2+\gamma^2)=kBx,\qquad x^2+\gamma^2\le kBx.
 \label{eq:scalar-erasure-circle}
\end{equation}
This preserves the phase-dependent real coefficient: no term has been
estimated separately or discarded. The identity also describes each
integer erasure circle exactly.

\paragraph{Proof: the actual reference strip and the height maximum.}
The positive order \(B\) makes the affine reference an actual zero. Since
its real part is below~\(1\), it avoids the principal-character pole. The
installed unconditional all-character \(7/8\) theorem then implies
\[
 \Re q_d(\rho)=\frac{\beta+d-1}{d}\le\frac78.
\]
This gives \(\beta\le1-d/8\), and \(\beta>0\) gives \(d<8\), proving
\eqref{eq:scalar-erasure-domain}. Put \(U=7d/8\); thus \(x\le U\).
In the regular cases the required monotonicity inequality is
\[
 kB\ge2U.
\]
For \(d\ge3\), it follows from \(B\ge1\) and
\(d(d-1)\ge2d\ge7d/4\). For \(d=2,B\ge2\), it is \(2B\ge7/2\).
The complete height comparison is
\[
 \bigl(kBU-U^2\bigr)-\bigl(kBx-x^2\bigr)
   =(U-x)(kB-U-x)\ge0.
\]
Combining this with~\eqref{eq:scalar-erasure-circle} gives
\(\gamma^2+U^2\le kBU\). Substituting \(U=7d/8\) and multiplying by
\(64\) proves~\eqref{eq:scalar-erasure-height}.
In the exceptional case, \(B=1\) and
\(A=2-\delta_2(\rho)\) is an integer strictly between~\(0\) and~\(2\).
Hence \(A=1\). The exact circle identity then reduces to
\(\beta^2+\gamma^2=1\). Conversely, the fixed integer code \((A,B)=(1,1)\)
has zero real quadratic residue precisely on that unit circle. This completes
all degree and reference-order cases.

\paragraph{Sharpness of the regular cost in the order model.}
More generally, let \(0\le\beta\le u\), put \(U=u+d-1\), and suppose
\(M\ge1\) and \(kM\ge2U\). The same proof gives
\[
 \gamma^2+U^2\le kMU
\]
for every erased code with reference order \(B=M\). Equality is attained by
the explicit positive-integer code
\[
 A=dM-1,\qquad B=M,\qquad
 \rho=u+i\sqrt{kMU-U^2}.
\]
The radicand is nonnegative because \(kM\ge2U\) and \(U>0\). At this
point \(I=1\), so the exact denominator identity verifies \(r=0\).
For \(2\le d\le7\) in the regular cases, take \(u=1-d/8\). The target
is inside the open strip, its reference has real part exactly~\(7/8\),
and equality holds in~\eqref{eq:scalar-erasure-height}. This proves
optimality using only nonnegative integer orders and that strict zero-free
strip. It asserts no realized Dirichlet zero example.

\subsection{One original real coordinate recovers both zeta orders}
\label{sec:single-real-zeta}

The preceding integer-code erasure examples need not be realized by actual
zeta zeros. A global positive zero budget rules them out for zeta, for every
integer power degree, without an assumption on zero multiplicities or RH.
Write
\[
 S=\gamma_E+2-\log(4\pi)\le\frac1{20},\qquad
 q_d(\rho)=\frac{\rho+d-1}{d},\qquad
 \delta_d(\rho)=d-\Re\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}.
\]

\paragraph{Theorem (one-real-coordinate recovery).}
Let \(d\ge2\) be an integer and \(0<\Re\rho<1\), including real \(\rho\).
Set \(w=q_d(\rho)\), \(A=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta\),
\(B=\operatorname{ord}_{w}\zeta\), and
\(r=\Re\mathcal R_{\zeta,d}(\rho)\). Then
\[
 0<\delta_d(\rho),\qquad 0\le\delta_d(\rho)B\le\frac3{10},\qquad
 r=dB-A-\delta_d(\rho)B.
\]
In particular, \(r=0\) if and only if \(A=B=0\). A zero at either
point therefore survives in the original real coordinate, without rotating
or taking the imaginary part of the residue. For a real observation \(y\)
satisfying
\[
 |r-y|<\frac7{20},\qquad |r-y|<\frac{\delta_d(\rho)}2,
\]
both orders are recovered by
\[
 I=\left\lfloor y+\frac{13}{20}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 B=\left\lfloor\frac{I-y}{\delta_d(\rho)}+\frac12\right\rfloor,
 \qquad A=dB-I.
\]
If \(G\) is analytic throughout the open strip, the actual compensated
readout satisfies
\[
 \lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}
 \Re\!\left((s-\rho)\,
 \operatorname{affinePowerReadout}
 (-\zeta'/\zeta+G,d)(s)\right)=r.
\]
There is no nonreal-point hypothesis in this limit.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For any strip point \(z\), put
\[
 \lambda(z)=\Re\frac1{z(1-z)}
 =\frac{\Re z}{|z|^2}+\frac{1-\Re z}{|1-z|^2}>0.
\]
The existing unconditional Hadamard identity, with zeros counted according
to analytic multiplicity, gives
\(\sum_j\lambda(\rho_j)=S\). Absolute summability follows from the
installed symmetric QRH strip \(1/8\le\Re\rho_j\le7/8\).
The complete fibre at \(w\) has exactly \(B\) indices, including when
\(B=0\). Positivity of every remaining summand therefore gives
\[
 B\Re(1/w)\le B\lambda(w)\le S\le\frac1{20}.
\]
No conjugate, reflected zero, or multiplicity copy is dropped from the
sum used in this comparison. The affine identity gives exactly
\[
 \delta_d(\rho)=(d-1)\Re(1/w)>0.
\]
If \(B>0\), the installed QRH theorem forces \(\Re w\le7/8\).
Since \(d\Re w=\Re\rho+d-1\) and \(\Re\rho>0\), this implies
\(d<8\), hence \(d\le7\). Thus
\(\delta_d(\rho)B\le(d-1)/20\le3/10\). If \(B=0\), the same
bound is immediate, with no upper restriction on \(d\).

Now \(r=I-\delta_d(\rho)B\), where \(I=dB-A\) is an integer.
If \(r=0\), then \(\delta_d(\rho)B\) is an integer in
\([0,3/10]\), so it vanishes; positivity of \(\delta_d(\rho)\)
gives \(B=0\), then \(A=0\). The converse is immediate.
For noisy recovery, \(r\in[I-3/10,I]\) and \(|r-y|<7/20\)
imply \(I<y+13/20<I+1\), proving the first rounding formula.
The fine error condition then gives
\(\left|(I-y)/\delta_d(\rho)-B\right|<1/2\), proving the second.
The last formula is the integer identity \(A=dB-I\).
Finally, the all-character local-factorization limit at the principal
character of modulus one gives the displayed zeta limit, including the
real axis; continuity of the real projection preserves it.

The certified global QRH boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{Rational powers approaching the identity}
\label{sec:rational-single-real-zeta}

The integer-degree restriction is unnecessary for a specified infinite
rational family. Let \(n\ge1\) and \(1\le k\le19\) be integers and put
\[
 d=1+\frac{k}{n},\qquad q_d(\rho)=\frac{\rho+d-1}{d},\qquad
 \delta=d-\Re\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}.
\]
Here \(d\) parametrizes the affine compensator and \(n\) is its denominator
parameter. Coprimality is not required; duplicate degree representations
are allowed. For fixed \(k\), these degrees approach \(1\) from above.

\paragraph{Theorem (rational one-coordinate recovery).}
For every \(0<\Re\rho<1\), including real \(\rho\), write
\[
 A=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta,\qquad
 B=\operatorname{ord}_{q_d(\rho)}\zeta,\qquad
 r=\Re\mathcal R_{\zeta,d}(\rho).
\]
Then \(\delta>0\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-real-budget}
 nr=(n+k)B-nA-u,\qquad
 u=n\delta B=kB\Re(1/q_d(\rho)),\qquad
 0\le u\le\frac{k}{20}<1.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(r=0\) if and only if \(A=B=0\). A target or reference zero
always gives a nonzero original real residue in this family.
For a real observation \(y\), assume the two explicit scaled error bounds
\[
 |n(r-y)|<\frac{1-k/20}{2},\qquad
 |n(r-y)|<\frac{n\delta}{2}.
\]
Then both multiplicities are recovered by
\[
 I=\left\lfloor ny+\frac{1+k/20}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 B=\left\lfloor\frac{I-ny}{n\delta}+\frac12\right\rfloor,
 \qquad A=\frac{(n+k)B-I}{n}.
\]
The last quotient is exact integer division. There is no multiplicity cap
and no RH hypothesis. For every analytic strip correction \(G\), the
actual compensated logarithmic readout has the real residue limit
\[
 \lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}
 \Re\!\left((s-\rho)\,
 \operatorname{affinePowerReadout}(-\zeta'/\zeta+G,d)(s)\right)=r.
\]
The scaled tolerance stays explicit in the denominator parameter; the
required error in the original observation decreases with \(n\).

\paragraph{Proof.}
The exact real-degree geometry gives
\[
 \delta=\frac{d(d-1)(\Re\rho+d-1)}
 {(\Re\rho+d-1)^2+(\Im\rho)^2}>0,
 \qquad \delta=(d-1)\Re(1/q_d(\rho)).
\]
Since \(n(d-1)=k\), the already proved multiplicity-weighted zeta budget
at the strip point \(q_d(\rho)\) gives
\[
 n\delta B=kB\Re(1/q_d(\rho))\le\frac{k}{20}.
\]
The full signed residue is \(r=dB-A-\delta B\). Multiplying by \(n\)
and using \(nd=n+k\) proves~\eqref{eq:rational-real-budget}.
If \(r=0\), the integer \(I=(n+k)B-nA\) equals \(u\in[0,1)\),
so \(u=I=0\). Positivity of \(n\delta\) gives \(B=0\), and then
\(A=0\). The converse follows directly from the residue formula.

For recovery, apply the preceding one-sided integer decoder with budget
\(c=k/20\), coefficient \(n+k\), defect \(n\delta\), target integer
\(nA\), reference integer \(B\), and observation \(ny\).
It returns exactly \((nA,B)\). Division by the nonzero integer \(n\)
then returns \(A\). Every term of the scaled residue is retained in this
application. Finally, specialize the existing all-character real-degree
local-factorization limit to the principal character of modulus one and
take its continuous real projection. This proves the actual limit,
including real target points.

\subsection{No finite cancellation in one original real coordinate}
\label{sec:finite-real-packet}

The preceding pointwise decoder has a finite-set strengthening that does
not multiply its defect budget by the number of observations. Fix integers
\(n\ge1\), \(1\le k\le19\), and \(d=1+k/n\). Let \(S\) be any finite
set of distinct complex points with \(0<\Re\rho<1\). Put
\[
 A_S=\sum_{\rho\in S}\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta,\qquad
 B_S=\sum_{\rho\in S}\operatorname{ord}_{q_d(\rho)}\zeta,\qquad
 R_S=\sum_{\rho\in S}\Re\mathcal R_{\zeta,d}(\rho),
\]
where \(q_d(\rho)=(\rho+d-1)/d\), and retain the exact signed quantities
\[
 I_S=(n+k)B_S-nA_S\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
 U_S=n\sum_{\rho\in S}\delta_d(\rho)
            \operatorname{ord}_{q_d(\rho)}\zeta.
\]
All sums are over the displayed finite sets. Target and reference locations
may overlap, and real points and the empty set are allowed.

\paragraph{Theorem (finite-packet separation and recovery).}
For every such \(S,n,k\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-real-packet-budget}
 nR_S=I_S-U_S,\qquad 0\le U_S\le k/20<1,\qquad
 R_S=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ A_S=B_S=0.
\end{equation}
If \(H\ge0\) and \(|\Im\rho|\le H\) for every \(\rho\in S\), define
\[
 L_d(H)=\frac{d(d-1)^2}{d^2+H^2},\qquad
 \ell(n,k,H)=\frac{\min\{1-k/20,nL_d(H)\}}{n}>0.
\]
Then
\[
 A_S+B_S>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |R_S|\ge\ell(n,k,H).
\]
Consequently any real observation \(y\) with
\(|R_S-y|<\ell(n,k,H)/2\) satisfies the exact threshold test
\[
 A_S+B_S>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |y|\ge\ell(n,k,H)/2.
\]
The coarser accuracy condition
\[
 |n(R_S-y)|<(1-k/20)/2
\]
recovers the signed aggregate index:
\[
 I_S=\left\lfloor ny+\frac{1+k/20}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
In particular, for \(n=k=1\), so that \(d=2\), constant accuracy
\(|R_S-y|<19/40\) recovers the parity of the total target multiplicity:
\[
 A_S\equiv-\left\lfloor y+\frac{21}{40}\right\rfloor\pmod2.
\]
Neither the budget nor these tolerances contains \(|S|\).

\paragraph{Proof of the global finite-set budget.}
Let \(\mathcal Z\) denote the set of distinct completed-zeta zeros, and
let \(m(w)\) be their full analytic multiplicity. Exact regrouping of the
already established convergent canonical zero sum gives
\[
 \sum_{w\in\mathcal Z}m(w)\Re\frac1{w(1-w)}
   =\gamma_E+2-\log(4\pi)\le\frac1{20}.
\]
The existing unconditional \(7/8\) theorem supplies the fixed strip
needed for this convergence argument; no RH premise enters it.
For \(0<\Re w<1\), the identity
\[
 \frac1{w(1-w)}=\frac1w+\frac1{1-w}
\]
shows that its real part is nonnegative and at least \(\Re(1/w)\).
Regular strip points have order zero. Thus every finite strip set \(T\)
satisfies
\[
 \sum_{w\in T}\operatorname{ord}_w\zeta\,\Re(1/w)\le\frac1{20}.
\]
The affine map \(q_d\) is injective and sends \(S\) into the strip.
Apply this estimate once to \(T=q_d(S)\), retaining each full
multiplicity fiber exactly once. Since \(n\delta_d(\rho)=k\Re(1/q_d(\rho))\),
this proves \(U_S\le k/20\); positivity of the defects proves \(U_S\ge0\).
Summing the exact pointwise residue identity proves
\(nR_S=I_S-U_S\), with its signs unchanged.

If \(R_S=0\), the integer \(I_S\) belongs to \([0,k/20]\subset[0,1)\).
Hence \(I_S=U_S=0\). Every defect is strictly positive and every order
is nonnegative, so \(U_S=0\) forces \(B_S=0\); the integer relation then
forces \(A_S=0\). The converse is immediate.

\paragraph{Proof of the quantitative conclusions.}
Write \(\rho=\sigma+i\tau\). The exact defect formula gives
\[
 \delta_d(\rho)=
 \frac{d(d-1)(\sigma+d-1)}
      {(\sigma+d-1)^2+\tau^2}\ge L_d(H),
\]
since \(0<\sigma<1\) and \(|\tau|\le H\).
If \(I_S\ne0\), the signed integer separation and \(0\le U_S\le k/20\)
give \(|I_S-U_S|\ge1-k/20\). If \(I_S=0\) and \(A_S+B_S>0\),
then \(B_S\ge1\), and therefore
\(U_S\ge nL_d(H)B_S\ge nL_d(H)\).
These two cases give the claimed lower bound after division by \(n>0\).
The observation threshold follows by applying the triangle inequality
only to the observation error after this signed lower bound has been proved.
Finally the coarse error condition places
\(ny+(1+k/20)/2\) in \([I_S,I_S+1)\), giving its floor.
At \(n=k=1\), \(I_S=2B_S-A_S\), which proves the parity formula.

\paragraph{Actual finite readout and formal scope.}
For every analytic strip correction \(G\), the same aggregate is the
actual common-increment readout limit
\[
 \lim_{\substack{t\to0\\t\ne0}}
 \sum_{\rho\in S}\Re\!\left(
 t\,\operatorname{affinePowerReadout}(-\zeta'/\zeta+G,d)(\rho+t)
 \right)=R_S.
\]
For each fixed \(\rho\), translation \(t\mapsto\rho+t\) sends the punctured
neighborhood of zero into the punctured neighborhood of \(\rho\).
Compose the proved pointwise residue limit with this translation and
take the finite sum. This argument includes real points and coincident
target/reference locations.

The finite zero-mass bound is checked in
\texttt{Helpers.NicolasFiniteZeroMass}; the height and signed integer
lemmas are checked in \texttt{Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.RealResiduePacket};
the full actual packet theorem, threshold, index, parity, and analytic
limit are checked in \texttt{Assembly.CarmichaelFiniteRealPacket}.
The old pointwise summation would only give \(U_S\le |S|k/20\);
the global finite-set argument gives \(U_S\le k/20\) for every finite \(S\).
A single aggregate is not claimed to recover \(A_S\) and \(B_S\)
separately, because the individual defects vary. The local limit keeps
\(S\) fixed and supplies no growing-packet sampling rate. Classical
trace, positivity, and integer rounding are inputs; the finite original-real
affine-residue application is distinct from the global Li/Weil functionals
discussed above. Literature priority remains unestablished.
This is a finite-packet observability result; the certified global
zero-free boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{Effective finite panels arbitrarily close to the identity}
\label{sec:near-identity-panel}

A finite panel removes the shrinking single-row accuracy requirement when
the aim is to recover the total target multiplicity. Let \(S\) be a finite
strip set, and suppose
\[
 0<s,\qquad H\ge0,\qquad
 s\le\Re\rho<1,\quad |\Im\rho|\le H\quad(\rho\in S).
\]
For \(j\ge0\), define
\[
 d_j=1+\frac1{j+1},\qquad
 q_j(\rho)=\frac{(j+1)\rho+1}{j+2},\qquad
 A=\sum_{\rho\in S}\operatorname{ord}_\rho\zeta,
\]
\[
 B_j=\sum_{\rho\in S}\operatorname{ord}_{q_j(\rho)}\zeta,\qquad
 r_j=\sum_{\rho\in S}\Re\mathcal R_{\zeta,d_j}(\rho),\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{s}{1+H^2}>0.
\]
The natural index \(j=0\) is allowed; its denominator parameter is one.
A parameter is called contaminated when \(B_j>0\).

\paragraph{Theorem (effective near-identity panel recovery).}
For every finite \(T\subset\mathbb N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-identity-bad-budget}
 \lambda\,\#\{j\in T:B_j>0\}\le\frac{|S|}{20}.
\end{equation}
The set of all contaminated natural parameters is finite.
Every row satisfies \(r_j\ge-A\), and \(B_j=0\) implies \(r_j=-A\).
Consequently, if \(T\ne\varnothing\) and
\[
 \lambda |T|>\frac{|S|}{20},
\]
then any real observations satisfying \(|r_j-y_j|<1/2\) for every \(j\in T\)
recover the full target total by the explicit formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-identity-panel-decode}
 A=\max_{j\in T}\left\lfloor-y_j+\frac12\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
No bound on \(A\) or on an individual zero multiplicity is an input.

In particular put
\[
 M=\left\lfloor\frac{|S|(1+H^2)}{20s}\right\rfloor.
\]
For every base \(b\ge0\), the block \(T=\{b,b+1,\ldots,b+M\}\) has
exactly \(M+1\) observations and satisfies the required strict size inequality.
The same decoder works on every such block, while all its powers obey
\[
 1<d_j\le1+\frac1{b+1}\qquad(j\in T).
\]
Thus the panel can lie arbitrarily close to the identity without shrinking
the half-unit accuracy allowance. The number of rows depends on the
rectangle and \(|S|\), but is independent of the base \(b\).

\paragraph{Proof of the parameter budget.}
Write \(t_j=(j+1)/(j+2)\). Then \(0<t_j<1\) and
\[
 q_j(\rho)=t_j\rho+(1-t_j).
\]
For fixed \(\rho\ne1\), the map \(j\mapsto q_j(\rho)\) is injective:
equality of two values gives
\((t_j-t_k)(\rho-1)=0\), hence \(t_j=t_k\), and then \(j=k\).
Furthermore
\[
 \Re q_j(\rho)\ge\Re\rho\ge s,\qquad
 \Re q_j(\rho)<1,\qquad |\Im q_j(\rho)|\le H.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \Re\frac1{q_j(\rho)}
 =\frac{\Re q_j(\rho)}{|q_j(\rho)|^2}
 \ge \frac{s}{1+H^2}=\lambda.
\]

For fixed \(\rho\), apply the proved global finite-set inverse-zero budget
to the injective image \(q_T(\rho)=\{q_j(\rho):j\in T\}\):
\[
 \sum_{j\in T}\operatorname{ord}_{q_j(\rho)}\zeta\,
                  \Re(1/q_j(\rho))\le\frac1{20}.
\]
Define the complete nonnegative weight
\[
 W_j=\sum_{\rho\in S}\operatorname{ord}_{q_j(\rho)}\zeta\,
                         \Re(1/q_j(\rho)).
\]
Finite summation in the parameter direction first gives
\[
 \sum_{j\in T}W_j\le\frac{|S|}{20},\qquad W_j\ge\lambda B_j.
\]
If \(B_j>0\), integrality gives \(B_j\ge1\), so \(W_j\ge\lambda\).
Summing over contaminated rows proves~\eqref{eq:near-identity-bad-budget}.
Images from different target points may overlap; the proof charges their
separate orbits through the displayed factor \(|S|\) and never assumes
disjointness across different \(\rho\).
If the contaminated natural-parameter set were infinite, it would contain
a finite subset of size \(M+1\); the same budget would contradict
\(\lambda(M+1)>|S|/20\). This also proves its global finiteness.

\paragraph{Proof of the signed envelope and recovery.}
Keep the exact original residue:
\[
 r_j=-A+
 \sum_{\rho\in S}
  \Re\!\left(\frac{d_j\rho}{\rho+d_j-1}\right)
                  \operatorname{ord}_{q_j(\rho)}\zeta.
\]
Every retained reference coefficient is nonnegative. Indeed, for
\(\rho=\sigma+i\tau\),
\[
 \Re\!\left(\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}\right)
 =\frac{d\sigma(\sigma+d-1)+d\tau^2}
        {(\sigma+d-1)^2+\tau^2}\ge0
 \qquad(d>1,\ \sigma\ge0).
\]
This proves \(r_j\ge-A\). If \(B_j=0\), all its nonnegative integer
summands vanish, so the full reference contribution vanishes and
\(r_j=-A\).

The strict size inequality and~\eqref{eq:near-identity-bad-budget}
force a clean \(j\in T\). For every row the noise bound and \(r_j\ge-A\)
give \(y_j>-A-1/2\), whence
\(\lfloor-y_j+1/2\rfloor\le A\).
For a clean row, \(|-A-y_j|<1/2\) gives equality.
Taking the finite maximum proves~\eqref{eq:near-identity-panel-decode}.
Finally, \(M+1>|S|/(20\lambda)\) proves the explicit block-size condition,
and monotonicity of \(1/(j+1)\) proves the power bound.

\paragraph{Actual analytic observations and formal scope.}
For an arbitrary analytic strip correction \(G\), use the actual observations
\[
 y_j(t)=\sum_{\rho\in S}
 \Re\!\left(t\,\operatorname{affinePowerReadout}
       (-\zeta'/\zeta+G,d_j)(\rho+t)\right).
\]
The completed finite-packet limit gives \(y_j(t)\to r_j\) as
\(t\to0,\ t\ne0\). For fixed finite \(T\), all the strict half-unit
error inequalities therefore hold simultaneously in a punctured neighborhood
of zero. The floor-maximum decoder is consequently exactly equal to \(A\)
throughout that neighborhood. The set, rectangle, base, and panel are fixed
before taking this limit; no uniform step-size or sampling-rate bound is claimed.

The affine geometry and full signed coefficient inequality are checked in
\texttt{Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.NearIdentityResiduePanel}; the actual zero
budget, contamination count, clean-row existence, decoder, block construction,
power bound, and eventual analytic recovery are checked in
\texttt{Assembly.CarmichaelNearIdentityPanel}.
The preceding fixed-degree real aggregate recovered an index and quadratic
parity. This panel recovers the target total by using several real observations;
it does not recover all the varying reference orders.

The background includes global Li coefficients and Weil functionals
(Lagarias, arXiv:math/0404394), and global inverse-power zero sums
(Sekatskii, \textit{On the Sums over Inverse Powers of Zeroes of the Hurwitz
Zeta-Function}, 2024,
\url{https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202401.0068/v1/download}).
Here the global budget instead controls collisions across compensation
parameters and gives a finite stable inverse formula. Targeted searches

\subsection{Phase multiplicity profiles and the QRH zero-mass budget}
\label{sec:phase-multiplicity-mass}

Write \(m(z)=\operatorname{ord}_z\zeta\), and retain the full residue
\[
 R_d(\rho)=-m(\rho)+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}m(q_d\rho),
 \qquad q_d\rho=(\rho+d-1)/d.
\]
For \(d>1\) and \(\Im\rho\ne0\), define the normalized phase
\[
 \Phi_d(\rho)=
 \frac{|\rho+d-1|^2}{d(d-1)\Im\rho}\Im R_d(\rho).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-reference-order}
 \Phi_d(\rho)=m(q_d\rho).
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
 \Im\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}
   =\frac{d(d-1)\Im\rho}{|\rho+d-1|^2};
\]
the target term is real and disappears from this component.
No reference order is discarded or assumed zero.

Set \(\rho_{d,t}=\tfrac12+i\,dt\), \(t\ne0\), and
\(P(d,t)=\Phi_d(\rho_{d,t})\). The exact identity becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-phase-profile}
 P(d,t)=m\left(1-\frac1{2d}+it\right)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\end{equation}
Every nonreal \(z\) with \(\tfrac12<\Re z<1\) occurs uniquely, with
\(d=1/(2(1-\Re z))\) and \(t=\Im z\).
For each \(b\ge\tfrac12\) and integer \(M\ge0\), the following are
therefore equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(m(z)\le M\) at every nonreal \(z\) with \(b<\Re z<1\);
 \item \(P(d,t)\le M\) whenever \(d>1\), \(t\ne0\), and
       \(b<1-1/(2d)\).
\end{enumerate}
This is an equivalence of the complete multiplicity profile, not only
a Boolean zero detector. In particular, the maintained theorem
\texttt{critical\_phase\_three\_quarters\_subunit\_iff} proves
\[
 [\,P(d,t)<1\text{ for every }d>2,\ t\ne0\,]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 [\,\zeta(z)\ne0\text{ for every nonreal }
       z\text{ with }3/4<\Re z<1\,].
\]
The integer gap makes the strict bound below one sufficient.
This statement explicitly concerns nonreal zeros; it does not provide
a phase test for real zeros of general Dirichlet \(L\)-functions.

These are actual analytic observations. For any correction \(G\)
analytic on the strip, the scaled compensated readout converges to
\(R_d(\rho)\); its normalized imaginary part converges to
\(m(q_d\rho)\). The maintained declaration is
\texttt{critical\_phase\_readout\_limit}. This is a limit theorem at
each fixed center, not a uniform finite-sample error estimate.

\paragraph{Independent zero mass supplied by the existing QRH theorem.}
The already proved complete positive trace is
\[
 \sum_{\rho\ {\rm distinct}}m(\rho)K(\rho)=C_\zeta,\qquad
 K(\rho)=\Re\frac1{\rho(1-\rho)},\qquad
 C_\zeta=\gamma_E+2-\log(4\pi)\le\frac1{20}.
\]
Its maintained proof already consumes the unconditional \(7/8\)

 K(\rho)=\frac{u+c}{u^2+(1-2c)u+c^2},\qquad

   =u+1-3c+\frac{c(4c-1)}{u+c}.
\]
Here \(7/64\le c\le1/4\), so
\(1\le(u+43/64)K(\rho)\).

 \sum_{\rho\in S}m(\rho)

\end{equation}
No cardinality factor is introduced. The maintained count theorem

\subsection{The seven-eighths strip bounds localized multiplicity mass}
\label{sec:localized-paired-multiplicity}

The preceding positive global budget can be localized more efficiently.
Let \(F=\xi'/\xi\), and let
\[
 A_r(T)=\frac12\left\{\Re F\left(\tfrac12+r+iT\right)
                   +\Re F\left(\tfrac12+r-iT\right)\right\}.
\]
The installed unconditional \(7/8\) theorem and the functional equation
place every xi zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) in
\(1/8\le\beta\le7/8\), with its full analytic multiplicity \(m(\rho)\).
The maintained theorem
\texttt{zeta\_multiplicity\_seven\_eighths\_log\_bound} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localized-multiplicity-log-bound}
 \exists C>0\quad
 m(\beta+i\gamma)\le\frac{3\sqrt2}{32}\log|\gamma|+C
 \qquad(0<\beta<1,\ |\gamma|\ge2).
\end{equation}
The constant is independent of the zero. The formal theorem also allows
nonzeros, where the analytic order is zero. The leading coefficient is
approximately \(0.13258\); the additive constant is existential, and no
numerical starting height is supplied.

\paragraph{A jointly positive compensated kernel.}
For real \(x,y\) and \(|x|<r<R\), define
\[
 Q_r(x,y)=\frac12\left\{
 \frac{r-x}{(r-x)^2+y^2}+\frac{r+x}{(r+x)^2+y^2}\right\},
 \qquad K_{r,R}=Q_r-\frac rR Q_R,
\]
and
\[
 D_r=((r-x)^2+y^2)((r+x)^2+y^2).
\]
The complete numerator identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-resolvent-numerator}
 K_{r,R}(x,y)=\frac{r(R^2-r^2)}{D_rD_R}
 \left\{(r^2+y^2-x^2)(R^2+y^2-x^2)-4x^2y^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
All four resolvent terms remain in this identity. Set \(k=r^2-2x^2\).
If \(k\ge0\), its expression in braces equals
\[
 ((y-x)^2+k)((y+x)^2+k)
 +(R^2-r^2)(r^2+y^2-x^2)\ge0.
\]
Thus \(K_{r,R}(x,y)\ge0\) for every \(y\) whenever
\(2x^2\le r^2\).
The infinitesimal threshold is sharp: the first product is nonnegative
for every \(y\) if and only if \(2x^2\le r^2\).
For necessity, setting \(y=x\) gives \(r^4-4x^4\).

At the zero's own ordinate,
\[
 K_{r,R}(x,0)=
 \frac{r(R^2-r^2)}{(r^2-x^2)(R^2-x^2)}
 \ge K_{r,R}(0,0)=\frac1r-\frac r{R^2}>0.
\]
Indeed both denominator factors are positive and their product is at
most \(r^2R^2\). These identities, positivity statements, sharpness,
and the central minimum are proved in
\texttt{Mathlib/Analysis/Complex/PairedResolventKernel.lean}.

\paragraph{The actual complete zero trace.}
The genus-one Hadamard formula has the same polynomial constant at
every evaluation point:
\[
 F(s)=B+\sum_\rho m(\rho)\left\{\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\right\}.
\]
For each fixed \(r>1/2,T\), subtract the formulas at
\(s_\pm=1/2\pm r+iT\). The constant \(B\) and every reciprocal
correction cancel exactly. Both points are separated from the zero
strip. The inverse-square xi-zero summability theorem therefore
justifies the subtraction and taking real parts. More explicitly,
if \(|s-\rho|\ge\delta>0\), then
\[
 \left|\frac1{s-\rho}+\frac1\rho\right|
 =\frac{|s|}{|\rho|\,|s-\rho|}
 \le \frac{|s|(\delta+|s|)}{\delta|\rho|^2},
\]
using \(|\rho|\le|s-\rho|+|s|\).

The functional equation gives \(F(1-s)=-F(s)\), hence
\[
 A_r(T)=\sum_\rho m(\rho)
 Q_r\left(\beta-\tfrac12,T-\gamma\right).
\]
In particular, with no reindexing or loss of reciprocal terms,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-compensated-xi-trace}
 A_r(T)-\frac rR A_R(T)=
 \sum_\rho m(\rho)
 K_{r,R}\left(\beta-\tfrac12,T-\gamma\right).
\end{equation}
Every sum here is absolutely convergent after the displayed real
pairing, at each fixed \(T\). No simultaneous growing-height
interchange is used.

For \(r>1/2\) and \(r^2\ge9/32\), the \(7/8\) strip ensures
\(2(\beta-1/2)^2\le r^2\). Every term of
\eqref{eq:complete-compensated-xi-trace} is nonnegative.
Charging the entire canonical index fibre of a chosen zero, rather
than one representative, gives the compiled inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qrh-compensated-multiplicity}
 m(\rho)\left(\frac1r-\frac r{R^2}\right)
 \le A_r(\gamma)-\frac rR A_R(\gamma).
\end{equation}
The canonical fibre has cardinality \(m(\rho)\), by the existing
xi--zeta multiplicity equivalence. This is the actual \(7/8\)
consumer, proved as
\texttt{zeta\_multiplicity\_le\_compensated\_xi\_trace}.

\paragraph{Independent bounds with the logarithmic coefficient retained.}
For \(\Re s>1\), the exact shifted-gamma identity is
\[
 F(s)=\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)+\frac1{s-1}
       -\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\psi(s/2+1).
\]
The shift by one absorbs the separate \(1/s\) term.
The existing Euler-product theorem bounds
\(|\zeta'/\zeta(\sigma+it)|\le1/(\sigma-1)+C_E\)
uniformly in \(t\), for every \(\sigma>1\).
On any fixed closed half-plane \(\Re s\ge a>1\), Cauchy's estimate
on balls of radius \((a-1)/2\), followed by the convex-domain
mean-value inequality, also gives a uniform Lipschitz constant for
\(\zeta'/\zeta\). The pole term has the same property.

For completeness, the sharp gamma estimate is obtained directly
from its convergent series, for \(1\le\Re z\le b\) and
\(|\Im z|\ge1\). Put \(N=\lceil|\Im z|\rceil+1\). Then
\[
 \psi(z)+\gamma_E
 =H_N-\sum_{n<N}\frac1{z+n}
   +\sum_{n\ge N}\left(\frac1{n+1}-\frac1{z+n}\right).
\]
The inverse-resolvent head has norm at most
\(N/|\Im z|\le3\). The full tail has norm at most \(b+2\):
\[
 \left|\frac1{n+1}-\frac1{z+n}\right|
 \le\frac{|z|+1}{(n+1)^2},\qquad
 \sum_{n\ge N}\frac1{(n+1)^2}\le\frac1N,\qquad
 |z|+1\le(b+2)N.
\]
Also \(H_N\le\log|\Im z|+1+\log3\). Consequently
\[
 \Re\psi(z)\le\log|\Im z|
       +b+6+\log3+|\gamma_E|.
\]
For \(\Re z,\Re w\ge1\), subtracting the two complete digamma series
and retaining their common harmonic cancellation gives
\[
 |\psi(z)-\psi(w)|
 \le |z-w|\sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{(n+1)^2}.
\]
These two estimates are compiled in
\texttt{Helpers/RealDigammaHeight.lean}. Their Euler and pole
consumers in \texttt{Helpers/XiVerticalLogDerivative.lean} prove:
for each fixed \(r>1/2\), there exist \(C_0,L\ge0\) such that
\[
 A_r(T)\le\tfrac12\log|T|+C_0\quad(|T|\ge2),\qquad
 A_r(T)-A_R(T)\le L(R-r)\quad(R>r),
\]
uniformly in height. The estimates are independent of an assumed
zero. Absolute estimates are used for these bounded arithmetic and
gamma remainders, after the signed zero trace has been retained.

\paragraph{Cancelling the common scale and taking the endpoint.}
Preserve the subtraction as
\[
 A_r-\frac rR A_R
 =\frac{R-r}{R}A_r+\frac rR(A_r-A_R).
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:qrh-compensated-multiplicity} yields
\[
 m(\rho)\left(\frac1r-\frac r{R^2}\right)
 \le\frac{R-r}{R}\left(\tfrac12\log|\gamma|+C_0\right)
     +\frac rR L(R-r).
\]
For \(R>r\), multiplication by \(rR^2/(R-r)>0\) gives
\[
 m(\rho)(R+r)
 \le rR\left(\tfrac12\log|\gamma|+C_0+rL\right).
\]
Both sides are continuous polynomials in \(R\). The right-hand
limit \(R\downarrow r\) therefore proves
\[
 m(\rho)\le\frac r4\log|\gamma|
                 +\frac r2 C_0+\frac{r^2}2L.
\]
Choose \(r=3\sqrt2/8>1/2\), so \(r^2=9/32\), and enlarge the
constant to obtain \eqref{eq:localized-multiplicity-log-bound}.
The maintained general-radius theorem and its radical specialization
are in \texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelLocalizedMultiplicity.lean}.

This bounds the multiplicity mass of each zero through an independently
controlled signed observable. It supplies neither simplicity nor a
smaller zero-free boundary. The classical unconditional order
\(m(\rho)=O(\log|\gamma|)\) is not claimed as new; see A.~Ivi\'c,
\emph{On the multiplicity of zeros of the zeta-function},
arXiv:math/0501434. The contribution here is the proved compensated
strip-to-coefficient argument and its consumption of the installed
\(7/8\) strip. Novelty priority for the coefficient is unestablished.

\subsection{Exact finite-radius optimization of the multiplicity coefficient}
\label{sec:sharp-finite-resolvent}

The preceding sufficient condition loses information when the two radii
are unequal. Keeping their full dependence gives an exact classification.
For \(0\le w<r<R\), put
\[
 a=\sqrt{r^2-w^2},\qquad b=\sqrt{R^2-w^2}.
\]
Then the maintained theorem \texttt{pairedResolventStrip\_nonneg\_iff} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-finite-strip}
 \bigl[K_{r,R}(x,y)\ge0\quad\text{for all }|x|\le w,
                       \ y\in\mathbb R\bigr]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a+b\ge2w.
\end{equation}
This includes every imaginary displacement; no zero phase is discarded.

\paragraph{Proof of the exact strip condition.}
Let \(H(x,y)\) denote the square-bracket numerator in
\eqref{eq:paired-resolvent-numerator}. Its boundary value is
\[
 H(w,y)=(y^2-ab)^2+\bigl((a+b)^2-4w^2\bigr)y^2.
\]
Moreover, for every \(|x|\le w\),
\[
 H(x,y)-H(w,y)
 =(w^2-x^2)(r^2+R^2+6y^2-x^2-w^2)\ge0.
\]
The denominator and the prefactor in
\eqref{eq:paired-resolvent-numerator} are positive. Thus \(a+b\ge2w\)
implies positivity on the whole strip. Conversely, \(a,b>0\), and the
explicit choice \(x=w\), \(y=\sqrt{ab}\) gives
\[
 H(w,\sqrt{ab})=ab\bigl((a+b)^2-4w^2\bigr).
\]
It is negative if \(a+b<2w\). This proves both directions, including
an explicit counterexample when the condition fails. The algebraic
identities, monotonicity across the strip, equivalence and negative
witness are formalized in \texttt{PairedResolventSharpStrip.lean}.

\paragraph{The complete constrained optimizer for width \(3/8\).}
Restrict to the compensated two-line class with weight \(r/R\), and to
\(1/2\le r<R\). Define
\[
 \ell=\frac{\sqrt7}{8},\qquad
 R_* = \frac{\sqrt{13-3\sqrt7}}4,\qquad
 c(r,R)=\frac{rR}{2(r+R)},\qquad
 c_* = \frac{\sqrt{13-3\sqrt7}}
              {4(2+\sqrt{13-3\sqrt7})}.
\]
Among every pair satisfying \eqref{eq:sharp-finite-strip} for
\(w=3/8\), the maintained optimizer proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-finite-coefficient}
 c(r,R)\ge c_*,\qquad
 c(r,R)=c_*\ \Longleftrightarrow\ r=1/2,\ R=R_*.
\end{equation}
The ordering of the radii removes the swapped equality case.

Here is the optimization proof. Write
\(u=\sqrt{r^2-w^2}\), \(v=\sqrt{R^2-w^2}\). Then
\[
 \ell\le u<v,\qquad u+v\ge2w=\frac34,
 \qquad g(t)=\frac1{\sqrt{w^2+t^2}}.
\]
On \([\ell,\infty)\), \(g\) is strictly decreasing and strictly convex:
\[
 g'(t)=-t(w^2+t^2)^{-3/2},\qquad
 g''(t)=(2t^2-w^2)(w^2+t^2)^{-5/2}>0,
 \qquad 2\ell^2-w^2=\frac5{64}>0.
\]
For a strictly decreasing strictly convex function on this interval,
\[
 g(u)+g(v)\le g(\ell)+g(2w-\ell),
\]
with equality precisely at the two endpoints in the displayed order.
Indeed, if \(v\ge2w-\ell\), monotonicity gives the result, strictly
unless both coordinates are endpoints. Otherwise both coordinates
lie between the endpoints. Apply their strict chord inequalities and
add: the constraint \(u+v\ge2w\), together with
\(g(\ell)>g(2w-\ell)\), bounds the resulting chord sum by the endpoint
sum. This also gives strictness in the interior. These statements,
including the equality case, are formalized for general strictly
convex decreasing functions in \texttt{ReciprocalRadiusConvexity.lean}.
Finally,
\[
 g(u)+g(v)=\frac1r+\frac1R
 \le 2+\frac1{R_*},\qquad
 c(r,R)=\frac1{2(1/r+1/R)}.
\]
The positive-denominator comparison proves
\eqref{eq:sharp-finite-coefficient}. The exact endpoint identities and
unique optimizer are formalized in \texttt{PairedResolventOptimizer.lean}.
Optimality here concerns this two-line compensation class; it is not
an optimality claim among all zero observables or multiplicity bounds.

\paragraph{Euler-admissible approximation and the actual zero bound.}
The endpoint \(r=1/2\) itself is outside the independent Euler estimate.
For each \(\varepsilon>0\), set
\[
 d=\min\left\{\frac{R_*-1/2}{2},\varepsilon\right\},\qquad
 r=\frac12+d,\qquad R=R_*.
\]
Then \(1/2<r<R\). Increasing the inner radius preserves
\eqref{eq:sharp-finite-strip}, and the exact rational comparison gives
\[
 c(r,R_*)\le c(1/2,R_*)+\frac{r-1/2}{2}<c_*+\varepsilon.
\]
This entire approximation, with its quantified positive epsilon,
is formalized; it is not a finite numerical test.

For these fixed radii the canonical xi zero enumeration and
\eqref{eq:sharp-finite-strip} give the finite mass inequality
\[
 \sum_{j\in S}K_{r,R}(\beta_j-1/2,T-\gamma_j)
 \le A_r(T)-\frac rR A_R(T)
\]
for every finite subset \(S\) of the enumeration, which includes all
multiplicity copies. The same-height minimum charges the full fiber
of a zero \(\rho\) by \(m(\rho)(1/r-r/R^2)\). Retaining the exact signed
compensation and the independent bounds on \(A_r\) and \(A_r-A_R\)
therefore proves, for \(|\gamma|\ge2\),
\[
 m(\rho)(r+R)
 \le rR\left(\frac12\log|\gamma|+C_0+rL\right).
\]
Consequently the maintained theorem
\texttt{zeta\_multiplicity\_seven\_eighths\_sharp\_log\_bound} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-global-multiplicity}
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists C_\varepsilon>0\ \forall\rho:
 \quad 0<\Re\rho<1,\ |\Im\rho|\ge2
 \ \Longrightarrow\quad
 m(\rho)\le(c_*+\varepsilon)\log|\Im\rho|+C_\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The radii are chosen after epsilon and before the zero height. The
constant may diverge as epsilon decreases: no assertion of
\(c_*\log|\gamma|+O(1)\) is made. The closed-form coefficient is about
\(0.132354\), compared with \(3\sqrt2/32\approx0.132583\) above.
The weighted mass and fixed-radius analytic bound are formalized in
\texttt{Helpers/XiSharpMultiplicity.lean}; the actual installed \(7/8\)
specialization is in \texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelSharpMultiplicity.lean}.
The unconditional zero-free boundary remains \(7/8\); this theorem
bounds multiplicity mass, and does not prove simplicity or RH.
Novelty priority for the coefficient remains unestablished.

\subsection{A three-line kernel strictly improves the multiplicity coefficient}
\label{sec:three-line-multiplicity}

The sharp constant \(c_*\) above belongs to the compensated two-line
class \(K_{r,R}\). It is not a lower bound for general signed finite
combinations of reflected resolvents. We now give an explicit strict
improvement using three lines.

\paragraph{Theorem.}
Put
\begin{align*}
 (a_1,a_2,a_3)&=\left(\frac{5001}{10000},\frac7{10},\frac65\right),\\
 (c_1,c_2,c_3)&=\left(\frac{1209493}{1000000},
             -\frac{1059063}{1000000},\frac{56907}{500000}\right),\\
 g&=\sum_{j=1}^3\frac{c_j}{a_j}
       =\frac{7004201657}{7001400000},\qquad S=\sum_{j=1}^3c_j,\\
 c_3^{\rm new}&=\frac{S}{2g}
       =\frac{4625194854}{35021008285}
       =0.1320691516\ldots.
\end{align*}
There is a fixed explicitly defined \(C_3\ge0\) such that every
\(w\) with \(0<\Re w<1\), \(T=|\Im w|\ge2\), satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-line-global-multiplicity}
 \operatorname{ord}_w\zeta
       \le c_3^{\rm new}\log T+C_3
       \le\frac{1321}{10000}\log T+C_3.
\end{equation}
The exact strict comparison is
\[
 c_3^{\rm new}<\frac{1321}{10000}<c_*.
\]
This uses the installed unconditional \(7/8\) strip, rather than RH.

\paragraph{The full signed kernel.}
For real \(u,v\), write
\[
 \mathcal R_a(u,v)=\frac12\left(
 \frac{a-u}{(a-u)^2+v^2}+\frac{a+u}{(a+u)^2+v^2}\right),\qquad
 K_3(u,v)=\sum_{j=1}^3 c_j\mathcal R_{a_j}(u,v).
\]
The two geometric statements proved below are
\begin{align}
 K_3(u,v)&>0&&(|u|\le3/8,\ v\in\mathbb R),\label{eq:three-line-positive}\\
 K_3(u,0)&\ge g&&(|u|\le1/2).\label{eq:three-line-charge}
\end{align}
All imaginary displacements and all cross terms are retained. The first
horizontal moment is \(\sum_{j=1}^3c_ja_j=1001493/10^{10}>0\).
The single-line trace identities are absolutely convergent, so this
retained tail is included in the assembly.

\paragraph{Exact polynomial proof of positivity.}
Let \(W=9/64\), and define the explicit rational polynomials
\begin{align*}
 d_j(x,y)&=(a_j^2+y-x)^2+4xy,\\
 N(x,y)&=\sum_{i=1}^3 c_i a_i(a_i^2+y-x)
           \prod_{\substack{1\le j\le3\\j\ne i}}d_j(x,y),\\
 Q(q,y)&=N(W-q,y)=\sum_{i=0}^5\sum_{j=0}^5Q_{ij}q^iy^j.
\end{align*}
Coefficients outside the degree-five support are zero. These definitions
specify every coefficient by rational arithmetic from the displayed
radii and weights.
Set
\[
 \alpha=-Q_{14}>0,\qquad \eta=-Q_{22}>0,\qquad\delta=-Q_{41}>0,
\]
and let \(B(y)=\sum_{j=0}^5b_jy^j\), where \(b_j=Q_{0j}\) for
\(j\ne4\), and \(b_4=Q_{04}-\alpha W\).
Exact expansion gives the identity
\begin{align*}
 Q(q,y)=B(y)
 &+q\{Q_{10}+(Q_{11}-\delta W^3)y
                   +(Q_{12}-\eta W)y^2+Q_{13}y^3\}\\
 &+\delta q(W-q)(W^2+Wq+q^2)y+\eta q(W-q)y^2\\
 &+q^2(Q_{20}+Q_{21}y+Q_{23}y^3)
       +q^3(Q_{30}+Q_{31}y+Q_{32}y^2)\\
 &+Q_{40}q^4+Q_{50}q^5+\alpha(W-q)y^4.
\end{align*}
Every coefficient in the braces and in the remaining correction terms
is strictly positive. For \(0\le q\le W\), \(y\ge0\), these corrections
are therefore nonnegative.

It remains to check \(B(y)>0\). Here \(b_2,b_3,b_4,b_5>0\).
Put \(p_0=B(1/4)\), \(p_1=B'(1/4)\), and
\[
 R(y)=b_3(y+1/2)+b_4(y^2+y/2+3/16)
             +b_5(y^3+y^2/2+3y/16+1/16).
\]
Then \(R(y)\ge0\) for \(y\ge0\), and the exact identity
\[
 4b_2B(y)=\{2b_2(y-1/4)+p_1\}^2
      +(4b_2p_0-p_1^2)+4b_2(y-1/4)^2R(y)
\]
proves strict positivity. The unscaled rational remainder is
\[
 p_0-\frac{p_1^2}{4b_2}
 =\frac{42582294214966263093549136221118761182085742929055283}
 {952122753923491722551436902400000000000000000000000000000}>0.
\]
This is the same certificate after division by the positive integer
scale used in Lean. Its polynomial identity, coefficient signs and
strict discriminant are checked exactly, without a frequency grid.

For \(x=u^2\), \(y=v^2\), the denominator product is positive because
\(|u|\le3/8<a_j\), and
\[
 K_3(u,v)\prod_{j=1}^3 d_j(u^2,v^2)=N(u^2,v^2).
\]
Taking \(q=W-u^2\) now proves \eqref{eq:three-line-positive}.

\paragraph{The full zero-fiber charge.}
The same-height statement has the larger domain \(|u|\le1/2\).
To see this, put
\[
 s=\sum_{j=1}^3a_j^2,\quad
 p=\sum_{1\le i<j\le3}a_i^2a_j^2,\quad
 \tau=\sum_{j=1}^3c_ja_j,\quad
 \nu=\sum_{j=1}^3c_ja_j^3,
\]
\[
 A_0=\nu-s\tau+gp,\quad A_1=\tau-gs,\quad
 R_0=A_0+A_1/4+g/16>0,\quad R_1=-A_1-g/2>0.
\]
Direct rational algebra gives
\[
 \{K_3(u,0)-g\}\prod_{j=1}^3(a_j^2-u^2)

\subsection{A joint multiplicity budget and horizontal rigidity}
\label{sec:horizontal-multiplicity}

The same signed three-line kernel controls more than an individual
multiplicity.  It charges horizontal displacement from the critical line
by a definite positive amount.  Retain the exact radii, signed weights,
charge \(g\), coefficient \(c_3\), and constant \(C_3\) from the preceding
subsection, and put
\[
 \kappa=\frac{13}{2},\qquad \lambda=\frac{\kappa}{g},\qquad
 B(T)=c_3\log|T|+C_3.
\]
For a finite set \(W\) of distinct complex points in the open critical
strip, all with imaginary part \(T\), define
\[
 N_W=\sum_{w\in W}m(w),\qquad
 M_W=\sum_{w\in W}m(w)(\Re w-\tfrac12)^2,
 \qquad m(w)=\operatorname{ord}_w\zeta.
\]
A nonzero value of \(\zeta\) has order zero, so it is unnecessary to
assume separately that every point in \(W\) is a zero.

\paragraph{Theorem.}
Unconditionally, for every such finite \(W\) and every \(|T|\ge2\),
\begin{equation}
 N_W+\lambda M_W\le B(T).
 \label{eq:horizontal-multiplicity-budget}
\end{equation}
In particular, every critical-strip point \(w\) with \(|\Im w|\ge2\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 m(w)\bigl(1+\lambda(\Re w-\tfrac12)^2\bigr)
 \le B(\Im w).
 \label{eq:horizontal-multiplicity-profile}
\end{equation}
If \(N_W\ge(1-\eta)B(T)\), then
\begin{equation}
 \lambda M_W\le\eta B(T).
 \label{eq:horizontal-multiplicity-rigidity}
\end{equation}
Thus a collection whose multiplicity mass is near the available ceiling
must have a small multiplicity-weighted horizontal second moment.
The statement concerns the explicitly specified finite support \(W\);
no unqualified sum over all complex numbers is used.

\paragraph{Exact quadratic charge.}
Write \(s=\sum_j a_j^2\), \(p=\sum_{i<j}a_i^2a_j^2\),
\(d=\prod_j a_j^2\), and \(\tau=\sum_jc_ja_j\), and set
\[
 D(x)=\prod_{j=1}^3(a_j^2-x),\qquad
 G_0=d\sum_{j=1}^3\frac{c_j}{a_j^3},\qquad G_1=\tau-gs.
\]

\[
 (K_3(u,0)-g)D(u^2)=u^2(G_0+G_1u^2+gu^4)
\]
follows by putting the three resolvents over their common denominator.
Define the rational constants
\[
 A=G_0-\kappa d,\qquad C=\kappa s-g,\qquad
 B_0=G_1+\kappa p-\frac{C}{4}.
\]
For the stated radii and weights, exact rational arithmetic proves
\(A>0\), \(B_0>0\), and \(C>0\).  The complete retained difference is
\begin{align*}
 &(K_3(u,0)-g-\kappa u^2)D(u^2)\\
 &\quad=u^2\left(A+B_0u^2+C u^2(\tfrac14-u^2)+\kappa u^6\right).
\end{align*}
If \(|u|\le1/2\), every term on the right is nonnegative and every
factor of \(D(u^2)\) is positive, including at \(|u|=1/2\), since
\(a_1=5001/10000>1/2\).  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 K_3(u,0)\ge g+\frac{13}{2}u^2\qquad(|u|\le1/2).
 \label{eq:horizontal-kernel-charge}
\end{equation}
This proof keeps the signed second weight inside the full rational
identity.  It does not estimate the three main resolvents separately.

\paragraph{Full multiplicity-fiber proof.}
For each \(w\in W\), the canonical xi divisor index has a finite fiber
of exactly \(m(w)\) elements, by the proved xi--zeta order identity.
The fibers of distinct points are disjoint.  Let \(S_W\) be their finite
union.  Every index in \(S_W\) has ordinate \(T\), and hence
\eqref{eq:horizontal-kernel-charge} gives
\[
 gN_W+\kappa M_W
 \le\sum_{q\in S_W}
 K_3(\Re\rho_q-\tfrac12,T-\Im\rho_q).
\]
The actual unconditional canonical strip is
\(|\Re\rho_q-1/2|\le3/8\).  The previously proved joint kernel
positivity on this strip and absolute summability allow the finite

signed analytic upper bound from the preceding subsection is
\[

\]
Combining these inequalities and dividing by \(g>0\) proves
\eqref{eq:horizontal-multiplicity-budget}.  Taking \(W=\{w\}\) proves
\eqref{eq:horizontal-multiplicity-profile}.  Subtracting the assumed
lower bound for \(N_W\) proves
\eqref{eq:horizontal-multiplicity-rigidity}.  This charges every
multiplicity copy and retains the same analytic constant \(C_3\).

\[
 \frac{g}{q(3/20)}\le\frac{g}{g-3/16}.
\]
Since \(C_3\ge0\) and \(\log|T|\ge0\), these two inequalities prove
\eqref{eq:exact-window-domination} through the full signed consumer.

The continuous family, strict minimum classification, arbitrary-candidate

\subsection{Effective constants and a numerical simplicity window}
\label{sec:effective-multiplicity-window}

The additive constant in \eqref{eq:sharp-global-multiplicity} can be
made explicit. Put \(B(a)=|\zeta'(a)/\zeta(a)|\),
\(h=(a-1)/2\), and
\begin{align*}
 H(a)&=\frac1{a-1}+B(a)+\frac{|\log\pi|}{2}
       +\frac{a+8+|\log3|+|\gamma_E|}{2},\\
 L(a)&=\frac{2B(1+h)}h+\frac1{(a-1)^2}
           +\frac14\sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{(n+1)^2}.
\end{align*}
For the radii \(r=r_\varepsilon\), \(R=R_*\) selected above,
\(a=1/2+r\), the maintained explicit witness is
\[
 C_\varepsilon^{\rm coarse}
       =\frac{rR}{r+R}\bigl(H(a)+rL(a)\bigr).
\]
This witness is greater than two. That fact concerns this particular
witness, not the existence of a better constant. A direct signed
estimate at fixed radii gives the following different tradeoff.

\paragraph{Effective multiplicity and simplicity window.}
For every \(w\) with \(0<\Re w<1\), writing
\(T=|\Im w|\) and \(m(w)=\operatorname{ord}_w\zeta\),
\begin{align*}
 T\ge2&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       m(w)\le\frac35\log T+\frac45,\\
 T\ge6&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       m(w)\le\frac35\log T+\frac{11}{100}.
\end{align*}
In particular, every nontrivial zero with
\[
                   2\le T<e^{63/20}\approx23.34
\]
is simple. The two inequalities hold without assuming RH and do not
require the narrower \(7/8\) strip.

\paragraph{Proof.}
We retain the two-line compensation at \(r=2\), \(R=3\).
The positive finite-strip kernel is valid on the entire critical strip,
and its same-height charge is \(g=1/r-r/R^2=5/18\).
Thus the proved zero-fiber inequality gives
\[
 m(w)\le\frac{18}{5}\left(A_2(\Im w)-\frac23A_3(\Im w)\right).
\]
We give every independent analytic input to the upper bound.

First, the real Euler bound is
\(B(5/2)\le3/10\). Its certificate uses the convergent logarithmic
Dirichlet series and the exact von Mangoldt convolution:
\[
 B(5/2)\,|\zeta(5/2)|
     =\left|\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\log n}{n^{5/2}}\right|.
\]
The following rational bounds are proved with infinite tails retained:
\[
 \sum_{n=1}^{6}\frac{\log n}{n^{5/2}}
   +6^{-3/2}\left(\frac23\log6+\frac49\right)
       \le\frac{159}{400},\qquad
 \sum_{n=1}^{12}n^{-5/2}\ge\frac{53}{40}.
\]
Indeed \(f(x)=x^{-5/2}\log x\) decreases for \(x\ge6\), and
\(-x^{-3/2}(\frac23\log x+\frac49)\) is its exact primitive.
The finite sum--integral inequality bounds every partial tail by its
value at six; summability then passes the inequality to the full tail.
The rational certificate uses
\(\log2<0.693148\), \(\log3<1.098613\),
\(\log5\le\log6-1/6\), and rational square-root enclosures.
The ratio \((159/400)/(53/40)\) is exactly \(3/10\).

The common-height Euler comparison is essential. For \(\Re s=a>1\)
and \(0\le\lambda\le1\), the entire compensated quotient satisfies
\[
 \left|\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)
       -\lambda\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s+1)\right|\le B(a).
\]
Before estimation, its von Mangoldt coefficient is
\(\Lambda(n)n^{-s}(1-\lambda/n)\), up to the common minus sign.
The factor \(1-\lambda/n\) lies in \([0,1]\); no separate bounds
on the two Euler quotients are substituted. At \(a=5/2\),
\(\lambda=2/3\), the normalized Euler allowance is therefore
\((18/5)(3/10)=27/25\).

For the gamma contribution the maintained remainder theorem proves
\[
 \left|\Re\psi(z)-\log|z|+\frac12\Re\frac1z\right|
       \le\frac1{8(\Re z)^2}\qquad(\Re z\ge1).
\]
For completeness, set \(f_z(x)=\Re(1/(z+x))\). The finite
trapezoid identity retains its endpoint term, and on each unit interval
its error is bounded by
\[
 \frac1{8(u+n)^2}-\frac1{8(u+n+1)^2},\qquad u=\Re z.
\]
This follows from the Peano weight \(x(1-x)/2\) and
\(|f_z''(x)|\le2/(u+x)^3\). Summing telescopes; the digamma series
and harmonic logarithm limit give the displayed remainder. In
particular, the inverse endpoint correction is retained when taking
upper and lower bounds on the two gamma terms.

Put \(q=1/T^2\), \(y=T^2\), and define
\begin{align*}
 D(q)&=\log(1+81q/4)-\frac23\log(1+121q/4),\\
 P(y)&=\frac{3/2}{9/4+y}-\frac{5/3}{25/4+y},\\
 J(y)&=\frac{9/4}{81/16+y/4}-\frac{11/6}{121/16+y/4}.
\end{align*}
Using the exact identity
\[
 \frac{\xi'}{\xi}(s)=\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)+\frac1{s-1}
             -\frac12\log\pi+\frac12\psi(s/2+1),
\]
the complete normalized signed bound is
\begin{align*}
 m(w)\le{}&\frac35\log T+\frac{27}{25}-\frac35\log(2\pi)
              +\frac{70}{1089}\\
          &+\frac9{10}D(1/T^2)+\frac{18}{5}P(T^2)
                                      -\frac9{10}J(T^2).
\end{align*}
The remainder \(70/1089\) is the sum of the two explicit gamma
errors at real parts \(9/4\) and \(11/4\), after compensation
and division by \(g\). The same bound applies at both signs of the
height, so averaging gives the required paired trace.

All remaining inequalities are rational or elementary logarithmic
inequalities, proved on their complete ranges:
\begin{align*}
 0\le q\le1/4&:\quad D(q)\le1/2,\\
 0\le q\le1/36&:\quad \frac9{10}D(q)\le\frac{17043}{449440},\\
 y\ge4&:\quad \frac{18}{5}P(y)\le\frac7{25},\\
 y\ge36&:\quad P(y)\le0,\qquad
 y\ge0:\quad J(y)\ge0.
\end{align*}
For example, \(3D(q)\) is the logarithm of
\((1+81q/4)^3/(1+121q/4)^2\). On the first range this ratio is at
most \(49/16=(7/4)^2\), so \(\log x\le x-1\) proves
\(D(q)\le1/2\). On the second range the ratio is at most
\(50625/44944\); the same logarithmic inequality gives the stated
bound. These ratio bounds are polynomial inequalities on the full
intervals, not finite samples.

Finally \(\log(2\pi)>9/5\), so the Euler allowance is canceled
by the retained constant \(-(3/5)\log(2\pi)\). The remaining
allowances are
\[
 \frac{70}{1089}+\frac9{20}+\frac7{25}<\frac45,
 \qquad
 \frac{70}{1089}+\frac{17043}{449440}<\frac{11}{100}.
\]
This proves both multiplicity bounds. For \(2\le T\le6\),
\(\log6<2\) makes the first bound strictly less than two.
For \(6\le T<e^{63/20}\), the second is strictly less than two.
Integrality of the analytic order completes the proof.

The effective constant \(4/5<2\) uses logarithmic coefficient
\(3/5\), not a coefficient arbitrarily close to \(c_*\).
The near-optimal asymptotic theorem is retained separately.
The maintained declarations are
\texttt{zeta\_multiplicity\_effective\_low},
\texttt{zeta\_multiplicity\_effective\_high}, and
\texttt{zeta\_simplicity\_window} in
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelEffectiveMultiplicity.lean}.
The independent analytic inputs are in
\texttt{Helpers/RealDigammaRemainder.lean},
\texttt{Helpers/EulerRealHalfIntegerBounds.lean}, and
\texttt{Helpers/CompensatedXiConstants.lean}.
This finite window does not establish simplicity at unbounded heights;
the positive logarithmic term still grows. No improvement of the
certified zero-free boundary is asserted.

\subsection{A probability-carrier criterion for simplicity of all nontrivial zeros}

The numerical window above is now established. The corresponding global
question admits an exact criterion in terms of small values of a positive
probability Dirichlet series. Define
\[
 a_n=\frac{\varphi(n)}n\quad(n\ge1),\qquad a_0=0,\qquad
 \mathcal P(s)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+1)}.
\]
The number \(a_n\in[0,1]\) is the probability that a uniform residue modulo
\(n\) is coprime to \(n\). For \(\Re s>1\),
\[
 \mathcal P(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n n^{-s}.
\]
For \(w\) in the open critical strip, let
\[
 A_{w,R}(\varepsilon)=\operatorname{Area}
 \{z\in\mathbb C:|z-w|<R,\ |\mathcal P(z)|\le\varepsilon\},
\]
where Area denotes two-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

\paragraph{Global simplicity criterion.}
All nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta\) are simple if and only if
\[
 \forall w\ (0<\Re w<1)\quad\exists R>0\quad
 A_{w,R}(\varepsilon)=o(\varepsilon)
       \qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\]
Explicitly, after choosing \(R\), for every \(c>0\) there is
\(\delta>0\) such that
\(0<\varepsilon<\delta\) implies
\(A_{w,R}(\varepsilon)\le c\varepsilon\).
The radius and constants may depend on \(w\). This is a local condition
at every point of the strip; no uniformity in height is assumed.

\paragraph{Proof of the carrier identity and order transfer.}
The exact arithmetic convolution
\(\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n\) gives
\[
 \left(\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi(n)n^{-(s+1)}\right)\zeta(s+1)=\zeta(s).
\]
All products and sums here converge absolutely when \(\Re s>1\):
\(0\le\varphi(n)\le n\) bounds the first series by the ordinary
zeta series at \(\Re s\). Dividing by the nonzero \(\zeta(s+1)\)
and shifting each term proves the probability-series identity.
If \(\Re w>0\) and \(w\ne1\), then \(\zeta\) is analytic at \(w\),
while \(\zeta(w+1)\) is analytic and nonzero because
\(\Re(w+1)>1\). Thus \(\mathcal P\) is analytic there and
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_w\mathcal P=\operatorname{ord}_w\zeta.
\]
These orders are finite, as follows also from the existing nonzero entire
xi function and its local unit factorization. No RH hypothesis enters.

\paragraph{Proof of the area threshold.}
For an analytic function \(f\) of finite order \(m\) at \(w\), write
\(f(z)=(z-w)^m g(z)\) near \(w\), where \(g\) is analytic and
\(g(w)\ne0\). If \(m\le1\), shrink a ball of radius at most one
so that \(|g(z)|\ge c>0\). Then
\(|f(z)|\ge c|z-w|\), including the case \(m=0\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Area}\{|z-w|<R,\ |f(z)|\le\varepsilon\}
       \le\pi(\varepsilon/c)^2=o(\varepsilon).
\]
Conversely, if \(m\ge2\), shrink a ball of radius at most one so
that \(|g(z)|\le C\), with \(C>0\). On that ball,
\(|f(z)|\le C|z-w|^2\). For every sufficiently small positive
\(\varepsilon\), the disk of radius \(\sqrt{\varepsilon/C}\)
is contained in both that ball and any proposed ball in the criterion.
Its area is \(\pi\varepsilon/C\), so the sublevel area cannot be
\(o(\varepsilon)\). This proves the equivalence. Applying it to
\(\mathcal P\) and using exact order transfer proves the assertion.
The distinction between planar area and length along a line is essential.

\paragraph{What the numerical window already supplies.}
The area condition is proved for every \(w\) with
\(0<\Re w<1\) and \(2\le|\Im w|<e^{63/20}\). Consequently global
simplicity is equivalent to the same condition only on the complement
of this window, namely \(|\Im w|<2\) or \(|\Im w|\ge e^{63/20}\).
The low-height complement has not been silently removed.

The three maintained modules
\texttt{ZeroSmallValueArea}, \texttt{CoprimalityDirichletCarrier}, and
\texttt{CarmichaelSimplicityCriterion} contain the full proofs, including
the probability interpretation, convergence, both geometric implications,
and the window specialization. The criterion does not itself establish
the independent global small-value estimate. Positive coefficients alone
do not imply such an estimate, and the logarithmic multiplicity bound
still grows with height. No proof of global simplicity or improvement
of the certified \(7/8\) zero-free boundary is claimed. The local analytic
mechanism is standard; novelty priority for this formulation is unestablished.

\subsection{Exact Mellin action on general windows}
\label{sec:general-window-mellin}

Let
\[
 \mathcal M W(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}W(x)\,dx,\qquad
 \widetilde W(x)=\frac{W(x)-3W(2x)+2W(4x)}6.
\]
For every \(s\in\mathbb C\) where the first integral is absolutely
convergent, the filtered integral is also absolutely convergent and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-window-mellin}
 \mathcal M\widetilde W(s)
 =\frac{(1-2^{-s})(1-2^{1-s})}{6}\,\mathcal M W(s).
\end{equation}
This is an integral identity for a general window, not only a calculation
on the formal power response \(D^s\).

\emph{Proof.}
For any positive real \(a\), the substitution \(u=ax\) gives
\[
 \mathcal M[W(a\,\cdot)](s)=a^{-s}\mathcal M W(s)
\]
and preserves Mellin convergence. Linearity applied to the three
convergent integrals gives the coefficient
\((1-3\cdot2^{-s}+2\cdot4^{-s})/6\), which factors as in
\eqref{eq:general-window-mellin}. \(\square\)

If \(0<\beta=\Re s<1\), the existing multiplier estimate now applies
directly to the actual integral:
\[
 \frac{(1-2^{-\beta})(2^{1-\beta}-1)}6\,|\mathcal M W(s)|
 \le |\mathcal M\widetilde W(s)|.
\]
Its coefficient is positive. Thus a nonzero base Mellin value survives
the filter. The base value itself is still present in this bound;
no uniform lower bound for it in height is asserted.
The maintained proofs are
\texttt{GaussianMellinWavelet.window\_hasMellin},
\texttt{window\_mellin\_ne\_zero}, and
\texttt{window\_mellin\_norm\_lower}, in
\texttt{Assembly/DetectorPackets/GaussianMellinWavelet.lean}.
The construction of a responsive radial bandlimited base window
remains a separate obligation.

\subsection{An exact band-limited Type I estimate}
\label{sec:bandlimited-type-i}

A different window class removes the principal Type I block exactly.
Here \emph{Gaussian} refers to the lattice \(\mathbb Z[i]\).
An exponential Gaussian test function is not band-limited.

Let \(g\) be a complex Schwartz function on \(\mathbb C\), with
\(\widehat g(0)=0\) and \(\widehat g(\xi)=0\) for \(|\xi|\ge1\).
For every real \(q>0\), \(X>0\), \(q^2\le X\), and \(r\in\mathbb C\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandlimited-coset-zero}
 \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^2}
   g\!\left(\frac{qn+r}{\sqrt X}\right)=0.
\end{equation}
The sum is absolutely convergent. Identifying \(n=n_1+in_2\),
affine Poisson summation gives
\[
 \frac{X}{q^2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^2}
   e^{2\pi i\, k\cdot r/q}\,
   \widehat g\!\left(\frac{\sqrt X}{q}k\right).
\]
The zero frequency vanishes by hypothesis. Every other frequency has
norm at least \(\sqrt X/q\ge1\), and vanishes by support.
Every shift phase remains in this calculation.
For an integer \(q\ge1\), the complete combination of all \(q^2\)
cosets with weights \(\chi(r_1^2+r_2^2)\) therefore also vanishes,
for any Dirichlet character modulo \(q\). The theorem includes
\(q=1,2\) and composite moduli.

Suppose \(g(z)=V(|z|^2)\) and \(V(0)=0\). For nonzero ideals and
a nonzero ideal \(H\), write
\[
 S_H(V;X)=\sum_{\substack{C\ne0\\(C,H)=1}}V(N(C)/X).
\]
The four-unit reindexing and complete divisor mask give
\[
 S_H(V;X)=\sum_{E\mid H}\mu(E)
          \sum_{C\ne0}V\bigl(N(C)/(X/N(E))\bigr).
\]
Hence \(S_H(V;X)=0\) whenever \(N(H)\le X\). For any finite sets
\(A,B\) of nonzero ideals, the entire signed block satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandlimited-type-i-zero}
 \sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}\mu(a)\mu(b)
       S_H\!\left(V;\frac{D}{N(a)N(b)}\right)=0
\end{equation}
if \(D>0\) and \(N(H)N(a)N(b)\le D\) for every retained pair.
All divisor terms, including ramified and repeated factors, remain.
No norm estimate is applied to this main block.

The existing three-scale wavelet supplies both origin conditions.
If \(f(z)=W(|z|^2)\) and \(\widehat f(\xi)=0\) for \(|\xi|\ge1/2\),
then \(g=\operatorname{wavelet}(f)\) has Fourier support inside the
unit disk, \(g(0)=\widehat g(0)=0\), and
\(V=\operatorname{window}(W)\). The dilation identity
\[
 \widehat{f(\sqrt a\,\cdot)}(\xi)
       =a^{-1}\widehat f(\xi/\sqrt a)
\]
proves the support statement for \(a=2,4\).
The maintained owner is
\texttt{Assembly/DetectorPackets/GaussianBandlimitedTypeI.lean},
including \texttt{wavelet\_typeI\_zero} and
\texttt{character\_coset\_sum\_zero}.

\paragraph{Its complete consumer and remaining corrections.}
The earlier generic estimate was
\[
 |T_I-\mathrm{main}_I|
       \le (81/4)C_f\,\tau(H)\,UV.
\]
On the new window class the entire block is zero under
\(N(H)UV\le D\). Fixed masks thus permit \(U,V\) of order
\(\sqrt D\), rather than imposing \(UV\le D^{3/4}\) merely to
fit a \(D^{3/4}\) allowance. This is a component estimate.

Finite support remains explicit. If \(L_{\le Y}\) is the low-low
block in a finite ideal prefix, the theorem concerns its full
cofactor counterpart \(L_\infty\). Set
\(L_{\rm out}=L_\infty-L_{\le Y}\). After the required reindexing,
the two-cutoff identity retains
\[
 P_{\le Y}=E_U+E_V-L_{\le Y}+H_{\le Y}
          =E_U+E_V+H_{\le Y}+L_{\rm out},
 \qquad L_\infty=0.
\]
The outside correction has a plus sign. A total-ideal coprimality
restriction also restricts \(a,b\); a cofactor-only mask cannot
replace it. The endpoint, outside-tail and high-high bounds, their
energy cross terms, and the twisted-ideal adapter remain separate.
Removing the physical origin from the character coset sum also
uses \(g(0)=0\).

The wavelet multiplier is nonzero throughout the critical strip.
Applying this window class to zero exclusion additionally requires
a nonzero base Mellin response at the hypothetical zero; Fourier
support alone does not establish that condition. This section
proves component vanishing, not a completed band-limited RH
criterion. Novelty priority is unestablished. The certified
boundary remains \(7/8\).
