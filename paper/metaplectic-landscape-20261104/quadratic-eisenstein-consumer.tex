\section{A quadratic Levi with a complete cubic-packet consumer}
\label{sec:quadratic-eisenstein-consumer}

This is a further source-dependent comparison, not a new analytic
estimate or a newly formalized automorphic theorem. It uses
\cite[Propositions 5.9--5.10 and Theorem 5.11]{GaoInverseEis} for
the double Kazhdan--Patterson cover of \(\mathrm{GL}_3\), with
\(p=0,q=1\). The inducing theta representation is on its
\(\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathrm{GL}_1\) Levi. It is not the
nongeneric theta representation on \(\mathrm{GL}_3\) itself.

\paragraph{The exceptional datum and character image.}
Take \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\), so the source hypothesis \(\mu_4\subset K\)
holds. For the upper-triangular bisector with diagonal zero and
off-diagonal parameter one, the central torus has coordinates
\((ta^2,tb^2,t)\). Its cocycle is \((t,u)_2\).
Use the normalized quadratic Weil cochain \(\gamma_\psi\), with
\[
 \gamma_\psi(t)\gamma_\psi(u)
      =(t,u)_2\gamma_\psi(tu),\qquad
 \gamma_\psi(a^2)=1.
\]
Its adelic rational product formula is the usual global Weil-index
input; the local convention is as in \cite[Section 2.1]{PrasannaWeil}.
This input is not formalized here. Define
\[
 \chi_0(\zeta,t_1,t_2,t_3)
    =\epsilon(\zeta)\gamma_\psi(t_3)
      |t_1/t_2|_{\mathbb A}^{1/4}.
\]
The cochain identity proves multiplicativity on the covered centre,
and the product formulas prove rational descent. Its root values are
\[
 (\chi_0)_{\alpha_1}=|\cdot|_{\mathbb A},\qquad
 (\chi_0)_{\alpha_2}=|\cdot|_{\mathbb A}^{-1/2}.
\]
For example the root lifts at \(a^2\) have coordinates
\((a^2,a^{-2},1)\) and \((1,a^2,a^{-2})\); their Weil factors
are \(1\). Both Levi central norm exponents vanish.
Twisting by \(\tau\circ\det_{\mathrm{GL}_2}\) preserves
exceptionality and multiplies the second root character by \(\tau^2\).
Consequently every finite odd-order Hecke character \(\eta\)
is realized on this fixed quadratic field, by choosing \(\tau^2=\eta\).
The source factorization becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}
 E_{\eta,\psi}(s)=B_\eta(s)/L_K^S(2s+3/2,\eta).
\end{equation}
The earlier finite-point window argument applies with its same
explicit analytic hypotheses and no moving norm assertion.

\paragraph{What cancels in the entire unramified integral.}
The integration unipotent is abelian of dimension two. Gao's exact
shell argument leaves the integral region and the joint shell
\((v(x_1),v(x_2))=(-1,-1)\); mixed negative supports vanish.
Write \(Q\) for the residue cardinality,
\(a_v=\chi(h_{\alpha_2}(\varpi^2))\), and \(g\ne0\) for the
normalized nonprincipal quadratic Gauss integral with the source
additive convention. The joint shell, including its Levi value, is
\[
 Q^{-2s}\left(Q^{-1}a_vg^{-1}\right)
                 \left(-Q^{-1}\right)g
       =-a_vQ^{-2s-2}.
\]
The integral region contributes \(1\). Since
\(a_v=Q^{1/2}\eta(v)\), the full result is
\(1-\eta(v)Q^{-2s-3/2}\), as required by
\eqref{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}. No Gauss factor has been bounded
in this calculation: its inverse cancels it with its phase intact.
The principal Ramanujan factor \(-Q^{-1}\) survives.
Thus improving the isolated nonprincipal Gauss norm is not an
independent estimate of this global inverse observable.

\paragraph{A restricted cubic packet with its principal row retained.}
Let \(\chi\) be primitive cubic of prime conductor \(q\), and put
\(\eta_j=\chi^j\circ N_{K/\mathbb Q}\), \(j=0,1,2\).
For \(u=2s+3/2\), define
\[
 F_j(u)=L_{\mathbb Q}^S(u,\chi^j),\quad
 M_j(u)=L_{\mathbb Q}^S(u,\chi^j\chi_{-4}),\quad
 L_j(u)=F_j(u)M_j(u)=L_K^S(u,\eta_j).
\]
Then \(E_j(s)=B_j(s)/L_j(u)\). Put
\(B_* = B_0B_1B_2\) and
\(B_{\ne j}=\prod_{k\ne j}B_k\). Product differentiation gives
the complete signed operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-full-cubic-packet}
 \sum_{j=0}^2 B_{\ne j}(s)L_j(u)E_j'(s)-B_*'(s)
 +2B_*(s)\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{M_j'(u)}{M_j(u)}
 =-2B_*(s)\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{F_j'(u)}{F_j(u)}.
\end{equation}
Indeed each differentiated native identity contributes
\(B_j'-2B_jL_j'/L_j\); the \(B_j'\) terms sum to \(B_*'\),
and \(L_j'/L_j=F_j'/F_j+M_j'/M_j\). This is a meromorphic
identity. It retains the reciprocal \(M_j\), every section derivative,
the row \(j=0\), and all common-window convolutions.
It uses a sum of logarithmic readouts, not a product of three
inverse detectors with all six \(L\)-factor lengths multiplied.
Each row still has its actual two-factor plain companion, and
moments of different rows retain their cross terms.

At a zeta zero the residue on the right, in coordinate \(u\), is
\(-2B_*(s_\rho)(m_\zeta+m_\chi+m_{\chi^2})\).
It is nonzero if the chosen window multiplier is nonzero.
The two nonprincipal characters need not share that zero.
This is a zero-free-packet implication including zeta; a bound for
the whole packet is not asserted equivalent to RH for zeta alone.

Its physical prime-power expression, up to the displayed window
multiplier and finite \(S\)-corrections, is the centred sum
\[
 3\sum_{\substack{p^k\le X,\ p\notin S\\\chi(p)^k=1}}
           \log p-X.
\]
All \(k\ge1\) occur. In particular, when \(3\mid k\), every
\(p\notin S\) occurs, even if \(\chi(p)\ne1\).
The main term is \(X\), not \(3X\), because just the principal
Dirichlet row has a pole at \(u=1\).

\paragraph{An alternative consumer that keeps all six factors.}
For detecting a zeta zero it is unnecessary to subtract the quadratic
companions. Keeping them as positive rows avoids that subtraction.
With a common analytic window \(R\), put \(Z_j=RE_j\). The native
factorization gives the complete alternative operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-positive-six-packet}
 \sum_{j=0}^2 B_{\ne j}L_j Z_j'
       -3R'B_*-RB_*'
   =-2RB_*\sum_{j=0}^2\frac{L_j'}{L_j}.
\end{equation}
All quantities on the left are evaluated at \(s\), and the
logarithmic derivatives on the right at \(u=2s+3/2\).
This is a direct product-rule consequence of
\eqref{eq:quadratic-gl3-native}: each row contributes
\(R'B_*+RB_{\ne j}B_j'-2RB_*L_j'/L_j\).
It retains every section and window derivative. There is no
companion-logarithmic-derivative subtraction in this consumer.

The six rational factors \(F_j,M_j\) all have weight one.
At a zeta zero their orders add, so the native-coordinate residue
is \(-R(s_\rho)B_*(s_\rho)\sum_j(m_{F_j}+m_{M_j})\).
Thus it is nonzero under the explicit window condition
\(R(s_\rho)B_*(s_\rho)\ne0\). This condition and the global native
identity must still be discharged for any analytic application;
the present algebra does not supply them. The unwindowed arithmetic
readout, including every prime power and the principal centering, is
\[
 6\!\!\sum_{\substack{p^k\le X,\ p\notin S\\
             \chi(p)^k=1,\ \chi_{-4}(p)^k=1}}\log p-X.
\]
For the cubic character modulo seven these are exactly the powers
\(p^k\equiv1,13\pmod{28}\), away from the excluded primes.
This changes the observable and its arithmetic estimate, rather than
merely inserting another local coefficient into the old cubic proof.
The three native rows still carry their full degree-two lengths and
all mixed terms. No estimate of this restricted prime-power sum is
deduced from positivity of the zero multiplicities.

\paragraph{The full cost and rate comparison.}
The three native denominators have total degree six and conductor
product \(2^6q^4\): the principal one has conductor \(4\),
and each other one has conductor \(4q^2\). A single nonprincipal
row has degree two, conductor \(4q^2\), and one quadratic-twisted
rational companion isolates its chosen \(F_j\). The logarithmic
packet \eqref{eq:quadratic-full-cubic-packet} uses its actual row
lengths, rather than the length of the inverse-product detector.
This reduces representation and conductor costs compared with the
Sp4 cubic packet, but gives no saving on its own.

The affine window map is now \(Y=X^2\) with amplitude \(2X^{3/2}\),
so a complete spectral rate \(r\) would give boundary \(3/2+2r\).
The corresponding rates for \(7/8,3/4,1/2\) are respectively
\(-5/16,-3/8,-1/2\). Every ramified \(q\)-window, archimedean
norm, reciprocal companion, height and truncation loss must enter
that rate. No cubic \(3/16\) or prefix-sieve bound transfers to it
by identity alone. The independent global estimate remains open;
the certified boundary is \(7/8\). The point of this comparison is
the complete alternative consumer and the exact surviving signed
term, not a claim that a local calculation has improved the boundary.

\subsection{Reference-independent nonreal power compensation}
\label{sec:nonreal-power-compensation}

The power probe admits a stronger signal theorem than the previously used
reference-strip argument. Let \(L_i(s)=L(s,\chi_i)\) be a finite collection
of Dirichlet \(L\)-functions of a common positive modulus, let
\(w_i\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), and let \(G\) be analytic on
\(\Re s>0\). Put
\[
 R(s)=-\sum_i w_i\frac{L_i'(s)}{L_i(s)}+G(s),\qquad
 q_d(s)=\frac{s+d-1}{d},\qquad
 P_dR(s)=R(s)-\frac{s}{s+d-1}R(q_d(s)).
\]
Here \(d>1\) is any fixed real number. If
\(\Re\rho>0\), \(\Im\rho\ne0\), and \(L_{i_0}(\rho)=0\)
for an index of positive weight, then \(P_dR\) is not analytic at
\(\rho\). No zero-free hypothesis at \(q_d(\rho)\), no RH
hypothesis, and no simplicity or disjointness assumption on the zeros
is needed.

\emph{Proof.}
Neither \(\rho\) nor \(q_d(\rho)\) equals \(1\), and both have positive
real part. Local analytic factorization gives finite nonnegative
orders \(n_i\) at \(\rho\) and \(m_i\) at \(q_d(\rho)\).
Set
\[
 A=\sum_iw_i n_i>0,\qquad B=\sum_iw_i m_i\in\mathbb R.
\]
The analytic correction has zero scaled residue. Keeping the reference
zeros, the affine derivative \(q_d'=1/d\), and the complete scalar
factor gives
\[
 \lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}(s-\rho)P_dR(s)
   =r=-A+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}B.
\]
The denominator is nonzero since its real part is positive. Direct
multiplication, without estimating the reference term separately, yields
\[
 \Im\bigl(r(\rho+d-1)\overline{\rho}\bigr)
       =A(d-1)\Im\rho.
\]
The right side is nonzero. Thus \(r\ne0\); analyticity would instead
force the displayed limit to be zero. Moreover the same identity gives
the quantitative certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-residue-height-cost}
 |r|\ \ge\
 \frac{A(d-1)|\Im\rho|}{|\rho+d-1|\,|\rho|}.
\end{equation}
This proves the assertion with every shared-zero multiplicity retained.
\(\square\)

The maintained Lean declarations are
\texttt{CarmichaelWeightedSignal.real\_packet\_scaled\_residue},
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_scaled\_limit},
\texttt{real\_packet\_power\_nonreal\_zero\_not\_analytic}, and
\texttt{powerProbe\_nonreal\_zero\_not\_analytic}, all under
\texttt{PrimeFactorOscillations}. Their owner is
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean}.

Real weights are essential to this proof: the reference residue \(B\)
is real, so its contribution to the displayed imaginary part vanishes.
Nonnegative real parts of arbitrary complex weights do not supply that
argument. The assertion also excludes real target zeros. It is a
one-way signal-preservation theorem, not a pointwise equivalence between
all poles of the compensated probe and target zeros: reference zeros
can introduce additional poles.

\paragraph{Its place in the complete exponent argument.}
The old theorem \texttt{powerProbe\_order\_iff\_zero} uses
\(\Re\rho>1-d/8\) to place the reference in the installed
\(7/8\) zero-free region. The new nonreal theorem removes that premise;
powers \(d=1+\varepsilon\), with fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), are therefore
admissible for signal preservation. It does not remove the physical
reference scale \(x^d\), the height and \(d-1\) losses in
\eqref{eq:power-residue-height-cost}, or any mixed compensation term.
The rational main-pole remainder remains
\(-\kappa(d-1)/(s+d-1)\).

This is not a new ability of the overall detector library to see the
critical strip. The existing three-scale Mellin wavelet already has
multiplier
\[
 m(s)=\frac{(1-2^{-s})(1-2^{1-s})}{6},
\]
which is nonzero for \(0<\Re s<1\). Its constant dilations preserve
critical-strip zeros and cancel the origin and radial principal
Poisson mode. That alternative must be compared with power compensation
using its own arithmetic consumer, rather than the cubic moment
consumer by default.

In particular, for the previously proved fixed smooth compact
character-portfolio estimate, the complete difference between the
power-compensated and original signed quadratic packets is bounded by
\[
 \frac73 K^2\left(
  2D^{(15-d)/8+(d+1)u}+D^{(8-d)/4+2du}\right).
\]
Here \(d\) is a natural number, \(u>0\), \(7/8+u\le2\);
\(K\) depends on the fixed window, character portfolio and depletion
mask, but not on \(D>0\) or the divisor cutoff. This is
\texttt{CarmichaelPowerCompensation.portfolio\_power\_compensation\_bound}.
It retains the mixed terms in the difference and leaves the original
signed packet unestimated. It is not uniform in a growing conductor
or an arbitrary noncompact carrier. At the \(D^{3/2}\) target scale its
first exponent requires \(d\ge3\) as \(u\downarrow0\), with slack
needed at \(d=3\); at the \(D\) scale it requires \(d\ge7\).
Consequently making \(d\) approach one in the new signal theorem cannot
by itself improve this arithmetic consumer. There is also no transfer
here from the natural-power estimate to nonintegral \(d\).

A new global construction may replace both this packet estimate and
the old cubic moment stage. It must supply its own exact transformed
observable, signal normalization, full signed arithmetic bound, and
conductor, height, window and truncation costs. The quadratic
\(\mathrm{GL}_3\) consumer above needs \(r\le-3/8\) to reach \(3/4\);
the unchanged cubic consumer needs a full low error at most
\(X^{1/16+o(1)}\), together with control of every other error.
The theorem proved here supplies signal preservation only. No
independent arithmetic saving or improved QRH boundary is asserted;
the certified boundary remains \(7/8\).

\subsection{Two-point multiplicity recovery and stability}
\label{sec:power-multiplicity-recovery}

The complex residue above contains more information than nonvanishing.
Write \(\gamma=\Im\rho\ne0\), \(a=d-1>0\), and define, for \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
 {\cal A}(z)=
 \frac{\Im\!\left(z(\rho+a)\overline\rho\right)}{a\gamma},
 \qquad
 {\cal B}(z)=
 \frac{\Im\!\left(z(\rho+a)\right)/\gamma+{\cal A}(z)}{d}.
\]
For the full signed residue \(r=-A+d\rho B/(\rho+a)\), one has
\[
 {\cal A}(r)=A,\qquad {\cal B}(r)=B.
\]
Indeed, multiplication gives
\[
 r(\rho+a)=\rho(dB-A)-aA,
\]
so its imaginary part is \(\gamma(dB-A)\), while multiplying once
more by \(\overline\rho\) gives imaginary part \(aA\gamma\).
These two equations prove the assertion. They retain the entire
reference contribution, including shared zeros and repeated zeros.
Only reality of \(A,B\) is needed for the inversion. For a single
\(L\)-function they are its two local integer orders; for a weighted
packet they are the two weighted totals, not the individual orders
of its constituent rows.

Both readout maps are continuous real linear functions of \(z\).
Consequently, applying them to
\((s-\rho)P_dR(s)\) recovers \(A\) and \(B\) in the limit.
This is formalized by
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_multiplicity\_recovery}, using the same
local scaled-limit hypotheses as the preceding residue theorem.

The recovery also has an explicit stability cost. For arbitrary
\(z,z'\in\mathbb C\), the exact difference identity implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-multiplicity-stability}
 |a\gamma|\,|{\cal A}(z)-{\cal A}(z')|
 \le |z-z'|\,|\rho+a|\,|\rho|.
\end{equation}
Here the norm estimates only the separate observation error;
no signed arithmetic main term or compensation term is discarded.
Taking \(z=r+e\), \(z'=r\), and \(|e|\le\varepsilon\), gives
\[
 |{\cal A}(r+e)-A|
 \le \varepsilon\,\frac{|\rho+a|\,|\rho|}{a|\gamma|}.
\]
For a single function, a right side below \(1/2\) uniquely
determines the integer \(A\) by rounding. This rounding statement is
an elementary consequence of the displayed proved error bound.
The height cost is explicit and is not uniform as \(\gamma\to0\);
at real targets the two real equations lose rank.

The additional maintained declarations in
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean} are
\texttt{power\_residue\_target\_multiplicity},
\texttt{power\_residue\_reference\_multiplicity}, and
\texttt{powerTargetMultiplicity\_error\_bound}, together with the
limit theorem just named. These are multiplicity readouts, not an
independent upper estimate for their values or a proof of simplicity.

\paragraph{Relation to the thin probability detector.}
The existing \texttt{Helpers/ProbabilityZeroRecovery.lean} uses two
adjacent Bernoulli masses at one moving rank, with known centering
and normalization, at quartic cutoffs \(N=n^4\).
For a qualified quadratic prime law, its eventual comparison is
equivalent to RH. Under RH its harmonic coefficient at frequency
\(\gamma\) is exactly
\[
 \frac{m(1/2+i\gamma)}{1/4+\gamma^2}.
\]
The RH assumption on this reconstruction must be distinguished
from the unconditional logical equivalence.
The single eventual comparison retains less information than the
values of the normalized observable. The generalized compensated
residue retains multiplicity information, but does not by itself
provide the probability-law adapter and harmonic limit for every
new family.

There is also an existing multiplicity energy theorem in
\texttt{Helpers/XiMultiplicityBudget.lean}. Under RH, its excess
energy can be written as
\[
 {\cal D}=
 \sum_{\substack{\rho=1/2+i\gamma\\\text{distinct zeros}}}
 \frac{m(\rho)(m(\rho)-1)}{(1/4+\gamma^2)^2}\ge0,
\]
and \({\cal D}=0\) if and only if every zero is simple.
Thus an independent amplitude or excess-energy bound can yield
multiplicity results without a better QRH exponent. The identities
and readout stability alone do not supply that bound.

\subsection{Integer powers constrain weak zero responses}
\label{sec:integer-power-multiplicity}

Fix the same underlying logarithmic readout \(R\) as in
Section~\ref{sec:nonreal-power-compensation}, and let \(\rho\) be a nonreal
target zero. For an integer \(d\ge2\), write \(m\in\mathbb N\) for its
target order and \(n_d\in\mathbb N\) for the order at \(q_d(\rho)\).
Put \(\gamma=\Im\rho\ne0\), \(C_d=\rho+d-1\), and
\[
 r_d=\lim_{s\to\rho,\ s\ne\rho}(s-\rho)P_dR(s)
       =-m+\frac{d\rho}{C_d}n_d.
\]
For a single \(L\)-function the coefficient of its logarithmic derivative
is normalized to one. The argument also applies to integer-weighted
packets with integral aggregate orders. It does not assume integrality
for general real-weighted aggregates.

There is the following exact alternative:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-residue-alternative}
 m=d n_d
 \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
 |\gamma|\le |r_d|\,|C_d|.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-residue-multiplicity}
 |r_d|\,|C_d|<|\gamma|
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m=d n_d.
\end{equation}
Thus a weak response forces divisibility of the target multiplicity
and identifies the reference order exactly.

\emph{Proof.}
Keep the full reference term and multiply before estimating:
\[
 r_d C_d=\rho(d n_d-m)-(d-1)m,\qquad
 \Im(r_d C_d)=(d n_d-m)\gamma.
\]
If the integer \(d n_d-m\) is nonzero, its absolute value is at least
one. Therefore
\[
 |\gamma|\le |d n_d-m|\,|\gamma|
 =|\Im(r_d C_d)|\le |r_d C_d|=|r_d|\,|C_d|,
\]
which proves both assertions. The norm is applied to the full
compensated residue after its signed reference contribution has been
retained. No separate arithmetic main-term estimate is used.
\(\square\)

Two powers give a joint consequence. Let \(d,e\ge2\) be coprime
integers, apply both compensations to the same \(R\) at the same \(\rho\),
and suppose \(0<m<de\). Then at least one of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-power-response}
 |\gamma|\le |r_d|\,|\rho+d-1|,
 \qquad
 |\gamma|\le |r_e|\,|\rho+e-1|
\end{equation}
holds, irrespective of the two reference orders.
Indeed, if both strict reverse inequalities held,
\eqref{eq:small-residue-multiplicity} would give \(d\mid m\) and
\(e\mid m\). Coprimality would give \(de\mid m\), contrary to
\(0<m<de\). This is a multiplicity restriction from joint weak responses;
it supplies no new upper estimate for those responses.

The maintained declarations are
\texttt{power\_residue\_integer\_separation},
\texttt{power\_residue\_small\_forces\_multiplicity\_relation}, and
\texttt{coprime\_powers\_force\_large\_residue}, under
\texttt{PrimeFactorOscillations.CarmichaelWeightedSignal}.
The companion theorem
\texttt{affinePowerReadout\_integer\_residue\_dichotomy}
takes the two actual scaled-limit premises and produces the compensated
limit with \eqref{eq:integer-residue-alternative}. Their owner is
\texttt{Assembly/CarmichaelPowerSignal.lean}.
No RH, simplicity, or zero-free reference premise is used.
The separate arithmetic upper estimate remains open and the certified
QRH boundary is \(7/8\). No novelty priority is asserted here.

\subsection{Unconditional multiplicity energy survives compensation}
\label{sec:power-multiplicity-energy}

Here the symmetry of the actual zeta zero set gives a global estimate,
rather than only a pointwise residue identity. For \(T\in\mathbb R\), let
\[
 S_T=\{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\quad 0<\Re\rho<1,\quad
                         0<\Im\rho\le T\},
 \qquad m(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta.
\]
The elements of \(S_T\) are distinct zeros; multiplicity is carried by
\(m\), not by repetition in the set. This set is finite unconditionally.
If \(T\le0\), it is empty. Write \(\gamma_\rho=\Im\rho\).

\emph{Theorem (multiplicity-energy coercivity).}
For every real \(d\ge2\), put
\[
 q_d(\rho)=\frac{\rho+d-1}{d},\qquad
 a_\rho=\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho},\qquad
 b_\rho=\frac{d\,m(q_d(\rho))}{\gamma_\rho},
 \qquad E_T=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}a_\rho^2.
\]
At a nonzero value of zeta the analytic order is zero. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-multiplicity-energy}
 \frac{E_T}{12}\le
 H_{d,T}:=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}(a_\rho-b_\rho)^2 .
\end{equation}
There is no RH, zero-free-strip, simplicity, or zero-free-reference
hypothesis.

\emph{Proof.}
Conjugation of zeta and its functional equation preserve analytic
multiplicities and give
\[
 m(1-\overline\rho)=m(\rho),\qquad
 \Im(1-\overline\rho)=\gamma_\rho.
\]
Consider the finite support
\(P=\{\rho\in S_T:m(q_d(\rho))\ne0\}\), and its image
\(Q=q_d(P)\). The map is injective. Every \(\tau=q_d(\rho)\in Q\)
is a zeta zero, with
\[
 \tfrac12<\Re\tau<1,\qquad
 0<\Im\tau=\gamma_\rho/d\le T/d\le T.
\]
Thus \(Q\subseteq S_T\), and its reflection
\(Q^*=\{1-\overline\tau:\tau\in Q\}\) is a disjoint subset of
\(S_T\) with exactly the same multiplicity-height energy. Keeping the
complete reference support, including all reference zeros, gives
\[
 B_{d,T}:=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}b_\rho^2
 =\sum_{\tau\in Q}\frac{m(\tau)^2}{(\Im\tau)^2}
 \le\frac12\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)^2}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 =\frac{E_T}{2}.
\]
The cross term is not estimated separately. Direct expansion gives
the exact certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-energy-certificate}
 12H_{d,T}
 =E_T+2\sum_{\rho\in S_T}(2a_\rho-3b_\rho)^2
       +3(E_T-2B_{d,T}).
\end{equation}
Both retained corrections are nonnegative, proving
\eqref{eq:power-multiplicity-energy}. \(\square\)

This estimate applies to the actual logarithmic readout. Let
\(G:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) be analytic at every point of
\(0<\Re s<1\), and define
\[
 R_G(s)=-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}+G(s),\qquad
 P_dR_G(s)=R_G(s)-\frac{s}{s+d-1}R_G(q_d(s)).
\]
For \(d>1\) and a nonreal \(\rho\) in that strip, local analytic
factorization at both \(\rho\) and \(q_d(\rho)\) gives the punctured limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-power-multiplicity-residue}
 r_d(\rho):=\lim_{\substack{s\to\rho\\s\ne\rho}}
                 (s-\rho)P_dR_G(s)
 =-m(\rho)+\frac{d\rho}{\rho+d-1}m(q_d(\rho)).
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(\zeta(s)=(s-\rho)^m u(s)\), with \(u\) analytic and
nonzero near \(\rho\), gives residue \(-m\) for \(R_G\).
The affine substitution contributes the factor \(d\) at the reference.
Both points remain inside the strip; no analyticity at the pole \(1\)
is required of \(G\). The rational denominator is nonzero because
\(\rho\) is nonreal.

Keeping the full residue and taking one real projection yields
\[
 \frac{\Im(r_d(\rho)(\rho+d-1))}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 =\frac{d\,m(q_d(\rho))-m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho}
 =b_\rho-a_\rho.
\]
Consequently, for every real \(d\ge2\) and \(T\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-power-readout-energy}
 \frac1{12}\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)^2}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \le
 \sum_{\rho\in S_T}
 \left(\frac{\Im(r_d(\rho)(\rho+d-1))}{\gamma_\rho^2}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
The result also holds for any function of residues specified by the
actual punctured limits, by uniqueness of those limits.

Thus a single real projection retains a fixed proportion of the
multiplicity energy, even when target and reference zeros collide.
This is an unconditional lower bound on the detector response, or
equivalently an inverse estimate for multiplicity energy from that
response. An independent upper bound for the right-hand side would
bound the left-hand side. No such new arithmetic upper bound, numerical
multiplicity bound, simplicity theorem, or zero-free improvement is
claimed here. The cutoff \(T\) is a zero-height cutoff, not a sampling
or divisor cutoff. The sums include original zeta zeros only, and do
not assert an identity for every additional reference-only detector
pole. General complex-character portfolios require their own
conjugation and reflection adapters.

\subsection{Signed height weights and surviving multiplicity carriers}
\label{sec:power-weighted-trace}

The squared-energy estimate has a signed linear companion, uniform over
an entire class of height weights. Keep the finite set \(S_T\), the map
\(q_d\), and the actual residues \(r_d\) from
Section~\ref{sec:power-multiplicity-energy}. Let \(d\ge2\), \(T\in\mathbb R\),
\(c\ge0\), and \(w:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-trace-weight}
 w(t)\ge0,\qquad d\,w(dt)\le c\,w(t)\qquad(t>0).
\end{equation}
No continuity or differentiability of \(w\) is required. Define
\[
 A_w=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}m(\rho)w(\gamma_\rho),\quad
 B_w=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}d\,m(q_d(\rho))w(\gamma_\rho),\quad
 H_w=A_w-B_w.
\]
These sums use ordinary multiplicity, rather than its square.

\emph{Theorem (weighted signed trace and carrier).}
Under \eqref{eq:power-trace-weight},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-weighted-trace}
 (2-c)A_w\le2H_w
 =-2\sum_{\rho\in S_T}
       \frac{w(\gamma_\rho)}{\gamma_\rho}
       \Im\bigl((\rho+d-1)r_d(\rho)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Moreover, let
\[
 C_{d,T}=\{\rho\in S_T:
              \Im((\rho+d-1)r_d(\rho))<0\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-weighted-carrier}
 \sum_{\rho\in C_{d,T}}m(\rho)w(\gamma_\rho)
 \ge (1-c/2)A_w.
\end{equation}
The lower proportions are positive when \(c<2\) and \(A_w>0\).
Both assertions are unconditional for the normalized zeta readout
with any correction analytic throughout the open critical strip.

\emph{Proof.}
Retain the complete nonzero reference support
\(P=\{\rho\in S_T:m(q_d(\rho))>0\}\) and \(Q=q_d(P)\).
The previously proved geometry gives an injection onto \(Q\), with
\(Q\) lying in the right half of \(S_T\). Changing variables in the
finite sum gives the exact transport identity
\[
 B_w=d\sum_{\tau\in Q}m(\tau)w(d\gamma_\tau)
       \le c\sum_{\tau\in Q}m(\tau)w(\gamma_\tau).
\]
Reflection supplies a disjoint left copy of the complete right-half
zero set, preserving both multiplicity and height. Nonnegativity
therefore gives
\[
 2\sum_{\tau\in Q}m(\tau)w(\gamma_\tau)\le A_w,\qquad
 2B_w\le cA_w.
\]
No separate norm of the signed trace is introduced: subtract exactly,
\[
 2H_w-(2-c)A_w=cA_w-2B_w\ge0.
\]
The full canonical residue
\eqref{eq:canonical-power-multiplicity-residue} satisfies
\[
 \Im((\rho+d-1)r_d(\rho))
   =\gamma_\rho\bigl(d\,m(q_d(\rho))-m(\rho)\bigr),
\]
which proves the equality in \eqref{eq:power-weighted-trace}.
The actual punctured limits equal these canonical residues by the
proved scaled-limit theorem and uniqueness of limits.

For the carrier assertion, put
\(U=\{\rho\in S_T:m(\rho)\le d\,m(q_d(\rho))\}\).
On this precise support the nonnegative weights give
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in U}m(\rho)w(\gamma_\rho)
 \le\sum_{\rho\in U}d\,m(q_d(\rho))w(\gamma_\rho)
 \le B_w\le(c/2)A_w.
\]
The displayed imaginary-part identity and \(\gamma_\rho>0\) identify
\(C_{d,T}=S_T\setminus U\). Subtracting its complement mass from
\(A_w\) proves \eqref{eq:power-weighted-carrier}. \(\square\)

Two explicit weight choices are checked in Lean. For \(w(t)=1/t\),
the scaling constant is \(c=1\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-height-signed-trace}
 \sum_{\rho\in S_T}
       \frac{m(\rho)-d\,m(q_d(\rho))}{\gamma_\rho}
 \ge\frac12\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho}.
\end{equation}
For \(w(t)=1/t^2\), the scaling constant is \(c=1/d\), giving
\[
 H_{1/t^2}\ge(1-1/(2d))A_{1/t^2},\qquad
 \sum_{\rho\in C_{d,T}}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \ge(1-1/(2d))\sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}.
\]
In particular the last all-\(d\) theorem implies, at \(d=2\), that at
least three quarters of the inverse-square-height multiplicity mass
has the strict desired residue sign. This is a weighted mass fraction,
not an unweighted density of zeros and not a zero-free exponent.

Thus the generalized detector retains a quantitatively large carrier
for one signed real projection, even with reference-zero collisions.
It remains necessary to prove an independent upper estimate for that
response to obtain a numerical multiplicity upper bound or a simplicity
result. No QRH exponent improvement follows from these lower estimates
alone; the installed boundary remains \(7/8\). The finite support remains
the original zero set \(S_T\), with \(T\) a zero-height cutoff.

\subsection{Sharp joint carriers for coprime affine powers}
\label{sec:coprime-power-carrier-sharpness}

For the actual zeta readout, the preceding theorem immediately gives
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in C_{d,T}\cup C_{e,T}}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \ge \left(1-\frac1{2e}\right)
       \sum_{\rho\in S_T}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma_\rho^2}
 \qquad(2\le d<e).
\]
The union counts each zero once, and both residues come from the same
readout. The following construction shows how closely this coefficient
can be approached by finite symmetric spectra, even while every full
complex zero signal survives.

\emph{Theorem (coprime-power spectral sharpness).}
Let \(d,e\) be coprime integers with \(2\le d<e\).
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a finite positive-height spectrum
in the open critical strip, invariant under
\(\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho\), with positive natural multiplicities
preserved by reflection, for which the joint strict-sign carrier of the
\(d\)- and \(e\)-power compensations has inverse-square-height
multiplicity fraction less than
\[
 1-\frac1{2e}+\varepsilon.
\]
The readout is an actual finite logarithmic-derivative sum.
At every spectral point, its full compensated residue is nonzero for
every real power \(c>1\), including when the affine reference is another
spectral point. More precisely, the explicit models below satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-carrier-gap}
 0\le \frac{U_n}{A_n}-\left(1-\frac1{2e}\right)
 \le \frac1e\left(\frac de\right)^n
 \qquad(n\ge0).
\end{equation}

\emph{Proof.}
For \(0\le i,j\le n\), put
\[
 \gamma_{ij}=(e/d)^i e^j,\qquad
 \mu_{ij}=e^{i+j}d^{n-i},\qquad
 \delta_n=\frac1{4\gamma_{nn}},
\]
and take both points
\[
 \rho^R_{ij}=1-\delta_n\gamma_{ij}+\mathrm i\gamma_{ij},
 \qquad
 \rho^L_{ij}=\delta_n\gamma_{ij}+\mathrm i\gamma_{ij},
\]
each with multiplicity \(\mu_{ij}\).
The bases \(e/d\) and \(e\) exceed one, so
\(0<\delta_n\gamma_{ij}\le1/4\). The two rails lie on opposite
sides of the critical line and are reflected copies with equal weights.
Their heights are all distinct: an equality of two heights gives
\[
 e^{i+j}d^k=e^{k+\ell}d^i.
\]
Coprimality first forces \(i=k\); since \(e>1\), it then forces
\(j=\ell\). Thus the displayed atoms have exactly the stated
multiplicities, without hidden collisions. Crucially,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-grid-linear-weight}
 \mu_{ij}=d^n\gamma_{ij}.
\end{equation}

Let \(\mathcal S_n\) be this finite set, extend its multiplicity by zero
outside \(\mathcal S_n\), and define, away from its poles,
\[
 R_n(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal S_n}
                  \frac{\mu(\rho)}{s-\rho},
 \qquad
 R_{n,c}(s)=R_n(s)-\frac{s}{s+c-1}R_n(q_c(s)).
\]
Equivalently, \(R_n\) is the finite weighted sum of negative logarithmic
derivatives of \(s-\rho\). Local factorization, followed by the proved
affine scaled-limit transfer, gives the complete residue
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime-model-residue}
 r_{n,c}(\rho)=
 -\mu(\rho)+\frac{c\rho}{\rho+c-1}\mu(q_c(\rho)).
\end{equation}
This formula includes every target and reference atom.

All reference supports can be determined exactly. For \(c\ge2\), the
reference of a left-rail atom is absent. A right-rail reference cannot
hit the left rail. Indeed, using its imaginary part to fix the target
height and then comparing real parts would force, respectively,
\(\delta_n\gamma_{ij}=1/2\) or
\(\delta_n\gamma_{ij}=c/2\), contrary to the quarter bound
(the left-to-left case would require \(c=1\)).
On the right rail,
\[
 q_e(\rho^R_{i,j+1})=\rho^R_{ij},\qquad
 q_d(\rho^R_{i,j+1})=\rho^R_{i+1,j}.
\]
Height injectivity proves that these are the only possible matches.
Consequently an \(e\)-reference is present exactly when \(j>0\),
and a \(d\)-reference exactly when \(i<n\) and \(j>0\).

Whenever a reference is present, its height is \(\gamma_\rho/c\);
\eqref{eq:coprime-grid-linear-weight} therefore gives
\(c\mu(q_c(\rho))=\mu(\rho)\). Hence the exact selected projection is
\[
 Y_c(\rho):=\Im\bigl((\rho+c-1)r_{n,c}(\rho)\bigr)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 0,&q_c(\rho)\in\mathcal S_n,\\
 -\mu(\rho)\gamma_\rho,&q_c(\rho)\notin\mathcal S_n.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the two specified powers the common zero-projection set is exactly
\(\{\rho^R_{ij}:i<n,\ 1\le j\le n\}\).
All other atoms belong to the joint strict-negative carrier.
The full residue has not vanished on the common set: when a reference
is present, \eqref{eq:coprime-model-residue} becomes
\[
 r_{n,c}(\rho)=-\frac{(c-1)\mu(\rho)}{\rho+c-1}\ne0.
\]
When it is absent, the residue is \(-\mu(\rho)\ne0\).
This nonvanishing argument works for every \(c>1\).

Set \(a=d/e\), \(b=1/e\), and
\(G_k(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}x^r\).
Each atom has mass
\[
 \frac{\mu_{ij}}{\gamma_{ij}^2}=d^n a^i b^j.
\]
Keeping both rails and every boundary row, the total mass and the mass
missing from the joint carrier are therefore
\[
 A_n=2d^nG_{n+1}(a)G_{n+1}(b)>0,\qquad
 W_n=d^nG_n(a)\bigl(G_{n+1}(b)-1\bigr).
\]
Thus \(U_n=A_n-W_n\) and
\[
 \frac{U_n}{A_n}
 =1-\frac{G_n(a)\bigl(G_{n+1}(b)-1\bigr)}
            {2G_{n+1}(a)G_{n+1}(b)}.
\]
For completeness, put
\(x=a^n/G_{n+1}(a)\) and
\(y=b^{n+1}/G_{n+1}(b)\).
The finite geometric identities give the exact gap
\[
 \frac{U_n}{A_n}-\left(1-\frac b2\right)
 =\frac{b x+(1-x)y}{2}.
\]
Since \(0<b\le a<1\), both geometric blocks are at least one,
\(0\le x\le1\), and \(0\le y\le b a^n\).
It follows that the gap lies between zero and \(b a^n\).
This proves \eqref{eq:coprime-carrier-gap}; the geometric decay supplies
the required \(n\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\). \(\square\)

The construction shows that adding a second coprime power cannot force
a uniformly larger joint sign-carrier fraction from reflection and
natural multiplicities alone in this finite spectral class.
It also separates loss of one chosen real projection from loss of the
full multiplicity signal. Additional arithmetic information can exclude these spectra. In particular,
the rightmost rail tends to \(\Re\rho=1\), so the established \(7/8\)
strip excludes long grids as zeta models. No Euler-product realization
is claimed. The result identifies the scope of the reflection-only
carrier argument; it supplies no new numerical upper bound on zero
multiplicity and leaves the certified QRH boundary at \(7/8\).

\subsection{A zero strip strengthens joint multiplicity carriers}
\label{sec:strip-joint-carriers}

The reflection-only sharpness theorem permits zeros arbitrarily close to
the edge of the critical strip. A known smaller zero strip supplies an
additional constraint. Two reference maps couple that constraint to
multiplicity mass, including when the powers are not coprime.

Fix \(T>0\), and let
\[
 S_T=\{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ 0<\Re\rho<1,\ 0<\Im\rho\le T\},
 \qquad m(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta,\qquad
 w(\rho)=\frac{m(\rho)}{(\Im\rho)^2}.
\]
All sums below run over explicitly specified finite sets. Write
\(A_T=\sum_{\rho\in S_T}w(\rho)\), and, for real \(d,e\ge2\), set
\[
 \begin{split}
 U_{d,e}(T)&=\{\rho\in S_T:
 m(\rho)\le d\,m(q_d(\rho)),\
 m(\rho)\le e\,m(q_e(\rho))\},\\
 \mathcal C_{d,e}(T)&=S_T\setminus U_{d,e}(T),\qquad
 q_a(s)=\frac{s+a-1}{a}.
 \end{split}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C_{d,e}(T)\) is a union, and an overlap is counted once.

\paragraph{Theorem (Strip-dependent joint carrier mass).}
\label{thm:strip-joint-carrier}
Let \(2\le d<e\), \(1/2\le\sigma<1\), and suppose every
\(\rho\in S_T\) with \(\Re\rho>1/2\) satisfies \(\Re\rho\le\sigma\).
Put \(r=e/d\). If \(L\ge1\) is an integer such that
\[
 r^L(1-\sigma)\ge\frac12,\qquad H_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^j,
\]
then
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal C_{d,e}(T)}w(\rho)
 \ \ge\
 \left(1-\frac{1-H_L^{-1}}{2e}\right)A_T.
\]
No RH or simplicity assumption is made. In particular, coprimality of
\(d,e\) is not required.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Let \(R_T=\{\tau\in S_T:\Re\tau>1/2\}\), and define functions on
the entire complex plane by zero extension:
\[
 b(\tau)=
 \begin{cases}w(\tau),&\tau\in R_T,\\0,&\tau\notin R_T,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 c(\tau)=
 \begin{cases}
 e\,w(s_e(\tau)),&s_e(\tau)\in U_{d,e}(T),\\
 0,&s_e(\tau)\notin U_{d,e}(T),
 \end{cases}
 \quad s_e(\tau)=e\tau-(e-1).
\]
If \(\rho\in U_{d,e}(T)\), positivity of \(m(\rho)\) forces both
reference multiplicities to be positive. For \(a=d,e\), the reference
\(q_a(\rho)\) lies in \(R_T\): its real part is greater than \(1/2\)
and less than \(1\), and its height is \(\Im\rho/a\le T\).
The corresponding weak inequality gives the exact weighted comparison
\[
 a\,w(\rho)\le w(q_a(\rho)).
\]
For \(a=e\) this proves \(0\le c\le b\), including outside \(R_T\).

The other reference map must be retained at the same source. Its
transport on the \(e\)-reference coordinate is
\[
 M(\tau)=q_d(s_e(\tau))=1-r(1-\tau).
\]
The \(d\)-comparison gives
\[
 c(\tau)\le r\,b(M(\tau))
\]
on the whole complex plane. Set \(D=b-c\). Both \(c\) and \(D\) are
nonnegative and supported on \(R_T\). In particular, the complete signed
recurrence is
\[
 b(\tau)\le D(\tau)+r\,b(M(\tau)).
\]
Iteration, with every term retained, yields
\[
 b(\tau)\le
 \sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^jD(M^j(\tau))
 +r^Lb(M^L(\tau)).
\]
For \(\tau\in R_T\),
\[
 \Re M^L(\tau)=1-r^L(1-\Re\tau)
 \le1-r^L(1-\sigma)\le\frac12,
\]
so the terminal term is zero. Each \(M^j\) is injective, and \(D\)
vanishes outside the finite set \(R_T\). Consequently
\[
 \sum_{\tau\in R_T}D(M^j(\tau))
 \le\sum_{\tau\in R_T}D(\tau).
\]
Writing \(B=\sum_{\tau\in R_T}b(\tau)\) and
\(C=\sum_{\tau\in R_T}c(\tau)\), summation gives
\[
 B\le H_L(B-C),\qquad C\le(1-H_L^{-1})B.
\]

Reflection \(\rho\mapsto1-\overline{\rho}\) preserves height and
multiplicity, pairs the right and left half-planes, and leaves
nonnegative mass on the critical line. Thus \(2B\le A_T\).
The map \(q_e\) is injective, is inverse to \(s_e\), and sends
\(U_{d,e}(T)\) into \(R_T\). The definition of \(c\) therefore gives
\[
 C=e\sum_{\rho\in U_{d,e}(T)}w(\rho).
\]
Since \(H_L\ge1\), these inequalities imply
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in U_{d,e}(T)}w(\rho)
 \le\frac{1-H_L^{-1}}{2e}A_T.
\]
Subtract this finite mass from \(A_T\).
\hfill\textit{QED.}

The statement applies to the actual compensated residues. For a
correction analytic throughout the critical strip, the previously proved
local identity gives
\[
 \Im\!\bigl(r_a(\rho)(\rho+a-1)\bigr)
   =\bigl(a\,m(q_a(\rho))-m(\rho)\bigr)\Im\rho.
\]
Thus membership in \(\mathcal C_{d,e}(T)\) is exactly strict negativity
of at least one of these two projected residues. Both reference
multiplicities and every common-reference overlap remain in the proof.

\paragraph{Theorem (Unconditional fixed-power consequences).}
\label{thm:strip-carrier-seven-eighths}
For every \(T>0\), actual zeta compensated readouts satisfy
\[
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal C_{2,3}(T)}w(\rho)\ge\frac{111}{130}A_T,
 \qquad
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal C_{2,4}(T)}w(\rho)\ge\frac{11}{12}A_T.
\]
\paragraph{Proof.}
Use the installed unconditional nonvanishing theorem for
\(\Re s>7/8\), so \(\sigma=7/8\) is admissible.
For \((d,e)=(2,3)\), take \(L=4\). Then
\[
 (3/2)^4(1/8)=81/128\ge1/2,\qquad H_4=65/8,
\]
and the carrier coefficient is \(111/130\).
For \((d,e)=(2,4)\), take \(L=2\). Then
\(2^2(1/8)=1/2\), \(H_2=3\), and the coefficient is \(11/12\).
\hfill\textit{QED.}

The reflection-only guarantees for these fixed pairs are respectively
\(5/6\) and \(7/8\). The new coefficients are larger by \(4/195\)
and \(1/24\). There is no conflict with the earlier finite-model
sharpness theorem: its long models approach \(\Re\rho=1\), whereas
the present theorem uses a fixed upper strip. This is a quantitative
improvement in multiplicity mass retained by specified observables.
It proves neither a smaller zero-free boundary nor an upper bound on
individual multiplicities. The geometric transport summation is
elementary; publication priority for this particular coupled
compensated-residue application has not been established.

\subsection{Optimal fixed-strip quadratic and quartic carriers}
\label{sec:dyadic-fixed-strip-sharpness}

The coefficient \(11/12\) has an exact finite-model interpretation.
It is optimal using only a fixed zero strip, reflection, positive natural
multiplicities and the full quadratic and quartic compensation references.
The models below are finite rational logarithmic readouts; they are not
asserted to be zero sets of zeta or of an Euler product.

Let \(S\) be a finite set of distinct points \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\), with
\(\gamma>0\), \(1/8\le\beta\le7/8\), and positive natural multiplicities
\(m(\rho)\). Suppose \(1-\overline\rho\in S\) with the same multiplicity.
Extend \(m\) by zero outside \(S\), and put
\[
 A=\sum_{\rho\in S}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma^2},\qquad
 U=\{\rho\in S:m(\rho)\le2m(q_2(\rho)),\
                       m(\rho)\le4m(q_4(\rho))\},
 \qquad C=\sum_{\rho\in S\setminus U}\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma^2}.
\]
Every summation here has the displayed finite support. For the actual
rational readout \(R(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in S}m(\rho)/(s-\rho)\), write
\[
 P_a(s)=R(s)-\frac{s}{s+a-1}R(q_a(s)),\qquad
 r_a(\rho)=\lim_{s\to\rho}(s-\rho)P_a(s).
\]
Its complete residue is
\[
 r_a(\rho)=-m(\rho)+\frac{a\rho}{\rho+a-1}m(q_a(\rho)).
\]
Consequently \(S\setminus U\) is exactly the union of the strict-negative
projected residue carriers for \(a=2,4\).

\paragraph{Theorem (Optimal fixed-strip carrier coefficient).}
\label{thm:dyadic-fixed-strip-optimality}
Every such spectrum satisfies \(C\ge(11/12)A\).
Conversely, for every \(\epsilon>0\) there is a finite spectrum already
inside \(1/7\le\Re\rho\le6/7\), with \(A>0\), such that
\[
 \frac CA<\frac{11}{12}+\epsilon.
\]
The witnesses have positive natural multiplicities and actual atomic
readouts. Every one of their full complex residues is nonzero for every
real compensation power \(a>1\). Thus a real coefficient \(c\) satisfies
\(C\ge cA\) for all spectra in the stated class if and only if
\(c\le11/12\).

\paragraph{Proof of the universal bound.}
Write \(W=\sum_{\rho\in U}m(\rho)/\gamma^2\), so \(A=W+C\).
For \(\rho\in U\), the positive multiplicity forces both reference
points to belong to \(S\). The upper bound on \(q_4(\rho)\) gives
\(\beta\le1/2\). Hence
\[
 9/16\le\Re q_2(\rho)\le3/4,\qquad
 25/32\le\Re q_4(\rho)\le7/8.
\]
Both reference maps are injective and their images of \(U\) are disjoint.
For \(a=2,4\), their actual masses satisfy
\[
 \frac{m(q_a(\rho))}{(\gamma/a)^2}
 \ge a\,\frac{m(\rho)}{\gamma^2}.
\]
Thus the union of these images has mass at least \(6W\).
It lies strictly to the right of \(1/2\), so its reflected image is
disjoint, has the same mass and also belongs to \(S\).
Therefore \(A\ge12W\). Substituting \(A=W+C\) proves \(C\ge11A/12\).
No reference image or reflection overlap has been counted twice.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

\paragraph{Construction and complete reference classification.}
Use words in the two letters \(2,4\). The root and child coordinates are
\[
 x_{\varnothing}=1/4,\qquad
 x_{2w}=(1-x_w)/2,\qquad x_{4w}=(1-x_w)/4.
\]
All \(x_w\) lie in \([1/7,3/7]\); the two child images lie respectively
in \([2/7,3/7]\) and \([1/7,3/14]\). They are disjoint, and the root
lies strictly in the intervening gap. Induction on words therefore
proves injectivity across all finite lengths, including the empty word.

Fix a depth \(N\ge1\) and any \(H>0\). Let \(s(w)\) count a letter \(2\)
with weight one and a letter \(4\) with weight two. For every word of
length at most \(N\), include the two points
\[
 L_w=x_w+iH2^{-s(w)},\qquad
 R_w=1-x_w+iH2^{-s(w)},\qquad
 m(L_w)=m(R_w)=2^{\,2N-s(w)}.
\]
Since \(s(w)\le2|w|\le2N\), every multiplicity is a positive natural
number. The two rails are disjoint, all points are distinct, and their
real parts lie in \([1/7,6/7]\).

The full reference table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 \text{source}&q_2\text{ reference}&q_4\text{ reference}\\ \hline
 L_w,\ |w|<N&R_{2w}&R_{4w}\\
 L_w,\ |w|=N&\text{absent}&\text{absent}\\
 R_{2w}&R_{4w}&\text{absent}\\
 R_{\varnothing},\ R_{4w}&\text{absent}&\text{absent}.
\end{array}
\]
The child identities follow directly from the coordinates and heights.
Injectivity excludes any replacement reference when a child lies beyond
the depth cutoff. No reference hits the left rail. For the other absent
cases, \(q_2(R_{\varnothing})\) has real part \(7/8>6/7\);
\(q_2(R_{4w})\) and every \(q_4(R_w)\) have real part at least
\(25/28>6/7\). The right-child quadratic collision is present:
\(q_2(R_{2w})=R_{4w}\), with
\(m(R_{2w})=2m(R_{4w})\). Its quadratic projection vanishes, but its
quartic reference is absent. The internal left multiplicities satisfy
\(m(L_w)=2m(R_{2w})=4m(R_{4w})\).
It follows that \(U\) is exactly the nonterminal left nodes.

\paragraph{Exact masses and sharpness.}
Put \(b_N=2^{2N}/H^2>0\). Each point at word \(w\) has mass
\(b_N2^{s(w)}\). The sum of the word weights at level \(k\) is \(6^k\).
Writing \(T_N=\sum_{k=0}^N6^k=(6^{N+1}-1)/5\), we obtain
\[
 A_N=2b_NT_N,\qquad W_N=b_NT_{N-1},\qquad C_N=A_N-W_N,
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-fixed-strip-ratio}
 \frac{C_N}{A_N}
 =\frac{11}{12}+\frac{5}{12(6^{N+1}-1)}.
\end{equation}
The final term tends to zero, uniformly in every \(H>0\).
The formal epsilon witness can alternatively use the elementary bound
\(T_N\ge N+1\); no infinite spectrum or interchange of limits is required.

Finally, the actual atomic readout has the displayed local scaled limits.
For every \(a>1\) and every point \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) of the model,
\[
 \Im\!\left(r_a(\rho)(\rho+a-1)\overline\rho\right)
   =m(\rho)(a-1)\gamma>0.
\]
Thus its full residue cannot vanish, including at every cancelled
projected row in the table. This proves the asserted realization and
completes the sharpness proof.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

\texttt{CarmichaelDyadicSharpness.universal\_carrier\_coefficient\_iff},
\texttt{exists\_depth\_carrier\_ratio\_lt}, and
\texttt{treeSpectrum\_joint\_readout\_carrier}.
The exact formula is
\texttt{treeSpectrum\_carrier\_ratio\_closed}; its Lean depth is \(N+1\)
for a natural parameter \(N\), so its exponent is \(N+2\).
The finite lower-bound owner is

This settles optimality within the stated finite-spectrum class.
Additional arithmetic information about actual zeta zeros may still

remains \(7/8\). Publication priority for this particular finite

\subsection{Multiplicity-sensitive carriers and actual zeta certificates}
\label{sec:multiplicity-sensitive-carriers}

The complete complex readout recovers multiplicity even when its affine
reference is another zero. The projected carrier asks a different question:
how much weighted zero mass has a negative signed projection in at least one
of the quadratic and quartic rows? The following estimate quantifies how
excess multiplicity can reduce that projected mass.

Let \(S=\{\rho_i:i\in I\}\) be a finite injectively indexed spectrum with
positive natural multiplicities \(m_i\), positive heights
\(\gamma_i=\Im\rho_i\), and real parts in \([1/8,7/8]\).
Assume reflection \(\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho\) preserves the spectrum
and multiplicities. Extend the atomic order \(m(z)\) by zero off \(S\).
With \(q_d(\rho)=(\rho+d-1)/d\), define the finite sets and masses
\[
 U=\{i\in I:m_i\le2m(q_2(\rho_i)),\
                   m_i\le4m(q_4(\rho_i))\},\qquad V=I\setminus U,
\]
\[
 A=\sum_{i\in I}\frac{m_i}{\gamma_i^2},\quad
 C=\sum_{i\in V}\frac{m_i}{\gamma_i^2},\quad
 P_U=\sum_{i\in U}\frac1{\gamma_i^2},\quad
 E_U=\sum_{i\in U}\frac{m_i-1}{\gamma_i^2}.
\]
Every displayed sum is over its indicated finite support.

\paragraph{Theorem (multiplicity-sensitive carrier bound).}
For every such spectrum,
\[
 A\ge40P_U,\qquad
 C\ge\max\left\{\frac{11}{12}A,\frac{39}{40}A-E_U\right\}.
\]
In particular, if all multiplicities are one then \(C\ge39A/40\).
If \(C<39A/40\), some \(i\in U\) has \(m_i\ge2\).
No simplicity or RH hypothesis is used in the general estimate.
The simple-spectrum coefficient is optimal in this finite model class;
Section~\ref{sec:simple-carrier-sharpness} proves sharpness.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For \(i\in U\), positivity of \(m_i\) forces both actual reference
orders to be positive. Hence the reference points belong to \(S\),
and their natural multiplicities are at least one. The established
weak-row geometry places every source in \(\Re\rho_i\le1/2\).
The quadratic reference image lies in \(1/2<\Re z\le3/4\);
the quartic reference image lies in \(\Re z\ge25/32\).
Both maps are injective and these images are disjoint.
Since \(\Im q_d(\rho_i)=\gamma_i/d\), their combined mass is at least
\[
 \sum_{i\in U}\left(\frac4{\gamma_i^2}
                         +\frac{16}{\gamma_i^2}\right)=20P_U.
\]
Their reflected copies have equal mass and lie strictly to the left
of \(1/2\), so are disjoint from both right-hand images. Thus
\(A\ge40P_U\). The exact finite partition gives
\(A=C+P_U+E_U\), whence \(C\ge39A/40-E_U\).
Combine this with the preceding independently proved \(11A/12\) bound.
All excess summands are nonnegative; if every weak atom were simple
then \(E_U=0\), contradicting a strict deficit.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

\paragraph{Theorem (unconditional actual-zeta specialization).}
For every real \(T\), take the actual finite set
\[
 S_T=\{\rho:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ 0<\Re\rho<1,\ 0<\Im\rho\le T\}
\]
with \(m(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_{\rho}\zeta\).
The same max bound and repeated-weak-zero certificate hold, with both
reference orders evaluated in the actual zeta function.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The established unconditional seven-eighths theorem and reflection put
\(S_T\) in \([1/8,7/8]\). Reflection preserves both heights and analytic
multiplicities, which are positive natural numbers at these zeros.
For \(d\ge2\), if the actual order at \(q_d(\rho)\) is positive,
the proved power-map window theorem places that reference inside \(S_T\).
Injectivity of the indexing then makes its atomic order equal to its
actual zeta order. If that order is zero, no indexed zero can equal the
reference, and its atomic order is also zero.
Thus the complete reference orders agree in every case.
Transporting the finite sums through the subtype indexing proves the claim.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

To state the observable version, let \(G\) be analytic on
\(0<\Re s<1\), and let \(r_2(\rho),r_4(\rho)\) be the local scaled limits
of the corresponding affine compensated readouts of
\(\operatorname{zetaMultiplicityReadout}(G)\).
The local-limit hypotheses are retained explicitly in the Lean statement.
The exact residue identity gives
\[
 \Im\bigl(r_d(\rho)(\rho+d-1)\bigr)
   =\bigl(d\,m(q_d(\rho))-m(\rho)\bigr)\Im\rho.
\]
Consequently \(C\) is precisely the mass on the union of the two
negative-projection supports, and the same max bound holds for that
observable carrier. Theorem
\texttt{zeta\_readout\_carrier\_max\_bound} formalizes this transfer.

The certificate is one-sided: it provides a lower bound for weak excess
when the carrier has a deficit, not an upper bound for multiplicities.
Under RH the affine references of nontrivial zeros are not zeros, so
\(U\) is empty and \(C=A\), regardless of whether a critical-line zero
is multiple. Thus this carrier test alone does not settle simplicity.
It complements the full complex residue recovery and the existing
RH-dependent second-moment multiplicity budget.

The maintained owners are \texttt{CarmichaelMultiplicityCarrier},
\texttt{CarmichaelZetaFiniteSpectrum}, and
\texttt{CarmichaelZetaMultiplicityCarrier}. Their hypotheses and proofs
retain every reference order, finite-window endpoint and compensation term.

\subsection{Sharp simple-spectrum carriers}
\label{sec:simple-carrier-sharpness}

The preceding \(39/40\) coefficient is optimal over the stated finite
simple-spectrum class. This is a statement about complete atomic readouts,
not a claim that zeta zeros realize the examples.

\paragraph{Theorem (exact universal simple coefficient).}
For a real number \(c\), the following are equivalent:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{every finite reflected spectrum in }[1/8,7/8]
 \text{ with }m_i=1\text{ satisfies }cA\le C;\\
 c\le39/40.
 \end{gathered}
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), a finite depth can be chosen independently
of every positive root height \(H\), giving a complete simple atomic
readout with \(A>0\) and \(C/A<39/40+\varepsilon\).
All its points already lie in \([1/7,6/7]\), and its full complex
compensated residues are nonzero for every real \(d>1\).

\paragraph{Construction and exact finite support.}
For a finite word \(w\) in \(0,1\), let
\[
 x_{\varnothing}=\frac14,\quad
 x_{0w}=\frac{1-x_w}{2},\quad
 x_{1w}=\frac{1-x_w}{4},\qquad
 s(\varnothing)=0,\quad s(0w)=s(w)+1,\quad s(1w)=s(w)+2.
\]
At depth \(N+1\), retain both points
\[
 L_w=x_w+iH2^{-s(w)},\qquad
 R_w=1-x_w+iH2^{-s(w)}
       \quad (|w|\le N+1),
\]
each with multiplicity exactly one. The established word geometry makes
this entire finite indexing injective, including different word lengths;
it gives \(1/7\le x_w\le3/7\) and reflection interchanging the two rails.
Thus every point belongs to \([1/7,6/7]\) and has positive height.

For a nonterminal left point the actual references are
\[
 q_2(L_w)=R_{0w},\qquad q_4(L_w)=R_{1w}.
\]
Both reference orders are one. For a terminal left point the child words
are outside the finite support and both reference orders are zero.
For every right point,
\(\Re q_4(R_w)=1-x_w/4\ge25/28>6/7\), so its quartic reference order
is zero. Hence the common weak set is exactly
\[
 U=\{L_w:|w|\le N\}.
\]
The right quadratic row, including its possible reference collisions,
remains in the actual atomic readout. Its value cannot remove a right
point from the union carrier because that point's quartic signed
projection is already negative.

\paragraph{Exact mass and sharpness proof.}
Put
\[
 T_n=\sum_{|w|\le n}4^{s(w)}.
\]
The root and the two disjoint child supports give
\[
 T_0=1,\qquad T_{n+1}=1+(4+16)T_n=1+20T_n,\qquad
 19T_n+1=20^{n+1}.
\]
All sums here are over the indicated finite word support.
With every multiplicity one, \(E_U=0\), and the complete two-rail and
common-weak masses are
\[
 A=\frac{2T_{N+1}}{H^2}>0,\qquad W=\frac{T_N}{H^2},\qquad C=A-W.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac CA
 =1-\frac{T_N}{2T_{N+1}}
 =\frac{39}{40}+\frac1{40T_{N+1}}
 =\frac{39}{40}
       +\frac{19}{40(20^{N+2}-1)}.
\]
No term from the finite carrier or compensation has been omitted.
The denominator is positive. Also \(T_n\ge n+1\); choosing
\(N>1/(40\varepsilon)\) makes the correction smaller than
\(\varepsilon\), uniformly in \(H>0\).
Thus every coefficient strictly larger than \(39/40\) fails on some
finite simple spectrum. The converse is the preceding simple-spectrum
lower bound, together with nonnegativity of \(A\).

Finally use the complete atomic readout
\[
 R(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in S}\frac1{s-\rho}.
\]
Its scaled local limits give the actual target and reference orders.
The proved nonreal residue identity preserves a nonzero full complex
residue for every \(d>1\). Its quadratic-quartic negative-projection
union is exactly the carrier used above, so the examples are readout
realizations of the sharp constant.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

The unrestricted positive-natural-multiplicity class has sharp
coefficient \(11/12\); the all-simple subclass has sharp coefficient
\(39/40\). The excess-multiplicity certificate quantifies the difference.
These finite-model optimality statements provide no new upper bound on
actual zeta multiplicities and do not change the established \(7/8\)
zero-free boundary.

The five maintained owners are \texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeWeights},
\texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeSpectrum}, \texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeRows},
\texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeMass}, and \texttt{CarmichaelSimpleTreeSharpness}.
The exact universal statement is
\texttt{universal\_simple\_carrier\_coefficient\_iff}.

\subsection{Sharp multiplicity-support region and triple-zero certificates}
\label{sec:multiplicity-support-region}

Keep the finite reflected spectra in the closed strip
\([1/8,7/8]\), with positive heights and positive natural multiplicities.
Reference orders are the actual atomic orders, including zero for an absent
reference. Let \(U\) be the common-weak set for the complete powers \(2,4\);
thus \(m_\rho\le2m_{q_2(\rho)}\) and
\(m_\rho\le4m_{q_4(\rho)}\). All sums below are over the indicated finite
support. Define
\[
 P=\sum_{\rho\in U}\frac1{\gamma_\rho^2},\qquad
 Q=\sum_{\substack{\rho\in U\\m_\rho\ge2}}\frac1{\gamma_\rho^2},\qquad
 T_3=\sum_{\rho\in U}\frac{(m_\rho-2)_+}{\gamma_\rho^2},
 \qquad (t)_+=\max(t,0).
\]
Write \(A=\sum_{\rho\in S}m_\rho/\gamma_\rho^2\) and
\(C=\sum_{\rho\in S\setminus U}m_\rho/\gamma_\rho^2\).
Then \(0\le Q\le P\), \(E_U=Q+T_3\), and
\[
 A=C+P+Q+T_3.
\]

\paragraph{Theorem (complete affine support region).}
For real \(a,b\), the inequality \(aP+bQ\le C\) holds for every such
finite reflected spectrum if and only if
\[
 \boxed{\quad a\le39,\qquad a+b\le40.\quad}
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-multiplicity-support}
 \boxed{\quad C\ge39P+Q.\quad}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triple-excess-carrier}
 \boxed{\quad C+\frac{20}{21}T_3\ge\frac{20}{21}A.\quad}
\end{equation}
The coefficient \(20/21\) is optimal both in
\(c(A-T_3)\le C\) on the full class and in \(cA\le C\) when every
common-weak multiplicity is at most two. Multiplicities on the carrier
itself need not be bounded.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Index the atoms by \(i\), write \(i^*\) for reflection, and set
\[
 u_i=\gamma_i^{-2},\quad w_i=m_i u_i,\quad e_i=(m_i-1)u_i,\quad
 D_i=e_i+\mathbf1_{\{m_i\ge2\}}u_i.
\]
For \(m_i=1\), \(D_i=0\); for \(m_i\ge2\), \(D_i=w_i\).
Hence
\[
 0\le D_i\le w_i,\qquad D_i\le2e_i,\qquad D_{i^*}=D_i.
\]
Retain both compensation charges by defining the real weight
\[
 F_i=w_i-\frac12\mathbf1_U(i)D_i
          -\frac12\mathbf1_U(i^*)D_i.
\]
Both deductions are at most \(D_i/2\), so \(F_i\ge0\).
Reflection interchanges the two deductions, so \(F_{i^*}=F_i\), and
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}F_i=A-E_U-Q.
\]
This remains valid at a reflection-fixed atom: the two half charges add
to the full charge. The weight may vanish and is not asserted to define
a positive-natural-multiplicity spectrum.

Every common-weak atom lies at real part at most \(1/2\), by the existing
complete-reference argument. Therefore, at a point with real part \(>1/2\),
the first charge is absent, and
\[
 F_i\ge w_i-\frac12D_i\ge w_i-e_i=u_i.
\]
Let \(B=q_2(U)\cup q_4(U)\), interpreted through the actual reference
indices. Both maps are injective on \(U\); their images lie respectively
in \([9/16,3/4]\) and \([25/32,7/8]\), so they are disjoint and strictly
right of \(1/2\). Their heights are \(\gamma/2,\gamma/4\). Thus
\[
 \sum_{i\in B}F_i\ge\sum_{i\in B}u_i=(4+16)P=20P.
\]
The sets \(B,B^*\) are disjoint. Nonnegativity and reflection symmetry
give
\[
 A-E_U-Q=\sum_{i\in S}F_i
 \ge2\sum_{i\in B}F_i\ge40P.
\]
Substitute \(A=C+P+E_U\) to obtain
\eqref{eq:joint-multiplicity-support}. No compensation cross charge or
reference order has been discarded. If \(a\le39\) and \(a+b\le40\), then
\[
 aP+bQ=a(P-Q)+(a+b)Q
 \le39(P-Q)+40Q=39P+Q\le C.
\]

For necessity of \(a\le39\), restrict to the simple spectra of
Section~\ref{sec:simple-carrier-sharpness}. There \(Q=0\) and \(A=C+P\).
If \(a>39\), then \(a+1>0\), and \(aP\le C\) would imply
\[
 C\ge\frac{a}{a+1}A,\qquad \frac{a}{a+1}>\frac{39}{40},
\]
contradicting the proved simple-spectrum optimality.

The second face has an exact finite witness. For every \(H>0\), take
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 \rho&\frac12+iH&
       \frac14+iH/2&\frac34+iH/2&
       \frac18+iH/4&\frac78+iH/4\\ \hline
 m_\rho&2&1&1&1&1 .
 \end{array}
\]
Reflection fixes the center and exchanges each displayed pair.
The center has quadratic and quartic reference orders one. The right
point \(3/4+iH/2\) has quadratic reference \(7/8+iH/4\), of order one.
All other quadratic references and all other quartic references are
absent, as their real coordinates show. Thus the common-weak set is
exactly the center, and
\[
 P=Q=E_U=\frac1{H^2},\qquad T_3=0,\qquad
 A=\frac{42}{H^2},\qquad C=\frac{40}{H^2}.
\]
The inequality \(aP+bQ\le C\) therefore forces \(a+b\le40\).

Finally, \(Q\le P\) gives
\(39P+Q\ge20(P+Q)\). Using \(A=C+P+Q+T_3\) yields
\(21C+20T_3\ge20A\), proving \eqref{eq:triple-excess-carrier}.
The five-point model has \(C/A=20/21\), \(T_3=0\), and weak multiplicity
two, so neither universal coefficient can be enlarged.
For smaller real coefficients, \(A-T_3=C+P+Q\ge0\) justifies the
monotone coefficient comparison, including negative coefficients.

These are full readout examples. The complete atomic readout is
\(R(s)=-\sum_{\rho\in S}m_\rho/(s-\rho)\). Its actual scaled limits
retain every reference multiplicity, and its full complex residue is
nonzero at every atom for every real \(d>1\).
At the center the quadratic scalar projection vanishes, but the full
quadratic residue is \(-2/(\rho+1)\ne0\); the two facts are compatible.
\hfill\textit{QED.}

For each actual zeta window \(V_T\) used above, define \(P,Q,T_3\) by
the same finite sums with actual analytic orders. The existing
unconditional \(7/8\) strip and reflection theorem supply precisely
the finite-spectrum hypotheses, and complete transformed orders agree
with the finite model because the transformed height decreases.
Consequently \eqref{eq:joint-multiplicity-support} and
\eqref{eq:triple-excess-carrier} hold for actual zeta zeros without RH.
In particular,
\[
 C<\frac{20}{21}A
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{some common-weak zeta zero in }V_T\text{ has multiplicity at least }3.
\]
The complete compensated local-limit identities identify the same
carrier with the union of the two strict negative residue projections.
The formal readout theorem retains the analytic regular part and both
local-limit hypotheses.

The exact classification is
\texttt{universal\_affine\_multiplicity\_support\_iff};
the two optimal scalar coefficients are
\texttt{universal\_triple\_compensated\_coefficient\_iff} and
\texttt{universal\_double\_weak\_carrier\_coefficient\_iff}.
The actual-zeta certificate is
\texttt{zeta\_triple\_weak\_zero\_of\_deficit}.
Finite-model sharpness does not assert realization of the five points
as actual zeta zeros, prove any multiple zeta zero, or supply an upper
multiplicity bound. The certified zero-free boundary remains \(7/8\).
The limited primary-literature comparison does not establish publication
priority for this coefficient-region theorem.

\subsection{Sharp fifth-multiplicity stability}
\label{sec:fifth-multiplicity-stability}

For the same finite reflected spectra and common-weak set \(U\), define
\[
 R_5=\sum_{\substack{\rho\in U\\m_\rho\ge5}}\frac1{\gamma_\rho^2},
 \qquad \Delta=C-39P-Q.
\]
Every sum is over the displayed finite support. The quantity \(R_5\)
counts each zero of order at least five once, with inverse-square-height
weight.

\paragraph{Theorem (optimal fifth-tail coefficient).}
For real \(c\), the inequality
\[
 39P+Q+cR_5\le C
\]
holds for every finite reflected positive-natural-multiplicity spectrum
in \([1/8,7/8]\) if and only if \(c\le4\). In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fifth-tail-stability}
 \boxed{\qquad \Delta\ge4R_5.\qquad}
\end{equation}
Equality in the preceding joint support bound, \(\Delta=0\), forces
every common-weak multiplicity to be at most four.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Use the exact charged weight \(F_i\) of
Section~\ref{sec:multiplicity-support-region}, retaining both half
charges. At a right-hand point the atom itself is not common-weak, and
\(D_i\le w_i\); hence, in addition to \(F_i\ge u_i\), we have
\[
 F_i\ge w_i-\frac12D_i\ge\frac12w_i.
\]
If a common-weak source \(i\) has \(m_i\ge5\), its quadratic reference
has integer multiplicity \(m_j\) satisfying \(5\le m_i\le2m_j\).
Thus \(m_j\ge3\), and its height is \(\gamma_i/2\), giving
\[
 F_j\ge\frac32\,\gamma_j^{-2}=6u_i.
\]
For every other common-weak source the existing bound \(F_j\ge4u_i\)
still applies. Consequently the complete quadratic image satisfies
\[
 \sum_{j\in q_2(U)}F_j\ge4P+2R_5.
\]
The complete quartic image retains its previous bound \(16P\).
The two images are disjoint and strictly right of \(1/2\), and their
reflected copies are disjoint from them. Nonnegativity and symmetry
therefore give
\[
 A-E_U-Q=\sum_{i\in S}F_i
 \ge2(4P+2R_5+16P)=40P+4R_5.
\]
Substitution of \(A=C+P+E_U\) proves
\eqref{eq:fifth-tail-stability}. This is a joint signed budget:
the quadratic gain is inserted before the complete reflection sum,
with both compensation charges present.

Every unit weight is strictly positive. Therefore \(R_5=0\) if and
only if every atom in \(U\) has multiplicity at most four.
Together with \(\Delta\ge4R_5\) and \(R_5\ge0\), this proves the
equality-rigidity assertion.

To prove optimality, fix any \(H>0\) and take the following seventeen
points. Each pair in a table row has the indicated common height and
multiplicity.
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{real parts}&\text{height}&\text{multiplicity}\\ \hline
 1/2&H&5\\
 1/4,\;3/4&H/2&3\\
 1/8,\;7/8&H/4&2\\
 3/8,\;5/8&H/4&2\\
 3/16,\;13/16&H/8&1\\
 7/16,\;9/16&H/8&1\\
 7/32,\;25/32&H/16&1\\
 5/16,\;11/16&H/8&1\\
 5/32,\;27/32&H/16&1
 \end{array}
\]
Reflection fixes the center and exchanges the eight pairs. All points
are distinct and belong to the required closed strip. To check every
reference, let \(a_i=64\Re\rho_i\) and
\(b_i=H/\Im\rho_i\). The complete quadratic real-coordinate ownership
condition is
\[
 2a_j=a_i+64,
\]
and the quartic condition is \(4a_j=a_i+192\).
Every matching pair also satisfies \(b_j=2b_i\), respectively
\(b_j=4b_i\); thus these are exact point identities, not merely
real-coordinate matches. All other references are absent.

The quadratic references of the four common-weak points are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (1/2,H)&\longmapsto(3/4,H/2),&
 (1/4,H/2)&\longmapsto(5/8,H/4),\\
 (1/8,H/4)&\longmapsto(9/16,H/8),&
 (3/8,H/4)&\longmapsto(11/16,H/8).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here a displayed pair records real part and height.
The four additional right-hand quadratic references are

 \begin{aligned}
 (3/4,H/2)&\longmapsto(7/8,H/4),&
 (5/8,H/4)&\longmapsto(13/16,H/8),\\
 (9/16,H/8)&\longmapsto(25/32,H/16),&
 (11/16,H/8)&\longmapsto(27/32,H/16).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The only quartic references are those of the four common-weak points,
with targets
\[
 (7/8,H/4),\quad (13/16,H/8),\quad

in the same source order. These complete rows show that \(U\) consists

\subsection{The complete two-tail coefficient region}
\label{sec:two-tail-region}

The fifth tail has a spatial refinement. Keep the finite support \(S\),
the common-weak set \(U\), the unit weights \(u_i=\gamma_i^{-2}\), and
\(\Delta=C-39P-Q\) from the preceding section, and put
\[
 V_3=\sum_{\substack{i\in U\\ \Re\rho_i<1/2\\m_i\ge3}}u_i.
\]
The strict left-half condition is part of the definition: central
multiple zeros contribute to \(R_5\), but never to \(V_3\).

\paragraph{Theorem (complete coefficient region).}
For every real pair \(a,b\), the inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-tail-region}
 39P+Q+aR_5+bV_3\le C
\end{equation}
holds for every finite reflected positive-natural-multiplicity spectrum
in \([1/8,7/8]\) if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-tail-facets}
 \boxed{\quad a\le16,\qquad b\le1,\qquad a+4b\le4.\quad}
\end{equation}
In particular the two coupled inequalities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-tail-budgets}
 \Delta\ge V_3,\qquad \Delta+3V_3\ge16R_5.
\end{equation}
If \(V_3=0\), the fifth-tail bound improves to \(\Delta\ge16R_5\).
If \(\Delta=4R_5\), then necessarily \(V_3=4R_5\).

\paragraph{Proof of the coupled budgets.}
Write \(i^*\) for reflection. Retain the exact charged weight
\(F_i=w_i-D_i1_U(i)/2-D_i1_U(i^*)/2\) of
Section~\ref{sec:multiplicity-support-region}, where
\(w_i=m_i u_i\) and \(D_i=(m_i-1+1_{m_i\ge2})u_i\).
Define the one-sided marker and two symmetric weights by
\[
 t_i=1_U(i)1_{\Re\rho_i<1/2}1_{m_i\ge3}u_i,\qquad
 K_i=F_i-\frac{t_i+t_{i^*}}2,\qquad
 L_i=F_i+\frac32(t_i+t_{i^*}).
\]
Every charge, reference order and mirror remains present. The exact sums are
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}K_i=A-E_U-Q-V_3,\qquad
 \sum_{i\in S}L_i=A-E_U-Q+3V_3.
\]
Both weights are symmetric.

At a point strictly right of \(1/2\), \(t_i=0\).
If \(m_i\ge3\), then
\(F_i\ge w_i/2\ge3u_i/2\), while \(t_{i^*}\le u_i\).
Hence \(K_i\ge u_i\). If \(m_i<3\), reflection preserves multiplicity,
so \(t_{i^*}=0\), and the same conclusion follows from \(F_i\ge u_i\).
Reflection proves nonnegativity of \(K\) also on the strict left half;
on the critical line both markers vanish and \(K=F\ge0\).

Consequently the complete quadratic and quartic reference images carry,
respectively, at least \(4P\) and \(16P\) in the \(K\) weight.
The images are disjoint and strictly right of \(1/2\); applying the
symmetric nonnegative reflection budget gives
\[
 A-E_U-Q-V_3=\sum_{i\in S}K_i\ge2(4P+16P)=40P.
\]
Together with \(A=C+P+E_U\), this is \(\Delta\ge V_3\).

For \(L\), nonnegativity and the right unit floor follow from those of
\(F\). If a right-hand point has \(m_i\ge3\) and its mirror belongs to
\(U\), then \(t_{i^*}=u_i\), giving \(L_i\ge3u_i\).
If its mirror is not in \(U\), both original charges vanish at that
pair, so \(F_i=w_i\ge3u_i\), and again \(L_i\ge3u_i\).
A common-weak source of order at least five has quadratic reference
order at least three and reference unit weight \(4u_i\).
That reference therefore supplies at least \(12u_i\), rather than
the baseline \(4u_i\). Summing injectively over the complete quadratic
image yields \(4P+8R_5\). The complete quartic image still supplies
\(16P\). The same disjoint reflection budget now gives
\[
 A-E_U-Q+3V_3=\sum_{i\in S}L_i
 \ge2(4P+8R_5+16P)=40P+16R_5.
\]
This proves the second inequality in~\eqref{eq:two-tail-budgets}.
In particular, the earlier coefficient four is obtained by eliminating
the spatial tail, not by dropping it.

\paragraph{Sufficiency of all three facets.}
Both tails are nonnegative. If \(b\le-3\), then \(a\le16\) gives
\(aR_5+bV_3\le16R_5-3V_3\le\Delta\).
For \(-3\le b\le1\), multiply the first inequality in
\eqref{eq:two-tail-budgets} by \(b+3\) and the second by \(1-b\).
Adding and dividing by four gives
\[
 \Delta\ge4(1-b)R_5+bV_3.
\]
The condition \(a+4b\le4\) completes
\eqref{eq:two-tail-region}. This includes all negative coefficients.
If \(\Delta=4R_5\), the two budgets give
\(V_3\le4R_5\) and \(V_3\ge4R_5\), proving the stated rigidity.

\paragraph{Necessity and full-readout extremizers.}
All three facets are attained by explicit finite models for every \(H>0\).
The seventeen-point spectrum is the one in
Section~\ref{sec:fifth-multiplicity-stability}.
For the nine-point model retain its center and the four reflected pairs
whose left real parts are \(1/4,1/8,7/16,7/32\), with their original
heights and multiplicities. For the thirteen-point model remove instead
the two pairs whose left real parts are \(7/16,7/32\), change the central
multiplicity to two, and change the multiplicities at real parts
\(1/8,7/8\) to one. All remaining heights and orders are unchanged.

For each restriction every reference order is recomputed on the restricted
support. The complete integer ownership conditions remain
\(2a_j=a_i+64\), \(4a_j=a_i+192\), with the matching height denominators
multiplied by two or four. A removed target contributes order zero.
In the nine-point model the common-weak points are exactly
\((1/2,H)\) and \((1/8,H/4)\). In the thirteen-point model they are
exactly \((1/2,H),(1/4,H/2),(3/8,H/4)\).
Direct finite sums give the following homogeneous values:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 \#S&H^2P&H^2Q&H^2R_5&H^2V_3&H^2C&H^2\Delta\\ \hline
 9&17&17&1&0&696&16\\
 13&21&21&0&4&844&4\\

 \end{array}
\]
The nine-point model forces \(a\le16\); the thirteen-point model forces
\(b\le1\); and the seventeen-point model forces \(a+4b\le4\).
This proves necessity and completes the coefficient classification.

These are full-readout models. For any finite reflected spectrum \(S\),
positive imaginary parts make every denominator
\(\rho_i+d-1\) nonzero. The existing atomic residue theorem proves

Thus the sharpness examples do not replace a compensated residue by a

\subsection{Exact Mellin action on general windows}
\label{sec:general-window-mellin}

Let
\[
 \mathcal M W(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}W(x)\,dx,\qquad
 \widetilde W(x)=\frac{W(x)-3W(2x)+2W(4x)}6.
\]
For every \(s\in\mathbb C\) where the first integral is absolutely
convergent, the filtered integral is also absolutely convergent and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-window-mellin}
 \mathcal M\widetilde W(s)
 =\frac{(1-2^{-s})(1-2^{1-s})}{6}\,\mathcal M W(s).
\end{equation}
This is an integral identity for a general window, not only a calculation
on the formal power response \(D^s\).

\emph{Proof.}
For any positive real \(a\), the substitution \(u=ax\) gives
\[
 \mathcal M[W(a\,\cdot)](s)=a^{-s}\mathcal M W(s)
\]
and preserves Mellin convergence. Linearity applied to the three
convergent integrals gives the coefficient
\((1-3\cdot2^{-s}+2\cdot4^{-s})/6\), which factors as in
\eqref{eq:general-window-mellin}. \(\square\)

If \(0<\beta=\Re s<1\), the existing multiplier estimate now applies
directly to the actual integral:
\[
 \frac{(1-2^{-\beta})(2^{1-\beta}-1)}6\,|\mathcal M W(s)|
 \le |\mathcal M\widetilde W(s)|.
\]
Its coefficient is positive. Thus a nonzero base Mellin value survives
the filter. The base value itself is still present in this bound;
no uniform lower bound for it in height is asserted.
The maintained proofs are
\texttt{GaussianMellinWavelet.window\_hasMellin},
\texttt{window\_mellin\_ne\_zero}, and
\texttt{window\_mellin\_norm\_lower}, in
\texttt{Assembly/DetectorPackets/GaussianMellinWavelet.lean}.
The construction of a responsive radial bandlimited base window
remains a separate obligation.

\subsection{An exact band-limited Type I estimate}
\label{sec:bandlimited-type-i}

A different window class removes the principal Type I block exactly.
Here \emph{Gaussian} refers to the lattice \(\mathbb Z[i]\).
An exponential Gaussian test function is not band-limited.

Let \(g\) be a complex Schwartz function on \(\mathbb C\), with
\(\widehat g(0)=0\) and \(\widehat g(\xi)=0\) for \(|\xi|\ge1\).
For every real \(q>0\), \(X>0\), \(q^2\le X\), and \(r\in\mathbb C\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandlimited-coset-zero}
 \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^2}
   g\!\left(\frac{qn+r}{\sqrt X}\right)=0.
\end{equation}
The sum is absolutely convergent. Identifying \(n=n_1+in_2\),
affine Poisson summation gives
\[
 \frac{X}{q^2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^2}
   e^{2\pi i\, k\cdot r/q}\,
   \widehat g\!\left(\frac{\sqrt X}{q}k\right).
\]
The zero frequency vanishes by hypothesis. Every other frequency has
norm at least \(\sqrt X/q\ge1\), and vanishes by support.
Every shift phase remains in this calculation.
For an integer \(q\ge1\), the complete combination of all \(q^2\)
cosets with weights \(\chi(r_1^2+r_2^2)\) therefore also vanishes,
for any Dirichlet character modulo \(q\). The theorem includes
\(q=1,2\) and composite moduli.

Suppose \(g(z)=V(|z|^2)\) and \(V(0)=0\). For nonzero ideals and
a nonzero ideal \(H\), write
\[
 S_H(V;X)=\sum_{\substack{C\ne0\\(C,H)=1}}V(N(C)/X).
\]
The four-unit reindexing and complete divisor mask give
\[
 S_H(V;X)=\sum_{E\mid H}\mu(E)
          \sum_{C\ne0}V\bigl(N(C)/(X/N(E))\bigr).
\]
Hence \(S_H(V;X)=0\) whenever \(N(H)\le X\). For any finite sets
\(A,B\) of nonzero ideals, the entire signed block satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandlimited-type-i-zero}
 \sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}\mu(a)\mu(b)
       S_H\!\left(V;\frac{D}{N(a)N(b)}\right)=0
\end{equation}
if \(D>0\) and \(N(H)N(a)N(b)\le D\) for every retained pair.
All divisor terms, including ramified and repeated factors, remain.
No norm estimate is applied to this main block.

The existing three-scale wavelet supplies both origin conditions.
If \(f(z)=W(|z|^2)\) and \(\widehat f(\xi)=0\) for \(|\xi|\ge1/2\),
then \(g=\operatorname{wavelet}(f)\) has Fourier support inside the
unit disk, \(g(0)=\widehat g(0)=0\), and
\(V=\operatorname{window}(W)\). The dilation identity
\[
 \widehat{f(\sqrt a\,\cdot)}(\xi)
       =a^{-1}\widehat f(\xi/\sqrt a)
\]
proves the support statement for \(a=2,4\).
The maintained owner is
\texttt{Assembly/DetectorPackets/GaussianBandlimitedTypeI.lean},
including \texttt{wavelet\_typeI\_zero} and
\texttt{character\_coset\_sum\_zero}.

\paragraph{Its complete consumer and remaining corrections.}
The earlier generic estimate was
\[
 |T_I-\mathrm{main}_I|
       \le (81/4)C_f\,\tau(H)\,UV.
\]
On the new window class the entire block is zero under
\(N(H)UV\le D\). Fixed masks thus permit \(U,V\) of order
\(\sqrt D\), rather than imposing \(UV\le D^{3/4}\) merely to
fit a \(D^{3/4}\) allowance. This is a component estimate.

Finite support remains explicit. If \(L_{\le Y}\) is the low-low
block in a finite ideal prefix, the theorem concerns its full
cofactor counterpart \(L_\infty\). Set
\(L_{\rm out}=L_\infty-L_{\le Y}\). After the required reindexing,
the two-cutoff identity retains
\[
 P_{\le Y}=E_U+E_V-L_{\le Y}+H_{\le Y}
          =E_U+E_V+H_{\le Y}+L_{\rm out},
 \qquad L_\infty=0.
\]
The outside correction has a plus sign. A total-ideal coprimality
restriction also restricts \(a,b\); a cofactor-only mask cannot
