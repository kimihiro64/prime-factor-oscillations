\begin{abstract}
We study reversals in the density of the $k$th smallest distinct prime
factor and the finer arithmetic error in its consecutive-coefficient
ratio. For fixed local laws with $\eta_p^{-1}=p/\nu+\beta+o(1)$,
the finite maximal separated reversal count has exact rate
$\limsup_k k^{-1}\log\log(3+N_\eta(k))
=\sup_{H\ge2}\gamma_H/(H+\nu)$, where $\gamma_H$ records bounded-gap
frequency. This spectrum has a finite phase profile and arithmetic
realizations by affine prime sums.
A uniform comparison with a full entire-function reference transfers
the classical Nicolas error to the actual moving-rank coefficient
ratio throughout a class of local laws. Its eventual sign gives RH
criteria with exact forced-prime rank shifts and reference constants.
Persistent excursions yield positive-area criteria; explicit
short-interval sampling hypotheses also give localized reversal-count
criteria. Hardy--Littlewood-shaped gap counts give separate
conditional results on the last ascent.
For consecutive-root polynomials plus a prime, we identify the exact
complete correction and prove an unconditional negative sign when
the degree is $1+\lfloor N/(\log N)^j\rfloor$, for each fixed $j\ge1$.
If instead $d(N)\le CN^\alpha$ for fixed $1/2\le\alpha<1$,
an eventual nonpositive complete profile excludes zeta zeros to
the right of $\alpha$; the signed premise remains unproved.
A separate application to generalized divisor sums constructs explicit
modified least common multiples with a positive normalized logarithm,
uniformly for $1<\kappa\le3/2+2/(5\log\log M_N)$ at unbounded integer
cutoffs. Under RH the same window holds eventually at every integer
cutoff; consequently, every fixed $1<\kappa\le3/2$ has arbitrarily
large counterexamples to the generalized upper inequality.
The maintained results are formalized in Lean. Classical inputs,
potentially new applications, and unproved arithmetic premises are
distinguished; no new prime-gap bound or proof of RH is claimed.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction and historical context}\label{sec:intro}
Let $d_k(p)$ be the natural density of positive integers whose $k$th
smallest \emph{distinct} prime factor is $p$. The question whether
$p\mapsto d_k(p)$ first rises and then falls is now called Erd\H{o}s
Problem 690. Our subject is the finer structure left after unimodality
fails: the number of separated reversals, their possible locations, and
the arithmetic information encoded by their growth.

Erd\H{o}s formulated the question in 1979~\cite[p.~75]{Erdos}.
The same paper discusses the typical scale $\log\log p_k(n)\sim k$
for prime factors of integers. This concerns where probability mass
lies, not the mode of the individual-prime density. Erd\H{o}s--Tenenbaum
\cite[Corollary~5]{ET} later corrected the earlier assertion about the
mode: a maximizing prime satisfies $\log p\sim k/\log k$, rather than
$\log p\sim k$. Neither statement counts reversals. The occurrence of
a double exponential in classical prime-factor distribution does not
by itself supply the theorem below.

Cambie~\cite[Theorem~5]{Cambie} proved unimodality for $k=1,2,3$ and
verified its failure for $4\le k\le20$. His Theorem~3 gives
superpolynomially many local maxima for the different density
$\delta_1(n,m)$ in Erd\H{o}s Problem 692: the density of integers having
exactly one divisor in $\{n+1,\ldots,m-1\}$. Its moving variable is $m$,
with $n$ fixed. It is not a reversal count for $d_k(p)$.

Wang--Crapis~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{WC} proved non-unimodality for every
$k\ge4$. Their proof compares a symmetric-polynomial ratio with prime
gaps and produces a descent followed by an ascent. We extend the
comparison to local laws and make the construction quantitative.
The question here is how the maximal number of witnesses grows with
$k$. Its exact rate also requires a converse: a sufficiently late
ascent must come from an appropriately short gap.

The first contribution is the exact reversal
frequency spectrum, its arithmetic realization, and its inverse
late-ascent interpretation. Quantitative bounded-gap results are
existing analytic inputs, not new sieve results. The second is a
uniform transfer of a classical RH-sensitive error into the actual
moving-rank coefficient ratio, preserving its sign at a scale much
smaller than any fixed inverse power of $\log\log x$. The transfer holds
throughout an explicit class of local probability laws, rather than only
for the ordinary integers: each family contributes its full reference
profile and the same centered arithmetic signal.

Nicolas's primorial criterion and its signed error
\cite{Nicolas1983,Nicolas2012} supply the analytic source.
The finite generating product and its saddle-point analysis already
occur in Erd\H{o}s--Tenenbaum \cite{ET}; the normalized Euler product
also appears in mod-Poisson theory \cite{KN,FMN}. We claim neither
the generating function nor the Nicolas spectral expansion as new.
The potentially new statements are the exact gap-frequency spectrum
and the quantitative comparison with a full theta-centered
coefficient reference, together with their stated applications.
Priority remains provisional pending independent review.

There is also a separate divisor-sum application.
Fan--Kobayashi--Molnar~\cite[Theorem~6.1 and \S8]{FKM}
prove an eventual generalized Robin upper bound under RH for each
fixed real exponent $\kappa>3/2$ and ask about smaller exponents.
Section~\ref{sec:lcm-window} gives unconditional unbounded violations
for every $1<\kappa\le3/2$, and a shrinking window above the endpoint.
The additional growing-family calculations in
Section~\ref{sec:growing-families} retain complete arithmetic
corrections. Their independent signs and their RH-sensitive
conditional implications have different degree ranges.
Section~\ref{sec:power-degree} states the exact $N^\alpha$
zero-free implication and its still-open signed hypothesis.
The endpoint/window result and these family applications are
potentially new; independent priority assessment remains outstanding.

Sections~\ref{sec:ratio}--\ref{sec:affine} prove the unconditional
spectrum. Section~\ref{sec:late} treats recurrence and the separate
Hardy--Littlewood conditional location result.
Sections~\ref{sec:profile}--\ref{sec:capacity} establish the coefficient
transfer, pointwise RH criteria and reference capacities.
Sections~\ref{sec:area}--\ref{sec:local-count} treat persistent positive
area and the localized signed-count criteria. Sections
\ref{sec:growing-families} and~\ref{sec:lcm-window} give the growing
arithmetic families and the generalized-divisor-sum application;
Section~\ref{sec:power-degree} gives the power-degree criterion. The
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations}{public repository}
contains maintained proofs and a provenance ledger. This is an
AI-assisted research draft; independent expert review and assessment
of novelty remain outstanding.

\section{The argument in plain English}\label{sec:plain}
Choose a position in an integer's ordered list of prime factors,
say the tenth distinct prime factor. For each prime, ask what fraction
of sampled integers put that prime in the chosen position. These
fractions form a sequence. A reversal means that the sequence drops
and later rises again. We count occurrences separated far enough
that they do not reuse the same stretch of the sequence.

Two effects compete. Moving to a larger prime lowers its own chance
of dividing the integer. At the same time, the additional smaller
primes make it easier to have the required number of preceding
factors. An exact formula measures this competition. On the relevant
scale, a short gap between consecutive primes favors a rise, whereas
a long gap favors a drop.

The useful measuring scale is the logarithm of the logarithm of the
prime. On that scale the available rise threshold is approximately
the rank divided by the scale. This explains the double-exponential
locations in the argument. Eventually even the shortest possible
prime gap is too large for a rise, so every fixed rank still has a
final decreasing tail.

Many short gaps alone are not enough to count reversals. We must
put a long gap before each selected short gap and keep the witnesses
distinct. Periodic blocks of composite integers provide barriers
that consecutive primes must jump. Grouping short gaps between the
barriers and taking alternate occupied groups produces separated
witnesses, losing only a fixed factor in their number.

Finally, different short-gap sizes can have different frequencies.
Each size contributes its frequency divided by a threshold cost.
The greatest contribution is exactly the reversal growth rate.
For products of affine prime sums, the number of distinct factors
changes that cost, while finitely many exceptional primes change
only the initial details.

A second, much finer comparison uses the entire prime-product profile
as a reference. The ratio that decides an ascent differs from this
reference by a small signed error. We show that this error carries
the same RH information as Nicolas's primorial inequality.
Under RH it is eventually negative, with a bias from prime squares.
If RH is false it has larger excursions of both signs. The rank
moves outward with the prime cutoff in this statement; it does not
contradict eventual decline at each fixed rank.

Changing the sampled family changes its local factor probabilities and
its reference profile. We prove that a quadratic change in the local
odds preserves the small RH-sensitive error after this exact reference
has been subtracted. Forced primes shift the rank and also change the
reference constant; both effects are retained. Thus one independently
proved centered inequality in any qualifying family would imply RH.

This determines how the prefix changes the threshold for a possible
ascent. An actual ascent also needs an actual prime gap below that
threshold. RH controls the fine reference error, while a
Hardy--Littlewood twin-count assumption supplies twins near the
potential endpoint. Their roles and their different precision are
kept separate throughout.

Two further calculations test what arithmetic structure can actually
supply. A polynomial with many prescribed roots makes divisibility
by small primes less likely. We calculate its entire correction,
rather than just its leading local probability, and prove a complete
negative sign when the degree is only logarithmically smaller than
the prime cutoff. This range must be distinguished from the much
smaller power-sized degrees relevant to excluding zeta zeros.

For generalized divisor sums, increasing selected prime exponents
in a least common multiple balances an extra height cost against
a smaller local-factor loss. The full balance gives explicit
counterexamples in a shrinking parameter window around $3/2$.
The proof works whether RH is true or false. It answers a separate
inequality question; it does not resolve the signed premise needed
for an independent proof of RH.

The finer error can also be detected by counting. We choose actual short
prime gaps in many separate cells, place a long-gap descent before each,
and test a finely spaced range of local laws. If RH fails, its positive
error persists across a whole interval. Every selected cell then adds
more actual ascents than the full reference predicts. A sufficiently
accurate upper bound on this signed count would therefore imply RH.
This uses known short-interval bounded gaps at some fixed width; it
does not require twin primes, and it does not establish that upper bound.

\section{Definitions and main results}\label{sec:statements}
All logarithms are natural. Index the primes by $p_0=2,p_1=3,\ldots$,
and write $\LL(x)=\log\log x$ for $x>1$ and
$\EE(t)=\exp(\exp t)$. Ranks $k$ are positive integers.
A fixed \emph{local law} consists of probabilities $0\le\eta_p\le1$.
Define the finite products
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 M_r(p)=[z^r]\prod_{q<p}(1-\eta_q+\eta_qz),\qquad
 D_{\eta,k}(p)=\eta_pM_{k-1}(p),
\end{equation}
with $M_r=0$ for $r<0$. For the ordinary law $\eta_p=1/p$,
the Chinese remainder theorem identifies $D_{\eta,k}$ with $d_k$.
The second canonical law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd}
 \eta_2^{\mathrm{odd}}=0,\qquad
 \eta_p^{\mathrm{odd}}=\frac{p-2}{(p-1)^2}\quad(p>2).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}\label{def:reversal}
A reversal at rank $k$ consists of indices $d<a$ with
\[
 D_{\eta,k}(p_{d+1})<D_{\eta,k}(p_d),\qquad
 D_{\eta,k}(p_a)<D_{\eta,k}(p_{a+1}).
\]
A family of $m$ such witnesses is separated if $a_i+1<d_j$ for
$i<j$. Its maximal attainable size, when finite, is $N_\eta(k)$.
\end{definition}
This counts raw-density descent--ascent witnesses, not oscillations
of a centered error or a smoothed approximation.

For integers $H\ge0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
\begin{split}
 G_H(X)&=\#\{i:p_i\le X,\ p_{i+1}-p_i\le H\},\\
 \gamma_H&=\limsup_{\substack{X\to\infty\\X\in\N}}
       \frac{\log\log(3+G_H(X))}{\LL(X)},\\
 \Lambda(\nu)&=\sup_{H\ge2}\frac{\gamma_H}{H+\nu}\quad(\nu>0).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The $3$ keeps the logarithms defined when counts vanish.
We have $0\le\gamma_H\le1$ and $\gamma_0=\gamma_1=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact reversal spectrum]\label{thm:spectrum}
Suppose $\nu>0$, $\beta\in\mathbb R$, and, along sufficiently large primes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth}
 0<\eta_p<1,\qquad
 \eta_p^{-1}=p/\nu+\beta+\rho(p),\qquad \rho(p)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Every fixed-rank sequence $D_{\eta,k}$ is eventually nonincreasing,
its reversal maximum is finite and attained, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\log(3+N_\eta(k))}{k}
       =\Lambda(\nu)>0.
\end{equation}
The law, its parameters and its exceptional coordinates are fixed
before $k$ tends to infinity.
\end{theorem}
The weaker estimate $\eta_p=\nu/p+O(p^{-2})$ alone does not supply
the vanishing difference of reciprocal errors used in this theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical bounds]\label{thm:canonical}
For either canonical law and every $\varepsilon>0$, for all
sufficiently large $k$,
\[
 \EE(k/602)\le N_\eta(k)\le\EE((1/3+\varepsilon)k).
\]
Each fixed-rank canonical sequence is eventually strictly decreasing.
\end{theorem}
The positive constant uses a conservative quantitative width-$600$
input; it is not a claim about the best known numerical prime-gap bound.

\begin{theorem}[Affine arithmetic realization]\label{thm:affine}
Fix $m\ge1$, positive integers $c_j$, and integers $d_j$, with
$c_id_j\ne c_jd_i$ for $i\ne j$. Put
\[
 F(p,q)=\prod_{j=1}^m(c_j(p+q)+d_j).
\]
Sample all ordered prime pairs $p,q\le X$, including the diagonal,
with denominator $\pi(X)^2$. At every fixed rank $k$ and prime
$\ell$, the probability that $\ell$ is the $k$th smallest distinct
prime factor of $|F(p,q)|$ has a limit $D_{F,k}(\ell)$; zero
outputs are excluded from the event. The maximal separated reversal
count $N_F(k)$ is finite and attained, and
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\log(3+N_F(k))}{k}=\Lambda(m)>0.
\]
\end{theorem}
The size limit comes first, with family, rank and prime fixed.
Outputs are weighted by their prime-pair representations.

The second main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-ratio}, concerns the
ordinary coefficient ratio at rank
$r=\lfloor\alpha(\gamma+\log\log\theta(x))\rfloor$, for each fixed
$\alpha>0$. Its precise full-profile normalization is defined in
Section~\ref{sec:profile}. Eventual negativity of that centered ratio
is equivalent to RH. This statement is separate from
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}, whose hypotheses contain no RH assumption.

\section{The exact comparison and uniform estimates}\label{sec:ratio}
\subsection{First differences}
For consecutive primes $p<q$ with interior probabilities,
\[
 M_r(q)=(1-\eta_p)M_r(p)+\eta_pM_{r-1}(p).
\]
Direct subtraction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diff}
 D_{\eta,k}(q)-D_{\eta,k}(p)=
 \eta_p\eta_q\{M_{k-2}(p)-T_\eta(p,q)M_{k-1}(p)\},
\end{equation}
where $T_\eta(p,q)=\eta_q^{-1}-\eta_p^{-1}+1$.
When $M_{k-1}(p)>0$, an ascent is equivalent to
$M_{k-2}(p)/M_{k-1}(p)>T_\eta(p,q)$.
For the ordinary law, writing $R=M_{k-2}/M_{k-1}$ and $g=q-p$,
the exact real-valued gap threshold is $R-1$: a strict ascent occurs
if and only if $g<R-1$. Equality gives a flat step.
This threshold does not constrain the actual gap to be small.
The ordinary comparison level is $q-p+1$; for the odd law at $p>2$ it is
\[
 q-p+1-\frac{q-p}{(p-2)(q-2)}.
\]

\subsection{Finite exceptions and symmetric polynomials}
Only finitely many coordinates in \eqref{eq:smooth} can be forced
($\eta_p=1$) or excluded ($\eta_p=0$). Let $f$ count the forced
coordinates. Past them, each contributes a factor $z$ to the
generating polynomial; excluded coordinates contribute $1$.
Retain all interior coordinates, including the exceptional ones,
and set
\[
 w_p=\frac{\eta_p}{1-\eta_p},\qquad
 S(p)=\sum_{\substack{q<p\\0<\eta_q<1}}w_q.
\]
For $k>f$, put $r=k-1-f$. The common positive product of
$1-\eta_q$ cancels in the ratio, leaving $E_{r-1}(p)/E_r(p)$,
where $E_r$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $r$
in the finite list of interior odds.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:symmetric}
For at least $r\ge1$ positive weights, let $W_{r-1}$ be the largest
sum of $r-1$ distinct weights. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \frac rS\le\frac{E_{r-1}}{E_r}\le\frac r{S-W_{r-1}},
\end{equation}
and the right denominator is positive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The terms of $SE_{r-1}$ which choose a new index total $rE_r$.
The remaining terms are
\[
 \sum_{|A|=r-1}\left(\sum_{j\in A}w_j\right)\prod_{j\in A}w_j,
\]
between zero and $W_{r-1}E_{r-1}$. Rearrangement proves the bounds.
At least one positive weight remains after any $r-1$ are removed.
\end{proof}
This specializes to Wang--Crapis \cite[Lemmas~3.1--3.2]{WC};
the present use also retains the exact forced-rank shift.

Expanding \eqref{eq:smooth} gives $w_p=\nu/p+O(p^{-2})$.
The reciprocal-prime estimate and the convergent quadratic tail give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mertens}
 S(p)=\nu\LL(p-1)+O_\eta(1),\qquad
 W_{r-1}=O_\eta(1+\log(r+1)).
\end{equation}
For the second estimate, dominate all but finitely many odds by
$C/p$ and use $p_j\ge j+2$. The finitely many exceptional odds
contribute a fixed constant; their order need not be monotone.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform sign margins]\label{lem:bands}
Fix $0<u\le v$. Uniformly in the band
$uk\le\LL(p-1)\le vk$, as $k\to\infty$,
\[
 \frac{E_{r-1}(p)}{E_r(p)}
       =\frac{k}{\nu\LL(p-1)}+o(1),\qquad r=k-1-f.
\]
Gaps at most $H$ give strict ascents throughout the band if
$(H+\nu)v<1$. Gaps at least $D$ give strict descents there if
$Du>2$, for sufficiently large rank and lower prime.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The prime number theorem ensures more than $r$ interior primes
below $p$ uniformly in this band. Equations \eqref{eq:ratio} and
\eqref{eq:mertens} have numerator $k+O_\eta(1)$ and denominator
$\nu\LL(p-1)+O_\eta(\log(k+1))$, bounded below by a positive
multiple of $k$. This proves the uniform ratio statement.

Writing $g=q-p$, the threshold is
$g/\nu+1+\rho(q)-\rho(p)$. Its error is bounded by twice the
supremum of $|\rho|$ beyond $p$, even for large $g$.
For $g\le H$ the limiting lower ratio is
$1/(\nu v)>(H+\nu)/\nu$.
For $g\ge D$ the upper ratio is
$1/(\nu u)+o(1)<D/\nu$. Both margins are strict.
\end{proof}
Without an upper band endpoint, the same estimates give a
one-sided bound: on $\LL(p-1)\ge bk$ the ratio is at most
$1/(\nu b)+o(1)$, uniformly for fixed $b>0$.

\subsection{Every fixed rank}
For fixed $r\ge1$, the ratio tends to zero by
\eqref{eq:ratio}--\eqref{eq:mertens}. Consecutive sufficiently large
primes have gap at least two, so their threshold is uniformly
positive. Equation \eqref{eq:diff} gives strict descent for
supported degrees. If $k\le f$, the tail is zero. If $k=f+1$,
the remaining degree is zero, its predecessor coefficient vanishes,
and the same identity again gives descent.
Thus every fixed-rank tail is nonincreasing. For the ordinary law,
eventual decrease was already noted by Cambie \cite[\S3]{Cambie}.
The extension here retains the forced and excluded coordinates.

Once all ascent indices lie below an integer $I$, a separated
family has size at most $I$. Attainable sizes form a nonempty
bounded subset of $\N$ (zero is attainable), so their maximum
is attained. The canonical laws have no forced coordinates
and have positive supported tails, giving strict eventual decrease.

\section{From short gaps to separated reversals}\label{sec:selection}
The following finite argument supplies the counting step and its loss.
It turns actual small-gap supply into separated raw reversals.
The later RH coefficient correction is not an input to this construction.

\begin{lemma}[Periodic composite barriers]\label{lem:cells}
Fix integers $D\ge H+1$ and $M=D!$. Let $\mathcal S$ be a
nonempty finite set of integer starts such that
\[
 X\le n,\quad n+H\le Y,\quad
 \#([n,n+H]\cap\{\text{primes}\})\ge2,\quad X\ge M+2.
\]
There are $m$ separated pairs of consecutive-prime gaps, a gap at
least $D$ followed by one at most $H$, all endpoints in $[X,Y]$,
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cellloss}
 |\mathcal S|\le2(M+H)(m+1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose consecutive primes $p<q$ in each window, and assign them
to $c=\lfloor(p-2)/M\rfloor$. Since $cM+j$ is composite for
$2\le j\le D$, and $c\ge1$,
\[
 cM+D+1\le p<q\le(c+1)M+1.
\]
Indeed a prime $q$ beyond the last endpoint but within $H<D$
of $p$ would lie in the next composite block. A short pair cannot
cross that barrier.

Starts assigned to cell $c$ lie between $cM+D+1-H$ and
$(c+1)M+1$, so there are at most $M+H$ of them.
Order the occupied cells $c_0<\cdots<c_{t-1}$ and choose one
pair in each, with lower-prime index $u_j$. Between chosen
primes in two successive occupied cells, a consecutive-prime gap
crosses the destination cell's whole composite block. Its length
is at least $D$, and it precedes the chosen short gap there.

Take destination cells $c_2,c_4,\ldots,c_{2m}$, where
$m=\lfloor(t-1)/2\rfloor$. Choose each crossing after the lower
prime selected in the preceding occupied cell. The unused occupied
cell between successive destinations ensures $a_i+1<d_{i+1}$.
Every crossing lies between selected endpoints, so remains in
$[X,Y]$. Finally $t\le2(m+1)$ and
$|\mathcal S|\le(M+H)t$, proving \eqref{eq:cellloss}.
\end{proof}
No uniform distribution among cells is needed. Empty stretches
are harmless, since a crossing still exists between selected
occupied cells.

Fix $u,v>0$ with $(H+\nu)v<1$ and choose an integer
$D\ge H+1$ with $D>2/u$. If all primes from $X$ through $Y+H$
lie in the sign band, the two lemmas imply, at sufficiently large $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandcount}
 G_H(Y)\le X+B(N_\eta(k)+1),\qquad B=2(D!+H).
\end{equation}
At most $X$ lower primes are deleted below $X$; each retained
short gap supplies a window starting at its lower prime.
The upper endpoint is $Y+H$, since a counted gap may end above $Y$.
All constants are fixed before $k$ grows.

\begin{corollary}[Positive-power transfer]\label{cor:power}
Suppose that for fixed $H$ and $\delta>0$, for every sufficiently
large $X$ at least $X^\delta$ integer starts in $[X,2X]$ have
two primes in their width-$H$ windows. For every
$0<a<1/(H+\nu)$,
\[
 N_\eta(k)\ge\EE(ak)\quad\hbox{for all sufficiently large }k.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $a<b<1/(H+\nu)$ and $X=\lceil\EE(bk)\rceil$.
Every endpoint in $[X,2X+H]$ has $\LL(p-1)=bk+o(1)$.
Fixed $u<b<v<1/(H+\nu)$ therefore provide the sign band.
The cell lemma gives $N_\eta(k)+1\ge X^\delta/B$.
Since $\log(X^\delta/B)=\delta\exp(bk)+O(1)$ and $a<b$,
the right side eventually exceeds $1+\EE(ak)$.
\end{proof}

We use the existing quantitative input
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clusterinput}
 \#\{n\in[X,2X]\cap\N:
       \#([n,n+600]\cap\{\text{primes}\})\ge2\}\ge X^{1/3}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $X$. Its maintained declaration is
\path{PrimeGaps.eventually_many_prime_windows_600}.
Section~\ref{sec:formal} records its provenance. Quantitative
prime-cluster theory, including Maynard's work~\cite{Maynard},
is the analytic background. We use this count, not merely
infinitude of bounded gaps. With $\nu=1$ the corollary applies
at $a=1/602<1/601$.

\section{Proof of the exact spectrum}\label{sec:sharp}
The two directions preserve the frequency subsequences and each
individual gap's cutoff. This matching rate, rather than the presence
of a double exponential alone, is the principal unconditional result.
\subsection{Lower bound: arbitrary frequency subsequences}
Fix $H$ and $0<a<\gamma_H/(H+\nu)$. Choose
\[
 0<g<\gamma_H,\qquad
 0<v_0<v<\frac1{H+\nu},\qquad a<gv_0,
\]
then $0<u<a$ and $a<b<gv_0$.
There are arbitrarily large integers $Y$ with
$3+G_H(Y)>\EE(g\LL(Y))$. Assign
$k=\lceil\LL(Y)/v_0\rceil$. The bounds
$v_0k-v_0<\LL(Y)\le v_0k$ imply
$3+G_H(Y)>\EE(bk)$ at arbitrarily large assigned ranks.

Take $X=\lceil\EE(uk)\rceil+1$. The primes between $X$ and
$Y+H$ lie in a band with positive lower coefficient and upper
coefficient $v$. Equation \eqref{eq:bandcount} applies.
If $N_\eta(k)<\EE(ak)$, then
\[
 3+G_H(Y)\le3+X+B(1+\EE(ak))<\EE(bk)
\]
eventually, a contradiction. Therefore $N_\eta(k)\ge\EE(ak)$
for arbitrarily large $k$. Supremizing over $H$ proves the lower
bound $\Lambda(\nu)$ in \eqref{eq:rate}.
The argument keeps the actual frequency subsequence; it does not
replace it by an all-large-$X$ hypothesis.

\subsection{Upper bound: each gap retains its own cutoff}
For fixed integer $g\ge2$ and $\varepsilon>0$, the one-sided
ratio bound implies that an ascent across a gap of size $g$
must satisfy, apart from fixed initial exceptions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
 \LL(p-1)<\left(\frac1{g+\nu}+\varepsilon\right)k.
\end{equation}
The same estimate excludes all gaps greater than $J$ above
$\LL(p-1)=uk$ if $1/(J+1+\nu)<u$.
In both statements, the reciprocal error is uniformly small
beyond the lower prime.

Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 N_\eta(k)\le T_k+\sum_{2\le g\le J}G_g(U_g(k)),
\end{equation}
where we may take
\[
 T_k=\lceil\EE(uk)\rceil+O(1),\qquad
 U_g(k)=\left\lceil\EE\left(
       \left(\frac1{g+\nu}+\varepsilon\right)k\right)\right\rceil+1.
\]
Each witness has a distinct ascent index. There are at most $T_k$
initial ones; all others have an actual gap $g\le J$ and are
counted in that gap's term. Overcounting by the cumulative
$G_g$ only strengthens the upper bound.

Let $A>\Lambda(\nu)$. Choose $0<u<A$ and then $J$ as above.
For each fixed $g$ and $\delta>0$, eventually
\[
 \log\log(3+G_g(U))\le(\gamma_g+\delta)\LL(U).
\]
Since $\gamma_g/(g+\nu)\le\Lambda(\nu)$, choose
$\varepsilon,\delta>0$ small enough that
\[
 (\gamma_g+\delta)
       \left(\frac1{g+\nu}+\varepsilon\right)<A
       \quad(2\le g\le J).
\]
There are only finitely many such comparisons. Rounding changes
their double-logarithmic cutoffs by $o(1)$. Each term of
\eqref{eq:envelope}, including $T_k$, has rate strictly below $A$.
A fixed finite sum has rate at most the greatest term rate.
Letting $A$ decrease to $\Lambda(\nu)$ proves the matching bound.

The input \eqref{eq:clusterinput} implies $\gamma_{600}=1$.
A given consecutive pair belongs to at most $601$ integer-start
windows of width $600$, so the associated cumulative gap count
has a positive-power lower bound along large cutoffs.
The trivial bound $G_H(X)\le X$ gives the reverse inequality
on its exponent. Hence $\Lambda(\nu)\ge1/(600+\nu)>0$.
Together with the fixed-rank proof this completes
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}.

For the canonical upper bound use the minimum eventual gap,
two, and $\nu=1$ in \eqref{eq:cutoff}. Reducing $\varepsilon$
slightly absorbs the rounding, leaving all ascents below
$\EE((1/3+\varepsilon)k)$. Their number bounds $N_\eta(k)$.
Together with Corollary~\ref{cor:power}, this proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical}.

\section{A finite phase profile}\label{sec:phase}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:phase}
Let $H_*$ be the least $H\ge2$ for which $\gamma_H=1$.
This integer exists, and for every fixed $\nu>0$,
\[
 \Lambda(\nu)=\max_{2\le H\le H_*}\frac{\gamma_H}{H+\nu}.
\]
For all sufficiently large $\nu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recovery}
 \Lambda(\nu)=\frac1{H_*+\nu},\qquad
 \frac1{\Lambda(\nu)}-\nu=H_*.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Existence follows from $\gamma_{600}=1$.
If $H>H_*$, then
$\gamma_H/(H+\nu)\le1/(H+\nu)<1/(H_*+\nu)$.
For each $H<H_*$ put $\gamma=\gamma_H<1$.
With positive denominators, the inequality
$\gamma/(H+\nu)<1/(H_*+\nu)$ is equivalent to
$(1-\gamma)\nu>\gamma H_*-H$.
It holds beyond a finite threshold. Take the greatest threshold
over the finitely many smaller classes.
\end{proof}
Here full frequency means only $\gamma_H=1$. It need not imply
positive relative density or a fixed power lower bound at every
large cutoff. The theorem identifies a finite maximum without
determining its entries numerically. The rate is an upper envelope
of the gap-frequency data; the result recovers $H_*$, not every
individual value of $\gamma_H$.

\section{Arithmetic realization by affine prime sums}\label{sec:affine}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:affine} without discarding exceptional primes.

\subsection{Fixed-modulus limits and independence}
For fixed $Q$, the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions gives
\[
 \frac{\#\{p\le X:p\text{ prime},\ p\equiv a\pmod Q\}}{\pi(X)}
       \longrightarrow\frac1{\varphi(Q)}\quad((a,Q)=1).
\]
Nonunit prime classes contain only primes dividing $Q$, so their
normalized contribution vanishes. Full Cartesian sampling of the
ordered pairs makes each fixed pair of unit classes have limiting
probability $\varphi(Q)^{-2}$.

Take $Q$ to be the product of any fixed finite set of primes.
The Chinese remainder theorem identifies the unit-pair space with
the product of the local unit-pair spaces. Divisibility of $F$
at a prime depends only on the local pair, so the limiting
divisibility indicators are independent, with exact law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localcount}
 \eta_\ell=
 \frac{\#\{(u,v)\in(\mathbb F_\ell^\times)^2:
             \prod_j(c_j(u+v)+d_j)=0\}}{(\ell-1)^2}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $k,\ell$ the rank event uses only the primes at most
$\ell$. Summing their joint atoms gives \eqref{eq:density}.

If $F(p,q)=0$, then some $p+q=-d_j/c_j\le|d_j|
\le\sum_j|d_j|$. Only finitely many such pairs exist, independently
of $X$. Their normalized contribution is zero. The diagonal
was included throughout; its $\pi(X)$ pairs are also negligible
against $\pi(X)^2$. No modulus or rank was allowed to grow with $X$.

\subsection{Exact local counts}
Set $T_\ell=\{t\in\mathbb F_\ell:\prod_j(c_jt+d_j)=0\}$.
There are $\ell-1$ unit pairs with sum zero, and $\ell-2$
with any specified nonzero sum. Thus at every prime,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rootcount}
 \eta_\ell=\frac{(\ell-2)|T_\ell|+
                    {\bf1}_{0\in T_\ell}}{(\ell-1)^2}.
\end{equation}
If a factor is identically zero modulo $\ell$,
$T_\ell=\mathbb F_\ell$ and $\eta_\ell=1$.
An empty root set gives zero, and coincident roots are counted
once. The formula covers all coefficient, shift, determinant,
and small-prime exceptions.

Outside the finite set dividing the nonzero coefficients,
nonzero shifts, or determinants $c_id_j-c_jd_i$, there are
$m$ distinct roots. At most one shift is zero; let $z_0$ be
one if it is and zero otherwise. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:genericlocal}
 \eta_\ell=\frac{m(\ell-2)+z_0}{(\ell-1)^2},\qquad
 \eta_\ell^{-1}=\frac{\ell}{m}-\frac{z_0}{m^2}+O_F(\ell^{-1}).
\end{equation}
The law satisfies \eqref{eq:smooth} with $\nu=m$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} proves the arithmetic theorem.

The simplest instances illustrate the exceptional coordinate:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 F(p,q)&\eta_2&\eta_\ell\quad(\ell>2)\\ \hline
 p+q&1&1/(\ell-1)\\
 p+q+1&0&(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)^2\\
 2(p+q)-1&0&(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)^2 .
\end{array}
\]
The forced factor $2$ in $p+q$ shifts subsequent ranks by one.
Both odd examples have exactly the canonical odd law.
All three have rate $\Lambda(1)$, despite different finite-rank
behavior. Equations \eqref{eq:rate} and \eqref{eq:recovery}
also show how sufficiently large fixed numbers of distinct affine
factors recover $H_*$. There is no uniformity claim when the family
varies with the rank. The arithmetic realization uses fixed-modulus
prime progression limits. Section~\ref{sec:family-rh} supplies the
separate full-profile coefficient theorem that transfers the RH
criterion to every one of these fixed families, with dimension $\nu=m$
and exact forced-coordinate rank shifts.

\section{Late ascents and bounded prime gaps}\label{sec:late}
For a canonical law, a \emph{late ascent at coefficient $b$}
is an ascent across consecutive primes $p<q$ with
$\LL(p-1)\ge bk$. Unbounded supply requires existence beyond
every prescribed lower bound on both $k$ and $p$.

\begin{theorem}[Recurrence equivalence]\label{thm:late}
Let $H\ge1$ be an integer and
$1/(H+2)<b<1/(H+1)$. Gaps at most $H$ recur arbitrarily far out
if and only if for every $K,Q$ there exist $k\ge K$ and consecutive
primes $p<q$, with $p\ge Q$ and $\LL(p-1)\ge bk$, at which both
canonical densities ascend.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $b<a<1/(H+1)$. To a sufficiently late gap at most $H$
assign $k=\lfloor\LL(p-1)/a\rfloor$. Then $k\to\infty$ with $p$,
$\LL(p-1)\ge ak\ge bk$, and $\LL(p-1)/k\to a$.
A small fixed band around $a$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:bands} gives
both ascents.

Conversely, $b>1/(H+2)$ and the one-sided ratio bound exclude
gaps at least $H+1$ from sufficiently late ascents. In the odd
law the correction to the ordinary threshold has absolute value
at most $1/(p-2)$, absorbed by the fixed strict margin.
Unbounded lower primes therefore give recurring gaps at most $H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Critical location]\label{cor:critical}
Suppose gaps at most $h\ge1$ recur arbitrarily far out and every
sufficiently late consecutive gap is at least $h$. The critical
canonical late-ascent coefficient is $1/(h+1)$: every smaller
positive coefficient has unbounded simultaneous ascent supply;
every larger coefficient forces both densities to descend for
all sufficiently large ranks and lower primes in that region.
No assertion at equality is made.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the forward rank assignment on gaps at most $h$ and the
one-sided upper ratio bound on gaps at least $h$.
\end{proof}

For every fixed $b>1/5$, an ascent in either canonical law at
sufficiently large $k,p$ with $\LL(p-1)\ge bk$ must cross a gap
strictly below four. Gaps between odd primes are positive even
integers, so it must be two. An independently proved unbounded
supply would therefore imply infinitely many twin primes.
That supply is not established here. A large finite ascent
certifies a finite prime pair, not recurrence.


\subsection{Infinitude, frequency and the last ascent}
For either canonical family, let $P_k$ denote the prime starting its
last ascent, which exists for all sufficiently large $k$.
If $h$ is the least recurring prime gap, Corollary~\ref{cor:critical}
and the upper cutoff imply
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\LL(P_k)}{k}=\frac1{h+1}.
\]
Indeed every smaller coefficient occurs at unbounded ranks and
endpoints, whereas every larger coefficient is eventually excluded.
Bounded-gap infinitude at width $H$ therefore gives the lower
location coefficient $1/(H+1)$. For example, taking width $186$
as an input gives a coefficient at least $1/187$; it does not identify
the least recurring gap. Twin-prime infinitude would give the
location limsup exactly $1/3$. These implications concern location.
A frequency hypothesis is needed for the corresponding reversal count.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional last-ascent location]\label{thm:hl-location}
For either canonical law, let $X_k$ be the largest supported prime
$p$ at which a hypothetical twin step satisfies the strict ascent
criterion. Suppose that for some $C>0$,
\[
 \pi_2(X)\sim C X/(\log X)^2,
 \qquad
 \pi_2(X)=\#\{p\le X:p,\ p+2\text{ are prime}\}.
\]
Then, for every sufficiently large $k$, $P_k$ is exactly the last
lower twin member at or below $X_k$, and
\[
 \frac{P_k}{X_k}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \frac{\LL(P_k)}k\longrightarrow\frac13 .
\]
The same hypothesis and the count transfer give
$\log\log(3+N_\eta(k))/k\to1/3$ for the canonical reversal counts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a supported prefix, adding a positive weight decreases
$e_{r-1}/e_r$ by the elementary symmetric log-concavity inequality.
The ordinary hypothetical twin threshold is $3$; the odd threshold
is $3-2/(p(p-2))$, which increases with $p>2$.
Thus the supported eligible primes form an initial segment.
The uniform ratio estimates and Bertrand's theorem show that its
last prime $X_k$ exists for large $k$ and $\LL(X_k)/k\to1/3$.

For every fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, subtracting the two assumed
count asymptotics gives
\[
 \pi_2(X)-\pi_2((1-\varepsilon)X)
       \sim C\varepsilon X/(\log X)^2>0.
\]
First use dyadic coverage in a fixed scale band strictly between
$1/5$ and $1/3$. It supplies a twin ascent whose supported prefix
serves as an anchor. Every later ascent must be a twin by the
$1/5$ exclusion, and lies at most at $X_k$.
Conversely the last twin at or below $X_k$ follows the anchor,
has positive support and satisfies the strict criterion.
This proves the exact identification.
Now the displayed proportional coverage gives
$(1-\varepsilon)X_k<P_k\le X_k$ eventually, for each fixed
$\varepsilon$, and hence both location limits.

The global twin asymptotic also supplies a positive-power number of
width-two windows in every sufficiently large dyadic interval.
Corollary~\ref{cor:power} for every $a<1/3$, together with the
canonical upper bound, proves the count limit.
\end{proof}
The usual Hardy--Littlewood twin conjecture supplies a particular
positive constant $C$; the proof uses no value of that constant.
The fixed-tuple asymptotic and the stronger uniform tuple assumptions
used for short-interval distribution should be distinguished
\cite[(1) and Theorem~1]{Gallagher}.
Bare twin-prime infinitude does not supply the proportional coverage
used here. Nor does the displayed asymptotic give a polylogarithmic
or square-root bound for $X_k-P_k$ along these particular cutoffs.
A limit on $\LL(P_k)/k$ also does not imply
$P_k/\EE(k/3)\to1$: a constant shift in the inner exponent already
changes the original endpoint by a large multiplicative factor.


\section{Prime-product errors and the full coefficient reference}\label{sec:profile}
We first develop the finer comparison for the ordinary integer law.
Section~\ref{sec:family-rh} then extends the product and coefficient
criteria to qualifying local laws. The later arithmetic capacity,
area and localized-count results retain their stated ordinary-law
scope. We keep the arithmetic cutoff $x$ and the rank $r$ distinct:
$x$ tends to infinity,
while $r$ is comparable to $\log\log x$. All prime products in these
sections include $p\le x$. For the prefix before an ascent prime $p$,
the corresponding cutoff is $x=p-1$.

\subsection{Classical analytic quantities}
Write
\[
 \theta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p,\qquad
 \psi(x)=\sum_{p^a\le x}\log p,\qquad
 \kappa(t)=\frac{1/\log t+1/(\log t)^2}{t^2}.
\]
For sufficiently large $x$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analytic-objects}
\begin{split}
 J(x)&=\int_x^\infty(\psi(t)-t)\kappa(t)\,dt,\\
 K(x)&=\int_x^\infty(\theta(t)-t)\kappa(t)\,dt,\\
 P(x)&=J(x)-K(x)
       =\int_x^\infty(\psi(t)-\theta(t))\kappa(t)\,dt,\\
 f(x)&=e^\gamma\log\theta(x)
          \prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p),\qquad F(x)=\log f(x).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The quantitative prime number theorem gives convergence of these
integrals. These are Nicolas's quantities
\cite[(1.9), (1.15)--(1.19)]{Nicolas2012}; $P$ retains every prime power.
Let
\[
 A(x)=\sum_{p>x}\bigl(-\log(1-1/p)-1/p\bigr),
\]
and, when $\theta(x)>1$, let
\[
 D(x)=\frac{\theta(x)-x}{x\log x}
       -\bigl(\log\log\theta(x)-\log\log x\bigr).
\]
Partial summation and the convergent logarithmic prime-power series give
the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-nicolas}
 F(x)=K(x)-D(x)+A(x)=J(x)-P(x)-D(x)+A(x).
\end{equation}
Here $A(x)\ge0$, $A(x)=O(1/x)$, and $D(x)\ge0$ by concavity of
$\log\log t$ on $t>1$. No sign is assigned to $J(x)$.

We use the following classical facts, with their hypotheses retained.
Nicolas's criterion is equivalent to eventual $F(x)<0$. Under RH,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nicolas-wave}
 F(x)=-\frac{2+W(x)}{\sqrt{x}\log x}
          +O\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt{x}(\log x)^2}\right),
 \qquad |W(x)|\le\beta_0,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 W(x)=\operatorname{Re}\sum_\rho
       \frac{x^{i\operatorname{Im}\rho}}{\rho(1-\rho)},
 \qquad \beta_0=2+\gamma-\log(4\pi)<2.
\]
The zeros in this absolutely convergent series are counted with
multiplicity. Defining the real projection explicitly matches the
maintained formal statement. The expansion and its constant are
classical \cite[Proposition~2.1]{Nicolas2012}. In the decomposition
\eqref{eq:exact-nicolas}, the zero wave comes from $J$, while
\[
 P(x)\sim\frac2{\sqrt{x}\log x}
\]
holds unconditionally and gives the leading prime-square contribution.
Under RH the standard bounds
$|\psi(x)-x|+|\theta(x)-x|=O(\sqrt{x}(\log x)^2)$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlinear-error}
 D(x)=O((\log x)^3/x),\qquad
 |F(x)-K(x)|=O((\log x)^3/x).
\end{equation}
Indeed the second derivative of $\log\log t$ is
$-(\log t+1)/(t^2(\log t)^2)$; Taylor's integral remainder, with
$\theta(x)/x\to1$, proves the first estimate, and
\eqref{eq:exact-nicolas} proves the second.

If RH fails, $F$ has excursions of both signs of order $x^{-b}$ for
some $0<b<1/2$ \cite{Nicolas1983,Nicolas2012}. Section~\ref{sec:excursions}
records the zero-dependent version used in the coefficient transfer.
In particular, the estimate for $D$ in \eqref{eq:nonlinear-error}
is never used under the assumption that RH is false.

\subsection{An entire profile and two different clocks}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-def}
\begin{aligned}
 G_x(z)&=\prod_{p\le x}\left(1+\frac z{p-1}\right),&
 a_r(x)&=[z^r]G_x(z),\\
 \mathcal H(z)&=\prod_p
       \left(1+\frac z{p-1}\right)(1-1/p)^z,&
 C_r(u)&=[z^r]\mathcal H(z)e^{uz}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The power $(1-1/p)^z$ uses the real logarithm of its positive base.
The product for $\mathcal H$ converges uniformly on compact subsets
of $\mathbb C$: the logarithmic first-order terms cancel and the
remaining tails are bounded by a constant times $\sum_p p^{-2}$.
It defines an entire function, positive on the nonnegative real axis,
with $\mathcal H(0)=\mathcal H(1)=1$.

The finite generating product is classical in this problem
\cite[(1.6)]{ET}; the normalized Euler factor also appears in the
mod-Poisson description of prime divisibility \cite[\S4]{KN}.
Our reference retains all its Taylor coefficients. It is not a
finite-order truncation of a saddle-point expansion.

Let $\mathcal H_x$ be the finite product in \eqref{eq:profile-def}, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-clocks}
 B(x)=\log G_x(1),\qquad
 L(x)=\gamma+\log\log\theta(x).
\end{equation}
Then $F(x)=L(x)-B(x)$ and, exactly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-factorization}
 G_x(z)=\mathcal H_x(z)e^{B(x)z}.
\end{equation}
For positive supported degrees define
\[
 R_r(x)=\frac{a_{r-1}(x)}{a_r(x)},\qquad
 \mathcal R_r(u)=\frac{C_{r-1}(u)}{C_r(u)},\qquad
 \Delta_r(x)=R_r(x)-\mathcal R_r(L(x)).
\]
The denominator conditions below ensure that these ratios have their
ordinary real-valued meaning.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform coefficient transfer]\label{thm:linearization}
Fix $0<a\le b<\infty$. There exist $X$ and $K>0$ such that, for every
real $x\ge X$ and integer $r$ with $a\le r/L(x)\le b$, both denominators
are positive and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearization}
 \left|\Delta_r(x)-\frac r{L(x)^2}F(x)\right|
 \le K\left(\frac{|F(x)|}{L(x)^2}+\frac1{rx}\right).
\end{equation}
The constants may depend on $a,b$, but not on $r$ or $x$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal H(z)=\sum_{j\ge0}h_jz^j$ and
\[
 Q_r(u)=\frac{r!C_r(u)}{u^r}
       =\sum_{j=0}^r h_j\frac{(r)_j}{u^j}.
\]
For $t=r/u$ in a fixed positive compact interval, the falling-factorial
estimate
\[
 \left|\frac{(r)_j}{r^j}-1+\frac{j(j-1)}{2r}\right|
 \le \frac{j^4}{r^2}
\]
holds also for $j>r$ if $(r)_j$ is extended by zero. For $j\le r$,
apply the first two inclusion-exclusion bounds to
$\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(1-i/r)$; the other case is immediate.
Cauchy coefficient bounds on a circle of radius larger than the
compact interval justify summation with the factor $j^4$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-expansion}
 Q_r(u)=\mathcal H(t)-\frac{t^2\mathcal H''(t)}{2r}
                  +O(r^{-2}).
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal H(t)>0$, the normalized denominators are bounded
away from zero. Using $C_r'=C_{r-1}$ and the same expansion for
$r-1,r-2$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reference-slope}
 \mathcal R_r'(u)
 =\frac{C_{r-2}(u)}{C_r(u)}-\mathcal R_r(u)^2
 =-\frac r{u^2}+O(r/u^3).
\end{equation}
The first-order profile terms cancel in this paired difference.

On any fixed larger circle,
$\mathcal H_x-\mathcal H=O(1/x)$ by the quadratic prime tail.
To compare ratios without losing the cancellation, use
\[
 \frac{C_{r-1}(u)}{C_r(u)}
 =\frac r u\left(1+\frac{Q_{r-1}(u)-Q_r(u)}{Q_r(u)}\right).
\]
The identity $(r)_j-(r-1)_j=j(r-1)_{j-1}$ supplies a factor $1/u$
in the numerator difference. Replacing $\mathcal H$ by $\mathcal H_x$
therefore changes this ratio by $O(1/(rx))$, uniformly in the compact
rank range. Equation~\eqref{eq:finite-factorization} yields
\[
 R_r(x)=\mathcal R_r(B(x))+O(1/(rx)).
\]
Unconditionally $F(x)=o(1)$ by PNT and Mertens's theorem.
Integrate \eqref{eq:reference-slope} between $L$ and $B=L-F$.
The main term is $rF/L^2$; its error is
$O(|F|/L^2)$ since $r$ is comparable to $L$ and $F=o(1)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:linearization}.
\end{proof}

The coefficient expansion belongs to the saddle-point and mod-Poisson
tradition \cite{ET,KN,FMN}. The sign-preserving comparison
\eqref{eq:linearization}, its theta centering, and its application to
the actual ascent ratio are the potentially new statements here.
Any fixed error $O(L^{-m})$ would be larger than the RH signal
$1/(L\sqrt{x}\log x)$.

\section{RH criteria and quantitative excursions}\label{sec:rh}
\begin{theorem}[Moving-rank RH criterion]\label{thm:rh-ratio}
Fix $\alpha>0$ and set $r(x)=\lfloor\alpha L(x)\rfloor$.
Then RH is equivalent to
\[
 R_{r(x)}(x)<\mathcal R_{r(x)}(L(x))
 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }x.
\]
Under RH, more precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-wave}
 \frac{L(x)^2}{r(x)}\sqrt{x}\log x\,\Delta_{r(x)}(x)
       =-2-W(x)+o(1).
\end{equation}
If RH is false, there exists one $b\in(0,1/2)$ such that, for every
fixed $\alpha>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-excursions}
 \limsup_{x\to\infty}x^bL(x)\Delta_{r(x)}(x)=+\infty,\qquad
 \liminf_{x\to\infty}x^bL(x)\Delta_{r(x)}(x)=-\infty.
\end{equation}
The witnessing endpoints may depend on $\alpha$ and on the sign.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The floor rank is eventually in a fixed positive compact band.
Under RH, substitute \eqref{eq:nicolas-wave} into
\eqref{eq:linearization}. After multiplication by
$(L^2/r)\sqrt{x}\log x$, the two errors are respectively
$O(1/L)$ and $O((\log x)/\sqrt{x})$. Both vanish, giving
\eqref{eq:ratio-wave}. Since $2-\beta_0>0$, the sign is eventually
negative.

Under false RH, Theorem~\ref{thm:power-excursions} below supplies $b$
and excursions of $F$ with arbitrarily large amplitudes.
In a band $a\le r/L\le b_1$, \eqref{eq:linearization} gives, eventually,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F\ge0&\ \Longrightarrow\ L\Delta_r\ge(a/2)F-K/(ax),\\
 F\le0&\ \Longrightarrow\ L\Delta_r\le(a/2)F+K/(ax).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $b_1$ denotes the fixed band endpoint, not the excursion exponent.
For the floor rank one may take $a=\alpha/2$.
Since $x^{-1}=o(x^{-b})$, both excursions transfer with arbitrarily
large amplitudes. This proves \eqref{eq:ratio-excursions} and the
reverse implication.
\end{proof}

The equality \eqref{eq:ratio-wave} is an ordinary corollary of the
compiled linearization and spectral estimates. The maintained
criterion and the quantitative false-RH consumer are separate named
declarations; the ledger distinguishes these verification scopes.

\begin{samepage}
\subsection{The corrected logarithm at power-profile peaks}\label{sec:excursions}
\begin{theorem}[Zero-dependent excursions]\label{thm:power-excursions}
Suppose $\zeta(\rho)=0$ with $1/2<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$.
For every
\[
 1-\operatorname{Re}\rho<b\le1/2
\]
and each $U\in\{J,K,F\}$,
\[
 \limsup_{x\to\infty}x^bU(x)=+\infty,\qquad
 \liminf_{x\to\infty}x^bU(x)=-\infty.
\]
Consequently, false RH supplies a common exponent $0<b<1/2$.
\end{theorem}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
We give the analytic mechanism and the endpoint correction explicitly.
For $a=\log3$, initially in $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, put
\[
 \widehat J(s)=\int_a^\infty J(e^u)e^{-su}\,du,\qquad
 T(z)=\int_3^\infty(\psi(t)-t)t^{-z-1}\,dt.
\]
The Mellin identity is
$T(z)=-\zeta'(z)/(z\zeta(z))-1/(z-1)$ minus the entire contribution
of the finite interval from $1$ to $3$. Integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:landau-ode}
 s\widehat J''(s)+2\widehat J'(s)
 =T'(s+1)-T(s+1)+a^2J(3)e^{-as}.
\end{equation}
The cancellation at $z=1$ and the quantitative PNT extend
$\widehat J$ across $s=0$ and along the real interval $s>-1/2$.
At $s=\rho-1$, the double pole from $T'$ cannot cancel with the
simple pole from $T$. Thus $\widehat J$ cannot be holomorphic there.

An eventual bound $J(x)\ge-Cx^{-b}$ would make
$J(e^u)+Ce^{-bu}$ eventually nonnegative. Its Laplace transform has
the preceding nonreal singularity, but is analytic on the real
interval $s>-b$. Landau's singularity principle contradicts this:
its abscissa of convergence lies between
$\operatorname{Re}\rho-1$ and $0$, hence strictly above $-b$.
Applying the same argument to $-J$ rules out the opposite bound.
This is the classical Landau method; see \cite[Theorem~3]{Lay}.
Since $P(x)=O(x^{-1/2}/\log x)=o(x^{-b})$, $K=J-P$ inherits both signs.

For negative excursions, $F\le K+A$ and $A=O(1/x)$ suffice.
For positive excursions one must control $D$ without RH.
Fix $A_0>0$, and choose an integer local maximum $x=n+1$, $n\ge2$,
of the sequence $K(n)-A_0n^{-b}$ between two lower values bracketing a high positive value. Such maxima exist arbitrarily far out
because of the two unbounded excursions of $K$; rounding real
endpoints to integers costs $O((\log x)/x)=o(x^{-b})$.

The two adjacent comparisons are
\[
 K(n)-A_0n^{-b}\le K(n+1)-A_0(n+1)^{-b},
\]
\[
 K(n+2)-A_0(n+2)^{-b}\le K(n+1)-A_0(n+1)^{-b}.
\]
On each open unit interval $\theta$ is constant. Using
$K(v)-K(w)=\int_v^w(\theta(t)-t)\kappa(t)\,dt$ and the possible
endpoint jump of at most $\log x$ gives
\[
 0<\theta(x)-x\le(2A_0+1)x^{1-b}\log x.
\]
Taylor's integral remainder for $\log\log$ and $A(x)\ge0$ now yield
\[
 F(x)\ge K(x)
 -\frac{(2A_0+1)^2}{2}(\log x+1)x^{-2b}.
\]
The loss is $o(x^{-b})$. Choosing $A_0$ larger than any prescribed
amplitude proves the positive excursions of $F$. Finally, if RH
fails, symmetry of the zeros supplies such a $\rho$; choose any
$b$ strictly between $1-\operatorname{Re}\rho$ and $1/2$.
\end{proof}
The existence of false-RH oscillations of $F$ is classical
\cite{Nicolas1983,Nicolas2012}. The displayed proof records the
zero-dependent exponent and the exact nonlinear correction used by
the formal coefficient consumer.

\subsection{Integral, clock and positive-tilt criteria}
Define
\[
 \mathcal C(x)=\exp\bigl(e^{-\gamma}G_x(1)\bigr).
\]
The exact identity $\mathcal C(x)=\theta(x)^{1/f(x)}$ shows that
eventual $\theta(x)<\mathcal C(x)$ is another form of Nicolas's
criterion. Combining the preceding results gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rh-equivalences}
 \mathrm{RH}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K(x)<0\ \hbox{eventually}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \theta(x)<\mathcal C(x)\ \hbox{eventually}.
\end{equation}
Under RH,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock-wave}
 \frac{\mathcal C(x)-\theta(x)}{\sqrt{x}}
       =2+W(x)+o(1).
\end{equation}
Indeed $\log\mathcal C-\log\theta=(1/f-1)\log\theta$;
\eqref{eq:nicolas-wave}, $\theta(x)/x\to1$, and
$e^t=1+t+O(t^2)$ prove \eqref{eq:clock-wave}.
This is a change of variables in the classical Nicolas estimate.

For fixed $z>0$, put
\[
 T_z(x)=\log\left(
       \frac{e^{\gamma z}\mathcal H(z)(\log\theta(x))^z}{G_x(z)}
       \right).
\]
Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilt-tail}
 T_z(x)=zF(x)+\sum_{p>x}
 \left\{\log\left(1+\frac z{p-1}\right)
                 -z\log\left(1+\frac1{p-1}\right)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $z$ the tail is $O_z(1/x)$. It is nonpositive for
$z\ge1$, and lies between $0$ and $Cz/x$ for $0<z\le1$,
with an absolute $C$. Concavity of $\log(1+zw)$ proves the
signs; the upper bound follows from
$\log(1+zw)\le zw$ and $w-\log(1+w)\le w^2/2$.
Therefore
\[
 \mathrm{RH}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T_z(x)<0\ \hbox{eventually}
\]
for each fixed $z>0$. Under RH a \emph{single} sufficiently large
cutoff works simultaneously for all $z>0$, by splitting at $z=1$
in \eqref{eq:tilt-tail}; the converse follows from $T_1=F$.

The corresponding positive kernel is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \kappa_z(t)&=
 \frac{z}{(t-1)(t-1+z)\log t}
 +\frac{\log(1+z/(t-1))}{t(\log t)^2},\\
 I_z(x)&=\int_x^\infty(\theta(t)-t)\kappa_z(t)\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $t\ge e$,
$|\kappa_z(t)-z\kappa(t)|\le14(z+z^2)/(t^3\log t)$.
Chebyshev's bound gives $I_z=zK+O_z(1/(x\log x))$.
It follows that eventual $I_z<0$ is equivalent to RH and that
\[
 \sqrt{x}\log x\, I_z(x)=-z(2+W(x))+o(1)
\]
under RH. Under false RH every fixed positive tilt inherits the
zero-dependent two-sided integral excursions.
These tilt criteria transport the same leading error; they do not
supply independent sign information by themselves.

\section{RH transfer across qualifying local laws}\label{sec:family-rh}

\subsection{Qualification and the exact family constants}
Fix an independent local law with probabilities $0\le\eta_p\le1$.
For an arithmetic ensemble, identifying this model with its densities also
requires independence of its limiting finite-prime divisibility laws.
Marginal probabilities alone do not establish that identification. Put
\[
 w_p=\begin{cases}\eta_p/(1-\eta_p),&\eta_p<1,\\0,&\eta_p=1.\end{cases}
\]
A forced coordinate is removed from the odds product and retained in
the rank shift below. The concrete qualification condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-quadratic}
 \nu>0,\quad D\ge0,\quad w_p\ge0,\qquad
 \left|w_p-\frac{\nu}{p-1}\right|\le\frac{D}{(p-1)^2}
 \quad\hbox{for every prime }p.
\end{equation}
All parameters and the family are fixed before the cutoff tends to infinity.
The condition follows from
$\eta_p=\nu/p+O(p^{-2})$ along the primes, including arbitrary finite
exceptions. Indeed, eventually $\eta_p\le1/2$, so
\[
 \frac{\eta_p}{1-\eta_p}
 =\eta_p+\frac{\eta_p^2}{1-\eta_p}
 =\frac{\nu}{p}+O(p^{-2})
 =\frac{\nu}{p-1}+O(p^{-2}).
\]
The finite head is absorbed into $D$. This argument is formalized for
an explicit prime-tail bound as well as a bound at every prime.
A limiting reciprocal offset is not required for this RH transfer;
that stronger hypothesis remains part of the reversal-spectrum theorem.

For integer $N\ge1$, define
\begin{align*}
 G_{\eta,N}(z)&=\prod_{p\le N}(1+w_pz),&
 a_{\eta,r}(N)&=[z^r]G_{\eta,N}(z),\\
 \mathcal H_\eta(z)&=\prod_p(1+w_pz)(1-1/p)^{\nu z},&
 C_{\eta,r}(u)&=[z^r]\mathcal H_\eta(z)e^{uz}.
\end{align*}
The complex powers use the real logarithm of the positive base $1-1/p$.
Write
\[
 \kappa_\eta(z)=\log\mathcal H_\eta(z),\qquad
 \Gamma_\eta(z)=e^{\nu z\gamma}\mathcal H_\eta(z)
 \quad(z\ge0).
\]
These are exact convergent constants; exceptional primes remain in the
products. If $v_p=1/(p-1)$, then $w_p\le(\nu+D)v_p$. The elementary bounds
$0\le t-\log(1+t)\le t^2/2$ give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\log(1+w_pz)-\nu z\log(1+v_p)\right|
 &\le K_\eta(z)v_p^2,\\
 K_\eta(z)&=\frac{z^2(\nu+D)^2}{2}+zD+\frac{\nu z}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since $\sum_{p>N}v_p^2\le2/N$, we have the explicit bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-log-tail}
 \left|\kappa_\eta(z)-
   \sum_{p\le N}\bigl(\log(1+w_pz)-\nu z\log(1+v_p)\bigr)\right|
 \le \frac{2K_\eta(z)}N .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Family product criteria]\label{thm:family-product}
Under \eqref{eq:family-quadratic}, fix $z>0$ and set
\[
 E_\eta(N,z)=
 \nu z\bigl(\gamma+\log\log\theta(N)\bigr)+\kappa_\eta(z)
 -\log G_{\eta,N}(z).
\]
For $\theta(N)>1$,
\[
 |E_\eta(N,z)-\nu zF(N)|\le 2K_\eta(z)/N.
\]
RH is equivalent to each of the following eventual statements:
\[
 E_\eta(N,z)<0,\qquad E_\eta(N,z)\le A/N,\qquad
 \Gamma_\eta(z)(\log\theta(N))^{\nu z}<G_{\eta,N}(z),
\]
where $A$ is any fixed real number.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The finite identity
$F(N)=\gamma+\log\log\theta(N)-\sum_{p\le N}\log(1+v_p)$
and \eqref{eq:family-log-tail} give the error bound.
Under RH, the Nicolas negative margin dominates every fixed multiple
of $1/N$. If RH fails, its positive integer-cutoff excursions exceed
every such multiple. Multiplication by the fixed positive number
$\nu z$ preserves both conclusions. Positivity of all factors turns
the logarithmic criterion into the displayed product inequality.
These are applications of Nicolas's criterion and error estimates
\cite[(1.10), Proposition~2.1]{Nicolas2012}.
\end{proof}

There is a broader sufficient qualification for the product theorem.
For fixed nonnegative weights and fixed $\nu,z>0$, it is enough that
some real $\kappa$ and $C\ge0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-general-tail}
 \left|\kappa-\sum_{p\le N}
   \bigl(\log(1+w_pz)-\nu z\log(1+v_p)\bigr)\right|
 \le C/N
 \quad\hbox{eventually}.
\end{equation}
The same proof gives the product criterion with constant
$\exp(\nu z\gamma+\kappa)$. This qualification contains neither
$\theta$ nor an RH sign assumption. It is formalized separately.
We do not assert that either sufficient condition is a necessary
classification of every family that might admit an RH criterion.

\subsection{The coefficient ratio and deterministic rank shifts}
Let
\[
 U_\eta(N)=\nu L(N),\qquad
 R_{\eta,r}(N)=\frac{a_{\eta,r-1}(N)}{a_{\eta,r}(N)},\qquad
 \overline R_{\eta,r}(u)=\frac{C_{\eta,r-1}(u)}{C_{\eta,r}(u)}.
\]
Here $L(N)=\gamma+\log\log\theta(N)$ as before.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform family coefficient transfer]\label{thm:family-ratio}
Assume \eqref{eq:family-quadratic}. On every fixed positive compact
band for $r/U_\eta(N)$, both denominators are positive for large
$N,r$, and for a constant $K$ depending on the law and band,
\begin{align}
 &\left|R_{\eta,r}(N)-\overline R_{\eta,r}(U_\eta(N))
       -\frac{r}{U_\eta(N)^2}\nu F(N)\right|\notag\\
 &\hspace{1cm}\le
 K\left(\frac{|\nu F(N)|}{U_\eta(N)^2}+\frac1{rN}\right).
 \label{eq:family-linearization}
\end{align}
For every fixed $\alpha>0$, put $r(N)=\lfloor\alpha U_\eta(N)\rfloor$.
Each of the eventual inequalities
\[
 R_{\eta,r(N)}(N)<\overline R_{\eta,r(N)}(U_\eta(N)),
 \qquad
 R_{\eta,r(N)}(N)\le\overline R_{\eta,r(N)}(U_\eta(N))
\]
is equivalent to RH.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $|z|\le R$, the normalized local factor differs from one by at most
$K_R^{\mathbb C}v_p^2$, where one may take
\[
 K_R^{\mathbb C}=
 \bigl((D+\nu/2)R+\nu^2R^2e^{\nu R}\bigr)e^{\nu R}.
\]
The product therefore defines an entire function and its finite
prefix approximates it uniformly on each closed disk with error
$O_R(N^{-1})$. The proof uses product differences, so zeros of
individual factors on a disk cause no division problem. On $0\le t\le b$,
\[
 \mathcal H_\eta(t)\ge
 \exp\left(-K_\eta(b)\sum_p v_p^2\right)>0.
\]
Cauchy's estimate therefore bounds the coefficient of degree $j$ in
the prefix error by $O_R(N^{-1}R^{-j})$.

Apply the factorial-polynomial argument of Section~\ref{sec:profile}
to this entire profile. If $h_j=[z^j]\mathcal H_\eta(z)$, its normalized
coefficient polynomial is
\[
 P_r(t)=\sum_{j=0}^r h_jt^j\frac{(r)_j}{r^j}.
\]
The entire coefficient series and its derivatives give uniform
approximations to $\mathcal H_\eta$ and $\mathcal H_\eta'$ on a
compact interval. The displayed positive lower bound controls the
denominator. The exact identity
\[
 \overline R_{\eta,r}(u)
 =t-\frac{t^2}{r}\frac{P_r'(t)}{P_r(t)},\qquad t=r/u,
\]
then gives a paired prefix/reference error $O((rN)^{-1})$ and reference
slope $-r/u^2+O(u^{-2})$. These are the same general polynomial
estimates used for Theorem~\ref{thm:linearization}; their hypotheses
have now been proved for $\mathcal H_\eta$.

The actual finite product is restored at
$B_\eta(N)=\nu\sum_{p\le N}\log(1+v_p)$, and
$U_\eta(N)-B_\eta(N)=\nu F(N)$ exactly. Integrating the slope over
the interval between these two clocks proves
\eqref{eq:family-linearization}. The floor-rank band has positive
lower and upper bounds. The negative RH margin and the positive
false-RH excursions dominate its inverse-cutoff error, giving
both asserted equivalences.
\end{proof}

To interpret the ratio arithmetically, put
\[
 f_N=\#\{p\le N:\eta_p=1\},\qquad
 M_{\eta,N}(j)=[z^j]\prod_{p\le N}(1-\eta_p+\eta_pz).
\]
For $r\ge1$ the exact identity, with total division at a zero denominator, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-forced-ratio}
 \frac{M_{\eta,N}(f_N+r-1)}{M_{\eta,N}(f_N+r)}
   =R_{\eta,r}(N).
\end{equation}
Indeed, replace each forced probability by zero, shift by its exact
count, and factor out the strictly positive survival product of the
remaining coordinates. Excluded coordinates contribute zero odds and
no rank shift. Both the identity and its two RH consumers are
formalized. At a prime prefix $N=p-1$, the corresponding distinct-factor
rank is $k=f_N+r+1$.

Every reciprocal-smooth law qualifies by its proved odds estimate.
Consequently every fixed affine prime-pair family in
Section~\ref{sec:affine} has these product and mass-ratio RH criteria,
with $\nu=m$ and its exact residue probabilities.
No bounded-gap or short-interval hypothesis is needed for these
equivalences. Arithmetic interpretation still takes the input-size
limit first, with the finite prime prefix fixed. Sparse support
families require a separate qualification check; the affine
constructor makes no assertion about them.

\subsection{Reference-scale constants and combinations of families}
The same entire-profile estimates give a locally unique positive-level
reference crossing with
\[
 \overline R_{\eta,r}(u_{r,s})=s,\qquad
 u_{r,s}=\frac r s-\frac{\mathcal H_\eta'(s)}{\mathcal H_\eta(s)}
          +O_{\eta,s}(r^{-1}).
\]
For fixed $g\ge0$, take $s=1+g/\nu$ and
$T_{\eta,r,g}=\exp(\exp(u_{r,s}/\nu-\gamma))$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-reference-constant}
 \log\log T_{\eta,r,g}
 =\frac r{g+\nu}-\gamma
   -\frac1\nu\frac{\mathcal H_\eta'(1+g/\nu)}
                    {\mathcal H_\eta(1+g/\nu)}
   +O_{\eta,g}(r^{-1}).
\end{equation}
This generalizes the reference constant, including every finite
exception in $\mathcal H_\eta$. In a density application, the free
rank $r$ must still be replaced by $k-f_N-1$.
The theorem describes the reference crossing. Identifying the actual
last ascent additionally requires the stated arithmetic supply and
sufficient precision of the actual local gap threshold.

Finally, for a fixed finite collection of qualifying laws and fixed
real coefficients $a_i$, let $z_i\ge0$ and $\lambda=\sum_i a_i\nu_i z_i$.
The exact family bounds imply
\[
 \left|\sum_i a_iE_{\eta_i}(N,z_i)-\lambda F(N)\right|
 \le\frac1N\sum_i |a_i|\,2K_{\eta_i}(z_i).
\]
If $\lambda>0$, eventual negativity of this combination is another RH
criterion. If $\lambda=0$, only the inverse-cutoff remainder remains.
Thus fixed finite family averaging retains one common arithmetic
signal. An infinite or cutoff-dependent collection needs a separate
uniform summability argument; no such transfer is asserted here.

\section{Reference crossings and RH ascent capacities}\label{sec:capacity}
The preceding centered criterion concerns $R_r-\mathcal R_r(L)$.
A raw ascent instead concerns $R_r-(g+1)$ for an actual gap $g$.
We now keep both quantities in the comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp reference crossing]\label{thm:root-shift}
For every fixed $s>0$ there are $A,C>0$ and $r_0$ such that, for
$r\ge r_0$, the equation $\mathcal R_r(u)=s$ has a unique solution
$u_{r,s}$ in $[r/s-A,r/s+A]$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-shift}
 u_{r,s}=\frac r s-\frac{\mathcal H'(s)}{\mathcal H(s)}
                         +O_s(1/r).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expanding the adjacent normalized coefficients as in
\eqref{eq:profile-expansion} gives, for $t=r/u$ in a compact
neighborhood of $s$,
\[
 \mathcal R_r(u)
 =t-\frac{t^2}{r}\frac{\mathcal H'(t)}{\mathcal H(t)}
       +O(r^{-2}).
\]
Its values on either side of $r/s$ at a sufficiently large fixed
distance straddle $s$. The derivative \eqref{eq:reference-slope}
is strictly negative throughout that interval, giving existence
and uniqueness. Substitute $u=r/s+v$, with bounded $v$, into the
last display to obtain \eqref{eq:root-shift}.
\end{proof}

For $s=h+1$, put $X_{r,h}=\EE(u_{r,h+1}-\gamma)$.
The reference crossing admits a local spatial interpretation.
Fix a comparison constant $D_0\ge1$. In the window
\[
 B(x)\in[r/(h+1)-A,r/(h+1)+A],\qquad
 \mathcal C(x)\le D_0x,\qquad X_{r,h}\le D_0x,
\]
the maintained coefficient comparison gives, for sufficiently large
$r,x$ and a constant $K$ depending on the fixed parameters,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-spatial}
\begin{split}
 X_{r,h}-\mathcal C(x)>K\log x&\ \Longrightarrow\ R_r(x)>h+1,\\
 \mathcal C(x)-X_{r,h}>K\log x&\ \Longrightarrow\ R_r(x)<h+1.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed $R_r(x)-\mathcal R_r(B(x))=O(1/(rx))$, whereas
$|\mathcal R_r'|\gg1/r$ in the window. The uncertainty in $B$
is $O(1/x)$; the derivative of $\EE(u-\gamma)$ is
$\EE(u-\gamma)\log\EE(u-\gamma)$, giving the stated spatial margin.
For an actual gap $h$, these are ascent/descent tests.
Equation~\eqref{eq:clock-wave} locates their RH transition in the
$\theta$ coordinate, with displacement $(2+W(x))\sqrt{x}+o(\sqrt{x})$.

\begin{theorem}[RH endpoint and reversal-count envelope]\label{thm:rh-capacity}
Assume RH. Let $P_k$ be the lower prime of the last ordinary ascent
and $N_k=N_{\rm ordinary}(k)$. For all sufficiently large $k$, both
are attained, and with $r=k-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rh-endpoint}
 \theta(P_k-1)<\EE(u_{r,3}-\gamma).
\end{equation}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually
\[
 (1-\varepsilon)N_k\log N_k\le\theta(P_k-1),\qquad
 N_k\le(1+\varepsilon)
 \frac{\EE(u_{r,3}-\gamma)}
      {\log\EE(u_{r,3}-\gamma)}.
\]
There is a constant $C$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rh-count}
 \log\log N_k\le
 \frac{k-1}{3}-\gamma-\frac{\mathcal H'(3)}{\mathcal H(3)}
                      +\frac C{k-1}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The unconditional lower reversal bound supplies a last ascent whose
endpoint is at least double-exponential in a positive multiple of
$k$. The unconditional upper cutoff puts it below
$\EE((1/3+\varepsilon)k)$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$.
PNT therefore places $r/L(P_k-1)$ in a fixed positive compact band.
Under RH, Theorem~\ref{thm:linearization} and
\eqref{eq:nicolas-wave} give
$R_r(P_k-1)<\mathcal R_r(L(P_k-1))$ uniformly in that band.
The actual ascent requires $R_r>g+1\ge3$.

Near $u_{r,3}$, the reference is strictly decreasing. Away from
its fixed neighborhood the uniform leading expansion separates
its value from $3$. These two comparisons imply
$L(P_k-1)<u_{r,3}$, which is \eqref{eq:rh-endpoint}.
Every separated witness uses a distinct ascent index, so
$N_k\le\pi(P_k)$. PNT yields the first two count inequalities.
Equivalently one can count all primes with $\theta(p-1)$ below
the reference endpoint; this monotone capacity has asymptotic
$T/\log T$ as $T\to\infty$.

Taking two logarithms of that capacity gives
$u_{r,3}-\gamma+o(1/r)$: the correction from division by $\log T$
is negligible on this scale, and the multiplicative PNT error is
bounded eventually. Equation~\eqref{eq:root-shift} now proves
\eqref{eq:rh-count}.
\end{proof}

The same comparison keeps the actual gap of each ascent:
in a fixed positive compact rank band, RH implies
\[
 g_i+1<\mathcal R_{k-1}(L(p_i-1)).
\]
Thus, on any sufficiently late finite band of prime indices, the
number of ascents is at most the number of indices satisfying this
reference inequality with their \emph{actual} $g_i$. Adding the
number of earlier primes bounds every reversal family.
Combining this upper count with the unconditional reversal supply
forces many reference-eligible actual gaps, but does not lower their
maximum width without an additional distribution argument.

Neither the square-root clock displacement nor the $O(1/k)$
count correction changes a leading coefficient measured by
$\log\log X/k$. The rank is fixed in the raw reversal count;
it moves with $x$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-ratio}.

\section{Persistent excursions and positive-area criteria}\label{sec:area}
The pointwise criterion also admits a one-sided integral formulation.
This retains information lost by the leading reversal spectrum.
Write $v_+=\max(v,0)$ and define
\begin{align*}
 Q_F(X)&=\int_X^{2X}F(t)_+\,dt,\\
 S_\alpha(x)&=\frac{L(x)^2}{\lfloor\alpha L(x)\rfloor}
                \Delta_{\lfloor\alpha L(x)\rfloor}(x),\\
 Q_\alpha(X)&=\int_X^{2X}(S_\alpha(t))_+\,dt .
\end{align*}
All statements below concern sufficiently large $X$; the rank and
coefficient denominators are then positive. Arbitrary definitions on
the bounded initial range do not change an eventual criterion.

\begin{lemma}[Persistence at a controlled peak]\label{lem:persistence}
If RH is false, there is $0<b<1/2$ such that for every fixed
$A>0$ and $d<1-b/2$, arbitrarily large integers $n$ satisfy
\[
 F(y)>A n^{-b}\qquad(n\le y\le n+n^d).
\]
More precisely, controlled positive peaks with $K(n)>A_0n^{-b}$
give intervals of length
$c_{A_0}n^{1-b/2}/\sqrt{\log n}$ on which
$F(y)\ge(A_0/2)n^{-b}$, for some fixed $c_{A_0}>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two-sided peak construction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:power-excursions} supplies arbitrarily late integer
peaks $x$ with $K(x)>A_0x^{-b}$ and
$0<\theta(x)-x\le(2A_0+1)x^{1-b}\log x$.
For $0<h\le x$ and $x\le y\le x+h$, distinct integer prime
positions give
\[
 |\theta(y)-y|\le M:=(2A_0+1)x^{1-b}\log x+3(h+1)\log x
\]
eventually. The kernel satisfies
$\kappa(t)\le2/(x^2\log x)$ on this interval.
Taylor's integral remainder for $\log\log t$, on arguments at
least $x/2$, gives
\[
 F(y)\ge K(x)-\frac{2Mh+8M^2}{x^2\log x}
 \quad\hbox{when }M\le x/2.
\]
The nonnegative tail $A(y)$ was discarded only in this lower bound.
Take $h=\eta x^{1-b/2}/\sqrt{\log x}$, with
$\eta=\sqrt{A_0}/100$. The term $2Mh$, after the displayed
normalization and division by $x^{-b}$, tends to zero.
Using
$M^2\le2(2A_0+1)^2x^{2-2b}\log^2x+
18(h+1)^2\log^2x$, the remaining loss has limsup at most
$144\eta^2<A_0/2$. Also $M=o(x)$.
This proves the more precise assertion. Choose $A_0>2A$;
every fixed $x^d$ with $d<1-b/2$ is eventually shorter than $h$.
\end{proof}
The maintained formal proof establishes every fixed-power version
and the finite loss envelope. The logarithmic critical-length
refinement is the ordinary calculation displayed above.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-area RH criteria]\label{thm:area}
For every fixed $\alpha>0$, each of the following is equivalent to RH:
\[
 Q_F(X)=O\bigl(X^{1/4}(\log X)^B\bigr)
 \quad\hbox{for some fixed }B\ge0,
\]
\[
 Q_\alpha(X)=O\bigl(X^{1/4}(\log X)^B\bigr)
 \quad\hbox{for some fixed }B\ge0.
\]
The bounds are required at every sufficiently large real $X$.
For $1/4\le a<1$, either bound
$Q(X)=O_\varepsilon(X^{a+\varepsilon})$ for every
$\varepsilon>0$ implies that every nontrivial zero satisfies
$\operatorname{Re}\rho\le(1+2a)/3$.
These are conditional zero restrictions, not unconditional strips.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient transfer gives, with a fixed $C_\alpha$,
\[
 |S_\alpha(x)-F(x)|
 \le \frac{C_\alpha}{L(x)}|F(x)|+\frac{C_\alpha}{x}.
\]
Once $C_\alpha/L(x)\le1/2$, taking positive parts and integrating yields
\[
 Q_\alpha(X)\le\tfrac32Q_F(X)+C_\alpha\log2,\qquad
 Q_F(X)\le2Q_\alpha(X)+2C_\alpha\log2.
\]
Under RH both positive parts vanish eventually.

Under false RH, Lemma~\ref{lem:persistence} gives
$Q_F(n)\ge A n^{d-b}$ for every fixed $d<1-b/2$ at arbitrarily
large $n$. Since $b<1/2$, choose $d$ with $d-b>1/4$.
This defeats every fixed logarithmic factor at exponent $1/4$.
The two area comparisons transfer the contradiction.
For a zero of real part $\sigma>(1+2a)/3$, choose fixed exponents with
\[
 1-\sigma<b<\frac{2(1-a)}3,\qquad a+b<d<1-b/2.
\]
A sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$ gives the same contradiction.
\end{proof}

For an explicit sufficient estimate, put
\[
 Z(x)=\sqrt{x}\log x\,J(x),\qquad
 q(X)=2\left\lceil\frac{\log X}{8\log2}\right\rceil.
\]
The estimate is the following unproved assertion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-target}
 \frac1X\int_X^{2X}|Z(t)|^{q(X)}\,dt\le C\,2^{-q(X)}
 \quad\hbox{eventually, with fixed }C.
\end{equation}
Indeed $P(x)\sim2/(\sqrt{x}\log x)$, $D\ge0$ and $A=O(1/x)$
give $F(x)\le(Z(x)-1)/(\sqrt{x}\log x)$ eventually.
For $q\ge1$, $(u-1)_+\le|u|^q$. Thus
\eqref{eq:moment-target} implies
$Q_F(X)\le C X^{1/4}/\log X$ and hence RH.
A fixed second-moment envelope instead gives only
$O(\sqrt X/\log X)$ by $(v-m)_+\le v^2/(4m)$.
This identifies the strength needed by that particular estimate;
it does not prove that every second-moment argument must fail.
No unconditional bound \eqref{eq:moment-target} is asserted.

\subsection{A growing positive tilt}
The exact identity \eqref{eq:tilt-tail} also permits a growing
parameter, provided its tail is estimated directly.
For fixed $c>0$, set $z=c\sqrt{x}$. PNT and partial summation give
\[
 \sum_{p>x}(p-1)^{-2}\sim\frac1{x\log x},\qquad
 \sum_{p>x}(p-1)^{-3}=O\left(\frac1{x^2\log x}\right).
\]
The estimate needed here is uniform in the growing parameter. Put
$N=\lfloor x\rfloor$ and $S_2(N)=\sum_{p>N}(p-1)^{-2}$. The elementary
bounds $0\le\log(1+v)-v+v^2/2\le v^3/3$ for $v\ge0$ give
\[
 \left|\log\mathcal H(z)-\log\mathcal H_N(z)
       +\frac{z^2-z}{2}S_2(N)\right|
 \le\frac{2(z^3+z)}{3N^2}\qquad(N\ge1,\ z\ge0).
\]
This weaker cubic bound already suffices: after division by
$z=c\sqrt{x}$, its error is $O_c(x^{-1})$, which is
$o_c(1/(\sqrt{x}\log x))$. PNT supplies
$N\log N\,S_2(N)\to1$, and $N/x\to1$ retains the real cutoff.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growing-tilt}
 \frac{T_{c\sqrt{x}}(x)}{c\sqrt{x}}
 =F(x)-\frac{c}{2\sqrt{x}\log x}
       +o_c\left(\frac1{\sqrt{x}\log x}\right).
\end{equation}
It follows that RH is equivalent to eventual
$T_{c\sqrt{x}}(x)<0$ for each fixed $c>0$.
The RH direction follows from the negative source margin; the converse
uses the positive $x^{-b}$ excursions with $b<1/2$, which dominate
every fixed square-root-scale allowance.
This argument does not apply a fixed-tilt error term uniformly without
proof. Nor does a larger $c$ establish the resulting product inequality.
For comparison, a finite fixed combination satisfies
$\sum_j a_jT_{z_j}=(\sum_j a_jz_j)F+O(1/x)$:
canceling its leading coefficient cancels this RH signal as well.

\section{RH detected by localized reversal counts}\label{sec:local-count}
This section uses a specified collection of witnesses and a growing
finite tilt grid. It does not replace the original maximal count
$N_\eta(k)$ by a monotone proxy. In fact, ascent inclusion alone does
not imply monotonicity of maximal separated reversal counts: the step
signs $(-,+,+,-,+,+)$ allow two separated witnesses, while
$(-,+,+,+,+,+)$ allow only one.

\subsection{Actual tilted laws and exact rounding}
For each $t>0$ put $\eta_p(t)=t/(p-1+t)$. Its odds are
$t/(p-1)$, so its degree-$r$ elementary coefficient is exactly
$t^r a_r$. At a supported prefix $x=p-1$ and a consecutive
gap $g=q-p$, the rank-$(r+1)$ density therefore ascends exactly when
\[
 g+t<R_r(x);
\]
it descends exactly when $R_r(x)<g+t$.
These are statements about actual local-law densities.
No unweighted arithmetic realization for every real $t$ is assumed.

For $t_j=1+j/M$, $0\le j\le HM$, let
$A_M(R,g)=\#\{j:g+t_j<R\}$. If $M\ge1$ and
$1\le R-g\le H+1$, then
\[
 A_M(R,g)=\lceil M(R-g-1)\rceil.
\]
Thus, when both $R-g$ and $U-g$ lie in this interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grid-rounding}
 |A_M(R,g)-A_M(U,g)-M(R-U)|<1.
\end{equation}
The weak lower and strict upper endpoints follow from the strict
ascent test; a threshold exactly on a grid point is not counted.
For a nonempty finite set of pairs, summing this identity loses
less than one unit per pair.

\subsection{The sampling input and the specified cells}
Alweiss--Luo \cite[Corollary~1.2]{AL} prove that for each fixed
$\delta\in[0.525,1]$, some fixed width $H$ and constants $c>0,m$
give at least $ch/(\log Y)^m$ consecutive prime pairs of gap at most
$H$ in every sufficiently late $[Y-h,Y]$, whenever
$Y^\delta\le h\le Y$. We use $\delta=3/5$ and enlarge $H$ to at
least two. This $H$ is not supplied by a theorem about global
infinitude at a numerical width such as $186$.

For integer $X$ sufficiently large, set
\[
 h_X=\lceil3X^{3/5}\rceil,\quad
 m_X=\left\lfloor\frac{X^{7/10}}{10h_X}\right\rfloor,\quad
 M_X=\lceil\sqrt X\rceil,\quad
 r_X=\lfloor(H+2)L(X)\rfloor.
\]
The cell indexed by $0\le i<m_X$ starts at
$z_i=X+1+10h_Xi$. Its three sampling windows are
\[
 [z_i,z_i+h_X],\quad
 [z_i+3h_X,z_i+4h_X],\quad
 [z_i+6h_X,z_i+7h_X].
\]
The explicit $+1$ ensures that every relevant prefix $p-1$ is at
least $X$. All prefixes used below are at most $X+X^{7/10}$.
The source theorem applies to all three windows simultaneously,
since their right endpoints are at most $2X$ and
$h_X\ge(2X)^{3/5}$ eventually.

Fix $\ell=H+3$. Every multiple of $\ell!$ starts a composite
block at offsets $2,\ldots,\ell$. Since $h_X\ge\ell!+2$
eventually, a block can be placed between the left and middle
windows. Choose the first qualifying consecutive bounded pair in each
window and use its lower prime in the left and middle windows.
A consecutive-prime step crossing the intervening block
has length at least $\ell$. Fix a choice of these crossings once
and for all, using only their prime geometry.
Denote its lower-prime index by $d_i$
and that of the selected right pair by $a_i$.
These deterministic choices use only primes and the fixed schedule.

The crossing lies between the selected left and middle primes,
so $d_i<a_i$. The right endpoint in one cell is strictly before
the next cell's descent lower prime; therefore
$a_i+1<d_j$ for $i<j$. Finally
$m_X\sim X^{1/10}/30$. These are genuine separated witnesses,
not repeatedly counted copies of one reversal.

\subsection{Two finite integer counts}
Use the same grid $t_j=1+j/M_X$, $0\le j\le HM_X$, in every cell.
Define
\begin{align*}
 C_H(X)&=\sum_{i=0}^{m_X-1}\sum_{j=0}^{HM_X}
 {\bf1}\{d_i<a_i\text{ and the tilted rank-$(r_X+1)$ density}\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{42mm}\text{descends at }d_i\text{ and ascends at }a_i\},\\
 \overline C_H(X)&=\sum_{i=0}^{m_X-1}\sum_{j=0}^{HM_X}
 {\bf1}\{g_{a_i}+t_j<\mathcal R_{r_X}(L(p_{a_i}-1))\}.
\end{align*}
Use index zero when a specified selection is unavailable on the
bounded initial range. The finite formulas then define total counts;
their initial values do not enter either eventual criterion.
The reference uses the same actual prime pairs and their actual gaps.

Uniformly on the window, both actual and reference ratios are
$H+2+o(1)$, with positive denominators. This follows from
$R_r,\mathcal R_r=r/L+O(1/L)$ on compact rank bands and
$|L(y)-L(X)|\le1$ on $[X,2X]$ eventually.
Every chosen long step is consequently a strict descent for every
grid tilt. For the short step, $2\le g_{a_i}\le H$ puts both
ratio-minus-gap thresholds inside $[1,H+1]$.
Writing $\Delta_i=\Delta_{r_X}(p_{a_i}-1)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-residual}
 C_H(X)-\overline C_H(X)
 =M_X\sum_{i=0}^{m_X-1}\Delta_i+e_X,\qquad |e_X|<m_X
\end{equation}
whenever $m_X>0$.

\begin{theorem}[Localized separated-reversal criteria]\label{thm:count-rh}
For the preceding fixed width and deterministic construction, RH is
equivalent to each of the following:
\begin{align}
 C_H(X)&\le\overline C_H(X)\quad\hbox{eventually},\label{eq:count-rh-sign}\\
 C_H(X)-\overline C_H(X)&\le C X^{1/10}
 \quad\hbox{eventually for some fixed }C>0.\label{eq:count-rh-power}
\end{align}
Under false RH the signed difference divided by $X^{1/10}$ is
unbounded above along integer $X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under RH, the uniform coefficient sign comparison applies to
$r_X$ at every selected prefix. Actual ascent tests are included
in reference tests, proving \eqref{eq:count-rh-sign} and hence
\eqref{eq:count-rh-power}.

If RH is false, Lemma~\ref{lem:persistence}, followed by the
uniform coefficient transfer, supplies arbitrarily large integer
$n$ such that
\[
 \Delta_r(y)>T n^{-1/2}\qquad
 (n\le y\le n+n^{7/10})
\]
for every prescribed fixed $T>0$ and every rank in a fixed positive
compact $r/L(y)$ band. Indeed its retained main term is
$\gg n^{-b}/L(y)$ for some $b<1/2$, and
$n^{1/2-b}/\log\log n\to\infty$.
All selected prefixes at $X=n$ lie in this interval, and the single
rank $r_n$ lies in the required band throughout.
Since $M_n\ge\sqrt n$, \eqref{eq:count-residual} gives
\[
 C_H(n)-\overline C_H(n)>m_n(T-1).
\]
Now $m_n\ge c_0n^{1/10}$ eventually, with fixed $c_0>0$.
Choosing $T>1+C/c_0$ contradicts any proposed eventual bound
\eqref{eq:count-rh-power}. This proves both converses.
\end{proof}

For each individual tilt, the selected witnesses are bounded by
$N_{\eta(t)}(r_X+1)$. The sum in the theorem ranges over a growing
finite grid, so it is not the maximal count for one fixed law.
Its reference also retains $\theta$ and the entire profile.
An independent proof of either eventual count inequality would
prove RH; the equivalence itself supplies no such estimate.

An ascent-only variant uses every consecutive gap at most the source
width in $[X+1,X+1+\lceil2X^{7/10}\rceil]$ and the same grid and rank.
Let its actual and reference counts be $A_H(X),B_H(X)$.
The source at $\delta=3/5$, applied to this longer window, supplies
$\gg X^{7/10}/(\log X)^m$ pairs. At the persistent false-RH peaks,
\eqref{eq:grid-rounding} then gives
\[
 A_H(X)-B_H(X)\gg
 \frac{X^{6/5-b}}{(\log X)^m\log\log X},\qquad b<1/2.
\]
Thus RH is also equivalent to eventual $A_H\le B_H$, and to
an eventual one-sided bound $A_H-B_H=O(X^{7/10})$.
The persistence interval may be taken with any fixed exponent
between $7/10$ and $3/4$, which absorbs the shift and rounding
in this ascent-only window.

\section{Growing families and signed bounds}\label{sec:growing-families}

A fixed-family RH criterion does not by itself justify varying the family
with the prime cutoff. We first give a transfer with explicit error
intervals, then two arithmetic calculations in which the complete
correction can be retained. Throughout this section, a finite-prime
limiting law is taken before the cutoff or polynomial degree varies.
We make no assertion uniform in a finite sampling box.

\subsection{Correction intervals}
\begin{theorem}[Transfer through a correction interval]\label{thm:correction-interval}
Let $E,\ell,u:\N\to\mathbb R$ and let $c>0$ be fixed. Suppose that
\[
 \ell(N)\le E(N)-cF(N)\le u(N)
 \quad\hbox{for all sufficiently large }N.
\]
Assume that, for every fixed $0<b<1/2$, there is a constant $C_b>0$
such that $u(N)-\ell(N)\le C_bN^{-b}$ eventually.
Then RH is equivalent to eventual $E(N)\le u(N)$, and also to
eventual $E(N)<u(N)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under RH, $F(N)<0$ eventually, so the upper interval bound gives
$E(N)<u(N)$. If RH fails, the integer-cutoff Nicolas excursion theorem
gives a fixed $0<b<1/2$ and arbitrarily large $N$ with
$cF(N)>(C_b+1)N^{-b}$. At these $N$, the lower interval bound implies
$E(N)>u(N)$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
The interval center need not be small or positive. What matters is the
width and its proved arithmetic relation to the same $E$ and $F$.
Defining an endpoint from the unknown error would not provide an
independent estimate. The maintained theorem has a fixed coefficient
$c$; a moving coefficient requires a separate uniform argument.

\subsection{An explicit polynomial-plus-prime family}
For a positive integer $d$, put
\[
 P_d(a)=\prod_{j=1}^d(a-j),\qquad V_d(a,q)=P_d(a)+q,
\]
where $a$ ranges over integers and $q$ over primes, with ordered
representations retained. For each fixed finite set of primes, uniform
integer residues, prime progression limits and the Chinese remainder
theorem give independent local divisibility laws. The polynomial has
exactly $\min(d,p)$ roots modulo $p$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-local-law}
 \eta_d(p)=\frac{p-\min(d,p)}{p(p-1)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, every prime $p\le d$ is excluded. Use this exact law,
including those exclusions, to form $E_d(N)=E_{\eta_d}(N,1)$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:family-product}. Set
\[
 Q_2(N)=\sum_{p>N}\frac1{(p-1)^2},\qquad
 T(N,b)=\sum_{p>N}\log\left(1+\frac b{(p-1)^2}\right)
 \quad(b\ge0).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact correction and finite bounds]\label{thm:root-exact}
For $N\ge\max(d,3)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-error}
 E_d(N)=F(N)-T(N,d-1).
\end{equation}
For $N\ge1$ and $b\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-tail-bounds}
 \left(b-\frac{b^2}{2N^2}\right)Q_2(N)
 \le T(N,b)\le bQ_2(N),\qquad 0\le Q_2(N)\le\frac2N.
\end{equation}
Moreover $N\log N\,Q_2(N)\to1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $p>d$, direct substitution into the odds gives
\[
 (1+w_d(p))(1-1/p)
   =\left(1+\frac{d-1}{(p-1)^2}\right)^{-1}.
\]
The exact profile identity expresses $E_d-F$ as the sum of these
logarithms over $p>N$. All excluded primes already lie in the finite
prefix; their profile factors cancel exactly, proving
\eqref{eq:root-error}. For $t\ge0$,
$t-t^2/2\le\log(1+t)\le t$. Since $p>N$ implies $p-1\ge N$,
summing these inequalities proves \eqref{eq:root-tail-bounds}.
Comparison with $\sum_{n\ge N}n^{-2}\le2/N$ gives the elementary
upper bound. Partial summation of the prime number theorem gives
the stated asymptotic for $Q_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Independent signs at logarithmically reduced degree]
\label{thm:log-root-sign}
For every fixed integer $j\ge1$, let
\[
 d_j(N)=1+\left\lfloor\frac{N}{(\log N)^j}\right\rfloor .
\]
Then, unconditionally,
\[
 (\log N)^{j+1}E_{d_j(N)}(N)\longrightarrow-1.
\]
In particular $E_{d_j(N)}(N)<0$ for every sufficiently large integer
$N$. The diagonal degree $d=N$ also satisfies
$E_N(N)\log N\to-1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For large $N$, $d_j(N)\le N$, and
$b_N=d_j(N)-1=N/(\log N)^j+O(1)$. The two bounds in
\eqref{eq:root-tail-bounds}, together with $b_N\le N$, imply
\[
 (\log N)^{j+1}T(N,b_N)\longrightarrow1.
\]
For every fixed positive integer $A$, the quantitative prime number
theorem gives the unconditional limit
\[
 F(N)(\log N)^A\longrightarrow0.
\]
Subtracting in \eqref{eq:root-error} proves the result.
For the diagonal, use $b=N-1$ in the same bounds to obtain
\[
 T(N,N-1)\log N\longrightarrow1. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}
These are complete independent signed estimates, but the family degree
is $N^{1-o(1)}$. For every fixed $\alpha<1$ and fixed $j$,
$d_j(N)/N^\alpha\to\infty$. The theorem therefore supplies no
fixed-power zero-free conclusion. Neither its onset nor its estimates
are asserted to be uniform when $j$ grows with $N$.

\subsection{The effect of adding two primes}
Let a base residue family have zero probability $r_p\in[0,1]$ at
each prime $p$. Add two independent uniform nonzero residues.
Their exact zero probability is
\[
 \eta(p)=\frac{p-2+r_p}{(p-1)^2}.
\]
Indeed there are $p-1$ ordered nonzero pairs with sum zero and
$p-2$ with any prescribed nonzero sum. Thus the laws of $q_1+q_2+1$
and $q_1+q_2$ attain the two endpoints, independently of the
complexity of the base family.

\begin{theorem}[Attained two-prime bracket]\label{thm:two-prime-bracket}
For every such finite residue family, every $N\ge3$, and $z\ge0$,
\[
 E_{q_1+q_2+1}(N,z)\le E_{\mathrm{base}+q_1+q_2}(N,z)
       \le E_{q_1+q_2}(N,z).
\]
At $z=1$, define the convergent tails
\[
 T_3(N)=\sum_{p>N}\log\left(1+\frac1{(p-1)^3}\right),\qquad
 U_2(N)=\sum_{p>N}-\log\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right).
\]
Then the exact bracket is
\[
 F(N)-T_3(N)\le E_{\mathrm{base}+q_1+q_2}(N,1)\le F(N)+U_2(N).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For tail primes $p>N\ge3$, all three probabilities are less than one.
Both $\eta/(1-\eta)$ and $\log(1+z\eta/(1-\eta))$ increase with
$\eta$. Compare the convergent tail corrections to $zF(N)$,
including their exact normalizations. Substitution of $r_p=0,1$
gives the two displayed tails at $z=1$.
\end{proof}
The favorable endpoint correction is asymptotic to
$1/(2N^2\log N)$ by partial summation. Thus adding arbitrary base
complexity cannot enlarge this correction within the stated
two-prime-addition class. This controls a correction, not the sign
of the complete expression containing $F(N)$. Additional independent
prime additions can be included in the base distribution; this
ordinary specialization does not assert uniform finite-box limits
or a result for changing nonunit coefficients.

\section{Power-sized degrees and zero-free regions}\label{sec:power-degree}
The distinction between a logarithmic saving and a fixed power saving
can be made precise for the actual polynomial family of
Section~\ref{sec:growing-families}. The result below is a conditional
implication. It does not assume a zero-free region, but it does assume
the eventual sign of the complete arithmetic profile.

\begin{theorem}[Power-degree sign criterion]\label{thm:power-degree}
Let $d:\N\to\N$ satisfy $d(N)\ge1$. Fix real numbers $C>0$ and
$1/2\le\alpha<1$. Suppose that, for every sufficiently large integer $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-degree-hypotheses}
 d(N)\le CN^\alpha,\qquad E_{d(N)}(N)\le0,
\end{equation}
where $E_d$ is the complete profile of the consecutive-root polynomial
$P_d(a)+q$, with all small-prime exceptions included.
Then
\[
 \zeta(\rho)\ne0\qquad
 \hbox{whenever }\alpha<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1.
\]
The degree bound and the signed inequality refer to the same chosen
sequence of families and both hold eventually, not merely along
possibly different unbounded subsequences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $\alpha<1$, the degree bound implies $d(N)\le N$ eventually.
The exact identity \eqref{eq:root-error} and
\eqref{eq:root-tail-bounds} therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-degree-F}
 F(N)\le T(N,d(N)-1)
       \le(d(N)-1)Q_2(N)
       \le2C N^{\alpha-1}
 \quad\hbox{eventually}.
\end{equation}
Suppose $\zeta(\rho)=0$ with $\beta=\operatorname{Re}\rho>\alpha$
and $\beta<1$. Choose
\[
 b=\frac{(1-\beta)+(1-\alpha)}2.
\]
Then $0<1-\beta<b<1-\alpha\le1/2$. The zero-dependent
integer Nicolas excursion argument from
Section~\ref{sec:excursions} gives arbitrarily large integers $N$
with $F(N)>2C N^{-b}$. But $\alpha-1<-b$, so for $N\ge1$,
\[
 F(N)\le2C N^{\alpha-1}\le2C N^{-b}
\]
by \eqref{eq:power-degree-F}, a contradiction.
This uses the already established integer excursions, including the
nonlinear logarithm correction. It neither differentiates an
asymptotic error nor assumes a new real-to-integer interpolation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Square-root degree]\label{cor:sqrt-degree-RH}
If $d(N)\ge1$, $C>0$, and eventually
\[
 d(N)\le C\sqrt N,\qquad E_{d(N)}(N)\le0,
\]
then RH holds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:power-degree} excludes every nontrivial zero to
the right of $1/2$. The functional equation then places every
nontrivial zero on the critical line.
\end{proof}

The new content here is the exact passage from this arithmetic
degree budget to the classical zero-dependent Nicolas obstruction;
the underlying oscillation mechanism is classical
\cite{Nicolas1983,Nicolas2012}. External priority for this
polynomial-family formulation is not established.

The complete sign in \eqref{eq:power-degree-hypotheses} remains
unproved independently for power-sized degrees. In contrast,
Theorem~\ref{thm:log-root-sign} proves that sign at
$d(N)=1+\lfloor N/(\log N)^j\rfloor$ for every fixed $j\ge1$.
Such a degree exceeds $CN^\alpha$ eventually for every fixed
$C>0$ and $\alpha<1$. Thus that unconditional theorem cannot be
substituted into \eqref{eq:power-degree-hypotheses}. Increasing
a fixed logarithmic saving does not reach any fixed power saving.

This criterion concerns the specified polynomial-plus-prime law.
No degree-only assertion is made for arbitrary changing
coefficients or sampling rules: those can introduce additional
excluded primes and a different correction. Nor does an arbitrary
sequence of individually qualifying fixed laws automatically
satisfy the uniform estimate used here.

\section{A generalized Robin critical window}
\label{sec:lcm-window}

The same signed prime-product calculation has a separate application
to generalized divisor sums. It gives actual counterexamples to a
proposed extension of a generalized Robin inequality. It does not
supply an independent RH criterion.

For a real parameter $\kappa>1$, define
\[
 S_\kappa(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\sigma(d)^\kappa,\qquad
 \mathcal G_\kappa(n)=
 \frac{\zeta(\kappa)S_\kappa(n)}
 {(e^\gamma n\log\log n)^\kappa}\quad(n\ge3).
\]
Here $\kappa$ is a real divisor-sum exponent, not the prime-factor
rank $k$. These are the LCM-power functions of
Fan--Kobayashi--Molnar~\cite[Definition~1.2]{FKM}.
For an integer $N\ge8$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modified-lcm}
 M_N=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)
       \prod_{\substack{p\le N\\N<p^2\le2N}}p,\qquad
 K_N=\frac32+\frac{2}{5\log\log M_N}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform critical window]\label{thm:lcm-window}
For every $X\in\N$, there is an integer $N>X$, $N\ge8$, such that
simultaneously for every real $1<\kappa\le K_N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-window-margin}
 \log\mathcal G_\kappa(M_N)>
       \frac1{100\sqrt N\log N}.
\end{equation}
Under RH, this holds for every sufficiently large integer $N$,
with a common cutoff for the entire parameter window.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:lcm-counterexamples}
For every fixed real $1<\kappa\le3/2$, there are arbitrarily large
integers $n$ for which $\mathcal G_\kappa(n)>1$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Exact arithmetic and the full signed comparison}
Let $a_p=v_p(M_N)$ for $p\le N$, and define
\begin{align*}
 u_p&=p^{-a_p-1},& U_N&=\sum_{p\le N}u_p,\\
 R_N&=4\sum_{p\le N}\frac{u_p}{p}
             +32\sum_{p\le N}u_p^2,&
 D_N&=\log\log\log M_N-\log\log\theta(N).
\end{align*}
All finite sums have the same prime support $p\le N$. Write
\[
 \mathcal T_\kappa(N)=
       \sum_{p>N}-\log(1-p^{-\kappa}).
\]
For $\kappa>1$ this converges and equals the logarithm of the
omitted part of the zeta Euler product.

The defining divisor sum is multiplicative, and at a prime power
\[
 S_\kappa(p^a)=\sigma(p^a)^\kappa-\sigma(p^{a-1})^\kappa
 =\left(\frac{p^a}{1-1/p}\right)^\kappa
       (1-p^{-\kappa})\,C_\kappa(p,a),
\]
where
\[
 C_\kappa(p,a)=
 \frac{(1-p^{-a-1})^\kappa-p^{-\kappa}(1-p^{-a})^\kappa}
      {1-p^{-\kappa}}>0.
\]
Therefore the exact normalized identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-exact-log}
 \log\mathcal G_\kappa(M_N)=
 \mathcal T_\kappa(N)-\kappa\bigl(F(N)+D_N\bigr)
       +\sum_{p\le N}\log C_\kappa(p,a_p).
\end{equation}
This retains the actual integer height $\log M_N$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform finite lower bound]\label{lem:lcm-finite}
For $N\ge8$ and $1<\kappa\le2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-signed-bound}
 \log\mathcal G_\kappa(M_N)\ge
 \mathcal T_\kappa(N)-\kappa(F(N)+D_N+U_N)-R_N.
\end{equation}
If $1<\kappa\le K\le2$, its right side may be replaced by
\[
 \mathcal T_K(N)-K\max(F(N)+D_N+U_N,0)-R_N.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $u=p^{-a-1}$, $v=p^{-\kappa}$ and $t=p^{-a}$.
For $1\le\kappa\le2$, $u\le1/8$ and $v\le1/2$, Bernoulli's
inequality gives
\[
 C_\kappa(p,a)\ge1-\frac{\kappa u}{1-v}.
\]
The number $w=\kappa u/(1-v)$ satisfies $0\le w\le4u\le1/2$
and $w\le\kappa u+4uv$. The bound
$\log(1-w)\ge-w-2w^2$ yields
\[
 \log C_\kappa(p,a)\ge-\kappa u-4uv-32u^2
                  \ge-\kappa u-4u/p-32u^2.
\]
For the exponents of $M_N$, $p^{a_p+1}>N$; hence $u_p\le1/8$
when $N\ge8$. Sum the bound in \eqref{eq:lcm-exact-log}.
Finally $\mathcal T_\kappa\ge\mathcal T_K$, and for every real $A$,
$-\kappa A\ge-K\max(A,0)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two arithmetic costs and the positive tail}
The ordinary prime number theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-cost-limits}
 \sqrt N\log N\,D_N\longrightarrow\sqrt2,\qquad
 \sqrt N\log N\,U_N\longrightarrow\sqrt2,\qquad
 \sqrt N\log N\,R_N\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Here is a direct explanation of all three limits. The exact height is
\[
 \log M_N=\psi(N)+\theta(\sqrt{2N})-\theta(\sqrt N).
\]
Since $\psi(N)-\theta(N)=\theta(\sqrt N)+O(N^{1/3}\log N)$,
we have $\log M_N-\theta(N)\sim\sqrt{2N}$.
The derivative of $\log\log t$ is $1/(t\log t)$, giving the first limit.

For $p>\sqrt{2N}$, $a_p=1$, so the upper-band contribution is
\[
 \sum_{\sqrt{2N}<p\le N}p^{-2}.
\]
Its normalized limit is $\sqrt2$. Below $\sqrt{2N}$, split at
$N^{1/3}$ to bound the remaining contribution by
\[
 N^{-1}\pi(N^{1/3})+\sum_{p>N^{1/3}}p^{-3}.
\]
Both terms are negligible on the displayed scale. Finally,
$u_p<1/N$ gives $R_N=O((1+\log N)/N)$ and proves the third limit.

We need only a finite lower comparison for the moving zeta tail.
For $q>1$, $m\in\N$, and $1<K\le2$, put
\begin{align}
 A_2(x)&=\sum_{p>x}p^{-2},\notag\\
 L_m(N,q,K)&=N^{2-K}A_2(N)\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
 \bigl((Nq^{j+1})^{2-K}-(Nq^j)^{2-K}\bigr)A_2(Nq^{j+1}).
 \label{eq:lcm-finite-tail}
\end{align}
Then $\mathcal T_K(N)\ge L_m(N,q,K)$. To see this, first use
$-\log(1-p^{-K})\ge p^{-K}$, and then bound the increasing function
$p^{2-K}$ from below by its step approximation at the displayed
thresholds. Every increment is nonnegative, so summation preserves
the bound.

Fix $s>1$ and $m$ before taking $N\to\infty$, and put $q=s^2$.
Because $x\log x\,A_2(x)\to1$ and
$\log\log M_N/\log N\to1$, the finitely many terms give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-finite-limit}
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt N\log N\,L_m\left(N,s^2,\frac32+
                        \frac c{\log\log M_N}\right)
     &\longrightarrow C_m(s)e^{-c},\\
 C_m(s)&=1+\frac1s-\frac1{s^{m+1}}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Only fixed-dilation prime-square-tail limits are used. In particular,
we have not assumed an asymptotic for a prime sum uniform in a
moving exponent.

Choose $s=1001/1000$ and $m=10^6$. Bernoulli's inequality gives
$C_m(s)>999/500$. With $\beta_0\le1/20$ from the classical
Nicolas constant, the limiting complete RH lower bound exceeds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-margin}
 C_m(s)e^{-2/5}-3(\sqrt2-1)-\frac32\beta_0>\frac7{600}.
\end{equation}
For example, $\log(3/2)>2/5$ and $\sqrt2<99/70$ show that the
left side is greater than
$333/250-87/70-3/40=99/7000>7/600$.
The finite choice $m=10^6$ is a symbolic parameter choice; no
million-term numerical verification is used.

\subsection{Proof in the two RH cases}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lcm-window}]
Under RH, \eqref{eq:nicolas-wave} gives
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\sqrt N\log N\,F(N)\le\beta_0-2.
\]
Insert this and \eqref{eq:lcm-cost-limits}--\eqref{eq:lcm-finite-limit}
into Lemma~\ref{lem:lcm-finite}, with $K=K_N$.
For large $N$, $K_N<2$. The limiting lower bound for the common
scaled comparison is at least the quantity in \eqref{eq:lcm-margin}.
Since $1/100<7/600$, one cutoff works for every
$1<\kappa\le K_N$.

If RH fails, negative Nicolas excursions give arbitrarily large
integer $N$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-negative-supply}
 F(N)\sqrt N<-1.
\end{equation}
For completeness, take real excursions $F(x)<-2x^{-b}$ with
$0<b<1/2$ and $x>3$, and let $N=\lfloor x\rfloor$.
The prime products and $\theta$ are constant on $[N,N+1)$, so
$F(N)=F(x)$. Also $x<N+1\le4N$. Thus
$F(N)<-2/\sqrt{x}\le-1/\sqrt N$. Only $F$ is rounded in
this argument; no floor invariance of $M_N$ or $W$ is used.

By \eqref{eq:lcm-cost-limits}, the positive arithmetic costs satisfy
$(D_N+U_N)\sqrt N\log N\to2\sqrt2$, whereas
\eqref{eq:lcm-negative-supply} gives
$F(N)\sqrt N\log N<-\log N$. Hence $F(N)+D_N+U_N<0$
at arbitrarily large integer cutoffs. At those cutoffs the maximum
in Lemma~\ref{lem:lcm-finite} vanishes. Equations
\eqref{eq:lcm-cost-limits} and \eqref{eq:lcm-finite-limit} then give
the stronger positive limiting lower bound $C_m(s)e^{-2/5}$.
Taking cutoffs beyond the common asymptotic thresholds proves
\eqref{eq:lcm-window-margin}. The same explicit integer family
therefore works in both logical cases.
\end{proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:lcm-counterexamples}]
We have $\log M_N\ge\theta(N)\to\infty$, so $M_N\to\infty$.
Also $K_N>3/2$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:lcm-window} beyond any
prescribed integer-height threshold and use positivity of
$\mathcal G_\kappa$ to exponentiate its positive logarithm.
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns integer cutoffs and supplies no effective onset
or frequency of the exceptional cutoffs without RH. It establishes
neither optimality of $2/5$ nor that $M_N$ is colossally abundant.
Every fixed $\kappa>3/2$ eventually lies outside the shrinking window,
so this result is consistent with the fixed-parameter upper theorem
of \cite{FKM}. In particular, the counterexamples do not violate
ordinary Robin's inequality and do not prove or disprove RH.

\section{Implications and scope}\label{sec:implications}
The spectrum gives a quantitative dictionary. Bounded-gap counts
yield reversal counts through the finite selection lemma; the
upper comparison shows that no other late ascents can improve
the rate. Finite changes in an admissible law preserve that rate.
Many different affine prime-pair expressions consequently share
a spectrum despite different forced primes and small ranks.

Location and frequency encode different information.
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical} depends on the least recurring gap.
The rate $\Lambda(\nu)$ depends on how frequently bounded gap
classes occur. Infinitude alone need not give $\gamma_H>0$:
a hypothetical count growing as a power of $\log X$ has
$\gamma_H=0$. This is why bare infinitude cannot replace
the input to Corollary~\ref{cor:power}.

For Goldbach-type expressions, the arithmetic theorem describes
factorization in the full representation-weighted ensemble.
It gives no lower bound for representations of each fixed even
integer. Fixed-modulus independence likewise does not supply
uniform estimates when a prime cutoff grows with input size.
Moving-tail distribution laws require additional uniformity.

Repeated raw reversals and centered moving-rank excursions have
different quantifiers. At each fixed rank there is a final decreasing
tail. The RH criterion lets the rank grow with the endpoint and
compares its ratio with the full reference profile.
It is the transfer in Theorem~\ref{thm:linearization} that relates
this second question to Nicolas's error. Theorem~\ref{thm:family-ratio}
extends the centered comparison to the qualifying local laws and the
actual affine families. An independently proved centered inequality
in any one such family would therefore imply RH. A coarse reversal
rate or a numerical prime-gap bound does not itself provide that sign.

The exact raw comparison can be written as
\[
 R_r(x)-(g+1)=
 \bigl(\mathcal R_r(L(x))-(g+1)\bigr)+\Delta_r(x).
\]
RH controls the second term. The first term and the occurrence of
the actual gap remain necessary for an ascent. In particular,
for fixed $1/5<b<c<1/3$ and all sufficiently large $k$, an ascent
at a prime with $bk\le\LL(p-1)\le ck$ occurs exactly when the next
gap is two. This follows directly from the uniform ratio margins.
Proving positive ascent supply in this window independently would
prove twins; the identity itself is not such a supply proof.

The Hardy--Littlewood input in Theorem~\ref{thm:hl-location}
provides actual twins approaching the eligibility cutoff.
RH instead refines that cutoff's reference error and gives
Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-capacity}. The global twin asymptotic allows
a distance $o(X_k)$ from the cutoff, which can exceed
$\sqrt{X_k}$; it cannot by itself transfer the RH square-root
clock correction to the location of the last actual ascent.
No implication from RH to twin-prime infinitude is established
by these results.

The relation to Robin and Lagarias is through their established
equivalences with RH \cite{Lagarias}. Thus the eventual inequality
in Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-ratio}, if proved independently, would imply
the corresponding universal divisor-sum inequalities. A valid
violation of either criterion would imply the false-RH coefficient
excursions. These are logical consequences of known equivalences,
not new bounds for colossally abundant numbers.
Indeed, for $n=\prod p^{a_p}$,
\[
 \frac{\sigma(n)}n=
 \prod_{p\mid n}\frac{1-p^{-(a_p+1)}}{1-p^{-1}},
\]
and optimizing the exponent-dependent numerator factors together
with $\log\log n$ remains a separate problem.

The classical inputs include Nicolas's criterion and spectral
formula, Landau oscillation, prime-factor generating products and
saddle-point estimates. The potentially new contributions are the
exact reversal-frequency spectrum, its affine realization and
the sign-preserving moving-coefficient transfer with its
applications. These attributions are provisional: proof
verification does not establish priority. Speculative RH and
independent small-gap-supply investigations are excluded from the
theorem claims of this paper.

\subsection{Finite smaller gaps and the complete spectrum}
Suppose, for a fixed even $h\ge2$, that all sufficiently late
consecutive gaps are at least $h$. Every fixed initial prime prefix
has finite support in the rank variable. Consequently, for all
sufficiently large ranks, none of its steps can be an ascent.
The RH argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-capacity} then uses the
threshold $h+1$ throughout and gives
\[
 \theta(P_k-1)<T_{k-1,h}:=\EE(u_{k-1,h+1}-\gamma),\qquad
 N_k\le(1+\varepsilon)\frac{T_{k-1,h}}{\log T_{k-1,h}}
\]
eventually, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-gap-capacity}
 \log\log N_k\le\frac{k-1}{h+1}-\gamma
 -\frac{\mathcal H'(h+1)}{\mathcal H(h+1)}+\frac{C_h}{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Constants and onsets depend on the fixed $h$. There is no fixed-width
sign transition: $\mathcal H(s)>0$ at every positive $s$, and the RH
source multiplier $-(2+W)$ stays negative in every fixed positive
rank band.

Finite twins give $h=4$ as an eventual lower bound and
$\gamma_2=\gamma_3=0$. If additionally $\Lambda(1)=1/5$, the spectrum
forces $\gamma_4=1$: every $H\ge5$ contributes at most $1/6$, while
the only remaining possible maximizing term is $\gamma_4/5$.
It follows that
\[
 \Lambda(\nu)=\frac1{4+\nu}\qquad(\nu>0).
\]
Indeed the full-frequency width-four term dominates every larger
width, and smaller widths have zero exponent.
The RH ceiling \eqref{eq:general-gap-capacity} now has the same
leading coefficient $1/5$. The matching leading values yield no
opposing signed inequality. Nor does $\gamma_H=1$ specify an actual
density: both $G_H(X)\sim X^\delta$, with any fixed $\delta>0$,
and $G_H(X)\sim cX/\log^2X$ have that exponent.

\subsection{When local frequency reveals the RH displacement}
A stronger frequency hypothesis makes the reference displacement
visible in actual last-ascent locations.
Fix even $h\ge2$, assume smaller gaps occur only finitely often, and
assume every sufficiently late interval
\[
 [X,X+\ell(X)],\qquad \ell(X)=\frac{\sqrt X}{(\log X)^2},
\]
contains the lower member of a consecutive gap exactly $h$.
Let $V_k$ be the last supported prime eligible for a hypothetical
gap-$h$ ascent, and $P_k$ the last actual ascent prime. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-tracking}
 P_k/V_k\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 T_{k-1,h}-\mathcal C(P_k-1)=o(\sqrt{P_k}).
\end{equation}
Under RH this gives
\[
 \frac{T_{k-1,h}-\theta(P_k-1)}{\sqrt{P_k}}
 =2+W(P_k-1)+o(1).
\]

Here is the precision argument. Monotonicity of the supported
ordinary coefficient ratio makes eligible primes an initial segment.
Ordinary prime existence provides a supported seed below $V_k/2$.
Apply local coverage at $X=V_k-2\ell(V_k)$. For large $V_k$,
$X\ge V_k/2$ and $\ell(X)\le2\ell(V_k)$, so the supplied gap-$h$
pair has lower prime in $(V_k-2\ell(V_k),V_k]$. Support propagates
from the seed, and monotonicity makes that step an actual ascent.
Beyond $V_k$, the eventual lower gap bound $h$ makes any ascent
eligible for the hypothetical gap-$h$ test, contradicting the
definition of $V_k$. Thus all finite exceptions are accounted for and
$V_k-2\ell(V_k)<P_k\le V_k$.

At $x=V_k-1$ and $y=w-1$, with $w$ the next prime after $V_k$,
the finite-product comparison gives
$R_r(x)=\mathcal R_r(B(x))+O_h(1/(rx))$, and likewise at $y$.
Only the prime $V_k$ is added between these prefixes, so
$B(y)-B(x)=\log(V_k/(V_k-1))=O(1/V_k)$.
Ordinary prime existence and the eventual cutoff bound first place
$r/B(x)$, $r/B(y)$ and $r/u_{r,h+1}$ in a common fixed positive
compact interval. The uniform signed coefficient comparison then
applies on both connecting segments. The strict eligible sign at
$x$, the noneligible sign at $y$, and the displayed one-prime jump give
$|B(x)-u_{r,h+1}|=O_h(1/V_k)$.
No prior spatial closeness of the two clocks is assumed.

The quantitative PNT-strengthened Mertens estimate gives
$e^{-\gamma}G_x(1)-\log x=o(1)$, and hence $\mathcal C(x)/x\to1$.
The weaker relation $\log\mathcal C(x)\sim\log x$ would not suffice.
Exponentiating the proved clock difference now gives
$|\mathcal C(V_k-1)-T_{r,h}|=O_h(\log V_k)$.
Each prime-clock jump in the remaining interval is $O(\log V_k)$.
There are at most $2\ell(V_k)+1$ integer sites, so their total change
is $O(\sqrt{V_k}/\log V_k)=o(\sqrt{V_k})$.
This proves \eqref{eq:local-tracking}; add \eqref{eq:clock-wave}
at the same actual endpoint for the RH assertion.

A global pair-count asymptotic alone gives only proportional coverage.
For the exact-gap count $G_h^{=}(X)$, the maintained sufficient condition is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 G_h^{=}(X)=c_h\operatorname{Li}_2(X)+E(X),\qquad c_h>0,\\
 E(X)=o(\sqrt X/\log^4X),\qquad
 \operatorname{Li}_2(X)=\int_2^X\frac{dt}{(\log t)^2}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Subtract this estimate at $X$ and $X+\ell(X)$. The main increment is
of order $\sqrt X/\log^4X$ and dominates both endpoint errors.
The full integral main term is essential at this precision;
$c_hX/\log^2X$ cannot replace it. A square-root-plus-epsilon
global error is insufficient for this subtraction.
This is a sufficient route to local coverage, not its necessary strength.

Finally, on any of the finite supports in Section~\ref{sec:local-count},
the signed excess is exactly the number of grid thresholds in
$[U_i,R_i)$ minus the number in $[R_i,U_i)$, where
$U_i=\mathcal R_r(L(p_i-1))$.
A positive excess arbitrarily far out contradicts RH.
Neither finite twins nor a positive main term for another gap class
has been shown to force that signed excess. These frequency statements
therefore identify additional information needed by this route;
they establish neither RH nor twin-prime infinitude.

and the maintained transfers. Likewise \eqref{eq:ratio-wave}
combines verified estimates, but is not advertised as a separately
compiled normalized-wave declaration. These qualifications concern
formal integration, not an assumption that Hardy--Littlewood or RH
has been proved.

The public
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations/blob/main/Challenge.lean}{Challenge}
states two headline theorems over Mathlib primitives.
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations/blob/main/Solution.lean}{Solution}
contains their actual proofs. The two deliberate Challenge
placeholders are absent from the Solution closures.
The 5 October proof audit checked the two public compiled statement
pairs and 539 selected theorem closures, finding only \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
A fresh 6 October audit, importing the maintained family owners and
Solution, checks 18 exact theorem statements and dependency closures
for the additions in Sections~\ref{sec:growing-families}--
\ref{sec:lcm-window}. Every axiom set is exactly the same three
standard foundations. This includes all four power-degree
declarations and the unconditional modified-LCM endpoints.
The power-degree proof was already maintained before this editorial
revision; it was independently rechecked, not replaced by an
assumption wrapper. Its eventual signed premise remains explicit.
The RH statements have maintained consumers in Solution with their
RH, false-RH or zero hypotheses explicit. An axiom audit does not
remove any of these hypotheses. The two Challenge statements concern
the original reversal and affine-spectrum headlines; they are not
claimed to be a Challenge comparison for every later RH declaration.
