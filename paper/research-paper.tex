\begin{abstract}
We identify the exact double-logarithmic upper growth rate of maximal
separated descent--ascent counts for reciprocal-smooth prime-factor laws.
Every fixed rank has an attained finite maximum. We prove the arithmetic
application to products of distinct affine forms in the sum of two primes,
including all finite local exceptions. The rate is a spectrum of actual
bounded-prime-gap frequencies and has a finite phase profile. We also prove
an exact threshold equivalence between recurring short gaps and late
ascents in the two canonical laws. The maintained Lean proofs use only
standard foundational axioms. This AI-assisted research draft records
statements and proof structure; independent review and novelty assessment
remain separate work.
\end{abstract}

The source and theorem ledger are available in the
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations}{public repository}.
The first-difference and symmetric-polynomial method builds on
Wang--Crapis~\cite{WC}; quantitative prime clusters provide an established
analytic input~\cite{Maynard}. The spectrum and affine-product application
are separate developments whose novelty remains subject to review.

\section{Objects and theorem}
Index the primes by $p_i$, starting at $i=0$. For fixed local probabilities
$\eta_p$, define
\[
 D_k(p)=\eta_p[z^{k-1}]\prod_{q<p}(1-\eta_q+\eta_q z).
\]
We consider the ordinary law $\eta_p=1/p$ and the generic odd law
$\eta_2=0$, $\eta_p=(p-2)/(p-1)^2$ for odd primes.
The defining input-size density limit precedes the limit in rank $k$.

A witness consists of $d<a$ with $D_k(p_{d+1})<D_k(p_d)$ and
$D_k(p_a)<D_k(p_{a+1})$. Witnesses are strictly separated:
$a_i+1<d_j$ for $i<j$. Let $N(k)$ be the attained maximum number.

\begin{theorem}
For both canonical laws, there is a common $a>0$ such that for every
$\epsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large $k$,
\[
 \exp(\exp(ak))\le N(k)\le
 \exp\!\left(\exp\!\left((1/3+\epsilon)k\right)\right).
\]
The implementation takes $a=1/602$.
\end{theorem}
Each fixed-rank sequence eventually strictly decreases. This theorem
concerns growing ranks, not infinitely many reversals at one fixed rank.

\section{Proof structure}
Put $w_q=\eta_q/(1-\eta_q)$ and let $E_r$ be the elementary symmetric
polynomial in the weights below $p$. With supported degree and positive
denominators, the sign of the step from $p$ to the next prime $p'$ is the
sign of
\[
 \frac{E_{r-1}}{E_r}
 -\left(\frac1{\eta_{p'}}-\frac1{\eta_p}+1\right).
\]
The threshold is $p'-p+1$ ordinarily, and for generic odd primes is
\[
 p'-p+1-\frac{p'-p}{(p-2)(p'-2)}.
\]
Exceptional small primes are handled before using these formulas.

Writing $S=\sum_{q<p}w_q$ and $W_{r-1}$ for the largest $r-1$ weights,
the bounds
\[
 \frac rS\le\frac{E_{r-1}}{E_r}\le\frac r{S-W_{r-1}}
\]
combine with $S=\log\log p+O(1)$ and a uniform bound for $W_{r-1}$.
Above $\log\log p=(1/3+\epsilon)k$, they force strict descent.
An eventual nonincreasing tail bounds every separated witness family;
a finite maximum is attained.

For the lower bound, the quantitative prime-window input supplies at least
$X^{1/3}$ starts in $[X,2X]$ whose width-600 windows contain two primes,
for all sufficiently large $X$. The source extraction and its external
analytic artifacts are pinned and audited. Bare infinitude of bounded
prime gaps is insufficient here.

Factorial cells provide a preceding long gap. A bounded-fiber argument
and ordered thinning give genuine consecutive-prime indices with the required
strict disjointness. The uniform sign criteria turn each selected pair
of long and short gaps into a reversal. More generally, an eventual
$X^\delta$ supply of fixed width $H$, with $\delta>0$, gives the lower
bound for every $0<a<1/(H+1)$. All loss constants are fixed before $k$
grows; they are absorbed on the double-exponential scale.
Taking $H=600$, $\delta=1/3$ and $a=1/602$ closes the displayed theorem.
The chosen width is a conservative formally closed input, not a literature
claim about the smallest available gap.

\section{Late ascents and recurring prime gaps}
A late ascent at coefficient $b>0$ means consecutive primes $p<q$ and a
rank $k$ satisfying
\[
 \log\log(p-1)\ge bk,\qquad D_k(q)>D_k(p).
\]
Unbounded supply means that such a pair exists for every prescribed lower
bound on \emph{both} $k$ and $p$.

\begin{theorem}
Let $H\ge1$ be an integer and
$1/(H+2)<b<1/(H+1)$. Infinitely many consecutive prime gaps are at most $H$
if and only if for every $K,Q$ there exist $k\ge K$ and consecutive primes
$p<q$ with $p\ge Q$ and $\log\log(p-1)\ge bk$, at which both the ordinary
and generic odd rank densities strictly ascend.

More precisely, suppose gaps at most $h\ge1$ recur arbitrarily far out,
and every sufficiently late gap is at least $h$. Then $1/(h+1)$ is the
critical coefficient: every smaller positive coefficient has unbounded
simultaneous ascent supply; above every larger coefficient, both densities
strictly descend for all sufficiently large ranks and lower primes.
No assertion at the boundary coefficient is included.
\end{theorem}

Here is the mechanism behind the converse. For any fixed integer $H\ge0$
and $b>1/(H+1)$, choose
\[
 \frac1{H+1}<a<b.
\]
The uniform symmetric-ratio estimates give $E_{k-2}/E_{k-1}\le1/a$
at sufficiently large $k,p$ with $\log\log(p-1)\ge bk$.
Since $1/a<H+1$, the ordinary threshold $q-p+1$ forbids an ascent
whenever $q-p\ge H$. In the odd law its correction is bounded by
$1/(p-2)$, which is absorbed by the same fixed strict margin.
Thus unbounded ascent supply forces recurring gaps strictly below $H$.
Apply this with $H+1$ to obtain gaps at most $H$ in the equivalence.

For the forward direction choose a scale $a>b$ with $(H+1)a<1$,
and assign ranks to arbitrarily late short gaps so that
$\log\log(p-1)/k$ tends to $a$. The lower symmetric-ratio bound tends to
$1/a>H+1$, forcing both ascents. The rank-rounding and finite starting
exceptions disappear inside the fixed strict margins. For the upper
critical statement, use the eventual lower bound $q-p\ge h$; for its
lower statement, use recurrence at most $h$.

In particular, for every fixed $b>1/5$, a sufficiently late ascent in
either canonical law must cross a twin-prime gap. Indeed, the converse
with $H=4$ excludes every gap at least four, and odd-prime gaps are
positive even integers. An independently proved unbounded supply would
therefore imply infinitely many twin primes. This conditional implication
is proved; its supply hypothesis is open. It yields no unconditional new
gap bound, and one finite example cannot certify the supply.

\section{Exact spectrum and every fixed rank}
Let $G_H(X)$ count consecutive prime gaps at most $H$ whose lower prime is
at most $X$, and put
\[
 \gamma_H=\limsup_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\log\log(3+G_H(X))}{\log\log X},\qquad
 \Lambda(\nu)=\sup_{H\ge2}\frac{\gamma_H}{H+\nu}.
\]
The cutoffs $X$ may be taken over the natural numbers.

\begin{theorem}
Fix $\nu>0$ and a prime probability law $0\le\eta_p\le1$ such that
\[
 \eta_p^{-1}=p/\nu+b+o(1)
\]
along the primes. Every fixed-rank density sequence is eventually
nonincreasing and has an attained finite maximal reversal count $N_\eta(k)$.
Moreover,
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\log(3+N_\eta(k))}{k}
   =\Lambda(\nu)>0.
\]
\end{theorem}
All parameters of the law are fixed before $k$ grows. This is a limsup
statement; the weaker assumption $\eta_p=\nu/p+O(p^{-2})$ alone is not its
hypothesis.

Finite forced coordinates give exact rank shifts, and excluded coordinates
can be erased. On prime prefixes, the interior odds sum is
$\nu\log\log p+O(1)$ and the largest $r$ odds have total $O(1+\log r)$.
These estimates yield uniform growing-rank ratios even at arbitrarily
large prime cutoffs.

For the lower spectrum, choose a rate below $\gamma_H/(H+\nu)$. Infinitely
late actual gap counts supply short gaps in a suitable double-exponential
band. Preceding factorial-cell descents, bounded fibers and ordered thinning
retain the rate and give strictly separated witnesses. The deleted initial
segment and fixed multiplicity losses are kept before taking limits.
For the upper spectrum, retain each short gap's individual cutoff. The
finite envelope has the form
\[
 N_\eta(k)\le T_k+\sum_{2\le g\le J}G_g(U_g(k)),
 \qquad
 \log\log U_g(k)\le \left(\frac1{g+\nu}+\epsilon\right)k.
\]
The initial term and the fixed finite sum are absorbed before letting
$\epsilon$ tend to zero. This yields the matching upper rate.

For a fixed degree $r\ge1$, write $M_r(p)$ for its Bernoulli prefix mass.
The same finite bounds, with the exact forced-coordinate cases, give
$M_{r-1}(p)\le M_r(p)/2$ eventually. At consecutive sufficiently large
primes $p<q$, the exact signed identity is
\[
 D_{r+1}(q)-D_{r+1}(p)=
 \eta_p\eta_q\left[
 M_{r-1}(p)-(\eta_q^{-1}-\eta_p^{-1}+1)M_r(p)\right].
\]
Reciprocal control makes the threshold at least $1/2$ eventually.
The degree-zero recurrence handles rank one. Hence all fixed ranks,
including eventual zero tails, are eventually nonincreasing.

The quantitative window input gives a class with $\gamma_H=1$. Let $H_*$
be the least such class. The spectrum is attained among $2\le H\le H_*$
for every fixed $\nu>0$. Since there are only finitely many smaller classes
and each has exponent less than one, for all sufficiently large fixed $\nu$,
\[
 \Lambda(\nu)=\frac1{\nu+H_*},\qquad
 \frac1{\Lambda(\nu)}-\nu=H_*.
\]
No numerical value of $H_*$ is asserted. The condition $\gamma_H=1$
is a double-logarithmic limsup condition; it does not assert positive
density, nor a fixed positive-power lower bound at every large cutoff.

\section{Actual affine prime-pair densities}
Fix a nonempty family
\[
 F(p,q)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(c_j(p+q)+d_j\bigr),
\]
where $c_j$ are positive integers, $d_j$ are integers, and
$c_i d_j\ne c_j d_i$ for $i\ne j$. Sample all ordered prime pairs
$p,q\le X$, including the diagonal, with denominator $\pi(X)^2$.
\begin{theorem}
For every fixed positive rank $k$ and prime $\ell$, the density of $\ell$
as the $k$th smallest distinct prime factor of $|F(p,q)|$ exists.
Its sequence over $\ell$ has finite maximal reversal counts at every
rank, and its exact upper growth rate is $\Lambda(m)$.
\end{theorem}
For each fixed modulus, normalized prime input counts become uniform over
the units, while nonunit input classes vanish. Exact unit-pair CRT counting
therefore proves joint divisibility independence for every fixed finite
set of primes. Summing its atoms by cardinality gives the finite Bernoulli
rank formula. Zero outputs satisfy $p+q\le\sum_j|d_j|$ and contribute a
vanishing finite correction.

At every prime, the local probability is the exact count of unit pairs
annihilating $F$, divided by $(\ell-1)^2$. Outside the finite coefficient,
shift and determinant exceptions,
\[
 \eta_\ell=\frac{m(\ell-2)+z}{(\ell-1)^2},\qquad
 \eta_\ell^{-1}=\frac{\ell}{m}-\frac{z}{m^2}+o(1),
\]
where $z=1$ if one shift is zero, and $z=0$ otherwise. Thus the general
theorem applies with $\nu=m$ without discarding any exceptional prime.
The input-size limit is taken first at each fixed family, rank and prime;
the rank limsup follows separately for that fixed family. This concerns
representation-weighted outputs, not uniform integers.

\section{Formal statement and remaining research}
The maintained general and arithmetic assemblies prove the reciprocal-smooth
spectrum and the affine prime-pair application. Challenge states the canonical
bound and arithmetic spectrum directly over Mathlib primitives; Solution
contains their real proofs. The theorem ledger below identifies their exact
declaration names and source owners. The local audit compares both compiled types
in separate environments and checks twelve theorem closures, including the
late-ascent equivalence and the conditional inverse twin-prime transfer. The deliberate
Challenge placeholders do not enter the Solution proof closures.

The pinned Erdos 690 dependency supplies narrow ordinary-density leaves;
its conditional classification is not promoted. The dependency is never
rebuilt or redistributed here: its published cache is reused directly.
Shared Windows Lean 4.34.0 artifacts are used by probes and maintained
builds. Additional analytic ports are hash-pinned, but their upstream
compatibility publication remains pending; the Erdos cache alone does not
provide a complete fresh-machine setup. Official Comparator/NanoDa
verification is separate from the local compiled-type check.
