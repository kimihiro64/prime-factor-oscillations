\begin{abstract}
We study reversals in the density of the $k$th smallest distinct prime
factor and the finer arithmetic error in its consecutive-coefficient
ratio. For fixed local laws with $\eta_p^{-1}=p/\nu+\beta+o(1)$,
the finite maximal separated reversal count has exact rate
$\limsup_k k^{-1}\log\log(3+N_\eta(k))
=\sup_{H\ge2}\gamma_H/(H+\nu)$, where $\gamma_H$ records bounded-gap
frequency. This spectrum has a finite phase profile and is realized
by products of distinct affine forms in the sum of two primes.
For the ordinary law, a uniform comparison with a full entire-function
reference transfers the classical Nicolas prime-product error to the
actual coefficient ratio. For each fixed positive moving-rank parameter,
eventual negativity of the centered ratio is equivalent to the Riemann
hypothesis. We give quantitative excursions under false RH and sharp
reference-location and reversal-count bounds under RH. A separate
Hardy--Littlewood-shaped twin-count hypothesis places the last actual
ascent at relative distance tending to zero from its potential cutoff.
We distinguish classical analytic inputs from the potentially new
spectrum and coefficient transfer. The main maintained results are
formalized in Lean; no new prime-gap bound or proof of RH is claimed.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction and historical context}\label{sec:intro}
Let $d_k(p)$ be the natural density of positive integers whose $k$th
smallest \emph{distinct} prime factor is $p$. The question whether
$p\mapsto d_k(p)$ first rises and then falls is now called Erd\H{o}s
Problem 690. Our subject is the finer structure left after unimodality
fails: the number of separated reversals, their possible locations, and
the arithmetic information encoded by their growth.

Erd\H{o}s formulated the question in 1979~\cite[p.~75]{Erdos}.
The same paper discusses the typical scale $\log\log p_k(n)\sim k$
for prime factors of integers. This concerns where probability mass
lies, not the mode of the individual-prime density. Erd\H{o}s--Tenenbaum
\cite[Corollary~5]{ET} later corrected the earlier assertion about the
mode: a maximizing prime satisfies $\log p\sim k/\log k$, rather than
$\log p\sim k$. Neither statement counts reversals. The occurrence of
a double exponential in classical prime-factor distribution does not
by itself supply the theorem below.

Cambie~\cite[Theorem~5]{Cambie} proved unimodality for $k=1,2,3$ and
verified its failure for $4\le k\le20$. His Theorem~3 gives
superpolynomially many local maxima for the different density
$\delta_1(n,m)$ in Erd\H{o}s Problem 692: the density of integers having
exactly one divisor in $\{n+1,\ldots,m-1\}$. Its moving variable is $m$,
with $n$ fixed. It is not a reversal count for $d_k(p)$.

Wang--Crapis~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{WC} proved non-unimodality for every
$k\ge4$. Their proof compares a symmetric-polynomial ratio with prime
gaps and produces a descent followed by an ascent. We extend the
comparison to local laws and make the construction quantitative.
The question here is how the maximal number of witnesses grows with
$k$. Its exact rate also requires a converse: a sufficiently late
ascent must come from an appropriately short gap.

There are two main contributions. The first is the exact reversal
frequency spectrum, its arithmetic realization, and its inverse
late-ascent interpretation. Quantitative bounded-gap results are
existing analytic inputs, not new sieve results. The second is a
uniform transfer of a classical RH-sensitive error into the actual
moving-rank coefficient ratio, preserving its sign at a scale much
smaller than any fixed inverse power of $\log\log x$.

Nicolas's primorial criterion and its signed error
\cite{Nicolas1983,Nicolas2012} supply the analytic source.
The finite generating product and its saddle-point analysis already
occur in Erd\H{o}s--Tenenbaum \cite{ET}; the normalized Euler product
also appears in mod-Poisson theory \cite{KN,FMN}. We claim neither
the generating function nor the Nicolas spectral expansion as new.
The potentially new statements are the exact gap-frequency spectrum
and the quantitative comparison with a full theta-centered
coefficient reference, together with their stated applications.
Priority remains provisional pending independent review.

Sections~\ref{sec:ratio}--\ref{sec:affine} prove the unconditional
spectrum. Section~\ref{sec:late} treats recurrence and the separate
Hardy--Littlewood conditional location result.
Sections~\ref{sec:profile}--\ref{sec:capacity} establish the coefficient
transfer, RH criteria and reference capacities. The
\href{https://github.com/kimihiro64/prime-factor-oscillations}{public repository}
contains maintained proofs and a provenance ledger. This is an
AI-assisted research draft; independent expert review and assessment
of novelty remain outstanding.

\section{The argument in plain English}\label{sec:plain}
Choose a position in an integer's ordered list of prime factors,
say the tenth distinct prime factor. For each prime, ask what fraction
of sampled integers put that prime in the chosen position. These
fractions form a sequence. A reversal means that the sequence drops
and later rises again. We count occurrences separated far enough
that they do not reuse the same stretch of the sequence.

Two effects compete. Moving to a larger prime lowers its own chance
of dividing the integer. At the same time, the additional smaller
primes make it easier to have the required number of preceding
factors. An exact formula measures this competition. On the relevant
scale, a short gap between consecutive primes favors a rise, whereas
a long gap favors a drop.

The useful measuring scale is the logarithm of the logarithm of the
prime. On that scale the available rise threshold is approximately
the rank divided by the scale. This explains the double-exponential
locations in the argument. Eventually even the shortest possible
prime gap is too large for a rise, so every fixed rank still has a
final decreasing tail.

Many short gaps alone are not enough to count reversals. We must
put a long gap before each selected short gap and keep the witnesses
distinct. Periodic blocks of composite integers provide barriers
that consecutive primes must jump. Grouping short gaps between the
barriers and taking alternate occupied groups produces separated
witnesses, losing only a fixed factor in their number.

Finally, different short-gap sizes can have different frequencies.
Each size contributes its frequency divided by a threshold cost.
The greatest contribution is exactly the reversal growth rate.
For products of affine prime sums, the number of distinct factors
changes that cost, while finitely many exceptional primes change
only the initial details.

A second, much finer comparison uses the entire prime-product profile
as a reference. The ratio that decides an ascent differs from this
reference by a small signed error. We show that this error carries
the same RH information as Nicolas's primorial inequality.
Under RH it is eventually negative, with a bias from prime squares.
If RH is false it has larger excursions of both signs. The rank
moves outward with the prime cutoff in this statement; it does not
contradict eventual decline at each fixed rank.

This determines how the prefix changes the threshold for a possible
ascent. An actual ascent also needs an actual prime gap below that
threshold. RH controls the fine reference error, while a
Hardy--Littlewood twin-count assumption supplies twins near the
potential endpoint. Their roles and their different precision are
kept separate throughout.

\section{Definitions and main results}\label{sec:statements}
All logarithms are natural. Index the primes by $p_0=2,p_1=3,\ldots$,
and write $\LL(x)=\log\log x$ for $x>1$ and
$\EE(t)=\exp(\exp t)$. Ranks $k$ are positive integers.
A fixed \emph{local law} consists of probabilities $0\le\eta_p\le1$.
Define the finite products
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 M_r(p)=[z^r]\prod_{q<p}(1-\eta_q+\eta_qz),\qquad
 D_{\eta,k}(p)=\eta_pM_{k-1}(p),
\end{equation}
with $M_r=0$ for $r<0$. For the ordinary law $\eta_p=1/p$,
the Chinese remainder theorem identifies $D_{\eta,k}$ with $d_k$.
The second canonical law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd}
 \eta_2^{\mathrm{odd}}=0,\qquad
 \eta_p^{\mathrm{odd}}=\frac{p-2}{(p-1)^2}\quad(p>2).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}\label{def:reversal}
A reversal at rank $k$ consists of indices $d<a$ with
\[
 D_{\eta,k}(p_{d+1})<D_{\eta,k}(p_d),\qquad
 D_{\eta,k}(p_a)<D_{\eta,k}(p_{a+1}).
\]
A family of $m$ such witnesses is separated if $a_i+1<d_j$ for
$i<j$. Its maximal attainable size, when finite, is $N_\eta(k)$.
\end{definition}
This counts raw-density descent--ascent witnesses, not oscillations
of a centered error or a smoothed approximation.

For integers $H\ge0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
\begin{split}
 G_H(X)&=\#\{i:p_i\le X,\ p_{i+1}-p_i\le H\},\\
 \gamma_H&=\limsup_{\substack{X\to\infty\\X\in\N}}
       \frac{\log\log(3+G_H(X))}{\LL(X)},\\
 \Lambda(\nu)&=\sup_{H\ge2}\frac{\gamma_H}{H+\nu}\quad(\nu>0).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The $3$ keeps the logarithms defined when counts vanish.
We have $0\le\gamma_H\le1$ and $\gamma_0=\gamma_1=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact reversal spectrum]\label{thm:spectrum}
Suppose $\nu>0$, $\beta\in\mathbb R$, and, along sufficiently large primes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth}
 0<\eta_p<1,\qquad
 \eta_p^{-1}=p/\nu+\beta+\rho(p),\qquad \rho(p)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Every fixed-rank sequence $D_{\eta,k}$ is eventually nonincreasing,
its reversal maximum is finite and attained, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\log(3+N_\eta(k))}{k}
       =\Lambda(\nu)>0.
\end{equation}
The law, its parameters and its exceptional coordinates are fixed
before $k$ tends to infinity.
\end{theorem}
The weaker estimate $\eta_p=\nu/p+O(p^{-2})$ alone does not supply
the vanishing difference of reciprocal errors used in this theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical bounds]\label{thm:canonical}
For either canonical law and every $\varepsilon>0$, for all
sufficiently large $k$,
\[
 \EE(k/602)\le N_\eta(k)\le\EE((1/3+\varepsilon)k).
\]
Each fixed-rank canonical sequence is eventually strictly decreasing.
\end{theorem}
The positive constant uses a conservative quantitative width-$600$
input; it is not a claim about the best known numerical prime-gap bound.

\begin{theorem}[Affine arithmetic realization]\label{thm:affine}
Fix $m\ge1$, positive integers $c_j$, and integers $d_j$, with
$c_id_j\ne c_jd_i$ for $i\ne j$. Put
\[
 F(p,q)=\prod_{j=1}^m(c_j(p+q)+d_j).
\]
Sample all ordered prime pairs $p,q\le X$, including the diagonal,
with denominator $\pi(X)^2$. At every fixed rank $k$ and prime
$\ell$, the probability that $\ell$ is the $k$th smallest distinct
prime factor of $|F(p,q)|$ has a limit $D_{F,k}(\ell)$; zero
outputs are excluded from the event. The maximal separated reversal
count $N_F(k)$ is finite and attained, and
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\log(3+N_F(k))}{k}=\Lambda(m)>0.
\]
\end{theorem}
The size limit comes first, with family, rank and prime fixed.
Outputs are weighted by their prime-pair representations.

The second main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-ratio}, concerns the
ordinary coefficient ratio at rank
$r=\lfloor\alpha(\gamma+\log\log\theta(x))\rfloor$, for each fixed
$\alpha>0$. Its precise full-profile normalization is defined in
Section~\ref{sec:profile}. Eventual negativity of that centered ratio
is equivalent to RH. This statement is separate from
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}, whose hypotheses contain no RH assumption.

\section{The exact comparison and uniform estimates}\label{sec:ratio}
\subsection{First differences}
For consecutive primes $p<q$ with interior probabilities,
\[
 M_r(q)=(1-\eta_p)M_r(p)+\eta_pM_{r-1}(p).
\]
Direct subtraction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diff}
 D_{\eta,k}(q)-D_{\eta,k}(p)=
 \eta_p\eta_q\{M_{k-2}(p)-T_\eta(p,q)M_{k-1}(p)\},
\end{equation}
where $T_\eta(p,q)=\eta_q^{-1}-\eta_p^{-1}+1$.
When $M_{k-1}(p)>0$, an ascent is equivalent to
$M_{k-2}(p)/M_{k-1}(p)>T_\eta(p,q)$.
For the ordinary law, writing $R=M_{k-2}/M_{k-1}$ and $g=q-p$,
the exact real-valued gap threshold is $R-1$: a strict ascent occurs
if and only if $g<R-1$. Equality gives a flat step.
This threshold does not constrain the actual gap to be small.
The ordinary comparison level is $q-p+1$; for the odd law at $p>2$ it is
\[
 q-p+1-\frac{q-p}{(p-2)(q-2)}.
\]

\subsection{Finite exceptions and symmetric polynomials}
Only finitely many coordinates in \eqref{eq:smooth} can be forced
($\eta_p=1$) or excluded ($\eta_p=0$). Let $f$ count the forced
coordinates. Past them, each contributes a factor $z$ to the
generating polynomial; excluded coordinates contribute $1$.
Retain all interior coordinates, including the exceptional ones,
and set
\[
 w_p=\frac{\eta_p}{1-\eta_p},\qquad
 S(p)=\sum_{\substack{q<p\\0<\eta_q<1}}w_q.
\]
For $k>f$, put $r=k-1-f$. The common positive product of
$1-\eta_q$ cancels in the ratio, leaving $E_{r-1}(p)/E_r(p)$,
where $E_r$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $r$
in the finite list of interior odds.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:symmetric}
For at least $r\ge1$ positive weights, let $W_{r-1}$ be the largest
sum of $r-1$ distinct weights. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
 \frac rS\le\frac{E_{r-1}}{E_r}\le\frac r{S-W_{r-1}},
\end{equation}
and the right denominator is positive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The terms of $SE_{r-1}$ which choose a new index total $rE_r$.
The remaining terms are
\[
 \sum_{|A|=r-1}\left(\sum_{j\in A}w_j\right)\prod_{j\in A}w_j,
\]
between zero and $W_{r-1}E_{r-1}$. Rearrangement proves the bounds.
At least one positive weight remains after any $r-1$ are removed.
\end{proof}
This specializes to Wang--Crapis \cite[Lemmas~3.1--3.2]{WC};
the present use also retains the exact forced-rank shift.

Expanding \eqref{eq:smooth} gives $w_p=\nu/p+O(p^{-2})$.
The reciprocal-prime estimate and the convergent quadratic tail give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mertens}
 S(p)=\nu\LL(p-1)+O_\eta(1),\qquad
 W_{r-1}=O_\eta(1+\log(r+1)).
\end{equation}
For the second estimate, dominate all but finitely many odds by
$C/p$ and use $p_j\ge j+2$. The finitely many exceptional odds
contribute a fixed constant; their order need not be monotone.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform sign margins]\label{lem:bands}
Fix $0<u\le v$. Uniformly in the band
$uk\le\LL(p-1)\le vk$, as $k\to\infty$,
\[
 \frac{E_{r-1}(p)}{E_r(p)}
       =\frac{k}{\nu\LL(p-1)}+o(1),\qquad r=k-1-f.
\]
Gaps at most $H$ give strict ascents throughout the band if
$(H+\nu)v<1$. Gaps at least $D$ give strict descents there if
$Du>2$, for sufficiently large rank and lower prime.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The prime number theorem ensures more than $r$ interior primes
below $p$ uniformly in this band. Equations \eqref{eq:ratio} and
\eqref{eq:mertens} have numerator $k+O_\eta(1)$ and denominator
$\nu\LL(p-1)+O_\eta(\log(k+1))$, bounded below by a positive
multiple of $k$. This proves the uniform ratio statement.

Writing $g=q-p$, the threshold is
$g/\nu+1+\rho(q)-\rho(p)$. Its error is bounded by twice the
supremum of $|\rho|$ beyond $p$, even for large $g$.
For $g\le H$ the limiting lower ratio is
$1/(\nu v)>(H+\nu)/\nu$.
For $g\ge D$ the upper ratio is
$1/(\nu u)+o(1)<D/\nu$. Both margins are strict.
\end{proof}
Without an upper band endpoint, the same estimates give a
one-sided bound: on $\LL(p-1)\ge bk$ the ratio is at most
$1/(\nu b)+o(1)$, uniformly for fixed $b>0$.

\subsection{Every fixed rank}
For fixed $r\ge1$, the ratio tends to zero by
\eqref{eq:ratio}--\eqref{eq:mertens}. Consecutive sufficiently large
primes have gap at least two, so their threshold is uniformly
positive. Equation \eqref{eq:diff} gives strict descent for
supported degrees. If $k\le f$, the tail is zero. If $k=f+1$,
the remaining degree is zero, its predecessor coefficient vanishes,
and the same identity again gives descent.
Thus every fixed-rank tail is nonincreasing. For the ordinary law,
eventual decrease was already noted by Cambie \cite[\S3]{Cambie}.
The extension here retains the forced and excluded coordinates.

Once all ascent indices lie below an integer $I$, a separated
family has size at most $I$. Attainable sizes form a nonempty
bounded subset of $\N$ (zero is attainable), so their maximum
is attained. The canonical laws have no forced coordinates
and have positive supported tails, giving strict eventual decrease.

\section{From short gaps to separated reversals}\label{sec:selection}
The following finite argument supplies the counting step and its loss.
It turns actual small-gap supply into separated raw reversals.
The later RH coefficient correction is not an input to this construction.

\begin{lemma}[Periodic composite barriers]\label{lem:cells}
Fix integers $D\ge H+1$ and $M=D!$. Let $\mathcal S$ be a
nonempty finite set of integer starts such that
\[
 X\le n,\quad n+H\le Y,\quad
 \#([n,n+H]\cap\{\text{primes}\})\ge2,\quad X\ge M+2.
\]
There are $m$ separated pairs of consecutive-prime gaps, a gap at
least $D$ followed by one at most $H$, all endpoints in $[X,Y]$,
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cellloss}
 |\mathcal S|\le2(M+H)(m+1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose consecutive primes $p<q$ in each window, and assign them
to $c=\lfloor(p-2)/M\rfloor$. Since $cM+j$ is composite for
$2\le j\le D$, and $c\ge1$,
\[
 cM+D+1\le p<q\le(c+1)M+1.
\]
Indeed a prime $q$ beyond the last endpoint but within $H<D$
of $p$ would lie in the next composite block. A short pair cannot
cross that barrier.

Starts assigned to cell $c$ lie between $cM+D+1-H$ and
$(c+1)M+1$, so there are at most $M+H$ of them.
Order the occupied cells $c_0<\cdots<c_{t-1}$ and choose one
pair in each, with lower-prime index $u_j$. Between chosen
primes in two successive occupied cells, a consecutive-prime gap
crosses the destination cell's whole composite block. Its length
is at least $D$, and it precedes the chosen short gap there.

Take destination cells $c_2,c_4,\ldots,c_{2m}$, where
$m=\lfloor(t-1)/2\rfloor$. Choose each crossing after the lower
prime selected in the preceding occupied cell. The unused occupied
cell between successive destinations ensures $a_i+1<d_{i+1}$.
Every crossing lies between selected endpoints, so remains in
$[X,Y]$. Finally $t\le2(m+1)$ and
$|\mathcal S|\le(M+H)t$, proving \eqref{eq:cellloss}.
\end{proof}
No uniform distribution among cells is needed. Empty stretches
are harmless, since a crossing still exists between selected
occupied cells.

Fix $u,v>0$ with $(H+\nu)v<1$ and choose an integer
$D\ge H+1$ with $D>2/u$. If all primes from $X$ through $Y+H$
lie in the sign band, the two lemmas imply, at sufficiently large $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandcount}
 G_H(Y)\le X+B(N_\eta(k)+1),\qquad B=2(D!+H).
\end{equation}
At most $X$ lower primes are deleted below $X$; each retained
short gap supplies a window starting at its lower prime.
The upper endpoint is $Y+H$, since a counted gap may end above $Y$.
All constants are fixed before $k$ grows.

\begin{corollary}[Positive-power transfer]\label{cor:power}
Suppose that for fixed $H$ and $\delta>0$, for every sufficiently
large $X$ at least $X^\delta$ integer starts in $[X,2X]$ have
two primes in their width-$H$ windows. For every
$0<a<1/(H+\nu)$,
\[
 N_\eta(k)\ge\EE(ak)\quad\hbox{for all sufficiently large }k.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $a<b<1/(H+\nu)$ and $X=\lceil\EE(bk)\rceil$.
Every endpoint in $[X,2X+H]$ has $\LL(p-1)=bk+o(1)$.
Fixed $u<b<v<1/(H+\nu)$ therefore provide the sign band.
The cell lemma gives $N_\eta(k)+1\ge X^\delta/B$.
Since $\log(X^\delta/B)=\delta\exp(bk)+O(1)$ and $a<b$,
the right side eventually exceeds $1+\EE(ak)$.
\end{proof}

We use the existing quantitative input
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clusterinput}
 \#\{n\in[X,2X]\cap\N:
       \#([n,n+600]\cap\{\text{primes}\})\ge2\}\ge X^{1/3}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $X$. Its maintained declaration is
\path{PrimeGaps.eventually_many_prime_windows_600}.
Section~\ref{sec:formal} records its provenance. Quantitative
prime-cluster theory, including Maynard's work~\cite{Maynard},
is the analytic background. We use this count, not merely
infinitude of bounded gaps. With $\nu=1$ the corollary applies
at $a=1/602<1/601$.

\section{Proof of the exact spectrum}\label{sec:sharp}
The two directions preserve the frequency subsequences and each
individual gap's cutoff. This matching rate, rather than the presence
of a double exponential alone, is the principal unconditional result.
\subsection{Lower bound: arbitrary frequency subsequences}
Fix $H$ and $0<a<\gamma_H/(H+\nu)$. Choose
\[
 0<g<\gamma_H,\qquad
 0<v_0<v<\frac1{H+\nu},\qquad a<gv_0,
\]
then $0<u<a$ and $a<b<gv_0$.
There are arbitrarily large integers $Y$ with
$3+G_H(Y)>\EE(g\LL(Y))$. Assign
$k=\lceil\LL(Y)/v_0\rceil$. The bounds
$v_0k-v_0<\LL(Y)\le v_0k$ imply
$3+G_H(Y)>\EE(bk)$ at arbitrarily large assigned ranks.

Take $X=\lceil\EE(uk)\rceil+1$. The primes between $X$ and
$Y+H$ lie in a band with positive lower coefficient and upper
coefficient $v$. Equation \eqref{eq:bandcount} applies.
If $N_\eta(k)<\EE(ak)$, then
\[
 3+G_H(Y)\le3+X+B(1+\EE(ak))<\EE(bk)
\]
eventually, a contradiction. Therefore $N_\eta(k)\ge\EE(ak)$
for arbitrarily large $k$. Supremizing over $H$ proves the lower
bound $\Lambda(\nu)$ in \eqref{eq:rate}.
The argument keeps the actual frequency subsequence; it does not
replace it by an all-large-$X$ hypothesis.

\subsection{Upper bound: each gap retains its own cutoff}
For fixed integer $g\ge2$ and $\varepsilon>0$, the one-sided
ratio bound implies that an ascent across a gap of size $g$
must satisfy, apart from fixed initial exceptions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
 \LL(p-1)<\left(\frac1{g+\nu}+\varepsilon\right)k.
\end{equation}
The same estimate excludes all gaps greater than $J$ above
$\LL(p-1)=uk$ if $1/(J+1+\nu)<u$.
In both statements, the reciprocal error is uniformly small
beyond the lower prime.

Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 N_\eta(k)\le T_k+\sum_{2\le g\le J}G_g(U_g(k)),
\end{equation}
where we may take
\[
 T_k=\lceil\EE(uk)\rceil+O(1),\qquad
 U_g(k)=\left\lceil\EE\left(
       \left(\frac1{g+\nu}+\varepsilon\right)k\right)\right\rceil+1.
\]
Each witness has a distinct ascent index. There are at most $T_k$
initial ones; all others have an actual gap $g\le J$ and are
counted in that gap's term. Overcounting by the cumulative
$G_g$ only strengthens the upper bound.

Let $A>\Lambda(\nu)$. Choose $0<u<A$ and then $J$ as above.
For each fixed $g$ and $\delta>0$, eventually
\[
 \log\log(3+G_g(U))\le(\gamma_g+\delta)\LL(U).
\]
Since $\gamma_g/(g+\nu)\le\Lambda(\nu)$, choose
$\varepsilon,\delta>0$ small enough that
\[
 (\gamma_g+\delta)
       \left(\frac1{g+\nu}+\varepsilon\right)<A
       \quad(2\le g\le J).
\]
There are only finitely many such comparisons. Rounding changes
their double-logarithmic cutoffs by $o(1)$. Each term of
\eqref{eq:envelope}, including $T_k$, has rate strictly below $A$.
A fixed finite sum has rate at most the greatest term rate.
Letting $A$ decrease to $\Lambda(\nu)$ proves the matching bound.

The input \eqref{eq:clusterinput} implies $\gamma_{600}=1$.
A given consecutive pair belongs to at most $601$ integer-start
windows of width $600$, so the associated cumulative gap count
has a positive-power lower bound along large cutoffs.
The trivial bound $G_H(X)\le X$ gives the reverse inequality
on its exponent. Hence $\Lambda(\nu)\ge1/(600+\nu)>0$.
Together with the fixed-rank proof this completes
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}.

For the canonical upper bound use the minimum eventual gap,
two, and $\nu=1$ in \eqref{eq:cutoff}. Reducing $\varepsilon$
slightly absorbs the rounding, leaving all ascents below
$\EE((1/3+\varepsilon)k)$. Their number bounds $N_\eta(k)$.
Together with Corollary~\ref{cor:power}, this proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical}.

\section{A finite phase profile}\label{sec:phase}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:phase}
Let $H_*$ be the least $H\ge2$ for which $\gamma_H=1$.
This integer exists, and for every fixed $\nu>0$,
\[
 \Lambda(\nu)=\max_{2\le H\le H_*}\frac{\gamma_H}{H+\nu}.
\]
For all sufficiently large $\nu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recovery}
 \Lambda(\nu)=\frac1{H_*+\nu},\qquad
 \frac1{\Lambda(\nu)}-\nu=H_*.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Existence follows from $\gamma_{600}=1$.
If $H>H_*$, then
$\gamma_H/(H+\nu)\le1/(H+\nu)<1/(H_*+\nu)$.
For each $H<H_*$ put $\gamma=\gamma_H<1$.
With positive denominators, the inequality
$\gamma/(H+\nu)<1/(H_*+\nu)$ is equivalent to
$(1-\gamma)\nu>\gamma H_*-H$.
It holds beyond a finite threshold. Take the greatest threshold
over the finitely many smaller classes.
\end{proof}
Here full frequency means only $\gamma_H=1$. It need not imply
positive relative density or a fixed power lower bound at every
large cutoff. The theorem identifies a finite maximum without
determining its entries numerically. The rate is an upper envelope
of the gap-frequency data; the result recovers $H_*$, not every
individual value of $\gamma_H$.

\section{Arithmetic realization by affine prime sums}\label{sec:affine}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:affine} without discarding exceptional primes.

\subsection{Fixed-modulus limits and independence}
For fixed $Q$, the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions gives
\[
 \frac{\#\{p\le X:p\text{ prime},\ p\equiv a\pmod Q\}}{\pi(X)}
       \longrightarrow\frac1{\varphi(Q)}\quad((a,Q)=1).
\]
Nonunit prime classes contain only primes dividing $Q$, so their
normalized contribution vanishes. Full Cartesian sampling of the
ordered pairs makes each fixed pair of unit classes have limiting
probability $\varphi(Q)^{-2}$.

Take $Q$ to be the product of any fixed finite set of primes.
The Chinese remainder theorem identifies the unit-pair space with
the product of the local unit-pair spaces. Divisibility of $F$
at a prime depends only on the local pair, so the limiting
divisibility indicators are independent, with exact law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localcount}
 \eta_\ell=
 \frac{\#\{(u,v)\in(\mathbb F_\ell^\times)^2:
             \prod_j(c_j(u+v)+d_j)=0\}}{(\ell-1)^2}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $k,\ell$ the rank event uses only the primes at most
$\ell$. Summing their joint atoms gives \eqref{eq:density}.

If $F(p,q)=0$, then some $p+q=-d_j/c_j\le|d_j|
\le\sum_j|d_j|$. Only finitely many such pairs exist, independently
of $X$. Their normalized contribution is zero. The diagonal
was included throughout; its $\pi(X)$ pairs are also negligible
against $\pi(X)^2$. No modulus or rank was allowed to grow with $X$.

\subsection{Exact local counts}
Set $T_\ell=\{t\in\mathbb F_\ell:\prod_j(c_jt+d_j)=0\}$.
There are $\ell-1$ unit pairs with sum zero, and $\ell-2$
with any specified nonzero sum. Thus at every prime,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rootcount}
 \eta_\ell=\frac{(\ell-2)|T_\ell|+
                    {\bf1}_{0\in T_\ell}}{(\ell-1)^2}.
\end{equation}
If a factor is identically zero modulo $\ell$,
$T_\ell=\mathbb F_\ell$ and $\eta_\ell=1$.
An empty root set gives zero, and coincident roots are counted
once. The formula covers all coefficient, shift, determinant,
and small-prime exceptions.

Outside the finite set dividing the nonzero coefficients,
nonzero shifts, or determinants $c_id_j-c_jd_i$, there are
$m$ distinct roots. At most one shift is zero; let $z_0$ be
one if it is and zero otherwise. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:genericlocal}
 \eta_\ell=\frac{m(\ell-2)+z_0}{(\ell-1)^2},\qquad
 \eta_\ell^{-1}=\frac{\ell}{m}-\frac{z_0}{m^2}+O_F(\ell^{-1}).
\end{equation}
The law satisfies \eqref{eq:smooth} with $\nu=m$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} proves the arithmetic theorem.

The simplest instances illustrate the exceptional coordinate:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 F(p,q)&\eta_2&\eta_\ell\quad(\ell>2)\\ \hline
 p+q&1&1/(\ell-1)\\
 p+q+1&0&(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)^2\\
 2(p+q)-1&0&(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)^2 .
\end{array}
\]
The forced factor $2$ in $p+q$ shifts subsequent ranks by one.
Both odd examples have exactly the canonical odd law.
All three have rate $\Lambda(1)$, despite different finite-rank
behavior. Equations \eqref{eq:rate} and \eqref{eq:recovery}
also show how sufficiently large fixed numbers of distinct affine
factors recover $H_*$. There is no uniformity claim when the family
varies with the rank. The arithmetic realization uses fixed-modulus
prime progression limits. It does not transfer the ordinary-law RH
criterion of Section~\ref{sec:rh} automatically to these families;
that extension would require a separate coefficient theorem.

\section{Late ascents and bounded prime gaps}\label{sec:late}
For a canonical law, a \emph{late ascent at coefficient $b$}
is an ascent across consecutive primes $p<q$ with
$\LL(p-1)\ge bk$. Unbounded supply requires existence beyond
every prescribed lower bound on both $k$ and $p$.

\begin{theorem}[Recurrence equivalence]\label{thm:late}
Let $H\ge1$ be an integer and
$1/(H+2)<b<1/(H+1)$. Gaps at most $H$ recur arbitrarily far out
if and only if for every $K,Q$ there exist $k\ge K$ and consecutive
primes $p<q$, with $p\ge Q$ and $\LL(p-1)\ge bk$, at which both
canonical densities ascend.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $b<a<1/(H+1)$. To a sufficiently late gap at most $H$
assign $k=\lfloor\LL(p-1)/a\rfloor$. Then $k\to\infty$ with $p$,
$\LL(p-1)\ge ak\ge bk$, and $\LL(p-1)/k\to a$.
A small fixed band around $a$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:bands} gives
both ascents.

Conversely, $b>1/(H+2)$ and the one-sided ratio bound exclude
gaps at least $H+1$ from sufficiently late ascents. In the odd
law the correction to the ordinary threshold has absolute value
at most $1/(p-2)$, absorbed by the fixed strict margin.
Unbounded lower primes therefore give recurring gaps at most $H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Critical location]\label{cor:critical}
Suppose gaps at most $h\ge1$ recur arbitrarily far out and every
sufficiently late consecutive gap is at least $h$. The critical
canonical late-ascent coefficient is $1/(h+1)$: every smaller
positive coefficient has unbounded simultaneous ascent supply;
every larger coefficient forces both densities to descend for
all sufficiently large ranks and lower primes in that region.
No assertion at equality is made.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the forward rank assignment on gaps at most $h$ and the
one-sided upper ratio bound on gaps at least $h$.
\end{proof}

For every fixed $b>1/5$, an ascent in either canonical law at
sufficiently large $k,p$ with $\LL(p-1)\ge bk$ must cross a gap
strictly below four. Gaps between odd primes are positive even
integers, so it must be two. An independently proved unbounded
supply would therefore imply infinitely many twin primes.
That supply is not established here. A large finite ascent
certifies a finite prime pair, not recurrence.


\subsection{Infinitude, frequency and the last ascent}
For either canonical family, let $P_k$ denote the prime starting its
last ascent, which exists for all sufficiently large $k$.
If $h$ is the least recurring prime gap, Corollary~\ref{cor:critical}
and the upper cutoff imply
\[
 \limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\LL(P_k)}{k}=\frac1{h+1}.
\]
Indeed every smaller coefficient occurs at unbounded ranks and
endpoints, whereas every larger coefficient is eventually excluded.
Bounded-gap infinitude at width $H$ therefore gives the lower
location coefficient $1/(H+1)$. For example, taking width $186$
as an input gives a coefficient at least $1/187$; it does not identify
the least recurring gap. Twin-prime infinitude would give the
location limsup exactly $1/3$. These implications concern location.
A frequency hypothesis is needed for the corresponding reversal count.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional last-ascent location]\label{thm:hl-location}
For either canonical law, let $X_k$ be the largest supported prime
$p$ at which a hypothetical twin step satisfies the strict ascent
criterion. Suppose that for some $C>0$,
\[
 \pi_2(X)\sim C X/(\log X)^2,
 \qquad
 \pi_2(X)=\#\{p\le X:p,\ p+2\text{ are prime}\}.
\]
Then, for every sufficiently large $k$, $P_k$ is exactly the last
lower twin member at or below $X_k$, and
\[
 \frac{P_k}{X_k}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \frac{\LL(P_k)}k\longrightarrow\frac13 .
\]
The same hypothesis and the count transfer give
$\log\log(3+N_\eta(k))/k\to1/3$ for the canonical reversal counts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a supported prefix, adding a positive weight decreases
$e_{r-1}/e_r$ by the elementary symmetric log-concavity inequality.
The ordinary hypothetical twin threshold is $3$; the odd threshold
is $3-2/(p(p-2))$, which increases with $p>2$.
Thus the supported eligible primes form an initial segment.
The uniform ratio estimates and Bertrand's theorem show that its
last prime $X_k$ exists for large $k$ and $\LL(X_k)/k\to1/3$.

For every fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, subtracting the two assumed
count asymptotics gives
\[
 \pi_2(X)-\pi_2((1-\varepsilon)X)
       \sim C\varepsilon X/(\log X)^2>0.
\]
First use dyadic coverage in a fixed scale band strictly between
$1/5$ and $1/3$. It supplies a twin ascent whose supported prefix
serves as an anchor. Every later ascent must be a twin by the
$1/5$ exclusion, and lies at most at $X_k$.
Conversely the last twin at or below $X_k$ follows the anchor,
has positive support and satisfies the strict criterion.
This proves the exact identification.
Now the displayed proportional coverage gives
$(1-\varepsilon)X_k<P_k\le X_k$ eventually, for each fixed
$\varepsilon$, and hence both location limits.

The global twin asymptotic also supplies a positive-power number of
width-two windows in every sufficiently large dyadic interval.
Corollary~\ref{cor:power} for every $a<1/3$, together with the
canonical upper bound, proves the count limit.
\end{proof}
The usual Hardy--Littlewood twin conjecture supplies a particular
positive constant $C$; the proof uses no value of that constant.
The fixed-tuple asymptotic and the stronger uniform tuple assumptions
used for short-interval distribution should be distinguished
\cite[(1) and Theorem~1]{Gallagher}.
Bare twin-prime infinitude does not supply the proportional coverage
used here. Nor does the displayed asymptotic give a polylogarithmic
or square-root bound for $X_k-P_k$ along these particular cutoffs.
A limit on $\LL(P_k)/k$ also does not imply
$P_k/\EE(k/3)\to1$: a constant shift in the inner exponent already
changes the original endpoint by a large multiplicative factor.


\section{Prime-product errors and the full coefficient reference}\label{sec:profile}
The remaining sections concern the ordinary integer law. They keep the
arithmetic cutoff $x$ and the rank $r$ distinct: $x$ tends to infinity,
while $r$ is comparable to $\log\log x$. All prime products in these
sections include $p\le x$. For the prefix before an ascent prime $p$,
the corresponding cutoff is $x=p-1$.

\subsection{Classical analytic quantities}
Write
\[
 \theta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p,\qquad
 \psi(x)=\sum_{p^a\le x}\log p,\qquad
 \kappa(t)=\frac{1/\log t+1/(\log t)^2}{t^2}.
\]
For sufficiently large $x$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analytic-objects}
\begin{split}
 J(x)&=\int_x^\infty(\psi(t)-t)\kappa(t)\,dt,\\
 K(x)&=\int_x^\infty(\theta(t)-t)\kappa(t)\,dt,\\
 P(x)&=J(x)-K(x)
       =\int_x^\infty(\psi(t)-\theta(t))\kappa(t)\,dt,\\
 f(x)&=e^\gamma\log\theta(x)
          \prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p),\qquad F(x)=\log f(x).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The quantitative prime number theorem gives convergence of these
integrals. These are Nicolas's quantities
\cite[(1.9), (1.15)--(1.19)]{Nicolas2012}; $P$ retains every prime power.
Let
\[
 A(x)=\sum_{p>x}\bigl(-\log(1-1/p)-1/p\bigr),
\]
and, when $\theta(x)>1$, let
\[
 D(x)=\frac{\theta(x)-x}{x\log x}
       -\bigl(\log\log\theta(x)-\log\log x\bigr).
\]
Partial summation and the convergent logarithmic prime-power series give
the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-nicolas}
 F(x)=K(x)-D(x)+A(x)=J(x)-P(x)-D(x)+A(x).
\end{equation}
Here $A(x)\ge0$, $A(x)=O(1/x)$, and $D(x)\ge0$ by concavity of
$\log\log t$ on $t>1$. No sign is assigned to $J(x)$.

We use the following classical facts, with their hypotheses retained.
Nicolas's criterion is equivalent to eventual $F(x)<0$. Under RH,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nicolas-wave}
 F(x)=-\frac{2+W(x)}{\sqrt{x}\log x}
          +O\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt{x}(\log x)^2}\right),
 \qquad |W(x)|\le\beta_0,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 W(x)=\operatorname{Re}\sum_\rho
       \frac{x^{i\operatorname{Im}\rho}}{\rho(1-\rho)},
 \qquad \beta_0=2+\gamma-\log(4\pi)<2.
\]
The zeros in this absolutely convergent series are counted with
multiplicity. Defining the real projection explicitly matches the
maintained formal statement. The expansion and its constant are
classical \cite[Proposition~2.1]{Nicolas2012}. In the decomposition
\eqref{eq:exact-nicolas}, the zero wave comes from $J$, while
\[
 P(x)\sim\frac2{\sqrt{x}\log x}
\]
holds unconditionally and gives the leading prime-square contribution.
Under RH the standard bounds
$|\psi(x)-x|+|\theta(x)-x|=O(\sqrt{x}(\log x)^2)$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlinear-error}
 D(x)=O((\log x)^3/x),\qquad
 |F(x)-K(x)|=O((\log x)^3/x).
\end{equation}
Indeed the second derivative of $\log\log t$ is
$-(\log t+1)/(t^2(\log t)^2)$; Taylor's integral remainder, with
$\theta(x)/x\to1$, proves the first estimate, and
\eqref{eq:exact-nicolas} proves the second.

If RH fails, $F$ has excursions of both signs of order $x^{-b}$ for
some $0<b<1/2$ \cite{Nicolas1983,Nicolas2012}. Section~\ref{sec:excursions}
records the zero-dependent version used in the coefficient transfer.
In particular, the estimate for $D$ in \eqref{eq:nonlinear-error}
is never used under the assumption that RH is false.

\subsection{An entire profile and two different clocks}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-def}
\begin{aligned}
 G_x(z)&=\prod_{p\le x}\left(1+\frac z{p-1}\right),&
 a_r(x)&=[z^r]G_x(z),\\
 \mathcal H(z)&=\prod_p
       \left(1+\frac z{p-1}\right)(1-1/p)^z,&
 C_r(u)&=[z^r]\mathcal H(z)e^{uz}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The power $(1-1/p)^z$ uses the real logarithm of its positive base.
The product for $\mathcal H$ converges uniformly on compact subsets
of $\mathbb C$: the logarithmic first-order terms cancel and the
remaining tails are bounded by a constant times $\sum_p p^{-2}$.
It defines an entire function, positive on the nonnegative real axis,
with $\mathcal H(0)=\mathcal H(1)=1$.

The finite generating product is classical in this problem
\cite[(1.6)]{ET}; the normalized Euler factor also appears in the
mod-Poisson description of prime divisibility \cite[\S4]{KN}.
Our reference retains all its Taylor coefficients. It is not a
finite-order truncation of a saddle-point expansion.

Let $\mathcal H_x$ be the finite product in \eqref{eq:profile-def}, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-clocks}
 B(x)=\log G_x(1),\qquad
 L(x)=\gamma+\log\log\theta(x).
\end{equation}
Then $F(x)=L(x)-B(x)$ and, exactly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-factorization}
 G_x(z)=\mathcal H_x(z)e^{B(x)z}.
\end{equation}
For positive supported degrees define
\[
 R_r(x)=\frac{a_{r-1}(x)}{a_r(x)},\qquad
 \mathcal R_r(u)=\frac{C_{r-1}(u)}{C_r(u)},\qquad
 \Delta_r(x)=R_r(x)-\mathcal R_r(L(x)).
\]
The denominator conditions below ensure that these ratios have their
ordinary real-valued meaning.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform coefficient transfer]\label{thm:linearization}
Fix $0<a\le b<\infty$. There exist $X$ and $K>0$ such that, for every
real $x\ge X$ and integer $r$ with $a\le r/L(x)\le b$, both denominators
are positive and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearization}
 \left|\Delta_r(x)-\frac r{L(x)^2}F(x)\right|
 \le K\left(\frac{|F(x)|}{L(x)^2}+\frac1{rx}\right).
\end{equation}
The constants may depend on $a,b$, but not on $r$ or $x$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal H(z)=\sum_{j\ge0}h_jz^j$ and
\[
 Q_r(u)=\frac{r!C_r(u)}{u^r}
       =\sum_{j=0}^r h_j\frac{(r)_j}{u^j}.
\]
For $t=r/u$ in a fixed positive compact interval, the falling-factorial
estimate
\[
 \left|\frac{(r)_j}{r^j}-1+\frac{j(j-1)}{2r}\right|
 \le \frac{j^4}{r^2}
\]
holds also for $j>r$ if $(r)_j$ is extended by zero. For $j\le r$,
apply the first two inclusion-exclusion bounds to
$\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(1-i/r)$; the other case is immediate.
Cauchy coefficient bounds on a circle of radius larger than the
compact interval justify summation with the factor $j^4$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-expansion}
 Q_r(u)=\mathcal H(t)-\frac{t^2\mathcal H''(t)}{2r}
                  +O(r^{-2}).
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal H(t)>0$, the normalized denominators are bounded
away from zero. Using $C_r'=C_{r-1}$ and the same expansion for
$r-1,r-2$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reference-slope}
 \mathcal R_r'(u)
 =\frac{C_{r-2}(u)}{C_r(u)}-\mathcal R_r(u)^2
 =-\frac r{u^2}+O(r/u^3).
\end{equation}
The first-order profile terms cancel in this paired difference.

On any fixed larger circle,
$\mathcal H_x-\mathcal H=O(1/x)$ by the quadratic prime tail.
To compare ratios without losing the cancellation, use
\[
 \frac{C_{r-1}(u)}{C_r(u)}
 =\frac r u\left(1+\frac{Q_{r-1}(u)-Q_r(u)}{Q_r(u)}\right).
\]
The identity $(r)_j-(r-1)_j=j(r-1)_{j-1}$ supplies a factor $1/u$
in the numerator difference. Replacing $\mathcal H$ by $\mathcal H_x$
therefore changes this ratio by $O(1/(rx))$, uniformly in the compact
rank range. Equation~\eqref{eq:finite-factorization} yields
\[
 R_r(x)=\mathcal R_r(B(x))+O(1/(rx)).
\]
Unconditionally $F(x)=o(1)$ by PNT and Mertens's theorem.
Integrate \eqref{eq:reference-slope} between $L$ and $B=L-F$.
The main term is $rF/L^2$; its error is
$O(|F|/L^2)$ since $r$ is comparable to $L$ and $F=o(1)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:linearization}.
\end{proof}

The coefficient expansion belongs to the saddle-point and mod-Poisson
tradition \cite{ET,KN,FMN}. The sign-preserving comparison
\eqref{eq:linearization}, its theta centering, and its application to
the actual ascent ratio are the potentially new statements here.
Any fixed error $O(L^{-m})$ would be larger than the RH signal
$1/(L\sqrt{x}\log x)$.

\section{RH criteria and quantitative excursions}\label{sec:rh}
\begin{theorem}[Moving-rank RH criterion]\label{thm:rh-ratio}
Fix $\alpha>0$ and set $r(x)=\lfloor\alpha L(x)\rfloor$.
Then RH is equivalent to
\[
 R_{r(x)}(x)<\mathcal R_{r(x)}(L(x))
 \quad\text{for every sufficiently large }x.
\]
Under RH, more precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-wave}
 \frac{L(x)^2}{r(x)}\sqrt{x}\log x\,\Delta_{r(x)}(x)
       =-2-W(x)+o(1).
\end{equation}
If RH is false, there exists one $b\in(0,1/2)$ such that, for every
fixed $\alpha>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-excursions}
 \limsup_{x\to\infty}x^bL(x)\Delta_{r(x)}(x)=+\infty,\qquad
 \liminf_{x\to\infty}x^bL(x)\Delta_{r(x)}(x)=-\infty.
\end{equation}
The witnessing endpoints may depend on $\alpha$ and on the sign.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The floor rank is eventually in a fixed positive compact band.
Under RH, substitute \eqref{eq:nicolas-wave} into
\eqref{eq:linearization}. After multiplication by
$(L^2/r)\sqrt{x}\log x$, the two errors are respectively
$O(1/L)$ and $O((\log x)/\sqrt{x})$. Both vanish, giving
\eqref{eq:ratio-wave}. Since $2-\beta_0>0$, the sign is eventually
negative.

Under false RH, Theorem~\ref{thm:power-excursions} below supplies $b$
and excursions of $F$ with arbitrarily large amplitudes.
In a band $a\le r/L\le b_1$, \eqref{eq:linearization} gives, eventually,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F\ge0&\ \Longrightarrow\ L\Delta_r\ge(a/2)F-K/(ax),\\
 F\le0&\ \Longrightarrow\ L\Delta_r\le(a/2)F+K/(ax).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $b_1$ denotes the fixed band endpoint, not the excursion exponent.
For the floor rank one may take $a=\alpha/2$.
Since $x^{-1}=o(x^{-b})$, both excursions transfer with arbitrarily
large amplitudes. This proves \eqref{eq:ratio-excursions} and the
reverse implication.
\end{proof}

The equality \eqref{eq:ratio-wave} is an ordinary corollary of the
compiled linearization and spectral estimates. The maintained
criterion and the quantitative false-RH consumer are separate named
declarations; the ledger distinguishes these verification scopes.

\begin{samepage}
\subsection{The corrected logarithm at power-profile peaks}\label{sec:excursions}
\begin{theorem}[Zero-dependent excursions]\label{thm:power-excursions}
Suppose $\zeta(\rho)=0$ with $1/2<\operatorname{Re}\rho<1$.
For every
\[
 1-\operatorname{Re}\rho<b\le1/2
\]
and each $U\in\{J,K,F\}$,
\[
 \limsup_{x\to\infty}x^bU(x)=+\infty,\qquad
 \liminf_{x\to\infty}x^bU(x)=-\infty.
\]
Consequently, false RH supplies a common exponent $0<b<1/2$.
\end{theorem}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
We give the analytic mechanism and the endpoint correction explicitly.
For $a=\log3$, initially in $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, put
\[
 \widehat J(s)=\int_a^\infty J(e^u)e^{-su}\,du,\qquad
 T(z)=\int_3^\infty(\psi(t)-t)t^{-z-1}\,dt.
\]
The Mellin identity is
$T(z)=-\zeta'(z)/(z\zeta(z))-1/(z-1)$ minus the entire contribution
of the finite interval from $1$ to $3$. Integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:landau-ode}
 s\widehat J''(s)+2\widehat J'(s)
 =T'(s+1)-T(s+1)+a^2J(3)e^{-as}.
\end{equation}
The cancellation at $z=1$ and the quantitative PNT extend
$\widehat J$ across $s=0$ and along the real interval $s>-1/2$.
At $s=\rho-1$, the double pole from $T'$ cannot cancel with the
simple pole from $T$. Thus $\widehat J$ cannot be holomorphic there.

An eventual bound $J(x)\ge-Cx^{-b}$ would make
$J(e^u)+Ce^{-bu}$ eventually nonnegative. Its Laplace transform has
the preceding nonreal singularity, but is analytic on the real
interval $s>-b$. Landau's singularity principle contradicts this:
its abscissa of convergence lies between
$\operatorname{Re}\rho-1$ and $0$, hence strictly above $-b$.
Applying the same argument to $-J$ rules out the opposite bound.
This is the classical Landau method; see \cite[Theorem~3]{Lay}.
Since $P(x)=O(x^{-1/2}/\log x)=o(x^{-b})$, $K=J-P$ inherits both signs.

For negative excursions, $F\le K+A$ and $A=O(1/x)$ suffice.
For positive excursions one must control $D$ without RH.
Fix $A_0>0$, and choose an integer local maximum $x=n+1$, $n\ge2$,
of the sequence $K(n)-A_0n^{-b}$ between two lower values bracketing a high positive value. Such maxima exist arbitrarily far out
because of the two unbounded excursions of $K$; rounding real
endpoints to integers costs $O((\log x)/x)=o(x^{-b})$.

The two adjacent comparisons are
\[
 K(n)-A_0n^{-b}\le K(n+1)-A_0(n+1)^{-b},
\]
\[
 K(n+2)-A_0(n+2)^{-b}\le K(n+1)-A_0(n+1)^{-b}.
\]
On each open unit interval $\theta$ is constant. Using
$K(v)-K(w)=\int_v^w(\theta(t)-t)\kappa(t)\,dt$ and the possible
endpoint jump of at most $\log x$ gives
\[
 0<\theta(x)-x\le(2A_0+1)x^{1-b}\log x.
\]
Taylor's integral remainder for $\log\log$ and $A(x)\ge0$ now yield
\[
 F(x)\ge K(x)
 -\frac{(2A_0+1)^2}{2}(\log x+1)x^{-2b}.
\]
The loss is $o(x^{-b})$. Choosing $A_0$ larger than any prescribed
amplitude proves the positive excursions of $F$. Finally, if RH
fails, symmetry of the zeros supplies such a $\rho$; choose any
$b$ strictly between $1-\operatorname{Re}\rho$ and $1/2$.
\end{proof}
The existence of false-RH oscillations of $F$ is classical
\cite{Nicolas1983,Nicolas2012}. The displayed proof records the
zero-dependent exponent and the exact nonlinear correction used by
the formal coefficient consumer.

\subsection{Integral, clock and positive-tilt criteria}
Define
\[
 \mathcal C(x)=\exp\bigl(e^{-\gamma}G_x(1)\bigr).
\]
The exact identity $\mathcal C(x)=\theta(x)^{1/f(x)}$ shows that
eventual $\theta(x)<\mathcal C(x)$ is another form of Nicolas's
criterion. Combining the preceding results gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rh-equivalences}
 \mathrm{RH}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K(x)<0\ \hbox{eventually}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \theta(x)<\mathcal C(x)\ \hbox{eventually}.
\end{equation}
Under RH,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock-wave}
 \frac{\mathcal C(x)-\theta(x)}{\sqrt{x}}
       =2+W(x)+o(1).
\end{equation}
Indeed $\log\mathcal C-\log\theta=(1/f-1)\log\theta$;
\eqref{eq:nicolas-wave}, $\theta(x)/x\to1$, and
$e^t=1+t+O(t^2)$ prove \eqref{eq:clock-wave}.
This is a change of variables in the classical Nicolas estimate.

For fixed $z>0$, put
\[
 T_z(x)=\log\left(
       \frac{e^{\gamma z}\mathcal H(z)(\log\theta(x))^z}{G_x(z)}
       \right).
\]
Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilt-tail}
 T_z(x)=zF(x)+\sum_{p>x}
 \left\{\log\left(1+\frac z{p-1}\right)
                 -z\log\left(1+\frac1{p-1}\right)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $z$ the tail is $O_z(1/x)$. It is nonpositive for
$z\ge1$, and lies between $0$ and $Cz/x$ for $0<z\le1$,
with an absolute $C$. Concavity of $\log(1+zw)$ proves the
signs; the upper bound follows from
$\log(1+zw)\le zw$ and $w-\log(1+w)\le w^2/2$.
Therefore
\[
 \mathrm{RH}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T_z(x)<0\ \hbox{eventually}
\]
for each fixed $z>0$. Under RH a \emph{single} sufficiently large
cutoff works simultaneously for all $z>0$, by splitting at $z=1$
in \eqref{eq:tilt-tail}; the converse follows from $T_1=F$.

The corresponding positive kernel is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \kappa_z(t)&=
 \frac{z}{(t-1)(t-1+z)\log t}
 +\frac{\log(1+z/(t-1))}{t(\log t)^2},\\
 I_z(x)&=\int_x^\infty(\theta(t)-t)\kappa_z(t)\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $t\ge e$,
$|\kappa_z(t)-z\kappa(t)|\le14(z+z^2)/(t^3\log t)$.
Chebyshev's bound gives $I_z=zK+O_z(1/(x\log x))$.
It follows that eventual $I_z<0$ is equivalent to RH and that
\[
 \sqrt{x}\log x\, I_z(x)=-z(2+W(x))+o(1)
\]
under RH. Under false RH every fixed positive tilt inherits the
zero-dependent two-sided integral excursions.
These tilt criteria transport the same leading error; they do not
supply independent sign information by themselves.

\section{Reference crossings and RH ascent capacities}\label{sec:capacity}
The preceding centered criterion concerns $R_r-\mathcal R_r(L)$.
A raw ascent instead concerns $R_r-(g+1)$ for an actual gap $g$.
We now keep both quantities in the comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp reference crossing]\label{thm:root-shift}
For every fixed $s>0$ there are $A,C>0$ and $r_0$ such that, for
$r\ge r_0$, the equation $\mathcal R_r(u)=s$ has a unique solution
$u_{r,s}$ in $[r/s-A,r/s+A]$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-shift}
 u_{r,s}=\frac r s-\frac{\mathcal H'(s)}{\mathcal H(s)}
                         +O_s(1/r).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expanding the adjacent normalized coefficients as in
\eqref{eq:profile-expansion} gives, for $t=r/u$ in a compact
neighborhood of $s$,
\[
 \mathcal R_r(u)
 =t-\frac{t^2}{r}\frac{\mathcal H'(t)}{\mathcal H(t)}
       +O(r^{-2}).
\]
Its values on either side of $r/s$ at a sufficiently large fixed
distance straddle $s$. The derivative \eqref{eq:reference-slope}
is strictly negative throughout that interval, giving existence
and uniqueness. Substitute $u=r/s+v$, with bounded $v$, into the
last display to obtain \eqref{eq:root-shift}.
\end{proof}

For $s=h+1$, put $X_{r,h}=\EE(u_{r,h+1}-\gamma)$.
The reference crossing admits a local spatial interpretation.
Fix a comparison constant $D_0\ge1$. In the window
\[
 B(x)\in[r/(h+1)-A,r/(h+1)+A],\qquad
 \mathcal C(x)\le D_0x,\qquad X_{r,h}\le D_0x,
\]
the maintained coefficient comparison gives, for sufficiently large
$r,x$ and a constant $K$ depending on the fixed parameters,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-spatial}
\begin{split}
 X_{r,h}-\mathcal C(x)>K\log x&\ \Longrightarrow\ R_r(x)>h+1,\\
 \mathcal C(x)-X_{r,h}>K\log x&\ \Longrightarrow\ R_r(x)<h+1.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed $R_r(x)-\mathcal R_r(B(x))=O(1/(rx))$, whereas
$|\mathcal R_r'|\gg1/r$ in the window. The uncertainty in $B$
is $O(1/x)$; the derivative of $\EE(u-\gamma)$ is
$\EE(u-\gamma)\log\EE(u-\gamma)$, giving the stated spatial margin.
For an actual gap $h$, these are ascent/descent tests.
Equation~\eqref{eq:clock-wave} locates their RH transition in the
$\theta$ coordinate, with displacement $(2+W(x))\sqrt{x}+o(\sqrt{x})$.

\begin{theorem}[RH endpoint and reversal-count envelope]\label{thm:rh-capacity}
Assume RH. Let $P_k$ be the lower prime of the last ordinary ascent
and $N_k=N_{\rm ordinary}(k)$. For all sufficiently large $k$, both
are attained, and with $r=k-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rh-endpoint}
 \theta(P_k-1)<\EE(u_{r,3}-\gamma).
\end{equation}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually
\[
 (1-\varepsilon)N_k\log N_k\le\theta(P_k-1),\qquad
 N_k\le(1+\varepsilon)
 \frac{\EE(u_{r,3}-\gamma)}
      {\log\EE(u_{r,3}-\gamma)}.
\]
There is a constant $C$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rh-count}
 \log\log N_k\le
 \frac{k-1}{3}-\gamma-\frac{\mathcal H'(3)}{\mathcal H(3)}
                      +\frac C{k-1}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The unconditional lower reversal bound supplies a last ascent whose
endpoint is at least double-exponential in a positive multiple of
$k$. The unconditional upper cutoff puts it below
$\EE((1/3+\varepsilon)k)$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$.
PNT therefore places $r/L(P_k-1)$ in a fixed positive compact band.
Under RH, Theorem~\ref{thm:linearization} and
\eqref{eq:nicolas-wave} give
$R_r(P_k-1)<\mathcal R_r(L(P_k-1))$ uniformly in that band.
The actual ascent requires $R_r>g+1\ge3$.

Near $u_{r,3}$, the reference is strictly decreasing. Away from
its fixed neighborhood the uniform leading expansion separates
its value from $3$. These two comparisons imply
$L(P_k-1)<u_{r,3}$, which is \eqref{eq:rh-endpoint}.
Every separated witness uses a distinct ascent index, so
$N_k\le\pi(P_k)$. PNT yields the first two count inequalities.
Equivalently one can count all primes with $\theta(p-1)$ below
the reference endpoint; this monotone capacity has asymptotic
$T/\log T$ as $T\to\infty$.

Taking two logarithms of that capacity gives
$u_{r,3}-\gamma+o(1/r)$: the correction from division by $\log T$
is negligible on this scale, and the multiplicative PNT error is
bounded eventually. Equation~\eqref{eq:root-shift} now proves
\eqref{eq:rh-count}.
\end{proof}

The same comparison keeps the actual gap of each ascent:
in a fixed positive compact rank band, RH implies
\[
 g_i+1<\mathcal R_{k-1}(L(p_i-1)).
\]
Thus, on any sufficiently late finite band of prime indices, the
number of ascents is at most the number of indices satisfying this
reference inequality with their \emph{actual} $g_i$. Adding the
number of earlier primes bounds every reversal family.
Combining this upper count with the unconditional reversal supply
forces many reference-eligible actual gaps, but does not lower their
maximum width without an additional distribution argument.

Neither the square-root clock displacement nor the $O(1/k)$
count correction changes a leading coefficient measured by
$\log\log X/k$. The rank is fixed in the raw reversal count;
it moves with $x$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-ratio}.

\section{Implications and scope}\label{sec:implications}
The spectrum gives a quantitative dictionary. Bounded-gap counts
yield reversal counts through the finite selection lemma; the
upper comparison shows that no other late ascents can improve
the rate. Finite changes in an admissible law preserve that rate.
Many different affine prime-pair expressions consequently share
a spectrum despite different forced primes and small ranks.

Location and frequency encode different information.
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical} depends on the least recurring gap.
The rate $\Lambda(\nu)$ depends on how frequently bounded gap
classes occur. Infinitude alone need not give $\gamma_H>0$:
a hypothetical count growing as a power of $\log X$ has
$\gamma_H=0$. This is why bare infinitude cannot replace
the input to Corollary~\ref{cor:power}.

For Goldbach-type expressions, the arithmetic theorem describes
factorization in the full representation-weighted ensemble.
It gives no lower bound for representations of each fixed even
integer. Fixed-modulus independence likewise does not supply
uniform estimates when a prime cutoff grows with input size.
Moving-tail distribution laws require additional uniformity.

Repeated raw reversals and centered moving-rank excursions have
different quantifiers. At each fixed rank there is a final decreasing
tail. The RH criterion lets the rank grow with the endpoint and
compares its ratio with the full reference profile.
It is the transfer in Theorem~\ref{thm:linearization} that relates
this second question to Nicolas's error.

The exact raw comparison can be written as
\[
 R_r(x)-(g+1)=
 \bigl(\mathcal R_r(L(x))-(g+1)\bigr)+\Delta_r(x).
\]
RH controls the second term. The first term and the occurrence of
the actual gap remain necessary for an ascent. In particular,
for fixed $1/5<b<c<1/3$ and all sufficiently large $k$, an ascent
at a prime with $bk\le\LL(p-1)\le ck$ occurs exactly when the next
gap is two. This follows directly from the uniform ratio margins.
Proving positive ascent supply in this window independently would
prove twins; the identity itself is not such a supply proof.

The Hardy--Littlewood input in Theorem~\ref{thm:hl-location}
provides actual twins approaching the eligibility cutoff.
RH instead refines that cutoff's reference error and gives
Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-capacity}. The global twin asymptotic allows
a distance $o(X_k)$ from the cutoff, which can exceed
$\sqrt{X_k}$; it cannot by itself transfer the RH square-root
clock correction to the location of the last actual ascent.
No implication from RH to twin-prime infinitude is established
by these results.

The relation to Robin and Lagarias is through their established
equivalences with RH \cite{Lagarias}. Thus the eventual inequality
in Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-ratio}, if proved independently, would imply
the corresponding universal divisor-sum inequalities. A valid
violation of either criterion would imply the false-RH coefficient
excursions. These are logical consequences of known equivalences,
not new bounds for colossally abundant numbers.
Indeed, for $n=\prod p^{a_p}$,
\[
 \frac{\sigma(n)}n=
 \prod_{p\mid n}\frac{1-p^{-(a_p+1)}}{1-p^{-1}},
\]
and optimizing the exponent-dependent numerator factors together
with $\log\log n$ remains a separate problem.

The classical inputs include Nicolas's criterion and spectral
formula, Landau oscillation, prime-factor generating products and
saddle-point estimates. The potentially new contributions are the
exact reversal-frequency spectrum, its affine realization and
the sign-preserving moving-coefficient transfer with its
applications. These attributions are provisional: proof
verification does not establish priority. Speculative RH and
independent small-gap-supply investigations are excluded from the
theorem claims of this paper.
